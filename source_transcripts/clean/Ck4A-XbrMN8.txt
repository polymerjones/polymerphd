Fourier's law of heat conduction says the rate at which heat flows through material is proportional to the temperature difference across it, the area of the surface, and critically, inversely proportional to the thickness of the material. Thicker insulation means less heat escapes per second. Thinner insulation means more. The thermal conductivity of adipose tissue is roughly 0.2 W/m K. Muscle tissue conducts heat at roughly 0.5 W/m K, more than twice as fast. Fat is a genuinely better insulator than muscle, which is why your body puts it directly beneath the skin rather deep in the core. Past 60, this insulation layer begins to thin. A process driven by declining hormone levels, reduced lipogenesis, and redistribution of fat from the subcutaneous layer to the visceral compartment around your organs.

This is forced convection, the same physics that governs how hot water moves heat through a radiator system, except the fluid is blood, the pump is your heart, and the pipes are your blood vessels. Your body even has a built-in heat conservation system for the extremities, a countercurrent exchange mechanism in your arms and legs, where warm arteries run directly alongside cool veins returning from the hands and feet.
