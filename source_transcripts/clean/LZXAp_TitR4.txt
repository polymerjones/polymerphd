Now, to understand why the dying brain does this, why it erupts instead of fading, you need to understand the single most expensive operation the brain performs. Every neuron in your brain maintains a voltage difference across its membrane, a charge separation between the inside of the cell and the outside. The inside sits at roughly -70 mV relative to the outside. That is a small number. A single AAA battery produces about 2,000 times more voltage, but that tiny gradient is everything. Maintained simultaneously across 86 billion neurons, it is what makes thought possible. [09:11] describe it, is a pattern of voltage changes. One protein is responsible for maintaining this gradient, the sodium-potassium ATPase pump, a molecular machine embedded in the membrane of every neuron. It burns ATP, the cell's energy currency, to continuously push three sodium ions out of the cell for every two potassium ions it pulls in. That is all it does. Push ions in, push ions out, maintain the charge. And it runs without stopping from the moment a neuron forms until the moment it dies. It is astonishingly expensive. It consumes roughly 40% of your brain's total energy budget. Your brain uses 20% of your body's total oxygen supply despite being only 2% of your body weight. [10:12] most energy-hungry organ in your body spent on one task, keeping the voltage at -70 mV, keeping you conscious. When your heart stops and oxygen delivery ceases, your pumps begin to fail.
