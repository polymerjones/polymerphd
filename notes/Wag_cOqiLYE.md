# Why Your Brain Produces A Pain It Cannot Feel

## Central claim

**The brain has no pain receptors anywhere in its tissue, and the pain of a migraine lives in the membranes
wrapped around it** `[00:00]`, `[28:33]`.

> "**A neurosurgeon can touch the surface of a conscious patient's exposed brain during awake craniotomy and
> the patient feels nothing. The organ that processes every pain signal from every part of your body is
> itself completely numb**" `[00:00]`.

> "**The paradox resolves not by finding pain receptors in the brain — there are none — but by finding the
> pain where it actually lives: in the membranes, in the vessels, in the nerve that wraps around both, one
> pulse at a time**" `[28:33]`.

## The wave

`[01:01]`–`[03:03]`

**Cortical spreading depression** *(first recorded by Leão at Harvard, 1944)* — **a front of neuronal
depolarisation propagating across the cortical surface at 3–5 mm per minute.**

| Position relative to the front | State |
|---|---|
| **Ahead of it** | Neurons functioning normally |
| **At the front** | **Massive depolarisation — an uncontrolled burst that exhausts energy reserves** |
| **Behind it** | **Electrically silent for 15–30 minutes** — unable to fire, unable to process |

**The aura is this wave passing through the visual cortex** `[01:01]`–`[02:03]`:

> "**The aura expands because the wave is moving.** The zigzag shape corresponds to the architecture of the
> visual cortex — orientation columns interacting with the wave."

> "**If you have experienced a migraine aura, you have watched a wave cross your own brain in real time.**
> The expanding blind spot was the wave's silent zone. The shimmering border was the depolarisation front"
> `[02:03]`.

**And the arithmetic checks out** `[02:03]`: 20–30 minutes to traverse the visual field corresponds
"**precisely to a wave moving at 3 to 5 mm per minute across the approximately 6 to 8 cm of primary visual
cortex — slower than the minute hand of a clock.**"

## Triggers as one balance, not many causes

`[03:03]`–`[04:05]`

> "**The excitation–inhibition balance tips toward excitation. Glutamate accumulates faster than the
> inhibitory systems can contain it. A runaway depolarisation develops. The wave radiates outward.**"

| Trigger | Biochemical route |
|---|---|
| **Sleep deprivation** | **Reduces GABAergic inhibition** — the brake weakens |
| **Dehydration** | **Shifts the ionic environment**, altering the depolarisation threshold |
| **Estrogen withdrawal** | **Modulates cortical excitability directly** |
| **Sensory overload** — bright light, loud sound, strong smell | **Drives excessive glutamate release** in the corresponding sensory cortex |
| **Skipped meals** | **Drops blood glucose, reducing the ATP available for the sodium-potassium pump** that stabilises membrane potential |

> "**Each trigger has a different biochemical pathway. All converge on one outcome: a focal point where the
> excitation-inhibition balance fails and the wave begins**" `[04:05]`.

## Where the pain actually is

`[08:09]`–`[11:11]`

**The meninges — three membranes wrapping the brain — can feel.** The **dura mater** is **densely innervated
by trigeminal nociceptors.**

> "**The brain has none. The membrane surrounding it has thousands**" `[08:09]`.

**Cerebral vessels running between the meninges are also wrapped in trigeminal fibres "like vines around a
trunk."**

**The cascade** `[09:10]`–`[10:11]`:

> **The wave releases potassium, ATP and inflammatory mediators → these diffuse through the pia mater to the
> dura → dural trigeminal fibres activate and release CGRP around the meningeal vessels → CGRP dilates them
> → the vessels become permeable, plasma proteins leak → mast cells degranulate, releasing histamine and
> further mediators → neurogenic inflammation.**

*(Attributed to Edvinsson, across three decades of trigeminovascular research at Lund University.)*

> "**A self-amplifying inflammatory cascade in the meninges driven by the trigeminal nerve's own neuropeptide
> release, rather than by infection or injury**" `[10:11]`.

> "**The pain is the inflamed, swollen, distended meninges pressing on and stimulating the trigeminal
> nociceptors that innervate them. The brain, sitting inside the inflamed membranes, caused the inflammation
> through the wave but is unable to feel it**" `[10:11]`.

**Why pressing the temples helps slightly** `[10:11]`: "you are compressing the superficial temporal artery
and transiently reducing the vessel distension that is stimulating the trigeminal nociceptors."

## The throbbing

`[11:11]`–`[12:13]`

> "**In normal vessels the pulse wave produces no sensation.** In inflamed meningeal vessels — where CGRP has
> dilated the walls, where plasma proteins have leaked, where the trigeminal fibres are sensitised —
> **each pulse wave stretches the already distended vessel wall. One throb per heartbeat.**"

**Which explains two familiar aggravators** `[12:13]`:
- **Exertion** — "increases heart rate, systolic pressure, and the amplitude of the pulse wave."
- **Bending forward** — "increased hydrostatic pressure in the cranial vessels from the head-down position."

## Light and sound sensitivity

`[12:13]`–`[14:16]`

*(Attributed to Noseda and colleagues, Harvard.)*

**The trigeminovascular system converges with visual pathway neurons in the posterior thalamus** — neurons
responding to **both trigeminal pain input and retinal light input.**

> "**The light does not worsen the meningeal inflammation. It amplifies the pain signal at the thalamic relay
> station where the visual and pain pathways intersect**" `[13:14]`.

> "**The dark room is a thalamic intervention** — reducing one of two inputs to a relay station already
> overloaded from the other" `[13:14]`.

**Sound works the same way** `[13:14]`–`[14:16]`: auditory processing neurons in the brainstem share
connections with the trigeminal nucleus, so "**sounds comfortable at normal sensitivity become painful
because the trigeminal activation has lowered the threshold.**"

## Allodynia — and why it predicts treatment failure

`[14:16]`–`[15:16]`

**Pain from normally painless stimuli — brushing hair, glasses touching the temples, a pillow against the
head — develops in approximately two-thirds of migraine sufferers during an attack.**

**The mechanism is central sensitisation:** sustained trigeminal firing sensitises second-order neurons in
the trigeminocervical nucleus "**to the point where they begin responding to inputs from skin
mechanoreceptors they normally ignore.**"

> **Its clinical value** `[15:16]`: "**Allodynia is a marker of how far the central sensitisation has
> progressed. Its presence indicates the nucleus has been firing long enough to sensitise beyond the
> meningeal input — and it predicts that triptans and other acute medications will be less effective,
> because the sensitisation has moved from the peripheral nerve terminals to the central processing
> centres.**"

## Prodrome and postdrome

`[15:16]`–`[17:19]`

**Prodrome** — yawning, food cravings, mood changes, neck stiffness, difficulty concentrating, frequent
urination.

> "**These are not triggers. They are early symptoms produced by the hypothalamic and brainstem changes that
> precede the cortical spreading depression**" `[15:16]`.

> **The correction that matters** `[16:18]`: "**The viewer who says *I always crave chocolate before a
> migraine* has been blaming the chocolate as a trigger, when the craving was actually the prodrome** — the
> brain's appetite centres signalling the neurochemical shift already underway."

**Postdrome** — fatigue, poor concentration, mood depression, fragile sensitivity.

> "**The migraine hangover is real and physiological.** The neurons behind the wave were electrically dead
> for 15 to 30 minutes and require hours to restore the ionic gradients and neurotransmitter stores the
> depolarisation depleted" `[16:18]`.

## Brain freeze — the same pathway in 60 seconds

`[04:05]`–`[05:07]`

> **Cold on the palate → rapid vasoconstriction → immediate reactive dilation → vessel stretch → trigeminal
> activation → pain referred to the forehead.**

> "**Identical nerve, identical central processing, identical location of perceived pain**… **The mechanisms
> are identical. The duration differs because the driving stimulus differs — a transient temperature change
> versus a sustained chemical cascade**" `[05:07]`.

## The sleep loop

`[05:07]`–`[06:08]`

> **Poor sleep reduces GABAergic inhibition → lowers the threshold → migraine → the pain fragments sleep
> architecture and reduces deep sleep → postdrome fatigue shifts sleep timing → disrupted circadian
> regulation of cortical excitability → increased vulnerability to the next migraine.**

> "**Breaking the cycle at the sleep point — maintaining consistent sleep timing even after a migraine day,
> avoiding the compensatory sleeping in that shifts the circadian clock — is one of the most effective
> threshold management strategies, because it interrupts the self-reinforcing loop at its most accessible
> point**" `[06:08]`.

## The hormonal pattern across a lifetime

`[06:08]`–`[08:09]`, `[19:22]`

> **"Estrogen does not trigger migraines while it is high. It triggers them when it falls."**

**High estrogen increases serotonin availability and GABAergic tone, raising the threshold.**

| Life stage | Pattern |
|---|---|
| **Days before menstruation** | **Estrogen drops sharply, removing the protective modulation** — the threshold falls |
| **Pregnancy** | **Often dramatic reduction** — estrogen stays high for nine months |
| **Postpartum** | **Severe recurrence** as estrogen crashes |
| **Perimenopause** | **The most unpredictable pattern** — "high one week, low the next, without the predictable cycling of reproductive years" |
| **Postmenopause** | **Often improves** — "the threshold is consistently lower, but the fluctuations that trigger the wave have stopped" |

> **Migraines are three times more common in women than men, often clustering around menstruation**
> `[03:03]`.

**And a shift in character with age** `[19:22]`: **migraine with aura may increase in older adults even as
migraine without aura decreases**, as "the cortical excitability threshold shifts in ways that favour the
spreading depression mechanism over the purely trigeminovascular mechanism." **Stiffer, less compliant
meningeal vessels produce disproportionate nociceptive activation from the same CGRP-driven dilation.**

## Two environmental modulators

`[17:19]`–`[19:22]`

### Caffeine — helps and harms by the same mechanism

| Use | Effect |
|---|---|
| **Acute, at onset** | **Adenosine receptor blockade → vasoconstriction**, counteracting CGRP-driven dilation. "Why caffeine is included in many migraine medications" |
| **Regular consumption** | **Adenosine receptor upregulation** — the vessels grow more receptors to compensate |
| **Withdrawal** | "**All those upregulated receptors respond to adenosine simultaneously, producing a rebound vasodilation more extreme than baseline**" |

> "**The weekend migraine — the headache that arrives on Saturday morning when the weekday coffee schedule is
> delayed — is caffeine withdrawal vasodilation**" `[18:20]`.

### Barometric pressure

> "When atmospheric pressure drops before a storm, **the reduced external pressure on the cranial
> vasculature allows the vessels to expand slightly.** In a sensitised trigeminal system, even this modest
> expansion is sufficient to push the distension past the nociceptive threshold" `[18:20]`.

> "**The viewer who says *I can predict the weather by my migraines* is detecting barometric pressure changes
> through the trigeminal innervation of the meningeal vessels**" `[18:20]`.

## The neck connection

`[20:22]`–`[21:26]`

*(Attributed to Bartsch and Goadsby.)*

**The trigeminocervical nucleus** — in the upper cervical cord at C1–C3 — **receives input from the trigeminal
nerve (meninges and head) *and* the upper cervical spinal nerves (neck muscles, cervical facet joints, upper
cervical ligaments).**

> "**The second-order neurons cannot distinguish the source. A signal from an inflamed meningeal vessel and a
> signal from a tense suboccipital muscle activate the same neurons**" `[20:22]`.

**Forward head posture multiplies cervical load three to four times — 5 kg becoming 20 at moderate tilt.**

> "**The viewer whose migraines consistently follow periods of sustained neck tension — prolonged desk work,
> forward head posture, sleeping in a twisted position — may be triggering the trigeminocervical pathway
> from the cervical input rather than from cortical spreading depression**" `[21:26]`.

**And it runs both ways** `[21:26]`: "**The meningeal inflammation activates the nucleus, and the nucleus
sends signals back to the neck muscles through reflex arcs, producing tightness that feels like a neck
problem.** The neck and the meninges share a processing centre, and input from either produces the output of
both."

## Medication overuse headache

`[21:26]`–`[24:30]` — **the most important practical section.**

*(Attributed to Diener and Limmroth, *Lancet Neurology*.)*

> **Taking pain medication more than 10–15 days per month risks a condition where the treatment increases
> the frequency of the headaches it was prescribed to treat.**

**The mechanism — nociceptive system upregulation** `[22:26]`:
> "When medication repeatedly suppresses nociceptive transmission, **the system compensates by increasing
> its sensitivity — upregulating pain receptors, increasing pro-nociceptive molecular expression, lowering
> the threshold for pain signal transmission. The system is attempting to maintain its ability to detect
> genuine threats despite the pharmacological suppression.**"

> **The loop** `[22:26]`: "A lower pain threshold when the medication wears off → the migraine that required
> severe intensity to break through now breaks through at moderate intensity → another dose → further
> upregulation → lower threshold → more headaches → more medication."

> "**The medication is now maintaining rather than treating**" `[23:28]`.

**And breaking it requires getting worse first** `[23:28]`:
> "**Stopping the overuse medication produces a rebound period of 1 to 2 weeks where headaches intensify
> before the nociceptive threshold recalibrates upward.** The viewer must get worse before getting better —
> **which is why the cycle is difficult to break without medical guidance.**"

> "**The medication that was suppressing pain was simultaneously training the pain system to produce more of
> it. The nociceptive system responded exactly as any adaptive system responds to chronic suppression**"
> `[23:28]`.

## What the physics points to

`[24:30]`–`[27:31]`

### Threshold management — keeping the balance away from the tipping point

| Intervention | What it maintains |
|---|---|
| **Adequate hydration** | **Ionic stability** that keeps the threshold away from wave initiation |
| **Consistent sleep timing** | **Circadian regulation of cortical excitability** and GABAergic inhibition |
| **Regular meals** | **Blood glucose fuelling the sodium-potassium pump** |
| **Head alignment — ears over shoulders, chin parallel to the ground** | **Reduced cervical loading feeding the trigeminocervical nucleus** |

> "**These are threshold management interventions — keeping the excitation-inhibition balance away from the
> tipping point rather than treating the pain after the wave has already started**" `[24:30]`.

### The CGRP medications — and why they differ categorically

`[25:30]`–`[26:31]`

**CGRP monoclonal antibodies — erenumab, fremanezumab, galcanezumab — block the specific neuropeptide the
trigeminal nerve releases around the meningeal vessels.**

> "**This is the first migraine treatment that addresses the mechanism rather than suppressing the pain
> system broadly — targeting the CGRP that dilates the vessels rather than silencing the nociceptive system
> that detects the dilation**" `[25:30]`.

> **And the consequence that follows** `[26:31]`: "**CGRP blockade does not trigger the nociceptive
> upregulation that broad pain suppression produces. The medication overuse cycle does not develop because
> the pain system is not being suppressed. The inflammatory trigger is being blocked.**"

## Clinical routing — stated by the video

`[26:31]`:

> "**Migraine with aura carries a specific vascular consideration you should discuss with your physician. The
> cortical spreading depression that produces the aura may temporarily affect cerebral blood flow in ways
> that interact with vascular risk factors, and the combination of migraine with aura and certain hormonal
> medications requires clinical assessment of the risk-benefit balance.**"

**And on breaking medication overuse** `[23:28]`: the rebound period is **"why the cycle is difficult to
break without medical guidance."**

## The inherited threshold

`[26:31]`–`[27:31]`

> "**If migraines run in a family, the children likely inherited a cortical excitability threshold that is
> lower than average.** The same triggers push their cortex toward the wave initiation threshold more
> easily."

> "**Teaching the threshold management early — consistent sleep, adequate hydration, regular meals — may keep
> the balance away from the tipping point and prevent the first episode from establishing the sensitisation
> pattern that makes subsequent episodes more likely**" `[27:31]`.

## Practices

1. **Maintain consistent sleep timing — including after a migraine day.** Compensatory sleeping in shifts
   the clock and lowers the threshold for the next one.
2. **Hydration and regular meals** — both maintain the ionic and metabolic conditions that hold the
   threshold up.
3. **Correct head alignment during sustained desk work** — ears over shoulders, chin parallel to the ground
   — to reduce cervical input to the shared nucleus.
4. **Dark and quiet rooms are mechanistic, not merely comforting** — they reduce one of two inputs to
   already-overloaded thalamic convergence neurons.
5. **Track medication days per month.** Above 10–15, the treatment may be maintaining the condition.
6. **Reinterpret prodrome symptoms as early signs rather than triggers** — the craving is not the cause.
7. **Discuss aura plus hormonal medication with a physician**, and do not attempt to break a medication
   overuse cycle unsupervised.
