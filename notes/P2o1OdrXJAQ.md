# Why Every Scan YOU Ever Had Is Still Written in Your DNA

## Central claim

**A CT scan deposits permanent entries on a ledger your body keeps and nobody reads** `[01:00]`, `[26:31]`.

> "**The scanner saw inside your body. Your body paid for the view in DNA. Whether the price justified the
> image is the question the physics leaves open**" `[26:31]`.

## The framing the video establishes first — and it matters

`[00:00]`–`[01:00]` — **stated before any of the mechanism, and it should never be separated from it:**

> "**A CT scan whose findings change treatment is worth the radiation cost. Every word of that.** A
> physician who orders a scan because your symptoms demand it, because clinical findings require it,
> because the image will determine whether you receive one treatment or another, **has made a decision the
> physics supports entirely.**"

**The distinction the video is actually about:**
> "**a scan ordered because something needs answering** versus **a scan ordered as annual surveillance that
> has never found anything and would not change your care if it did. The physics is identical in both
> cases. Whether the image justified the dose is a different question.**"

## The dose, and the comparison that makes it real

`[00:00]`, `[12:16]`

**During a scan, a ring of emitters rotates at four revolutions per second, firing photons of approximately
120 keV.** In 90 seconds, **7 to 20 millisieverts** pass through every cell in the field — **"the radiation
equivalent of 3 to 7 years of natural background exposure deposited in under 2 minutes."**

| Exposure | Dose | Background equivalent |
|---|---|---|
| **Dental X-ray** | **5–10 µSv** | ~2 days |
| **Chest X-ray** | **~0.1 mSv** | ~12 days |
| **Mammogram** | **0.4 mSv** | — |
| **Chest CT** | **7 mSv** | **~2⅓ years** |
| **Abdominal CT** | **10–20 mSv** | **3–7 years** |
| **Natural background** | **~3 mSv/year** | — |

*(Effective doses attributed to Mettler and colleagues, *Radiology*.)*

## The sequence — photon to enzyme

`[01:00]`–`[04:04]`

> **Photon strikes water. Water produces a radical. Radical breaks DNA. Enzyme finds the break. Enzyme
> repairs or fails** `[01:00]`.

**Step 1 — the photon frees an electron.** Two mechanisms dominate at 120 keV:

| Mechanism | What happens |
|---|---|
| **Photoelectric effect** | The photon is **completely absorbed** by an inner-shell electron, ejecting it at high speed |
| **Compton scattering** | The photon transfers **part** of its energy to an outer electron, ejecting it and **continuing onward with reduced energy** to interact again |

**Step 2 — the electron hits water.** In tissue that is **60% water by mass**, the most probable collision
is with a water molecule, **stripping a hydrogen atom and producing a hydroxyl radical — "one of the most
chemically reactive species in all of biology."**

**Step 3 — the radical attacks whatever is within 1–2 nanometres, during a lifetime of nanoseconds.**

| Damage | Repairability |
|---|---|
| **Single strand break** — one backbone cut | **Repairable with high fidelity** — the intact opposite strand provides a template |
| **Double strand break** — two breaks on opposite strands **within 10–20 base pairs** | **The template for clean repair is compromised on both sides** |

**The key structural point** `[03:02]`–`[04:04]`:
> "**The photon never touched your DNA directly.** It struck a water molecule. The water molecule produced
> the radical, and the radical broke the DNA. **Your own water, 60% of your mass, is the intermediary that
> converts the photon's energy into biological damage.**"

> "**A bullet passing through a plaster wall does not harm the person on the other side by reaching them.
> The shrapnel from the wall does the harm. The photon is the bullet. Your water is the wall. Your DNA is
> on the other side.**"

**The scale** `[04:04]`: **approximately one double strand break for every 20 cells in the scan field** —
across the billions of cells in an abdominal scan volume, **millions of DNA breaks from a single
exposure.**

## What the cell does

`[04:04]`–`[10:12]`

**Each damaged cell faces a decision governed by severity:**

| Damage | Response |
|---|---|
| **Minor** | **Repair** |
| **Severe — too complex or numerous for confident reconstruction** | **Apoptosis** — "the cell destroys itself rather than risk copying a dangerous mutation into its daughter cells" |

> "**Apoptosis is protective, a quality control mechanism built into every cell that has carried DNA for
> billions of years**" `[05:05]`.

> **And the biological cost sits between them** `[05:05]`: "**Breaks significant enough to mutate, but
> subtle enough to escape the self-destruct trigger.**"

### The background load the enzymes already handle

`[05:05]`–`[07:08]` — **an important calibration:**

**Four natural sources run continuously:**
- **Cosmic rays** from space
- **Radon gas** from soil, irradiating the lungs with every indoor breath
- **Potassium-40** in food, emitting radiation inside the digestive tract
- **Carbon-14 woven into your DNA itself**, decaying on its own schedule

> **Total: approximately 10,000 to 100,000 DNA lesions per cell per day**, "the vast majority repaired so
> quickly and so accurately that the cell never registers the event" `[06:06]`.

> "**The distinction between a break caused by a cosmic ray and a break caused by a CT scan photon is
> invisible at the molecular level. The chemistry is identical**" `[07:08]`.

### The three pathways

`[07:08]`–`[08:09]`

| Pathway | Handles | Speed and fidelity |
|---|---|---|
| **Base excision repair** | A single damaged base, filled from the opposite strand | **Fastest** — minutes, high fidelity |
| **Nucleotide excision repair** | **Bulkier damage that distorts the helix** — a larger section cut and rebuilt | Slower, still highly accurate |
| **Homologous recombination** | **Double strand breaks** — uses the **sister chromatid**, the complete duplicate that exists after replication | **"The biological equivalent of restoring a corrupted file from a saved duplicate"** |

**Baseline fidelity: below one error in a billion base pairs per cell division** — "an accuracy evolved
across billions of years of continuous exposure."

### Where errors survive

`[09:11]`–`[10:12]`

**Four named routes to imperfect repair:**
- **The template strand was also damaged** by a nearby radical.
- **The enzyme misread a base** during reconstruction.
- The break was too complex, so the cell **activated a faster, lower-fidelity pathway** to close the gap
  before the next division.

**Then a quality checkpoint intervenes** — clean repair means normal function resumes; a detected flaw can
trigger a second attempt or self-destruction.

> "**The mutations that survive onto the permanent record are the ones that passed both the repair system
> and the quality checkpoint**" `[09:11]`.

**Where they land** `[09:11]`–`[10:12]`:

| Territory | Consequence |
|---|---|
| **~98% of the genome** | **Silent passengers**, copied forward without consequence |
| **~2%** — tumour suppressors (**p53**, **retinoblastoma protein**) and growth-regulating **proto-oncogenes** | **The dice rolls that carry biological weight** |

**The road crew analogy** `[10:12]`: a storm opens hundreds of craters; the crew works through methodically;
most repairs are solid. "**A few are patched imperfectly because the damage was deeper than usual, because
two craters opened next to each other**… The road holds. The weakness is there."

## The ledger

`[11:14]`–`[14:18]`

> "**No dose ever resets. No cellular mechanism exists to zero the counter. Every exposure adds to one
> molecular total**" `[11:14]`.

| Period | Background accumulation |
|---|---|
| **1 year** | ~3 mSv |
| **10 years** | 30 mSv |
| **50 years** | **150 mSv** — "the baseline your repair enzymes evolved to manage without clinical consequence" |

> "**They handle it because they evolved under it**… **Every organism alive today descends from cells that
> survived this baseline load**" `[12:16]`.

**Three abdominal scans in a decade adds 30–60 mSv** — "the equivalent in years of background delivered
across four and a half minutes of total scan time" `[12:16]`.

**The odometer analogy** `[13:17]`:
> "Your car has an odometer… **The number only goes up. You see it every time you start the engine.** Your
> body has the equivalent of a radiation odometer written in accumulated mutations. **That number only goes
> up. The difference is that nobody reads it, nobody displays it, and no dashboard exists.**"

> "**Your body tracks the total in its DNA. Your medical system does not.** No centralised record
> aggregates your imaging radiation across providers, across hospitals, across the decades" `[13:17]`.

## Why nobody connects the scan to the diagnosis

`[14:18]`–`[17:21]`

**The multi-hit model** *(attributed to Knudson, *PNAS*, 1971)*: **a single mutation is rarely sufficient.**
Tumour suppressors like **p53 exist in two copies — one from each parent — and both must be inactivated**
before the cell loses growth control.

> "**This redundancy is the body's insurance against exactly the kind of damage a CT scan deposits.**"

**The worked timeline** `[15:20]`–`[16:20]`:
> **The first copy may be knocked out by the scan at 50.** The cell continues functioning because the
> second copy is intact. **Five years later, background radiation or a random copying error knocks out the
> second.** Full transformation requires multiple independent events across multiple genes.

> "**Cancer appearing at 65 is the product of mutations accumulated across 15 years from sources that were
> never connected to each other**… **The scan was ordered, the scan was normal, the scan was forgotten. The
> mutation it deposited was not**" `[16:20]`.

> "**Nobody connects the routine scan 15 years ago to the diagnosis today because the physics of multi-hit
> carcinogenesis spans a timeline longer than medical memory.**"

**The population estimate** *(attributed to Brenner and Hall, *New England Journal of Medicine*)*:
> "**The prevailing rate of CT usage may account for 1.5 to 2% of future cancers.** The risk per individual
> scan is small. **The risk across a population receiving hundreds of millions of examinations per year…
> is the arithmetic that justified their concern**" `[16:20]`.

## The age inversion

`[17:21]`–`[21:24]` — **the most useful practical section, and genuinely counterintuitive.**

**Two risks move in opposite directions with age:**

| Risk | At 45 | At 75 |
|---|---|---|
| **Radiation risk** | **Higher** — "a scan at 45 gives the deposited mutation three decades to accumulate the additional hits needed" | **Lower** — "the cancer may never develop within the remaining lifespan" |
| **Cascade risk** — from what the scan finds | **Lower** — the body tolerates biopsy, surgery, treatment | **Higher** — "anaesthetic clearance is slower, wound healing takes longer, and **the surgery a 50-year-old recovers from in 2 weeks can produce months of complications at 78**" |

> "**The dose was identical at both ages. The biological meaning of that dose changed because the body
> receiving it changed across every dimension that determines outcome**" `[19:22]`.

**The incidental finding cascade** `[20:24]`: a small pulmonary nodule, a renal cyst, a pancreatic density
**triggers a protocol** — follow-up imaging, biopsy if growth exceeds a threshold, surgical consultation if
the biopsy is indeterminate.

> "**Each follow-up scan adds to the radiation total. Each biopsy carries infection and bleeding risk that
> scales with age**… **The finding that would have been monitored and dismissed at 50 may trigger a
> sequence of investigations at 75 that the body tolerates poorly.**"

**The surveillance arithmetic** `[21:24]`:
> "**If you're 70 and have received annual chest CTs for a decade without a clinically significant finding,
> you have accumulated approximately 70 millisieverts — roughly 23 years of background equivalent — from
> scans that never changed management. Each scan was individually defensible. The cumulative cost was
> never discussed.**"

**The question the video puts to both ages** `[20:24]`:
> "**The question for both ages is whether the image will change what happens next. If the answer has been
> no for the past 5 or 10 years of identical scans, the cost has been real and the benefit has been
> theoretical.**"

## The inverted anxiety — airport scanners

`[21:24]`–`[23:27]`

**Airport body scanners use millimetre wave technology — radio-frequency electromagnetic waves at
frequencies that cannot break chemical bonds, cannot ionise atoms, cannot produce hydroxyl radicals.**

> **"The ionizing radiation dose from an airport body scanner is zero."** Zero breaks, zero radicals, zero
> mutations, zero entries on the record.

| | Dose |
|---|---|
| **Airport body scanner** | **0** |
| **Dental X-ray** | 5–10 µSv |
| **Mammogram** | 400 µSv |
| **Abdominal CT** | **10,000–20,000 µSv** |

> "**If you watch the airport scanner with unease and agree to the annual CT without a second thought, the
> risk hierarchy is inverted**… **The anxiety sits as far from the exposure that earned it as a millimetre
> sits from a kilometre**" `[22:27]`.

*(Attributed to Pearce and colleagues, *The Lancet* — **178,000 patients scanned in childhood**, with
subsequent cancer incidence **measurably elevated, dose-dependent, and consistent with a linear no-threshold
model.**)*

## What shifts the odds

`[23:27]`–`[26:31]` — **the only levers available, and the video is honest about their size.**

| Condition | Mechanism |
|---|---|
| **Endogenous radical scavengers** — glutathione, superoxide dismutase | **Intercept hydroxyl radicals before they reach DNA**, reducing the number of breaks that enter the repair queue at all |
| **Deep sleep** | **DNA repair activity peaks during deep sleep**, when cellular resources shift from daytime functions to maintenance. "A scan followed by a night of deep unbroken sleep gives the repair system its optimal operating window" |
| **Low systemic inflammation** | Chronic inflammation **suppresses repair enzyme expression and reduces fidelity** |
| **Hydration** | The radical is produced *from* water; "the concentration of reactive species depends on the composition of the surrounding cellular fluid" |

**And the honest framing of the size of the effect** `[25:29]`:
> "**Arriving at the scanner well slept, well hydrated, and with low systemic inflammation does not
> eliminate the damage from the scan. It shifts the probability of successful repair in your favour.** The
> shift is marginal per individual repair event. **Across the full repair load from a single scan, marginal
> shifts compound.**"

> "**The damage is fixed. The conditions under which the fixing occurs are variable, and probability is the
> only currency this system trades in.**"

## Practices

1. **Ask the dose before a scan.** "**The question takes 5 seconds. The answer tells me what my repair
   enzymes are about to process and whether the image justifies adding to their queue**" `[10:12]`.
2. **Ask whether the image will change management.** This is the video's central operational question, and
   it applies equally at 45 and 75.
3. **Keep your own cumulative imaging record**, because no medical system aggregates it across providers.
4. **Arrive well slept, well hydrated, and with low systemic inflammation** — marginal per repair event,
   compounding across millions of them.
5. **Recalibrate the anxiety** — the airport scanner is zero; the annual surveillance CT is years of
   background.

**What the video explicitly does not say:** it does not advise declining any scan a physician has ordered
for a clinical reason. Its stated position `[00:00]`–`[01:00]` is that such a scan **is worth the cost.**
