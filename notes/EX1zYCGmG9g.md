# What FLOSSING Does to Your Arteries — The Connection Nobody Explains

## Central claim

**Bleeding gums are a barrier failure, not a cosmetic problem.** A barrier one cell thick separates
roughly 700 bacterial species from the bloodstream, and when it opens, oral bacteria lodge at the same
arterial branch points where plaque ruptures `[00:00]`.

> "Two minutes per day — **the simplest vascular intervention nobody thinks of is vascular**" `[26:27]`.

**The headline evidence:** intensive periodontal treatment improved endothelial function **by a magnitude
comparable to starting a statin**, in a *New England Journal of Medicine* trial `[00:00]`, `[11:12]`.

## The border, and how it opens

`[01:01]`–`[04:03]`

The **gingival sulcus** — the shallow groove between tooth and gum — is sealed by epithelial cells joined
by **tight junction proteins** (claudins, occludins, ZO-1). The seal can be quantified as
**transepithelial electrical resistance (TER)**.

**Healthy tissue:** high TER, junctions tight, bacteria cannot cross.

**The failure sequence:**
1. Biofilm matures against the gum tissue for **48 hours or longer**.
2. Pathogenic species produce toxins triggering inflammation.
3. Inflammatory mediators — histamine, bradykinin, prostaglandins — **act directly on the tight junction
   proteins.** TER drops measurably.
4. The gaps between epithelial cells widen "from effectively zero to gaps large enough for bacterial
   cells to pass through."
5. **Simultaneously, inflammation dilates the capillaries beneath** — which is why inflamed gums look red
   and bleed on contact.

> "The pink in the sink is not cosmetic. **It is the visible evidence of dilated capillaries beneath a
> barrier whose structural integrity has failed under inflammatory load**" `[02:01]`.

**A subtlety worth keeping** `[03:03]`: the immune response is *part of* the damage. Neutrophils arriving
to fight the biofilm produce reactive oxygen species and enzymes that damage the tight junctions as
collateral. **"The inflammation is both the defence and the vulnerability."**

**The measured consequence** `[04:03]` *(attributed to Lockhart and colleagues, *Circulation*)*:
bacteraemia occurred in **23% of subjects after toothbrushing and 30% after flossing** — **in subjects
with pre-existing gingivitis, where the barrier was already compromised.** In those with healthy tight
junctions, rates were dramatically lower.

**This is an important nuance:** the transient bacteraemia risk belongs to the *inflamed* mouth. The
intervention closes the border rather than opening it — which is why the bleeding phase is temporary
(see the protocol below).

## Where the bacteria go

`[04:03]`–`[06:04]` — **the fluid dynamics link to the plaque material.**

| Flow condition | Location | Bacterial behaviour |
|---|---|---|
| **High laminar shear** | Straight segments | Bacteria "are swept past by the high velocity flow and cannot adhere" |
| **Low oscillatory shear** | **Branch points, bifurcations, curves** | Flow velocity drops below the adhesion threshold — **bacteria adhere, penetrate the endothelium and embed within existing plaque** |

*Porphyromonas gingivalis* produces adhesin proteins (**fimbriae**) binding receptors on damaged
endothelium.

**The finding** `[05:03]` *(attributed to Haraszthy and colleagues, *Journal of Periodontology*)*:
*P. gingivalis* DNA was detected in roughly **42% of atherosclerotic plaque specimens** — **not on the
surface, but inside the plaque tissue.**

> "**The geometry that builds the plaque is the geometry that catches the bacteria.** One physical
> principle — low shear stress at arterial bifurcations — selecting the location for both processes
> simultaneously" `[06:04]`.

**And they are not passive** `[06:04]`: their presence promotes local production of inflammatory cytokines
and **matrix metalloproteinases — the same cap-degrading enzymes** described in the plaque material, now
with a bacterial source.

> "The bacteria that entered through a one-cell-thick barrier in your mouth are now **thinning a
> 65-micrometre cap in your coronary artery**" `[06:04]`.

## The fortress and its 48-hour clock

`[07:05]`–`[10:11]` — **why brushing alone does not solve it.**

**A toothbrush reaches roughly 60% of tooth surface.** The **interproximal** surfaces between adjacent
teeth are inaccessible to bristles — and they are where biofilm is thickest, the sulcus deepest, and
gingivitis most commonly originates.

**The succession timeline:**

| Time since cleaning | Stage |
|---|---|
| Minutes | Bare enamel coated by a salivary glycoprotein pellicle |
| **2–4 hours** | **Pioneer species** adhere — not pathogenic, "they are the scaffolding" |
| **12–24 hours** | **Secondary colonisers** attach by coaggregation — complementary surface molecules binding "as specifically structured as a lock and key." ***Fusobacterium nucleatum* is the bridge organism**, binding both early and late colonisers |
| **24–48 hours** | **Late colonisers arrive** — the "red complex" pathogens, producing the virulence factors that degrade tight junctions |

**The matrix is the reason mechanical disruption is the only option** `[09:09]`: the community builds a
polysaccharide shield that **reduces antibiotic penetration roughly a thousandfold**, blocks neutrophil
access, and resists mouthwash.

> "Mouthwash cannot do it. Antibiotics at achievable oral concentrations cannot do it. The toothbrush
> cannot reach the interproximal surface to do it. **Floss physically contacts the fortress wall, shears
> it from the tooth surface, and collapses the community.** The biofilm clock resets to zero."

**A useful self-signal** `[09:09]`–`[10:11]`: the breath odour returning between sessions is **volatile
sulfur compounds** produced by anaerobic bacteria as metabolic waste. **"The stronger the odour, the more
mature the community, the closer it is to the 48-hour pathogenic threshold."**

> "If you brush twice daily and never floss, you are maintaining 60% of your tooth surfaces while allowing
> the biofilm on the remaining 40% — **the 40% most likely to produce gingivitis and bacteraemia** — to
> build an antibiotic-resistant, immune-evasive fortress indefinitely" `[10:11]`.

## The reversal evidence

`[10:11]`–`[11:12]` — **the load-bearing citation.**

*(Attributed to Tonetti and colleagues, *New England Journal of Medicine*, 2007)*: patients with severe
periodontitis received intensive treatment — scaling, root planing, comprehensive biofilm removal. **Six
months later, endothelial function measured by flow-mediated dilation had improved significantly, by a
magnitude comparable to adding a statin.**

**The mechanism given:** removing the chronic source of bacteraemia lowered inflammatory markers, which
reduced endothelial inflammation, **restored nitric oxide production**, and improved vasodilation.

## Why it gets worse with age

`[11:12]`–`[13:13]` — **every layer of defence weakens at once:**

- **The barrier thins** — fewer epithelial cell layers, weaker tight junctions at baseline.
- **Salivary flow decreases** — losing the antimicrobial enzymes (lysozyme, lactoferrin, secretory IgA)
  that suppress overgrowth.
- **Immune surveillance slows** — neutrophils in the sulcus respond more slowly and kill less
  effectively.

> "The 48-hour biofilm that was manageable at 30 is pathogenic at 60 **through no change in bacterial
> behaviour, only a change in the defences arrayed against it**" `[12:13]`.

**Medication-induced dry mouth** `[12:13]`–`[13:13]`: a side effect of **over 500 common medications**,
including antihypertensives, antidepressants, antihistamines and diuretics.

> "The flossing becomes **more** critical, not less, precisely because the pharmaceutical intervention you
> need for one condition is creating the oral environment that opens the pathway."

## Other destinations — with the evidence tiers separated

`[13:13]`–`[18:19]` — **the video is careful here and the tiers must be preserved.**

| Destination | Finding | Evidence status |
|---|---|---|
| **Infective endocarditis** | Oral bacteria colonising damaged or prosthetic heart valves | **Well established** — "antibiotic prophylaxis before dental procedures is standard medical practice for patients with specific cardiac conditions" |
| **Arterial plaque** | Bacterial DNA inside plaques; treatment improves endothelial function | **Confirmed by the NEJM trial** |
| **Prosthetic joints** | Oral bacteraemia seeding implant surfaces, where the immune system has limited reach | Described as an identical pathway; relevant given how common joint replacement is after 60 |
| **Rheumatoid arthritis** | A bacterial enzyme **citrullinates human proteins**; the immune system does not recognise the modified proteins as self. Anti-citrullinated protein antibodies correlate with periodontal severity | Mechanistic, with correlation |
| **Colorectal tumours** | *F. nucleatum* enriched in carcinoma versus adjacent normal tissue *(Castellarin, *Genome Research*)*; promotes growth and suppresses NK and T cell activity | Correlation plus mechanism |
| **Alzheimer's** | Gingipain enzymes in postmortem brain tissue correlating with tau pathology *(Dominy, *Science Advances*, 2019)* | **Explicitly labelled preliminary** |

> **The stated distinction** `[18:19]`: "The evidence for the Alzheimer's causal chain is **preliminary —
> supported by correlation, animal models, and mechanistic plausibility, but not confirmed by prospective
> human intervention trials.** The evidence for the arterial connection is confirmed by the New England
> Journal of Medicine trial. **The distinction matters and should be held.**"

**The second border** `[13:13]`–`[14:14]`: the blood–brain barrier uses the **same tight junction
proteins**, with TER 50–100× higher than peripheral capillaries. Those proteins degrade with age under
mechanical stress from hypertension and chronic systemic inflammation — **"loosened enough that bacteria
which could not cross at 30 can cross at 60."**

## Two amplifying conditions

- **Smoking** `[19:19]`–`[20:21]`: nicotine constricts gingival vessels, **so the bleeding that would warn
  you is suppressed.** Smokers often have advanced periodontitis with minimal bleeding. Meanwhile smoking
  impairs neutrophil function, reduces salivary IgA and promotes anaerobic growth.
  > "**If you smoke and your gums do not bleed when you brush, the absence of bleeding is not
  > reassurance. It is vasoconstriction concealing the inflammation.**"
- **Diabetes** `[20:21]`–`[21:21]`: a **bidirectional cycle.** High glucose impairs neutrophil function so
  the infection worsens; the resulting inflammation raises markers that **increase insulin resistance**.
  > "**For anyone managing type 2 diabetes, periodontal treatment is not separate from metabolic
  > management. It is part of it.**"

## The protocol

`[21:21]`–`[26:27]`

| Element | Specification |
|---|---|
| **Frequency** | **Once daily** — this resets the 48-hour clock |
| **Timing** | **Evening**, and **before brushing** |
| **Duration** | ~2 minutes for a full mouth |
| **Technique** | **C-shape**: guide the floss between two teeth, curve it around one tooth, **slide below the gum line 2–3 times**, curve around the adjacent tooth, repeat |
| **Type** | Waxed, unwaxed, tape, pick — **"matters less than the mechanical disruption"** |

**Why evening** `[22:23]`: salivary flow drops to its lowest overnight, so the chemical defence is
weakest exactly when an undisturbed biofilm would grow fastest. Disrupting it before sleep "resets the
community to its pioneer stage at the moment when overnight growth conditions would otherwise accelerate
its maturation."

**Why subgingival access matters** `[23:25]`: the pathogenic community is **not on the visible surface**
— it is in the 1–3 mm sulcus. "Cleaning only the visible interproximal surface removes the cosmetically
visible plaque but leaves the subgingival pathogens undisturbed."

**Water flossers** `[23:25]`–`[24:26]`: an alternative disrupting the biofilm **hydraulically** rather
than by string contact. Studies comparing them to string floss show **comparable reductions in gingivitis
and bleeding.** Specifically recommended for **arthritis in the hands, limited grip strength, or dental
work that catches floss** — "the primary reasons daily flossing stops" in the 55-plus group.

**Professional cleaning is complementary, not optional** `[25:26]`: once biofilm is left long enough,
salivary minerals harden it into **calculus** — "a mineralised bacterial fortress that no amount of
flossing will remove because the structure has converted from organic polymer to inorganic mineral." It
also provides a rough surface that biofilm reattaches to faster.
> "The professional cleaning handles the years of accumulated mineral. The daily flossing handles the
> 48-hour cycle going forward. **Both are necessary, neither alone is sufficient.**"

## What to expect, and when to escalate

`[24:26]`–`[27:29]`

**Bleeding in the first week is expected** — "the gingivitis responding to the disruption." It should
**resolve within 7–14 days** of daily flossing as inflammation subsides and the tight junctions restore.

> **Escalation stated in the source:** **if bleeding persists beyond two weeks of daily flossing**, the
> inflammation may have progressed beyond gingivitis into **periodontitis** — attachment loss, bone
> resorption, pocket depths exceeding 3 mm — **requiring professional treatment that flossing alone
> cannot resolve.**
