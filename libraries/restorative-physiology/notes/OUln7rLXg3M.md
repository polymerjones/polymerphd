# Why Eating Once a Day Helps YOUR Gut but Costs YOUR Muscle

## Central claim

**Three systems return three different verdicts on one protocol** `[01:01]`, `[22:21]`.

> "The gut votes yes. The muscle votes no. The insulin receptor abstains pending more information about
> meal composition and timing" `[02:01]`.

**And the video refuses to resolve it for you:** "The physics does not advocate… **The trade-off is the
physics. The decision is yours**" `[26:25]`.

**Its stated purpose is completing an argument other treatments leave half-finished** `[01:01]`: videos
teaching this as universally beneficial are "teaching the gut chapter and the autophagy chapter while
skipping the muscle chapter and the insulin chapter."

## System 1 — the gut votes yes

`[02:01]`–`[07:05]`

**The cleaning wave runs only when the gut is empty**, and any caloric input resets it — nutrient
detection in the duodenum suppresses the pacemaker cells that initiate the cycle.

> "**The gut does not distinguish between a full meal and a snack** for the purpose of cleaning
> suppression. Any caloric input stops the wave" `[04:04]`. The coffee with cream at 10 a.m. resets it.
> The handful of nuts at 3 p.m. resets it.

**The arithmetic** `[03:02]`–`[05:04]` — each cycle takes 90–120 minutes:

| Pattern | Resets per day | Cleaning cycles |
|---|---|---|
| Three meals plus two snacks | **5** | **6–7** |
| 16:8 intermittent fast | 3 | — |
| **One meal a day** | **1** | **12–15** |

Each cycle sweeps the full ~6 m of small intestine, "physically pushing residual material toward the
colon, scrubbing the mucosal surface and dislodging bacteria that colonised during the fed state."

**The cycle has four phases** `[06:05]`–`[07:05]`: quiescence, building irregular contractions, **the
propulsive cleaning wave (5–15 minutes)**, and transition back. *(Attributed to Deloose and colleagues,
*Neurogastroenterology and Motility*.)*

**The practical consequence** `[05:04]`–`[06:05]`: bloating, post-meal discomfort and bacterial
overgrowth respond to the extended window. "If you report that bloating decreased after starting OMAD,
you are describing the downstream consequence of 12 to 15 cleaning cycles replacing 6 to 7."

**And the motivation is irrelevant** `[05:04]`: "The cleaning wave runs whenever the gut is empty, for
whatever reason the gut is empty… **The fasting duration is everything.**"

## System 2 — the muscle votes no

`[07:05]`–`[13:10]` — **the strongest argument in the video.**

**The mTOR switch needs mechanical load and leucine together, and each activation opens a synthesis window
of roughly 2–4 hours.** But the window has a hard ceiling: **approximately 40–50 g of protein per
sitting produces maximal activation.**

> "Protein above 50 g does not increase the synthesis rate… **The body does not bank unused synthesis
> capacity from a skipped meal.** Missing breakfast does not double the ceiling at lunch. **Each meal opens
> its own window with its own ceiling**" `[08:06]`.

**Why the ceiling exists** `[08:06]`: once every ribosome in the fibre is engaged, additional amino acids
"have nothing to build with." The leucine sensors are already fully released — **"the switch is fully
open. More leucine cannot open it further."** The excess is oxidised for energy, its nitrogen excreted.

**The comparison** `[09:06]`–`[11:08]`, at identical daily protein:

| Pattern | Activations | Cumulative building time | Outcome |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Three meals of 35–40 g** | **3** | 12–16 hours | Each window starts with fully available ribosomes and fresh supply |
| **One meal of 120 g** | **1** | 2–4 hours | First ~50 g saturates the machinery; **the remaining ~70 g is oxidised** |

> "**Three separate manufacturing shifts at full capacity exceed one extended shift** where capacity is
> reached in the first hour and the remaining hours run at baseline" `[10:06]`.

**The stated arithmetic for someone over 55** `[11:08]`–`[12:10]`: roughly **120–150 g of synthesised
muscle protein per day from three activations versus ~40–50 g from one. Same protein intake, one third
the synthesis output.**

*(Ceiling attributed to Moore and colleagues, *JAMA Internal Medicine*; supported by stable isotope tracer
studies showing distributed protein produces greater 24-hour net synthesis in older adults.)*

**A secondary consequence worth noting** `[09:06]`: the kidneys process the nitrogen waste from ~70 g of
excess protein oxidation in a compressed timeframe, with blood urea nitrogen rising measurably after a
high-protein single meal.

## The second muscle problem — cortisol

`[18:16]`–`[20:19]` — **and this is the part most treatments omit entirely.**

During the final 6–8 hours of a 23-hour fast, once liver glycogen is exhausted, **cortisol drives
gluconeogenesis — and the amino acids come from muscle protein breakdown.**

> "The extended fast **simultaneously reduces muscle protein synthesis to one activation and increases
> muscle protein breakdown** through cortisol-driven gluconeogenesis. **The muscle faces a double deficit
> — fewer building signals and active cannibalisation**" `[19:17]`.

> "Net protein balance — synthesis minus breakdown — **can become negative** during extended fasting in
> older adults whose baseline synthesis rates are already reduced by anabolic resistance."

**And a specific counter-indication** `[19:17]`–`[20:19]`: for someone with already-elevated cortisol from
chronic stress or significant visceral fat, "the extended fast adds fasting-induced cortisol on top of
baseline cortisol… **the loop that drives visceral fat storage may be amplified rather than interrupted**
by a pattern that relies on cortisol for blood sugar maintenance."

## System 3 — the insulin receptor abstains

`[13:10]`–`[18:16]` — **two variables pulling opposite ways.**

| Variable | Direction | Detail |
|---|---|---|
| **Frequency** | **Favours OMAD** | Three spikes drop to one — a **67% reduction in daily signal count**, with **23 hours of recovery**, the longest any daily pattern provides. The receptor population resurfaces and is at maximum density when the meal arrives |
| **Amplitude** | **Argues against OMAD** | One meal carries the entire day's load. **Peak insulin potentially two to three times higher** than a single three-meal spike, elevated for 3–4 hours rather than 2 |

> "**OMAD gives the receptor more recovery time, but a harder single hit.** Three meals give less recovery
> time, but gentler individual hits" `[15:13]`.

**Total occupancy may be similar; the peak concentration is substantially higher.**

**What decides which dominates: meal composition** `[16:13]`:
> "OMAD with a carefully constructed meal approaches the insulin profile of two meals. **OMAD with a high
> glycaemic load amplifies the amplitude disadvantage beyond what three meals would have produced.**"

*(Attributed to Stote and colleagues, *American Journal of Clinical Nutrition* — single-meal eating
producing higher peak insulin and glucose than three meals of matched total calories.)*

## Three levers that reduce the amplitude cost

`[16:13]`–`[22:21]`

**1. Composition** — build the meal around protein, fat and complex carbohydrates rather than
concentrated starches and sugars, producing "a lower, wider insulin curve."

**2. Eating speed** `[16:13]`–`[17:15]` — **30–45 minutes rather than 15.** The stated effect: a glucose
curve peaking roughly **40% lower**, because slower absorption "gives the pancreatic beta cells time to
match insulin to glucose arrival incrementally rather than producing a massive bolus release."

> "The speed that mattered across three meals **matters more when the entire day's nutrition arrives in
> one sitting.**"

**3. Timing** `[20:19]`–`[22:21]` — **between 11 a.m. and 1 p.m.**

Insulin sensitivity is highest in the morning and declines through the day *(attributed to Sutton and
colleagues, *Cell Metabolism*, on early time-restricted feeding)*.

> Eating at 7 p.m. means **"maximum amplitude at minimum sensitivity"** — "the combination produces the
> highest possible peak insulin and the longest duration of elevated glucose from identical food. **The
> timing alone can shift the effective insulin response by 20 to 30%**" `[21:20]`.

**The window is chosen for two reasons at once:** after the cortisol awakening response has begun
declining, avoiding sympathetic compounding; and before insulin sensitivity drops in the afternoon.

## The autophagy argument

`[23:23]`–`[25:25]` — **the strongest point in OMAD's favour beyond gut cleaning.**

> "**Autophagy is a gradient rather than a binary switch**" — activating progressively with fasting
> duration, with measurable increases at 16 hours, further increases at 20, and continued elevation
> through 23.

So the extended window provides **4–7 additional hours beyond a standard 16-hour fast** — "during which
damaged organelles, misfolded proteins, and dysfunctional mitochondria are tagged for recycling and
dismantled for parts."

**Mitophagy is singled out as particularly relevant with age** `[24:23]`: selective clearance of damaged
mitochondria removes "the ones whose electron transport chains have accumulated mutations and leak free
radicals at rates the antioxidant system cannot fully neutralise. **Each damaged mitochondrion cleared is
a source of oxidative stress removed from the cell.**"

## How to decide

`[22:21]`–`[26:25]` — **the video's own framing, and it is a good one.**

> "**The physics applies to everyone. The priority differs by body**" `[23:23]`.

| Situation | Which way the trade-off falls |
|---|---|
| **Bloating, bacterial overgrowth, incomplete cleaning; no strength concerns** | "The 23-hour window is the strongest intervention the eating pattern can provide" |
| **Sarcopenia at 55+, no gut issues** | "The muscle trade-off is decisively unfavourable" — and the video adds that at 55+ **"the muscle should arguably be the priority"** |
| **Both matter — "and both always matter"** | "A calibrated compromise rather than a universal prescription" |

**If you use OMAD, the assembled protocol** `[17:15]`–`[18:16]`: **protein and fat with complex
carbohydrates, eaten slowly over 30–45 minutes, between 11 a.m. and 1 p.m.** — which "minimises the
amplitude disadvantage, avoids the cortisol compounding, captures the circadian sensitivity peak, and
preserves the full frequency advantage."

**If muscle is the priority:** three protein feedings of 35–40 g, giving three activations.

## Symptoms and body signals addressed

- Bloating that decreased after starting extended fasting `[06:05]`.
- Mental clarity and steady energy around hour 16 `[00:00]`.
- Loss of the afternoon slump `[00:00]`.
- Muscle loss or strength decline while eating adequate total protein `[12:10]`.
- Visceral fat persisting despite fasting `[19:17]`.

## Glossary terms introduced

- **Migrating motor complex** — the four-phase cleaning cycle, 90–120 minutes `[02:01]`.
- **Nutrient reset** — any caloric input suppressing the pacemaker `[04:04]`.
- **mTOR activation ceiling** — ~40–50 g protein per sitting `[07:05]`.
- **Fractional synthetic rate** — the measured rate of new protein building `[12:10]`.
- **Receptor internalisation** — receptors pulled off the cell surface under chronic stimulation `[13:10]`.
- **Frequency versus amplitude** — signal count against signal size `[13:10]`.
- **Gluconeogenesis** — glucose manufactured from amino acids and glycerol `[18:16]`.
- **Autophagy / mitophagy** — cellular recycling, and its mitochondrial-specific form `[23:23]`.
