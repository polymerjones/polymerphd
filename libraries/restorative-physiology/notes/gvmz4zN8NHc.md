# Why You Should LET Your Cat Sleep on You

## Central claim

**Contact is the entire mechanism.** A purring cat across the room delivers essentially nothing to your
skeleton; a purring cat *touching* you delivers mechanical vibration at amplitudes that meet clinical
vibration therapy thresholds `[00:00]`, `[04:06]`.

> "The physics requires nothing except contact" `[26:34]`.

**Note on scope:** this is a real mechanism video, but it is the most incidental intervention in the
collection — the claim is that an existing behaviour has an unrecognised benefit, not that anyone should
acquire a cat. Treat it as an argument for *not interrupting* something, not as a prescription.

## Why proximity does nothing and contact does everything

`[00:00]`–`[05:07]` — **the impedance argument, which is the core of the video.**

**Through air:** vibration attenuates with the inverse square of distance. At 1 metre, amplitude reaching
the body is **less than 1% of that at the cat's skin**. Across a room it disappears into building
vibration.

> "Your ear detects the purr… **your skeleton does not**" `[01:02]`.

**Acoustic impedance matching** governs how efficiently vibration crosses a boundary between materials.
Similar density, elasticity and speed of sound → efficient transfer. Dramatically different → reflection.

| Boundary | Result |
|---|---|
| **Air → human tissue** | **Over 99% of vibrational energy reflects.** It bounces off rather than entering |
| **Cat tissue → human tissue** | Impedance ratio close to one — **"nearly acoustically transparent."** The majority of energy crosses |

> "Direct tissue contact converts the cat's vibration into a mechanical input the skeleton can receive.
> **An air gap, any air gap at all, regardless of distance**, converts that same source into background
> noise" `[04:06]`.

So a cat on the same bed but not touching, or across the sofa with a cushion between, delivers nothing.

## Why the frequency happens to work

`[05:07]`–`[07:09]`

The purr sits at **25–50 Hz** *(amplitudes attributed to Muggenthaler, Acoustical Society of America)*.

**Low frequency penetrates; high frequency does not.** At 25–50 Hz, wavelengths in soft tissue are roughly
**30–60 cm**, so the vibration can traverse the full thickness of a thigh or torso. A 500 Hz vibration
"loses most of its energy within the first few centimetres."

**By the time it reaches bone** (typically 2–6 cm down), it has lost roughly **30–50% of original
amplitude** — and still meets or exceeds thresholds used in clinical vibration therapy.

*(Attributed to Rubin and colleagues, *Nature*, 2001)*: vibrations **below 1 G** produce significant
increases in bone density in weight-bearing regions. The finding was notable because the amplitudes were
"far below what anyone had previously considered mechanically significant."

## Site-specific dosimetry

`[07:09]`–`[11:12]` — **the most interesting section, because the anatomy differs by contact point.**

| Contact site | Target bone | Depth | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Lap** | **Femoral neck** | 3–5 cm | The most common osteoporotic fracture site over 65, carrying **20–30% one-year mortality**. Clinical platforms reach it through the feet — the full length of tibia and femur with attenuation at every joint. **The cat's path is far shorter** |
| **Chest** | **Sternum** | 0.5–1 cm | **Near full amplitude, almost no attenuation.** Ribs then act as **wave guides**, carrying the oscillation laterally to the vertebral bodies through the costovertebral joints — bone conducts with less loss per centimetre than soft tissue |
| **Abdomen** | **Lumbar vertebrae** | 6–10 cm | Reduced but measurable skeletal dose. The vibration also passes through the abdominal organs and **the vagal branches innervating the digestive tract**, during the parasympathetic window |

**The incidental convergence** `[10:10]`–`[11:12]`: cats seek warmth — thigh, chest, abdomen — and those
happen to be **exactly where bone sits closest to the skin.**

> "Nobody designed this alignment. The physics and the animal behaviour converge incidentally."

## What each tissue layer does on the way down

`[11:12]`–`[15:18]`

- **Fascia** — Ruffini endings and Pacinian corpuscles detect the 25–50 Hz oscillation and send
  proprioceptive signals centrally. **"The calm you feel when a cat purrs on you has a mechanical
  basis"** — measurable down-regulation of sympathetic tone.
- **Skeletal muscle** — the **tonic vibration reflex** *(Roll and Vedel, *Experimental Brain Research*,
  1982)* produces sustained subconscious contraction at the input frequency. At 25–50 Hz it maintains
  muscle tone without fatigue — the range used clinically for sarcopenia prevention. "You do not feel your
  thigh muscles contracting… but the motor units are firing."
- **Periosteum and bone** — mechanoreceptors interpret the vibration as mechanical load, triggering
  **osteocyte mechanotransduction** via fluid shear in the canalicular network, signalling osteoblasts to
  lay down new matrix. Vibration also **increases periosteal blood flow**, delivering the calcium and
  phosphate the osteoblasts need.

**The response is binary** `[14:16]`: "Below the threshold, nothing happens. Above it, deposition begins."
*(Bone formation rate increases at sub-1 G low-frequency vibration — attributed to Chin and colleagues,
*Journal of Biomechanics*, 2003.)*

**And the frequency match is not arbitrary** `[15:18]`: **trabecular bone has natural resonant modes in
the 20–50 Hz range.** At resonance, energy transfers more efficiently — "the bone amplifies the signal
rather than passively absorbing it."

## The circadian argument — why night contact is worth more

`[16:20]`–`[20:26]` — **the strongest practical point in the video.**

**Bone remodelling is circadian** *(attributed to Kawai and colleagues, *Journal of Bone and Mineral
Research*, 2014)*:
- **Osteoblast activity (building) peaks at night.**
- **Osteoclast activity (resorption) peaks during the day**, when cortisol is highest.

> "The body dismantles bone during waking hours and rebuilds it during sleep."

| | **Afternoon lap contact** | **Overnight contact** |
|---|---|---|
| Osteoblasts | Circadian low | **Maximally active** |
| Growth hormone | Low | **Peak — pulsatile release in the first hours of deep sleep, sustained overnight** |
| Cortisol | Near daytime peak, **suppressing** the anabolic response | **Circadian nadir — the brake is off** |
| Immune profile | — | Parasympathetic, reparative rather than inflammatory |
| Result | "The signal is present. The system is in the wrong phase to fully act on it" | **Every component at daily peak readiness** |

> "Every person who has pushed a cat off their lap at bedtime has interrupted a vibration therapy session
> that the bone was biologically primed to receive" `[20:26]`.

## Dose comparison

`[20:26]`–`[23:30]`

**What the cat delivers:** domestic cats purr an average of **~90 minutes per day**, in bouts of 10–30
minutes separated by quiet intervals. A cat sleeping on you gives 4–6 hours of contact.

**A distinctive anatomical detail** `[21:27]`: most animal vocalisations occur only on exhalation. The
cat's **bilateral laryngeal mechanism produces vibration on both inhale and exhale**, doubling the duty
cycle.

**Clinical whole-body vibration therapy prescribes** 10–20 minutes per session, 3–5 sessions weekly — a
weekly dose of **30–100 minutes at calibrated consistent amplitude**.

**The video's own honest comparison** `[22:29]`:
> "The comparison is not precise. **Clinical delivery is calibrated and consistent. The cat's delivery is
> intermittent and variable.**"

But cumulative weekly exposure of roughly **630 minutes** exceeds the clinical dose "by a factor of five
or more," through a shorter and more efficient transmission path.

**And one argument in the cat's favour** `[22:29]`: the intermittent pattern with natural rest intervals
**mirrors pulsed vibration protocols that have been found to produce stronger bone responses than
continuous exposure at equivalent total duration.** "The cat's delivery pattern is not a deficit. It may
be an advantage." *(Stated as "may" — keep the hedge.)*

## Practices

1. **Contact, not proximity.** A cat in the room delivers nothing. One touching you delivers everything.
2. **Let it stay overnight** — that is when the anabolic window is open.
3. **Fabric rule** `[24:31]`–`[25:32]`: **one layer, no air gap.** A single layer of cotton or flannel is
   "acoustically transparent" at 25–50 Hz — "the fabric vibrates with the cat rather than absorbing the
   vibration from it." **A thick duvet does attenuate**, because trapped air creates a secondary impedance
   boundary. So: through trousers, a t-shirt, or a bedsheet — fine.

> **Safety, stated in the source** `[25:32]`: anyone with **a bone-anchored implant, a recent fracture
> under healing, or a condition affected by sustained vibration** should discuss vibrational loading with
> their physician. "The mechanical input, while low in amplitude, is sustained and repetitive, and some
> conditions warrant caution about any vibration source."
