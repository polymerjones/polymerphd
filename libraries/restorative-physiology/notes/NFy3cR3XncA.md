# What Happens Inside YOUR Body When Sugar Stops Arriving for 21 Days

## Central claim

**Two different processes produce identical-looking changes, and there is only one way to tell them apart —
remove the substrate and see what reverses** `[01:00]`–`[02:01]`.

> "**What reverses within 21 days was substrate driven. What remains after 21 days is structural aging. The
> experiment separates the two**" `[02:01]`.

> "**21 days, one variable, the body answers**" `[27:31]`.

## Why the two look identical

`[00:00]`–`[02:01]`

**Real, irreversible aging changes** — collagen cross-linking, reduced lymphatic drainage, decreased skin
elasticity — **produce visually identical results to a reversible substrate-driven component.**

| Sign | Aging explanation | Substrate explanation |
|---|---|---|
| **Puffiness under the eyes** | Age-related lymphatic slowing | **Glycogen-bound water** |
| **A ring that no longer slides off** | Joint changes | **Interstitial fluid in the fingers** |
| **Afternoon heaviness** | Sleep fragmentation | **Glucose turbulence** |

> "**Most adults over 55 assign all of this to aging. The assignment is not wrong. It is incomplete**"
> `[01:00]`.

> "**For most adults over 55, the substrate-driven component is larger than they expect**" `[02:01]`.

**The daily version of the same demonstration** `[00:00]`: the morning face versus the same face 12 hours
later — "leaner, cheekbones more visible, jawline sharper" in the morning, "softened" by evening, as
overnight-mobilised glycogen water is fully replenished by the day's meals.

## The water

`[02:01]`–`[04:02]`

*(Ratio established by **Olsson and Saltin**, Karolinska Institute, 1970, through direct muscle biopsy
measuring glycogen content and tissue water in the same specimens.)*

| Store | Glycogen | Bound water |
|---|---|---|
| **Skeletal muscle** | **500 g** | **1.5 kg** |
| **Liver** | **80 g** | **240 g** |
| **Total (incl. kidneys, brain, cardiac muscle)** | **~600 g** | **~1.8 kg** |

> **Each gram of glycogen binds approximately 3 g of water** — "**not free-floating water moving through the
> bloodstream. Structurally bound water, hydrogen bonded to the glycogen molecules**."

**Where it shows** `[03:01]`–`[04:02]`: subcutaneous fluid over the cheekbones and orbital rims; interstitial
fluid in the dependent tissues of the ankles; the finger swelling that tightens a ring by evening; and **"in
your midsection as the soft fullness that exercise does not change, because it is not fat — it is substrate
bound fluid occupying volume no movement can specifically target."**

## The three baseline measurements

`[04:02]`–`[06:05]` — **taken on day zero, repeated at days 7, 14 and 21.**

### 1. The sock groove

> **Photograph the groove at the end of the day. Press the skin beside it for 5 seconds and time the indent
> recovery.**

| Recovery time | Interpretation |
|---|---|
| **3 seconds or less** | **Minimal fluid retention** |
| **5 seconds or more** | **Glycogen-bound water may be contributing significantly to tissue volume** |

### 2. The ring test

**Slide the ring on and off; note the resistance.** "The ring tightness is measuring interstitial fluid in
the fingers — the identical glycogen-bound water the sock groove measures at the ankle."

### 3. The face photograph

**Neutral lighting, straight ahead, same conditions each time.**

> "**The comparison is not looking for dramatic transformation.** It is looking for the periorbital puffiness
> and the jawline definition" `[05:03]`.

**Why the face changes first** `[06:05]`:
> "**The tissue is thinner and the water is closer to the surface. A 100 g reduction in facial fluid produces
> a visible change while a 100 g reduction in abdominal fluid produces none.**"

> "**These are not clinical measurements. They are personal reference points that anchor the physics to your
> own tissue**" `[06:05]`.

## Hour 4 — the gate opens

`[06:05]`–`[08:07]`

**Circulating insulin falls below the fasting threshold — approximately 5–8 µU/mL.**

> "**Insulin is a gate.** While insulin is elevated, **hormone-sensitive lipase — the enzyme that liberates
> stored fatty acids from adipocytes — is suppressed.** The body cannot efficiently access its stored fat
> while insulin is telling every cell that glucose is available and plentiful" `[06:05]`.

> "**This transition happens automatically. No decision required, no willpower involved.** The insulin drops
> because the glucose stopped arriving. The lipase activates because the insulin dropped. **Each step
> follows mechanically from the previous one**" `[07:06]`.

**Meanwhile the liver shifts from glycogen synthesis to glycogenolysis** to hold blood glucose in the narrow
range the brain requires — **~120 g of glucose per day, tolerating little deviation from 4–6 mmol/L.**

> "**You do not feel the shift. It is biochemical, subclinical, detectable only through blood sampling. But
> it is the prerequisite for everything that follows**" `[08:07]`.

## Days 1–5 — the water leaves

`[08:07]`–`[12:12]`

| Store | Depletion timeline | Water released |
|---|---|---|
| **Liver glycogen** | **12–18 hours** | ~240 g |
| **Muscle glycogen** | **2–5 days**, depending on activity | ~1.5 kg |

**Why they differ** `[10:09]`: liver glycogen is **exported as glucose to the bloodstream**; muscle glycogen
**is consumed locally by the fibres during contraction** — "walking, climbing stairs, carrying groceries,
each activity draws down the local glycogen store in the muscles that perform the work."

**The urination change** `[09:08]`: **an extra 200–300 ml per day for the first 24–48 hours** — "**not
because you are drinking more, but because your body is releasing water that was structurally bound to a
fuel substrate that no longer exists.**"

### The interpretation warning

`[10:09]`–`[12:12]` — **the most practically important passage:**

> "**The scale drops before any fat is oxidised. Two kilograms of weight lost without two kilograms of fat
> burned. The scale is accurate. The interpretation is where most people go wrong**" `[10:09]`.

> "**The change is real. The mechanism is hydraulic, not metabolic**" `[11:11]`.

**And the rebound is predictable** `[11:11]`:
> "**When the substrate returns, the glycogen stores refill within 24 to 48 hours and the water rebinds. The
> scale jumps back by the same 1.8 kg.** The person who thought they lost fat panics. **The person who
> understood the water mechanism expects the rebound and interprets it correctly. No fat was regained.**"

**Fat oxidation is happening in parallel** `[10:09]`–`[11:11]` — hormone-sensitive lipase is active, free
fatty acids are mobilising, the liver is producing ketone bodies — **but it is not what the early scale
movement measures.**

## Day 5 onward — the liver

`[12:12]`–`[16:16]`

*(Attributed to **Schwarz and colleagues**, University of California San Francisco, *Cell Metabolism*,
2017.)*

**41 subjects. Dietary fructose replaced with isocaloric starch. Same total calories, same protein, same
fat — only the sugar variable changed.** Measured by MRI, magnetic resonance spectroscopy and stable isotope
tracers.

| Marker | Before | After 9 days |
|---|---|---|
| **Liver fat (median)** | **7.2%** | **3.8%** |
| **Fractional de novo lipogenesis** | **68%** | **26%** |
| **Visceral fat** | **123 cm³** | **110 cm³** |

**All three with p < 0.01. No calorie deficit, no exercise intervention, one variable changed.**

### The evidence calibration — stated by the video

`[13:14]`–`[14:14]` — **and it should travel with the numbers:**

> "**Schwarz measured these changes in obese children and adolescents aged 9 to 18.** The hepatic de novo
> lipogenesis pathway is identical in adults… **The direction of the response is expected to be equivalent
> in adults. The specific magnitudes — the percentage drops, the timeline — have not been replicated in an
> adult cohort at this exact protocol. The physics is strong. The population context belongs with the
> numbers.**"

### Why fructose specifically

`[14:14]`–`[15:15]`

**Fructose enters through the portal vein and bypasses the phosphofructokinase checkpoint** — "the
regulatory brake that slows processing when the liver is already saturated."

> Without the brake, it feeds directly into the lipogenic pathway: **acetyl-CoA → malonyl-CoA → fatty acid
> synthase → palmitate → triglycerides.**

> "**The enzymes are still present… but without fructose arriving through the portal vein, the entire chain
> sits idle. The rate collapses — not because the machinery broke, but because the raw material stopped
> arriving**" `[15:15]`.

> "**The liver did not malfunction during the years of elevated lipogenesis. It processed what arrived with
> the efficiency the biochemistry demands. Change what arrives, and the processing changes with it**"
> `[15:15]`.

## The afternoon crash, dissected

`[16:16]`–`[18:17]`

**The sequence after a high-glycaemic meal:**

| Time | Event |
|---|---|
| **30–60 min** | **Glucose spike peaks**, triggering a proportional insulin surge |
| **60–90 min** | **Insulin drives glucose into cells faster than digestion supplies it — reactive hypoglycemia**, dipping below the fasting baseline |
| Then | **Cortisol and adrenaline rescue** mobilises liver glycogen to restore glucose |

> "**Not a single sensation, but a compound: mental fog from the glucose nadir, physical restlessness from
> the adrenaline, irritability from the cortisol — all arriving simultaneously and resolving within 30 to 45
> minutes**" `[16:16]`.

**And it runs three times a day** `[17:16]`:
- **Breakfast** → "the 10:30 dip that sends people to the coffee machine."
- **Lunch** → the classic afternoon crash.
- **Dinner** → "the post-dinner drowsiness most people attribute to a long day, but is partly a glucose
  nadir arriving on schedule 90 minutes after the meal."

> "**Three cycles per day, each one triggering a cortisol rescue**… **The body runs three stress hormone
> rescue cycles daily that it would not run if the substrate were absent**" `[17:16]`.

**By day 7–10** `[18:17]`: "**The afternoon becomes metabolically flat** — steady glucose, steady energy, no
crash, no rescue. The mid-morning coffee becomes optional rather than mandatory."

## Nocturia — a second mechanism

`[18:17]`–`[20:20]`

**The video is explicit that this adds to, rather than replaces, the gravitational explanation** it has
covered elsewhere.

**Osmotic diuresis:** when blood glucose exceeds the renal reabsorption threshold — **approximately 180
mg/dL** — glucose spills into the filtrate. **Glucose in the filtrate is osmotically active and pulls water
with it.**

> "**Most adults with prediabetes experience postprandial glucose excursions above this threshold without
> knowing it**" `[19:19]`.

**Why it is invisible clinically** `[19:19]`:
> "**The physician never sees it on the standard morning test, because the standard test measures fasting
> glucose — the valley, not the peak.** The excursion above 180 happens and resolves between office visits.
> **A continuous glucose monitor would capture it. A fasting blood test cannot.**"

> "**You experience it as a bladder appointment 4 hours later** — filling on a timeline that has nothing to
> do with how much water you drank before bed and everything to do with how high your glucose spiked after
> dinner" `[19:19]`.

**By day 10–14** `[20:20]`: excursions flatten below the renal threshold, the osmotic component resolves.
"**The gravitational mechanism may still contribute**… but the glucose-driven component — **often the larger
contributor in prediabetic adults** — has been removed."

> "**The change is renal, not neurological. A threshold that is no longer being breached**" `[20:20]`.

## Week 3 — receptor re-expression

`[20:20]`–`[23:26]`

**HOMA-IR** = fasting glucose × fasting insulin ÷ 405.

**The receptor biology** `[21:22]`:
> "When insulin levels are chronically elevated — when three to five sugar-containing meals per day maintain
> insulin above the fasting threshold for most of the waking hours — **the cells reduce the number of
> receptors they express on their surface.** This is down-regulation… **the cellular equivalent of turning
> down the volume when the music is too loud.**"

> "**Damaged is the wrong word. They are internalised** — pulled from the cell surface into the interior and
> stored. **Remove the excessive insulin signal and the cells re-express the receptors.**"

**Timeline: 2–4 weeks in most interventional studies**, "consistent with the rate at which cells recycle
receptor proteins back to the membrane surface."

> **The cascade** `[22:23]`: less glucose → less insulin secreted → receptors upregulate → cells more
> responsive per unit of insulin → **the pancreas secretes less** → "**the beta cells, which have been
> working at elevated output for years, receive their first sustained reduction in workload.**"

### The primary care cohort

`[22:23]`–`[23:26]` *(attributed to **David Unwin**, a general practitioner in Southport)*

**154 patients, mean age 63, carbohydrate restriction under medical supervision:**

| Outcome | Result |
|---|---|
| **Mean systolic blood pressure** | **Fell 10.9 mmHg** |
| **HbA1c** | Improved in the diabetic subgroup |
| **Antihypertensive prescriptions de-prescribed** | **21.5%** |
| **Type 2 diabetes medications de-prescribed** | **12%** |

> "**The timeline extended over months, but the direction established within the first 3 weeks and continued
> linearly**" `[23:26]`.

**Triglycerides** `[23:26]`: begin declining within **2–3 weeks** — "the triglyceride decline on the blood
test is the identical assembly line slowdown Schwarz measured in the liver with MRI, observed from a
different measurement point."

## SAFETY — stated by the video, and non-negotiable

`[24:28]`:

> "**Anyone on insulin or a sulfonylurea medication must not undertake sudden strict carbohydrate reduction
> without direct medical supervision. These medications are dosed to a specific glucose load. Removing the
> glucose load without adjusting the medication dose can produce dangerous hypoglycemia — blood glucose
> dropping below the threshold for normal brain function. This is not theoretical risk. Anyone on these
> medications needs their prescribing physician involved from day one.**"

## The protocol

`[24:28]`–`[25:29]`

> "**Substrate removal is the intervention, not calorie reduction. Total calories remain matched to the
> baseline. This is not a diet in the conventional sense of eating less.**"

| Element | Specification |
|---|---|
| **The one variable** | **Free sugar and refined carbohydrate below 50 g per day** |
| **Protein and fat** | **Stable or slightly increased**, replacing the caloric gap |
| **Everything else** | **Constant** |

## The 21-day shape

`[25:29]`–`[26:30]`

| Days | What happens |
|---|---|
| **1–3** | **Cravings peak** — "the hypothalamic appetite circuits respond to the absence with increased hunger signalling." **Mild headache** common, partly from glycogen depletion altering cerebral glucose supply, partly caffeine interaction if sweetened coffee was part of the pattern. **Urination increases** |
| **4–7** | **Cravings diminish** as the metabolic switch completes and the brain adapts to mixed fuel. Energy dips then steadies. **The face begins to change — depuffing visible against the day-zero photograph** |
| **8–14** | **Afternoon crashes flatten. Sleep consolidates if nocturia was glucose-driven.** "The distinction between aging and substrate effect becomes personally observable for the first time" |
| **15–21** | **Energy steadies at a baseline that may feel unfamiliar** — "not the peaks and crashes of glucose cycling, but a flat sustained availability." Biomarkers show the predicted direction: **fasting glucose lower, triglycerides lower, fasting insulin lower** |

## Practices

1. **Take the three baseline measurements on day zero** — sock groove with timed indent recovery, ring
   resistance, face photograph in fixed conditions. Repeat at days 7, 14 and 21.
2. **Reduce free sugar and refined carbohydrate below 50 g per day**, holding total calories, protein and
   fat constant. **This is a substitution, not a restriction.**
3. **Expect the first week's scale drop to be water, and expect it to return if the substrate returns.**
   Interpreting it as fat in either direction is the common error.
4. **Expect the sequence in order** — water, then liver fat and glucose stability, then nocturia, then
   insulin sensitivity.
5. **If on insulin or a sulfonylurea, involve the prescribing physician from day one.**
