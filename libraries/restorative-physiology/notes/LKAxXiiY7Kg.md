# Can Suppressing Tears Actually Harm You?

## Central claim

Crying is not an emotional event with physiological side effects. It is a **coordinated four-pathway physiological program** — chemical excretion, autonomic reset, pain modulation and social signalling — that the body initiates when stress hormone load exceeds what baseline clearance can handle. Suppressing it suppresses all four. `[27:25]` `[24:24]`

> "The emotion triggers it. The physiology is the function. The relief is the evidence that the function was performed." `[27:25]`

**The felt sequence, decoded** `[00:00]`: the tightness in the throat is the body preparing the laryngeal muscles for the motor pattern of sobbing; the pressure behind the eyes is the lacrimal gland engorging with blood as parasympathetic innervation increases secretory capacity; the trembling is the autonomic nervous system changing gears.

## Frey's 1981 experiment

**William Frey**, biochemist, University of Minnesota. He collected emotional tears — from grief, sadness, the states that make people cry — and compared their composition to reflex tears from the same subjects exposed to onion vapour. `[01:00]`

> "Something so simple that the fact it had not been done reveals how completely the scientific community had dismissed tears as a subject of biochemical investigation." `[01:00]`

The prior assumption: reflex and emotional tears come from one gland, look identical under a microscope, feel identical on the face, and had been assumed for centuries to be identical in composition — saline for eye protection, varying only in volume. `[01:00]` `[02:00]`

| Reflex tears | Emotional tears |
|---|---|
| Saline with lysozyme and lipid — the standard lubricating fluid protecting the corneal surface `[02:00]` | All of the above **plus**: cortisol (primary glucocorticoid stress hormone); ACTH (the pituitary hormone driving cortisol production); leucine enkephalin (an endogenous opioid peptide, a naturally produced painkiller); prolactin (elevated in stress, involved in immune regulation and emotional processing); higher potassium; higher manganese; higher total protein `[02:00]` `[08:06]` |

> "Stress hormones, opioid peptides, neuromodulators in eye fluid, compounds that had no business being in tears unless the body was using the tears as an excretion route." `[02:00]`

**Why this is a *selective* excretion** `[03:01]`: "The kidneys filter cortisol, but they filter everything. Cortisol is one molecule among thousands in the renal filtrate. The lacrimal gland, during emotional crying, selectively targets the stress-related compounds. The excretion is specific."

## Mechanism 1 — The lacrimal gland as a small kidney

**Chain:** limbic activation → parasympathetic signal via the greater petrosal nerve (a branch of the facial nerve, cranial VII) → blood flow to the gland increases, secretory rate increases, **the selectivity of the transport proteins changes** → the gland shifts from simple lubricating fluid to a secretion enriched in stress-related compounds → excreted through the eye surface. `[04:03]` `[05:04]`

**The kidney parallel, stated as structural rather than loose** `[04:03]` `[05:04]`: the lacrimal gland produces tear fluid by filtering blood plasma through a secretory epithelium, the way the kidney produces urine by filtering blood through the glomerulus. Both use membrane transport proteins to decide what stays in blood and what leaves. Both are under neural and hormonal control that changes filtration selectivity with the body's state.

- Difference in scale: the kidney processes litres per hour, the lacrimal gland microlitres — "but the selectivity is, if anything, more precise in the lacrimal gland during emotional crying, because the range of compounds it concentrates is narrower and more specifically stress-related." `[05:04]`
- **"The lacrimal gland is, in a meaningful sense, a small kidney behind your eye."** `[05:04]`
- The neural input is the **program selector**: "Change the input and the output changes, from lubrication to detoxification, from maintenance to excretion, from automatic to emotionally driven." `[06:05]`

## Three fluids from one gland

| Fluid | Composition | Trigger | Function |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Basal** | Water, mucin, lipid, lysozyme, lactoferrin | Continuous, unconscious | Maintains the thin film preserving corneal optical clarity `[07:06]` |
| **Reflex** | Similar composition, higher volume | Trigeminal sensory irritation — onion vapour (propanethial S-oxide), wind, a particle in the eye | Flushes irritants; protective, mechanical `[06:05]` `[07:06]` |
| **Emotional** | All of the above plus cortisol, ACTH, leucine enkephalin, prolactin, elevated potassium, manganese and total protein | Limbic activation — anterior cingulate cortex, insula, hypothalamus. Grief, joy, frustration, profound loss, overwhelming beauty | Excretion of stress-related chemistry `[06:05]` `[08:06]` |

**Why onion tears bring no relief** `[06:05]` `[07:06]`: "The onion tears flushed your cornea. The emotional tears would have flushed your cortisol. The gland cannot run both programs simultaneously, and the onion activates the wrong one for stress hormone excretion."

> "The body did not build three separate glands for three separate functions. It built one gland with three programs and wired each program to a different neural pathway." `[08:06]` `[09:06]`

Only the excretory program is connected to the limbic system, only it is triggered by emotion, and only it produces fluid chemically distinct from simple saline. `[09:06]`

## Mechanism 2 — Sobbing as an involuntary vagal reset

**The motor pattern** `[09:06]` `[10:07]`: a coordinated event involving respiratory system, larynx, diaphragm and abdominal muscles. Each sob is a convulsive inspiration — a rapid deep inhalation against a partially closed glottis — followed by an extended shuddering exhalation.

| Phase | Duration |
|---|---|
| Inspiratory | 0.5–1 second |
| Expiratory | 3–8 seconds |
| **Exhale:inhale ratio** | **approximately 4:1 to 8:1** |

This exceeds the 3:1–4:1 ratio the humming discussion identified as maximally parasympathetic. `[10:07]`

**Chain:** prolonged exhalation slightly raises intrathoracic pressure → stimulates baroreceptors in the aortic arch → vagal output slows the heart → **each sob cycle delivers a vagal pulse**. Five minutes of intense crying may produce **30–60 sob cycles** — "a sustained parasympathetic bombardment that no voluntary breathing exercise replicates." `[10:07]` `[11:09]`

**The involuntariness is the feature** `[11:09]`:
> "You cannot sob without stimulating the vagus. The anatomy prevents it. The exhalation is too long, the intrathoracic pressure too elevated, the baroreceptor response too reliable. **The body has built a vagal reset mechanism and disguised it as an emotional response.**"

Post-cry exhaustion — the specific fatigue, lowered heart rate, softened muscle tension, heaviness in the limbs — is parasympathetic rebound from that sustained vagal stimulation. `[12:10]`

## Why both mechanisms are required

`[12:10]` `[13:11]`

| Situation | What is delivered |
|---|---|
| Tears **and** sobs | Full relief — cortisol excreted **and** autonomic reset |
| Quiet tears rolling down the cheek without breathing convulsions | Partial — excretion without the reset |
| Dry heaving without tears (as extreme grief sometimes produces) | Partial — reset without the excretion |

> "The lightness is the cortisol leaving through the tears. The calm is the vagus resetting through the sobs." `[13:11]`

## Mechanism 3 — Leucine enkephalin and the nasolacrimal route

Leucine enkephalin binds **delta opioid receptors** in brain and spinal cord — part of the endogenous opioid system modulating pain perception, emotional distress and reward. Its presence in tears opens two possibilities. `[13:11]` `[14:13]`

**Possibility 1 — clearance:** the body is excreting excess enkephalin produced during the stress response, clearing the neurochemical environment as part of the return to baseline, a parallel excretion route to the kidneys' clearance of cortisol. `[14:13]`

**Possibility 2 — reabsorption:** tears do not evaporate off the eye surface, they *drain*. **Chain:** tear fluid → nasolacrimal duct (a narrow channel from the inner corner of the eye through the maxillary bone) → nasal cavity → nasal mucosa, a thin, highly vascular absorption surface that **bypasses first-pass hepatic metabolism**, which is why nasally administered drugs reach the bloodstream within minutes → possible mild analgesic effect. `[14:13]` `[15:13]`

> "Your body would be manufacturing an opioid in the stress response, excreting it through the lacrimal gland into the tear fluid, draining it through the nasolacrimal duct into the nasal cavity, and absorbing it through the nasal mucosa. A self-administered analgesic delivered through the body's own anatomical plumbing." `[15:13]`

*(This also explains why the nose runs when you cry — the tears are draining into the nasal cavity through a bony channel.)* `[14:13]`

**Numbness vs calm — two different post-cry states** `[16:14]` `[17:15]`:

| State | Pathway |
|---|---|
| **Calm** — lowered heart rate, softened muscle tension | Parasympathetic; the vagal reset |
| **Numbness** — flatness of affect, mild dissociation, emotional pain chemically dampened rather than resolved | Pharmacological; a pain-modulating compound acting on opioid receptors |

"The numbness is not psychological shutdown. It may be pharmacological." `[17:15]`

## Mechanism 4 — The social signal

**Optical component** `[21:20]`: the lacrimal film increases the reflectivity of the sclera, making eyes appear glossy, luminous, visually prominent. Pupils may dilate slightly from the autonomic shift. Periorbital skin reddens from increased blood flow. Wet skin below the eye reflects light differently than dry skin. The pattern of tear tracks is a visual signal other humans process **preconsciously** — "before deliberate evaluation, before the observer has decided to respond."

**Chemical component** `[21:20]` `[22:22]`: Hasson and colleagues at the Weizmann Institute, published in *Science* — emotional tears contain a **chemo signal**, a volatile compound that when inhaled by male subjects reduced testosterone levels and self-reported sexual arousal.

> "The tears communicated a chemical message through the air, a message the observer could not identify as a scent, could not trace to the tears, but that changed their hormonal state measurably within minutes… The response was precognitive." `[22:22]`

**Living alone** `[22:22]` `[23:22]`: for the person over 65 whose tears fall with no observer, three of four mechanisms function regardless of audience — excretion, vagal reset, enkephalin pathway. Only the communication function operates in an empty room. "The body is broadcasting a signal no one receives… The biology does not know the room is empty. It sends the signal anyway."

## The cost of suppression

**What suppression stops, itemised** `[17:15]` `[18:16]`:

| Suppressed pathway | Consequence |
|---|---|
| Lacrimal excretion | Cortisol, ACTH and prolactin **remain in circulation**, continuing to act on every cortisol-sensitive system — immunosuppression, glycation acceleration, telomere shortening |
| Vagal reset | The sympathetic activation that triggered the crying response persists without its countermeasure; heart rate stays elevated, muscle tension persists, autonomic balance stays shifted sympathetic |
| Enkephalin pathway | The opioid never reaches the nasal mucosa; pain modulation does not occur; emotional pain remains at its pre-crying intensity |
| Social signal | The chemo signal is never released, the optical signal never displayed; "the people who would have responded never received the communication the body was trying to send" `[25:24]` |

**Why it compounds** `[18:16]` `[19:17]`: "the cortisol that was not excreted continues circulating, the autonomic imbalance that was not corrected persists. And the next stress event arrives on top of the residual chemistry from the last event that was not cleared. The person who suppresses crying chronically is accumulating the biochemical residue of every stress response the body tried to clear through tears and was prevented from clearing."

**On the generation raised to suppress** `[24:24]` `[25:24]` `[26:25]`: for the person taught that crying is weakness, that composure is strength, that holding it in is what adults do — "you have been suppressing a detoxification system for decades."

> "Composure was real. The cost was biochemical… The strength was genuine. Suppressing an involuntary motor program requires significant cortical effort. The price of that strength was paid in every system the crying program was designed to service." `[26:25]`

## Depression and reduced catharsis

**Rottenberg and colleagues**, *Journal of Abnormal Psychology* — individuals with major depression cry with comparable frequency to non-depressed individuals but report **significantly less cathartic relief**. `[19:17]`

Proposed explanation: the parasympathetic innervation driving emotional tear production is modulated by serotonin and norepinephrine, which are dysregulated in depression. A person whose parasympathetic tone is compromised may produce tears with reduced stress hormone concentration — the excretory program running at reduced capacity because the neural signal driving it is attenuated. `[19:17]` `[20:19]`

> "They cry. The tears flow. The relief does not arrive. The crying looks identical from the outside. Something in the biochemistry is different. The person learns through repeated experience that crying does not help, and the learning is accurate for them because their crying is not performing the excretory function that produces the relief." `[20:19]`

## Practices

|---|---|---|---|
| **Allow the crying program to run** when the body initiates it — the throat tightness, the pressure behind the eyes, the trembling before the first sob | "Allowing the program to run is not weakness. It is permitting a detoxification system to function" | All four pathways | At the moment the body starts it `[26:25]` `[27:25]` |
| **Do not force tears** | "Forced crying does not activate the limbic lacrimal pathway because the emotional trigger must be genuine for the gland to shift into its excretory program" | Program selection is by neural input | — `[26:25]` |
| Let the sobbing happen, not only the tears | Full relief requires both systems; tears alone give excretion without reset, sobs alone give reset without excretion | Lacrimal excretion + baroreceptor vagal pulses | Within the episode `[13:11]` |

**The instruction, stated plainly:** *"What works is stopping the prevention."* `[26:25]`

## Symptoms and body signals addressed

- **Post-cry lightness and clarity** — measurable reduction in circulating stress molecules; "the relief is chemical. The tears are the delivery vehicle. The eye is the exit." `[04:03]`
- **Post-cry exhaustion, heaviness in the limbs, lowered heart rate** — parasympathetic rebound from 30–60 vagal pulses. `[12:10]`
- **Post-cry numbness and flatness of affect** — a mild opioid effect, distinct from the parasympathetic calm arriving alongside it. `[16:14]`
- **Throat tightness and pressure behind the eyes before crying** — laryngeal preparation and lacrimal engorgement; the body's initiation signal. `[00:00]`
- **No relief from onion tears despite equal volume** — the wrong secretory program was running. `[06:05]`
- **Crying without relief (in depression)** — attenuated parasympathetic drive producing tears with reduced stress hormone content. `[19:17]` `[20:19]`
- **Running nose while crying** — nasolacrimal drainage into the nasal cavity. `[14:13]`
- **Chronic accumulated stress in habitual suppressors** — unexcreted cortisol and uncorrected autonomic imbalance stacking across episodes. `[19:17]`

## Glossary terms introduced

- **Lacrimal gland** — a small exocrine gland above and lateral to each eye, producing tear fluid by filtering blood plasma through a secretory epithelium. `[04:03]`
- **Basal / reflex / emotional tears** — the three distinct fluids and their triggers. `[07:06]` `[08:06]`
- **ACTH (adrenocorticotropic hormone)** — the pituitary hormone driving cortisol production. `[02:00]`
- **Leucine enkephalin** — an endogenous opioid peptide binding delta opioid receptors. `[02:00]` `[13:11]`
- **Prolactin** — hormone elevated during stress, involved in immune regulation and emotional processing. `[02:00]`
- **Greater petrosal nerve** — the parasympathetic branch of the facial nerve (cranial VII) innervating the lacrimal gland. `[05:04]`
- **Propanethial S-oxide** — the irritant vapour onions release, acting on trigeminal sensory afferents. `[06:05]`
- **Nasolacrimal duct** — the channel from the inner corner of the eye through the maxillary bone to the nasal cavity. `[14:13]`
- **First-pass hepatic metabolism** — the liver processing that nasal absorption bypasses. `[15:13]`
- **Chemo signal** — a volatile compound that alters an observer's hormonal state without being consciously detected as a scent. `[21:20]`

## Source-stated confidence

- **Explicitly hedged — enkephalin nasal reabsorption** `[15:13]` `[16:14]`: "The anatomy permits it. The pharmacology of nasal absorption supports it. The specific measurement… has not been performed. The possibility is anatomically coherent. It is not a confirmed pathway." The clearance-versus-reabsorption question is left open, with both allowed to operate simultaneously.
- **Explicitly hedged — depression** `[19:17]` `[20:19]`: "The reduced relief is documented. The biochemical explanation is inferred. No study has compared the chemical composition of emotional tears in depressed versus non-depressed individuals… The inference is consistent with the parasympathetic impairment model, but has not been directly confirmed."
- **Explicitly bounded — the chemo signal** `[22:22]`: the Hasson study "specifically measured male response to female tears. Whether male tears produce equivalent chemo signals or whether the effect operates across all sex combinations has not been tested."
- **Presented as firm:** Frey's compositional finding and the specific compounds listed; the three-fluid model and their triggers; sob phase durations and the 4:1–8:1 exhale ratio; 30–60 sob cycles in five minutes; the anatomy of the greater petrosal nerve and nasolacrimal duct; Rottenberg's documented finding of comparable crying frequency with reduced relief in depression.
- **Open question stated as open** `[30:29]` `[31:31]`: *why the eyes?* Of all available routes — kidneys, sweat glands, lungs, liver — evolution built a stress hormone excretion pathway through the lacrimal gland, wired it to emotional processing rather than autonomic reflex, paired it with a breathing pattern no voluntary technique matches, made the output visible, equipped it with a chemo signal, and routed the drainage to a mucosal absorption surface. "Why this route exists… remains an open question. The chemistry says tears are functional. The anatomy says the coordination is deliberate. The body has an answer. We have not yet found it."

Named researchers: Frey (University of Minnesota, 1981, tear composition), Hasson (Weizmann Institute, *Science*, tear chemo signal), Rottenberg (*Journal of Abnormal Psychology*, crying in depression).
