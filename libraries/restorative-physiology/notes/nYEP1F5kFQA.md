# Why YOUR TEETH Start Breaking After 50

## Central claim

**The bread was the last cycle. The fracture was the history** `[00:00]`, `[25:29]`.

A tooth that survived roughly 30 million loading cycles fails on soft bread because each of those cycles
propagated a crack network by an increment too small to feel.

> "The failure appeared instantaneous from the outside. **The failure was 30 years in the making from the
> inside**" `[00:00]`.

## The material with no repair crew

`[00:00]`–`[02:02]`

Enamel is **96% mineral by weight** — the highest of any tissue — hydroxyapatite crystals packed in
parallel rods. **Harder than steel, more brittle than glass**, and laid down **once**, before the tooth
erupted, by cells that die when the job is done.

> "The enamel on your teeth at 60 is the same enamel that formed before the tooth erupted in childhood.
> **Every crack, every acid exposure, every thermal cycle it has absorbed since then is written into the
> crystal as accumulated damage that no biological process can erase**" `[01:01]`.

**The contrast that makes the point** `[02:02]`: bone remodels continuously; skin replaces its surface
every two to three weeks. The closest parallel is the lens of the eye — also laid down once — **but the
lens sits in a protected fluid chamber. The enamel sits in "the most mechanically, thermally, and
chemically hostile environment in the body."**

## Mechanism 1 — fatigue fracture

`[02:02]`–`[07:04]`

| Quantity | Value |
|---|---|
| Stress per chewing cycle | **~70 megapascals** |
| Ultimate fracture strength | **~380 megapascals** |
| Safety margin per bite | **A factor of five** |
| Cycles per meal | 1,000–2,000 |
| **Total by age 55** | **~30 million** |

**Every individual bite is safe. The accumulation is not.**

**Paris law** `[04:03]`: each cycle advances existing micro-cracks by an increment determined by the stress
concentrated at the crack tip. **And the process is self-accelerating** — stress intensity rises with the
square root of crack length, so "a crack twice as long concentrates stress approximately 1.4 times more
intensely at its leading edge."

> "The first 20 million cycles may propagate the network by a fraction of a millimetre. **The last million
> cycles may drive it through the remaining crystal in weeks**" `[04:03]`.

**Which is why it feels sudden** `[05:03]`: "growing slowly for years, then rapidly in the final months,
then catastrophically on the final cycle."

**The aircraft wing comparison** `[05:03]`–`[06:04]`: same physics, but a wing has **a certified fatigue
life, scheduled inspections, and the option of replacement.** Enamel has **none of these** — "no
inspection schedule, no replacement option, and no living cells maintaining the structure between
cycles."

**The food is irrelevant** `[06:04]`: "The patient who tells the dentist *it broke on bread* is describing
the trigger, not the cause… **A tooth that would have broken on bread would have broken on anything else
that applied the same chewing load. The failure was waiting for any load.**"

**Grinding accelerates the timeline** `[03:02]` by adding thousands of nightly cycles "at stress levels
that may exceed normal chewing because the grinding forces are unregulated by the feedback mechanisms
that limit conscious biting."

*(Crack propagation rates attributed to Arola and colleagues, *Journal of Dental Research*.)*

## Mechanism 2 — thermal cycling

`[07:04]`–`[11:12]` — **where the cracks start.**

The **dentino-enamel junction** bonds two materials with different thermal expansion coefficients —
roughly **11.4 versus 8.3 × 10⁻⁶ per °C.**

**Hot coffee:** both expand, **enamel faster than the dentine it is bonded to** → shear stress at the
interface. **Cold water after:** both contract at different rates → shear stress in the opposite
direction.

> "The same stress that causes tile to crack when the concrete beneath it expands at a different rate"
> `[08:06]`.

**So cracks nucleate preferentially at the junction** — the only place in the tooth where two dissimilar
materials are permanently bonded, "and every thermal swing tests that bond."

> "**Thermal cycling alone does not crack the enamel. It initiates micro-cracks at the interface that the
> mechanical chewing cycles then propagate**" `[09:08]`.

**Roughly 33,000 thermal cycles across 30 years** at three hot-to-cold transitions daily — and the video
notes this **under-counts for habitual hot-then-cold drinkers**, since coffee followed immediately by cold
water delivers "back-to-back shear reversals" within minutes `[10:09]`.

## Mechanism 3 — chemical dissolution

`[11:12]`–`[15:18]` — **the Stephan curve, and the frequency arithmetic that follows from it.**

Plaque bacteria cleave sugar and, under the anaerobic conditions beneath the biofilm, produce **lactic
acid** trapped against the enamel.

| Marker | Value |
|---|---|
| Resting surface pH | 7.0 |
| **Within 5 minutes of sugar** | **drops to ~4.5** |
| **Critical pH for enamel dissolution** | **5.5** |
| Recovery time | 20–40 minutes |

**Below 5.5 the crystal dissolves** — calcium and phosphate leaving the lattice.

**Saliva reverses it** `[12:13]`–`[13:15]`: it is a **supersaturated** solution of calcium and phosphate,
with bicarbonate buffering the acid. Once pH climbs back above threshold, minerals deposit back onto the
crystal — **remineralisation.**

**The balance is entirely a frequency question:**

| Pattern | Acid cycles | Dissolution time | Balance |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Three meals, no snacks** | 3 | **60–120 min** | **~12:1 in favour of repair** |
| **8–10 eating events** | 8–10 | **160–400 min** | **Net dissolution** |

> "Each new sugar exposure **resets the pH to 4.5 before the previous curve has fully recovered**"
> `[14:16]`.

**The reframe that matters** `[14:16]`–`[15:18]`: **"The sugar content mattered less than the
frequency."** The same total sugar across six eating events produces double the dissolution time and half
the recovery.

*(Attributed to Stephan, *JADA*, 1944.)*

## Fluoride — engineering the crystal

`[15:18]`–`[18:18]`

Fluoride ions substitute for hydroxyl groups in the lattice, converting hydroxyapatite to **fluorapatite**
— **one ion swapped per unit cell.**

> **The critical pH drops from 5.5 to approximately 4.5.** "The acid still forms. The pH still drops, but
> the crystal resists dissolution across a wider pH range" `[16:18]`.

**Which changes the outcome of an ordinary meal** `[16:18]`: a normal Stephan curve bottoming out around
4.5–5.0 sits **below** the original threshold but **above** the fluorapatite one.

**How it gets incorporated** `[17:18]`: toothpaste delivers ions to the surface; during the **next
remineralisation cycle**, they are built into the regrowing lattice.

> "The toothpaste is **engineering the crystal at the atomic level, one ion substitution at a time**,
> rebuilding the surface with a more durable material than the original."

**Which is why the evening application matters most** `[18:18]`: skipping it "eliminates the fluoride
delivery that would have been incorporated during the overnight remineralisation window — **the longest
uninterrupted repair period of the day.**"

*(Attributed to Featherstone, *JADA*.)*

## The repair system, and what suppresses it

`[18:18]`–`[21:23]` — **the explanation for accelerating decay in the late 50s.**

**Every component of the repair runs through saliva:** fluoride delivery, calcium and phosphate for
rebuilding, bicarbonate for buffering, and the washing action that clears acid.

**Dry mouth from medication** — antihistamines, antidepressants, antihypertensives, diuretics — reduces
flow either by blocking the receptors that trigger secretion or by reducing the fluid volume available.

> "**Less saliva means less buffer** — the Stephan curve drops deeper and recovers slower. **Less saliva
> means less supersaturated mineral solution** — the remineralisation that maintained the balance for four
> decades weakens. **The dissolution runs unopposed**" `[19:20]`.

**Nights are the vulnerable window** `[19:20]`–`[20:21]`: salivary flow falls naturally to **about 10% of
the daytime rate** during sleep. **If medication has already reduced the daytime rate, the overnight rate
may approach zero** — eight hours without buffering, mineral deposition, or washing, while bacteria
continue metabolising residual sugars from the evening meal.

> "**The teeth did not suddenly become vulnerable. The repair system that maintained them was
> pharmacologically compromised**" `[20:21]`.

**And the timeline is often identifiable** `[24:29]`–`[25:29]`: "Many viewers can identify the year the
dental problems accelerated, and that year often coincides with the year a new medication was added…
**The decay that followed was pharmacological, not dietary. The crystal was still dissolving after every
meal. The crystal was no longer being rebuilt between them.**"

> **Routed appropriately** `[21:23]`: whether medications should be adjusted, supplemented with saliva
> substitutes, or managed through modified oral hygiene "is a clinical conversation with your physician
> **and your dentist.**"

## Root exposure and the sharp pain

`[21:23]`–`[24:29]`

**How recession happens:** the bone on the outer face of the tooth is **the thinnest in the jaw —
sometimes under half a millimetre.** Aggressive brushing at the gum line, especially with hard bristles
and horizontal strokes, abrades the tissue against that thin plate.

**Why the exposed root is more vulnerable than enamel** `[22:24]`:

| Surface | Dissolution threshold |
|---|---|
| Enamel | pH 5.5 |
| **Root cementum** | **pH 6.2 — nearly a full unit higher** |

> "The root surface dissolves at a pH the mouth **routinely reaches during normal eating**" — material
> "designed for the protected environment below the gum line… now exposed to the oral environment it was
> never engineered to withstand."

**The pain mechanism** `[23:27]`–`[24:29]` — **the hydrodynamic theory** *(attributed to Brännström,
*Journal of Dental Research*)*: dentine is permeated by fluid-filled tubules 1–2 micrometres across,
running from the surface to the nerve. **Cold, heat or sugar makes the fluid move, deflecting
mechanoreceptors at the pulp end.**

> "Fluid dynamics in microscopic tubes, moving a column of liquid by micrometres, **deflecting a pressure
> sensor through the same mechanotransduction physics the ear uses to convert sound pressure into neural
> signals**" `[23:27]`.

**Two details that follow:**
- **Sensitivity scales with the number of exposed tubules**, so advancing recession means sharper pain
  from progressively milder stimuli.
- **Cold triggers it more reliably than heat** because the direction of flow matters: **outward flow
  depolarises the sensor.** "The pain is directional, not just thermal."

## Practices

1. **Reduce eating frequency** — the number of Stephan curves, not the sugar quantity, sets the daily
   dissolution total. Three meals with real gaps keeps the balance ~12:1 in favour of repair.
2. **Fluoride toothpaste twice daily, and do not skip the evening application** — it supplies the ions the
   longest remineralisation window will incorporate.
3. **Avoid back-to-back hot-then-cold** — each reversal loads the junction where cracks nucleate.
4. **Soft brush, no horizontal scrubbing at the gum line** — recession exposes a surface that dissolves at
   a pH the mouth reaches routinely.
5. **If a new medication coincided with accelerating dental problems**, that is a conversation worth
   having with both prescriber and dentist.

## Glossary terms introduced

- **Hydroxyapatite / fluorapatite** — the enamel crystal and its acid-resistant substituted form `[00:00]`, `[16:18]`.
- **Fatigue fracture** — failure from cyclic loading below the fracture threshold `[02:02]`.
- **Paris law** — crack growth per cycle as a function of tip stress `[04:03]`.
- **Dentino-enamel junction** — the bonded interface where cracks nucleate `[08:06]`.
- **Stephan curve** — the post-meal pH drop and recovery `[11:12]`.
- **Critical pH** — 5.5 for enamel, 6.2 for root cementum `[12:13]`, `[22:24]`.
- **Remineralisation** — supersaturated saliva depositing minerals back `[12:13]`.
- **Xerostomia** — reduced salivary flow `[18:18]`.
- **Hydrodynamic theory** — tubule fluid movement producing dentinal pain `[23:27]`.
