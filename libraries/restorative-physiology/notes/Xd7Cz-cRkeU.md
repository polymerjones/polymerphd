# Why YOUR Ears Ring And What the Sound Actually Is

## Central claim

**Nothing in the ear is making the sound. The brain is manufacturing it to fill a silence the auditory
cortex was never designed to experience** `[00:00]`.

> "**The hair cells are gone. The cortex remembers what they heard. The sound that nothing is making is the
> brain remembering a note the ear can no longer play**" `[26:28]`.

**And the pitch is not arbitrary** `[22:23]`:
> "**The frequency you hear is the frequency you lost.**"

## How the ear separates frequencies — mechanically

`[01:00]`–`[04:03]`

**A microphone flattens everything into one waveform. The ear decomposes sound into its frequency
components before the brain receives anything.**

> "A violin and a trumpet playing a single note arrive at your ear as one combined pressure wave. **By the
> time the signal reaches the auditory nerve, the ear has separated them into distinct frequency
> channels**" `[01:00]`.

**The basilar membrane** — approximately **35 mm** when the cochlea is uncoiled — is **tapered**:

| End | Property | Resonates with |
|---|---|---|
| **Base** (entrance) | **Stiff and narrow** | **High frequencies** — 20,000 Hz |
| **Apex** (far end) | **Flexible and wide** | **Low frequencies** — 20 Hz |

**The stiffness varies by a factor of ~100 from base to apex**, and that gradient alone maps the entire
audible spectrum onto 35 mm of tissue.

**The traveling wave** *(attributed to von Békésy's Nobel Prize research, 1961)*: the pressure wave rises in
amplitude as it approaches the position where local stiffness matches the incoming frequency. **At
resonance the wave peaks and the membrane vibrates maximally. Past it the wave dies rapidly**, its energy
absorbed at the matching position.

| Frequency | Position |
|---|---|
| 20,000 Hz | The stiff base |
| **Middle C (~262 Hz)** | **~20 mm from the base** |
| 20 Hz | The flexible apex |

> "**A piano keyboard stretching the length of your little finger**, with every key tuned to a specific
> position and every incoming sound striking multiple keys simultaneously" `[04:03]`.

> "**No computation, no digital processing. Pure mechanics** — a stiffness gradient, fluid dynamics and
> resonance **performing the mathematical operation of Fourier analysis** through the physics of a
> vibrating ribbon in a fluid-filled tube" `[04:03]`.

## The cells — and the most sensitive detector in the body

`[04:03]`–`[07:06]`

| Population | Count | Function |
|---|---|---|
| **Outer hair cells** | **~15,000** | **Amplifiers** — they physically change shape at the frequency of the incoming sound, **boosting membrane vibration by a factor of ~100** |
| **Inner hair cells** | **~3,500** | **The true sensory transducers** — converting motion into electrical signal |

> **A consequence worth noting** `[05:04]`: "**When outer hair cells die, the amplification disappears
> before the transduction does. You lose sensitivity and clarity in that frequency band long before you
> lose the ability to detect it entirely.**"

**The transduction chain** `[05:04]`–`[06:05]` *(attributed to Hudspeth, *Neuron*)*: membrane vibration →
fluid deflects the **stereocilia** bundle → **tip links** pull taut → **ion channels open** → potassium
floods in → the cell depolarises → calcium enters → neurotransmitter releases → **the nerve fires**,
carrying one piece of information: **this frequency is present at this intensity at this moment.**

> **The sensitivity figure** `[06:05]`: "**A displacement of 0.3 nanometres opens the channel — the diameter
> of a single gold atom.** Your ear's hair cells detect mechanical motion at the atomic scale, **the most
> sensitive mechanical detectors in the human body. No instrument engineered by human hands approaches this
> sensitivity.**"

## Why the loss is permanent

`[07:06]`–`[09:07]` — **an evolutionary trade the video explains rather than merely asserts.**

**Mammals are the exception:**

| Animal | Regeneration |
|---|---|
| **Birds** | **Routine** — a songbird exposed to damaging noise **recovers full hearing within weeks** |
| **Fish** | **Continuous throughout life** |
| **Amphibians** | After injury |
| **Mammals** | **None** |

> "**The genes for hair cell regeneration exist in the mammalian genome, conserved across hundreds of
> millions of years… The pathway is switched off**" `[08:06]`.

**The leading hypothesis** `[08:06]`–`[09:07]`: mammalian hearing requires structural precision that
regeneration would disrupt.

> "**Regenerating a cell is simple. Regenerating it in the right place, with the right connections, in the
> right orientation, is the problem evolution could not solve** while maintaining the frequency precision
> mammals require for speech recognition, predator detection, and **the auditory discrimination that
> separates a human voice from background noise across a crowded room.**"

> "**The cells laid down during fetal development are the total supply available for the remaining
> lifespan**" `[09:07]`.

## The decibel arithmetic

`[09:07]`–`[13:12]` — **the section that changes how the numbers read.**

**Sound intensity is proportional to pressure amplitude squared, and the decibel scale is logarithmic.
Every 3 dB increase doubles the energy arriving at the cochlea.**

| Source | Level | Energy relative to the 85 dB damage threshold |
|---|---|---|
| Normal conversation | ~60 dB | Below threshold |
| **Sustained damage threshold** | **~85 dB** | 1× |
| **Lawnmower** | **~90 dB** | **More than 3×** — "which sounds like a modest increase" |
| **Rock concert** | **110 dB** | **~30,000×** |

> "**30,000 times the safe level applied to structures at the atomic scale for 2 to 3 hours of exposure**"
> `[10:09]`.

> "**The numbers on the volume dial bear no intuitive relationship to the energy they deliver**" `[09:07]`.

**Headphones at maximum volume deliver a comparable load directly into the ear canal with no atmospheric
attenuation.** Power tools deliver **90–110 dB** hour after hour, year after year.

**What the energy does** `[11:09]`:
> "**Excessive sound energy physically breaks the stereocilia bundles. The tip links snap under forces they
> were never calibrated to sustain.** The ion channels can no longer open because the mechanical linkage
> that opened them no longer exists. Without ion flux, the cell loses its signalling capacity… **the cell
> undergoes apoptosis**, leaving a gap in the cochlear mosaic that will never be filled."

**Why high frequencies go first** `[11:09]`–`[12:11]`:
> "Sound enters at the base first and the traveling wave deposits its highest-frequency energy at the
> earliest position it encounters. **High-frequency hair cells are destroyed preferentially… the region
> most exposed, earliest, and longest.**"

### Why it is invisible for decades

`[13:12]` — **the redundancy schedule:**

| Cells lost at a frequency position | What you notice |
|---|---|
| **10%** | Nothing — the remaining 90% carry the signal |
| **30%** | Signal weakens, **but the brain compensates by increasing sensitivity** — perhaps slightly less distinct in noisy rooms |
| **50–60%** | **Below the threshold for reliable perception** — the audiologist's test reveals it |

**The signature: the high-frequency notch — a characteristic dip at 4,000 Hz** that noise-induced hearing
loss produces.

## The phantom — what the cortex does with silence

`[13:12]`–`[18:19]` — **the core of the video.**

**The auditory cortex carries a tonotopic map** — adjacent cortical regions processing adjacent frequency
bands, mirroring the membrane's layout. **The map is maintained by continuous input**; each region receives
a steady stream from its corresponding hair cells.

> **The map is held in place by the signals that define it** `[14:15]`.

**When hair cells die, the corresponding cortical neurons are deafferented — cut off from their designated
source.**

> "A strip of cortex that has processed one frequency for decades, **that built its synaptic architecture
> around the specific timing and amplitude patterns of that frequency signal, suddenly receives nothing**"
> `[15:16]`.

**And here the auditory cortex does the opposite of what other sensory systems do** `[15:16]`–`[16:17]`:

| System | Response to deafferentation |
|---|---|
| **Most sensory cortex** | Neurons **gradually reduce activity and go quiet** |
| **Auditory cortex** | **They begin firing spontaneously — at a rate *higher* than when real signals were arriving** |

> "**The neurons that spent decades processing a specific frequency begin generating activity at the rate
> and pattern that frequency would have produced.**"

**Plus cortical remapping** *(attributed to Eggermont and Roberts, *Trends in Neurosciences*)*: adjacent
regions still receiving input **expand into the vacated territory**, and "**the overlapping activity
patterns in the remapped territory produce a confused, persistent signal the brain's perceptual system
interprets as sound.**"

### The phantom limb parallel

`[17:18]`:
> "After amputation, the somatosensory cortex that processed touch from the missing limb loses its input.
> The deafferented neurons begin firing spontaneously and **the patient feels the limb that no longer
> exists.**"

> "**The phantom limb is felt because the brain remembers the limb. Tinnitus is heard because the brain
> remembers the frequency.** Both persist because **no sensory cortex evolved to interpret the permanent
> absence of a signal it was built to receive.**"

### Why some people hear it and others do not

`[17:18]`–`[18:19]` *(attributed to Rauschecker and colleagues, *Neuron*)*:

**A gating mechanism in the limbic system** normally filters irrelevant neural signals below conscious
perception — "the filtering that allows you to ignore the feeling of clothing on your skin."

> "**The deafferented cortex generates the phantom in nearly everyone who has lost hair cells. Whether you
> hear it depends on whether the gate holds**" `[18:19]`.

## Why it is louder at night

`[18:19]`–`[21:22]` — **and the answer is that it isn't.**

> "**The phantom signal does not grow louder at night. It runs at approximately constant amplitude** whether
> you are in a crowded restaurant or lying in bed. **What changes is everything around it**" `[18:19]`.

**During the day**, environmental sound floods the cortex with legitimate activity, and the phantom is
**masked — "drowned the way a candle flame is invisible in sunlight."**

> "Every voice, every air conditioner hum, every car passing outside, **every ambient noise you would
> normally ignore, contributes to the masking**" `[19:19]`.

**In a quiet room**, real input drops toward zero and **the only signal left in the auditory cortex is the
spontaneous firing.**

> "**The signal itself never changed. The signal-to-noise ratio inverted**… **The ringing that appears to
> arrive in silence was running the entire time. The silence removed what was hiding it**" `[20:21]`.

> **Which is exactly why masking works** `[20:21]`: "**The candle is still lit. The room is no longer dark
> enough to see it.**"

## The pitch is a map

`[21:22]`–`[23:25]`

**Tinnitus frequency corresponds to the basilar membrane position where hair cells were lost — precisely
enough to serve as a diagnostic readout.**

| Pitch | Maps to | Usual cause |
|---|---|---|
| **High — 4,000–8,000 Hz** (the most common presentation) | **The base of the cochlea** | **Cumulative noise energy** — concerts, power tools, headphones, industrial noise |
| **Low** | Further toward the apex | **Less commonly noise**; more often age-related degeneration of apical cells, or abnormal fluid pressure in the cochlear chamber |

> "**If you hear a high-pitched ring, you are hearing the ghost of the frequency band that cumulative sound
> energy destroyed**" `[21:22]`.

> "**The audiologist's tone test will confirm what the tinnitus already told you. The dip on the audiogram
> and the pitch of the phantom occupy one frequency**" `[22:23]`.

## What can and cannot be restored

`[23:25]`–`[26:28]`

### Cannot

- **Hair cells cannot be regenerated in humans with current medicine.** Gene therapy research is early;
  laboratory work has **partially reactivated the silenced pathway under controlled conditions**, but
  **"clinical application remains years away"** — and the challenge extends beyond regrowth: each new cell
  would need the correct nerve connection, the correct mechanical tuning for its position, and integration
  without disrupting surviving cells.
- **The cortical phantom cannot be directly silenced** because the deafferented neurons **share circuitry
  with neurons still receiving legitimate input. "Suppressing the spontaneous firing in the deafferented
  zone risks suppressing real signal processing in the adjacent zones."**

### Can shift

| Intervention | Mechanism |
|---|---|
| **Hearing aids** | **Amplify real sound in the lost band**, delivering input to the nerve fibres still connected to surviving cells. "**The cortex no longer needs to generate a replacement for a signal that is now arriving, however weakly.**" The aid **"does not treat the tinnitus directly. It restores the input the brain was building from nothing"** |
| **Sound therapy / masking** | **Raises the noise floor until the phantom falls below the perceptual boundary** — the quiet-room physics run in reverse |
| **Cognitive habituation** | Attention networks — **prefrontal cortex and the auditory–limbic connections** — **learn to deprioritise the phantom** through neuroplastic adaptation. **"The sound does not change. The weight the mind assigns to it does."** Measured in **months rather than days**; effectively **restoring the gate** |

> **Why hearing aids often help unexpectedly** `[25:27]`: "**the tinnitus and the hearing loss share a
> common origin in the death of the same hair cells at the same cochlear position.**"

## Practices

1. **Protect hearing during high-decibel exposure** — the arithmetic is the argument: 110 dB is roughly
   **30,000 times** the sustained damage threshold, applied to structures that detect motion at 0.3 nm and
   **never regenerate.**
2. **Use masking sound at night** — a fan, white noise, ambient sound. It does not remove the phantom; it
   restores the signal-to-noise ratio that hid it all day.
3. **Consider hearing aids if there is measurable loss in the tinnitus frequency band** — restoring real
   input in that band reduces the cortex's need to manufacture a replacement.
4. **Expect habituation to take months** — the mechanism is attentional reweighting, not silencing.
5. **Match the pitch to a tone generator** if you want to know which cochlear region was lost — it maps
   directly to the audiogram notch.
