# What Actually Happens to Vitamin D Inside YOUR Body

## Central claim

**"Vitamin D" is a vitamin in name only — the molecule from sun or a bottle is inert raw material that the body must convert through two separate organs before it becomes a hormone, and the blood test almost everyone gets measures the raw-material stockpile, not the hormone that actually gives cells orders.** The source states the working definition directly: a molecule "manufactured... using sunlight as the starting spark and your own skin, liver and kidney as the production line," released into blood "to give orders at a distance... that is the working definition of a hormone, word for word. Insulin answers to it, and so does thyroid hormone" `[01:01]`–`[03:04]`.

## Why it was ever called a vitamin

`[02:02]` — Named in the 1920s after Ricketts observed that cod liver oil cured the soft, bowed bones of children in smoky industrial cities; a Baltimore chemist showed the curing factor survived a treatment that destroyed vitamin A, so it earned "the next free letter." Ultraviolet light on children (or their food) was later shown to cure the same disease. **"The label records the order of discovery and the body records something else."**

## Station one: the skin

`[03:04]`–`[06:08]` — UVB light breaks a bond in **7-dehydrocholesterol**, held in the skin's outer tenth of a millimeter, converting it to the flexible **previtamin D3**; body heat at 37°C over the following day or two settles it into **cholecalciferol (D3)**, the substance sold in a bottle. Two consequences follow from the wavelength alone: **ordinary window glass absorbs UVB almost completely** (no vitamin D from sun through a car or office window), and in winter north of Rome/New York latitude, UVB is stripped out by the atmosphere and skin production stops for months. Production **plateaus at a fixed ceiling regardless of exposure length** — excess previtamin D is shunted into inert byproducts (Holick and colleagues — "Holik" in the source audio — *Science*, 1981) — "your skin cannot overdose you. A bottle can, because a bottle skips the ceiling." Skin in its 70s makes less than half the previtamin D of skin in its 20s from an identical dose of light.

## Station two: the liver makes the storage form

`[06:08]`–`[08:09]` — Because D3 is fat-soluble, it travels bound to a carrier protein to the liver, where an enzyme attaches one oxygen-hydrogen "handle," producing **25-hydroxyvitamin D** — the storage form, circulating with a half-life of roughly 2–3 weeks, and **"this is the molecule to hold on to because it comes back on a laboratory report later."** Certain drugs (older anti-seizure medicines, one tuberculosis antibiotic) speed its breakdown "from the inside." Body fat sequesters a share of raw material, which is why the storage-form reading tends to run lower in a person carrying more fat from an identical sun/capsule intake.

## Station three: the kidney makes the hormone, on demand

`[08:09]`–`[13:12]` — A second enzyme in kidney tubule cells adds a second handle, producing **1,25-dihydroxyvitamin D (calcitriol)** — the only form that can give a cell an order. It binds its carrier loosely (more circulates free), clears within hours, and is made **on demand**, not on a supply schedule. The trigger is **blood calcium**: four parathyroid glands taste blood continuously and release **parathyroid hormone (PTH)** within minutes when calcium dips, which switches on the kidney's activating enzyme, tells the kidney to stop excreting calcium, and can pull calcium directly from bone as an emergency route. Calcitriol then raises gut calcium absorption from roughly **10–15% to 30–40%**. A built-in brake: calcitriol switches on a second kidney enzyme that dismantles itself the moment it appears, and a bone-signaling hormone (released when phosphate runs high) shuts the activating enzyme down. In blood, the storage form outnumbers calcitriol roughly **1,000 to 1** — "one organ keeps a reservoir, the other meters a dose."

## Why the test measures the storage form, not the hormone

`[13:12]`–`[16:14]` — Standard tests measure 25-hydroxyvitamin D because it's stable and integrates weeks of supply; calcitriol swings hour to hour with the last meal and demand, so measuring it "reports that the loop is working and stays silent about the stockpile the loop is drawing down." Kidney failure demonstrates which molecule is actually in charge: a person with failed kidneys can have a full storage reservoir and still develop deficiency-pattern soft bone, because "the second station has closed" — treated with a calcitriol prescription directly. Doctors still debate the "low" threshold (roughly 20–30 ng/mL), which the source frames as "an argument about the stockpile," not the hormone.

## The receptor is everywhere — which explains the claims, not their truth

`[16:14]`–`[19:17]` — The **vitamin D receptor (VDR)** is a nuclear receptor found in gut and bone as expected, but also immune cells, muscle fibers, heart muscle, vessel lining, skin, brain, insulin-producing pancreas cells, and prostate — **"the receptor is nearly everywhere... that single fact explains the sprawl"** of cancer, heart, immunity, mood, and diabetes claims. Some immune cells carry their own copy of the activating enzyme for local calcitriol production — a real mechanism (and, in sarcoidosis, a pathological one, since it can push blood calcium up). But **"the receptor being present says only that a tissue can listen"** — whether extra hormone changes tissue behavior, and whether a normal body was ever short of it, are separate questions. Observational studies made the sprawl look proven because illness itself both keeps people indoors and lowers storage-form levels, and sick people are the ones most likely to get tested — **"a low number is as often a sign of being unwell as a cause of it."**

## What the randomized trials actually found

`[19:17]`–`[21:20]` — The **VITAL trial** (Manson et al., *NEJM* 2019; ~26,000 adults, men 50+/women 55+, 2,000 IU/day D3 vs. placebo, >5 years): **no reduction in invasive cancer, no reduction in major cardiovascular events.** Fracture sub-analysis (LeBoff et al. — "Liebaugh" in the source audio — 2022): **no reduction in fractures**, and no reduction in falls — participants mostly had adequate baseline levels, so the trial tested whether more helps an already-sufficient body. A secondary signal flagged a possible reduction in cancer deaths specifically, "a signal worth watching rather than a settled result." The one positive randomized sprawl-claim result: **autoimmune disease diagnoses (rheumatoid arthritis, psoriasis) fell roughly a fifth** in the supplemented group (the source's "trial zone investigators," *BMJ* 2022) — **"far from the one that sells the bottles."** What was never overturned, because it was never on trial: bone. Genuine deficiency causes rickets in children and osteomalacia in adults, "settled for a century."

## Symptoms and body signals addressed

- **Soft, bowed bones (rickets) in children and painful soft bone (osteomalacia) in adults** — the one deficiency-disease outcome the source states has never been contested by trial evidence `[21:20]`.
- **A "low" or "insufficient" lab result**, reframed as a measurement of the storage reservoir rather than a direct read of hormone activity `[13:12]`.
- **A normal-or-high active-hormone reading in someone who is actually short of vitamin D** — the parathyroid-driven control loop compensating by converting more of a dwindling reservoir `[14:12]`.

## Glossary terms introduced

- **Cholecalciferol (vitamin D3)** — the inert compound made in skin from UVB light and sold in bottles `[04:05]`.
- **25-hydroxyvitamin D** — the liver-made storage form measured by standard blood tests `[07:08]`.
- **1,25-dihydroxyvitamin D (calcitriol)** — the kidney-made active hormone; the only form that acts on cells `[09:09]`.
- **Vitamin D receptor (VDR)** — the nuclear receptor found in nearly every tissue type `[17:14]`.
- **Osteomalacia** — the adult soft-bone disease of genuine vitamin D deficiency `[21:20]`.

## Analogies worth reusing

- **A factory with raw material at the gate and two finishing stations on the floor** — the skin-liver-kidney activation line `[03:04]`.
- **A reservoir vs. a metered dose** — the storage form (25-OH-D) versus calcitriol, ~1,000:1 in blood `[12:12]`.
- **A stockpile counted vs. a hormone kept off the books** — why the routine test measures one molecule and not the one that acts `[13:12]`.

## Source-stated confidence

Each major claim is attributed to a specific study: Holick and colleagues (*Science*, 1981) for the skin production ceiling; Manson et al. (*NEJM*, 2019) for the VITAL trial's cancer/cardiovascular null result; LeBoff et al. (2022) for the fracture sub-analysis; the source's "trial zone investigators" (*BMJ*, 2022) for the autoimmune-disease reduction. The source explicitly separates settled from unsettled: bone/calcium is "where it stood in 1922"; the cancer-mortality signal is "worth watching rather than a settled result"; and the broader sprawl of organ-specific claims is explicitly labeled plausible-but-unproven — "the receptor being present says only that a tissue can listen."
