# What 60 Seconds of SMILING Does to Your Brain — Even When You're Faking It

## Central claim

**The face is an input device, not a display — and the brainstem cannot tell whether you chose the
expression** `[00:00]`, `[27:33]`.

> "**The expression was not displaying your mood. It was generating it**" `[00:00]`.

> "**The pathway was never built to distinguish, and it never will**" `[27:33]`.

*(Attributed to Coles and colleagues, *Nature Human Behavior*, 2022 — **138 studies across thousands of
participants**, confirming that **emotion produces expression and expression produces emotion.**)*

## The anatomy that makes it work

`[01:00]`–`[03:05]`

**Zygomatic major** runs from the cheekbone to the corner of the mouth. **Every smile — genuine, social,
forced or faked — activates it.**

**The distinguishing fact** `[02:03]`:
> "Every skeletal muscle has proprioceptors. **Facial muscles are unique in one respect: their proprioceptive
> signals travel through cranial nerve seven, which has direct anatomical connections to the brainstem
> nuclei that regulate emotional processing.**"

| Muscle | Where its proprioceptive signal goes |
|---|---|
| **Bicep** | **Somatosensory cortex** — "arriving as neutral spatial data about limb position" |
| **Zygomatic major** | **Nucleus of the solitary tract and the reticular formation** — "the nuclei that process visceral and emotional information from the vagus nerve" |

> **The facial nerve and the vagus converge in one brainstem region** `[02:03]`.

**And the brainstem integrates without checking the source** `[03:05]`:
> "A signal from the zygomatic major saying **smile is occurring** is processed alongside signals from the
> heart saying **rate is low** and signals from the diaphragm saying **breathing is slow.**"

> "**A smiling face combined with a calm heart and slow breathing produces a brainstem output that reads:
> safe, positive, approach.** The amygdala quiets, the ventral tegmental area releases dopamine, the dorsal
> raphe increases serotonin output" `[03:05]`.

> "**The input is identical to genuine happiness producing a real smile with relaxed breathing. The output
> is identical**" `[03:05]`.

## The chemistry

`[03:05]`–`[05:07]`

| Change | Source nucleus |
|---|---|
| **Serotonin increases** | **Dorsal raphe** — "the neurotransmitter SSRIs target" |
| **Dopamine increases** | **Ventral tegmental area** |
| **Cortisol decreases** | **HPA axis** |

> **Measured in blood serum and cerebrospinal fluid following deliberate facial muscle contraction.**

> **The honest calibration** `[04:05]`: "**The changes are modest — not equivalent to a dose of medication —
> but they are real, reproducible, and cumulative with repeated daily practice.**"

**The cumulative arithmetic** `[04:05]`–`[05:07]`:
> "**A single 60-second smile produces a shift that lasts 15 to 20 minutes.** Two deliberate smiles per day,
> morning and midday, produce **40 minutes of altered neurochemistry per day.** Across weeks, the cumulative
> exposure shifts the baseline the brainstem operates from — **not through pharmacology, but through
> repeated proprioceptive input.**"

### Why 60 seconds specifically

`[05:07]`

> "**Shorter contractions — a brief forced smile lasting two to three seconds — trigger the proprioceptive
> signal but do not sustain it long enough for the full cascade to complete.** The serotonin release from
> the dorsal raphe **requires sustained afferent input.**"

> "**60 seconds crosses that threshold. The brainstem transitions from *momentary expression detected* to
> *emotional state confirmed*.**"

## Why a smile works when a fist clench does not

`[05:07]`–`[07:09]` — **the objection, answered.**

> "**The brainstem does not weight all proprioceptive input equally. Facial muscle proprioception carries
> disproportionately high emotional weighting compared to limb muscle proprioception.**"

**The evolutionary reason** `[06:08]`:
> "**The face evolved as the primary social signalling organ in primate groups where survival depended on
> rapidly reading and producing accurate facial expressions.** Alliance, threat, mating, status, deception —
> all communicated through the face before language existed."

> "**Limb position is spatial data, useful for motor coordination but irrelevant to emotional state
> assessment. Facial configuration is social-emotional data**" `[07:09]`.

> **And the loophole this creates** `[07:09]`: "**The evolutionary environment did not include fake smiles.**
> The distinction between real and performed facial expressions is a modern social phenomenon operating on
> neural architecture that predates the concept by millions of years."

## The Botox proof — the inverse experiment

`[07:09]`–`[10:12]`

*(Attributed to Finzi and Rosenthal, randomised controlled trial, *Journal of Psychiatric Research*, 2014.)*

> **Patients with major depressive disorder receiving botulinum toxin in the corrugator supercilii showed
> 52% response at 6 weeks, versus 15% for saline placebo. "The effect size was larger than many approved
> antidepressant medications produce in comparable trials."**

**The mechanism** `[08:10]`:
> "**The patients could still feel frustration, sadness and anger. The emotions were intact, but the facial
> expression of those emotions was blocked at the muscle.** The brainstem, receiving no proprioceptive
> signal that says *frowning is occurring*, did not generate the limbic response."

> "**The negative feedback loop — low mood producing a frown, the brainstem interpreting the frown as
> confirmation of the low mood, reinforcing the mood, deepening the frown — was interrupted at the muscular
> source**" `[08:10]`.

**Confirmed by imaging** `[09:11]` *(Hennenlotter and colleagues, *Cerebral Cortex*)*: **reduced amygdala
activation during negative emotional stimuli in Botox patients.**

> "**The paralysed frown muscle was not just failing to display negative emotion. It was reducing the
> brain's processing of negative emotion at the neural level.**"

### Bell's palsy — the same proof from the other side

`[09:11]`–`[10:12]`

> "Patients with **unilateral Bell's palsy** who cannot smile on the affected side **report difficulty
> experiencing positive emotions at full intensity.** The subjective experience of happiness is blunted **on
> the side where the proprioceptive feedback loop has been interrupted**… **as if the feeling cannot fully
> arrive without the facial confirmation the brainstem expects.** Recovery of facial nerve function restores
> both the smile and the full emotional intensity."

> "**The Botox patients did not set out to prove that the face generates emotion. They set out to reduce
> wrinkles. The mood improvement was the accident that proved the pathway is real**" `[10:12]`.

## Does a fake smile count?

`[10:12]`–`[13:13]` — **and the video handles the replication controversy honestly.**

> **The pen-in-teeth study** *(Strack, Martin and Stepper, 1988)* "**was foundational, but failed to
> replicate in a large registered replication in 2016, triggering a decade of controversy.**"

> "**The Coles 2022 meta-analysis resolved the controversy definitively. Across 138 studies, the effect is
> real but smaller than the original study suggested.** The pen-in-mouth paradigm was a blunt instrument;
> **sustained zygomatic contraction at 60 seconds through deliberate smiling produces a more consistent and
> robust effect than the brief pen-holding ever could**" `[11:12]`.

**Duchenne versus non-Duchenne** `[11:12]`–`[13:13]`:

| Smile | Muscles | Signal |
|---|---|---|
| **Duchenne** (genuine) | **Zygomatic major + orbicularis oculi** (the crow's feet contraction) | **Stronger — two muscles feeding back instead of one** |
| **Non-Duchenne** (polite, social, forced) | **Zygomatic major alone** | **"Not zero. The brainstem responds to the mouth alone"** |

> "**You do not need to produce a convincing smile. A wide, sustained contraction of the zygomatic major —
> mouth corners pulled up, held for 60 seconds — is sufficient. The eyes do not need to participate**"
> `[13:13]`.

## The resting face drifting negative

`[14:16]`–`[16:18]` — **the age argument.**

| Muscle | Age change |
|---|---|
| **Zygomatic major** | **Loses mass and contractile force through sarcopenia — the smile muscle weakens** |
| **Corrugator supercilii** | **Often chronically contracted** from decades of screen work, driving, stress and habitual concentration |

> "**The resting face of a 55-year-old tends toward a more neutral-to-negative expression than the resting
> face of a 25-year-old — not from mood, but from muscular default**" `[14:16]`.

> "**Chronically contracted frown muscles sending continuous frowning input; weakened smile muscles sending
> less smiling input. The emotional baseline shifts subtly negative through the facial feedback loop alone
> — independent of life circumstances, independent of actual mood**" `[15:16]`.

> "**The drift feels like aging. The world becoming slightly heavier, slightly greyer, slightly less
> buoyant. Part of what changed was not the world, but the signal your face has been sending your brain
> about the world**" `[15:16]`.

**Why the intervention works on this** `[15:16]`–`[16:18]`: **the corrugator and zygomatic are antagonists.**

> "**Contracting one inhibits the other.** For 60 seconds, the brainstem receives the signal it has been
> receiving less and less of for decades."

## Contexts that push the loop negative

`[13:13]`, `[18:20]`–`[25:30]`

### Screen work

> "**Reading, watching, scrolling all produce sustained attentional concentration that engages the corrugator
> and relaxes the zygomatic. The screen face is a mild frown held for the duration of the session** — 30
> minutes, an hour, three hours" `[21:24]`–`[22:26]`.

> "**The fatigue and irritability many people attribute to screen content or digital overstimulation is
> partly the facial feedback loop running in negative territory**" `[22:26]`.

### Video calls versus phone calls

`[16:18]`–`[19:21]` — **a genuinely counterintuitive point.**

| Format | What happens to the face |
|---|---|
| **Phone** | "**You smile naturally in response to the other person's vocal tone**, without the self-monitoring that video produces… **your face free to respond to the other person rather than monitor itself**" |
| **Video** | "**Seeing your own face on screen triggers self-monitoring** — unconscious adjustment toward neutral or serious… **sustained corrugator engagement and minimal zygomatic activation throughout the call**" |

> "**The brainstem receives 45 minutes of neutral-to-frowning proprioceptive input during a single video
> call — input that would not have occurred during the same conversation face to face**" `[19:21]`.

> "**If you feel more drained after video calls than phone calls, part of the fatigue is the facial feedback
> loop running in negative territory for the duration**" `[19:21]`.

### Grief — handled carefully

`[19:21]`–`[20:22]`

**Bereavement produces sustained corrugator contraction, depressor anguli oris engagement and orbicularis
oculi tension** — "held for weeks and months during acute grief."

> "**The face becomes a sustained amplifier of the loss — not just displaying the sadness, but generating
> additional negative limbic input the brainstem processes as confirmation of ongoing negative
> experience**" `[20:22]`.

> **The explicit boundary** `[20:22]`: "**Deliberate facial feedback interruption is not a treatment for
> grief and should not be framed as one. But understanding that part of the sustained emotional weight may
> be the face's contribution to its own amplification, rather than purely the loss itself, gives the
> grieving person a variable they did not know existed.**"

### Chronic pain

`[20:22]`–`[21:24]`

> "**Deliberate smiling during chronic pain does not reduce the nociceptive signal. The pain impulse from
> the damaged tissue is unchanged.** But it interrupts the facial contribution to the emotional
> amplification of that signal. **The pain is the same. The suffering — the emotional experience of the pain
> — can be modulated.**"

### Overnight bruxism

`[23:26]`–`[25:30]`

**Nocturnal grinding affects roughly 8–13% of adults**, sustaining masseter and temporalis contraction
through the night — **"feeding the brainstem hours of proprioceptive input associated with tension,
aggression and stress."**

> "**If you wake with jaw pain or headache localised to the temples, the brainstem spent the night receiving
> a facial signal that contradicted the rest and recovery signals the body was trying to send**" `[24:28]`.

> "**A dental night guard addresses the joint damage. The 60-second morning smile addresses the
> neurochemical deficit the overnight facial input produced**" `[24:28]`.

## Contexts that push it positive

`[16:18]`, `[22:26]`–`[23:26]`

### Motor resonance — the smile that multiplies

> "**Your deliberate smile becomes their involuntary proprioceptive input through the mirroring pathway.
> One deliberate smile produces two brainstem signals — yours from the contraction, theirs from the
> mirrored contraction**" `[16:18]`.

> "**The social smile that people dismiss as superficial is a neural broadcast that changes the recipient's
> brain chemistry through a motor pathway they did not choose to activate**" `[17:19]`.

### Grandchildren

`[22:26]`–`[23:26]`

> **Interaction with grandchildren produces the highest spontaneous smile frequency of any social context
> measured in adults over 60 — higher than spousal interaction, higher than friendship groups.**

> "**Children smile more frequently, more broadly and more spontaneously than adults**, and the motor
> resonance response in the grandparent's premotor cortex mirrors each smile."

> "**The grandparent who reports that time with the grandchildren keeps me young is describing a
> neurochemical reality… The mood improvement is not sentimental. It is proprioceptive**" `[23:26]`.

### And the compensation argument

`[17:19]`–`[18:20]`

> "**For adults over 55 whose social interactions have narrowed — fewer colleagues, fewer daily encounters,
> fewer opportunities for spontaneous social smiling — the deliberate smile compensates for a frequency
> reduction the aging social world imposed. The brainstem does not know why the smile frequency decreased.
> It only knows the signal arrived less often, and it adjusted the emotional baseline accordingly.**"

## Medications

`[13:13]`–`[14:16]`

| Class | Effect on the loop |
|---|---|
| **Anticholinergics** — many antihistamines, bladder medications, older antidepressants | **Can increase facial muscle tension** through effects on neuromuscular transmission |
| **Antipsychotics, some anti-nausea medications** | **Tardive dyskinesia or facial rigidity** — "locking the face into expressions the brainstem reads as negative or neutral **regardless of actual emotional state**" |
| **Beta-blockers** | **Reduce spontaneous smile frequency** — "the autonomic arousal that accompanies positive emotion is blunted" |

> "**If you started a new medication and noticed a subtle mood shift you could not explain through the
> medication's primary mechanism, the medication may have changed your face — and your face changed your
> mood through a proprioceptive pathway the medication was never designed to affect**" `[13:13]`.

## The protocol

`[25:30]`–`[26:31]`

| Element | Specification |
|---|---|
| **Timing** | **Morning — before the first screen, the first interaction, the first task** |
| **Contraction** | **Wide, sustained zygomatic contraction, 60 seconds** |
| **Breathing** | **Inhale 4 seconds through the nose, exhale 6 seconds** — while holding the smile |
| **Cycles** | **Four breath cycles in 60 seconds** |

> "**Each cycle sends the brainstem two converging signals of safety through two independent cranial nerves**
> — CN VII from the zygomatic contraction, CN X from the extended exhale" `[25:30]`.

> "**The combination produces a stronger limbic shift than either intervention alone, because the
> brainstem's integrative function combines both inputs into a single emotional assessment**" `[25:30]`.

**And the point of the timing** `[25:30]`: **before the first expression of the day establishes the default
the face will carry for the next several hours.**

## Practices

1. **60 seconds of deliberate, wide zygomatic contraction each morning**, before the first screen or
   interaction.
2. **Combine it with 4-second nasal inhale / 6-second exhale** — four cycles, two cranial nerves reporting
   safety at once.
3. **It does not need to be genuine.** The eyes need not participate; the mouth alone reaches the
   brainstem.
4. **Break long screen sessions with a deliberate smile** — the screen face is a sustained mild frown.
5. **Prefer phone to video where the choice exists** — self-view pulls the expression toward monitoring and
   away from mirroring.
6. **Raise unexplained mood shifts after a new medication with the prescriber** — several common classes
   alter facial muscle behaviour.
