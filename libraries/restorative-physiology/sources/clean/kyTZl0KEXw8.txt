A single gram of garden earth may contain millions of them, and the characteristic smell of freshly turned soil is largely their chemical signature. The geosmin you smell is not produced by living bacteria. It is released when streptomyces die and their cells break apart, spilling geosmin into the spaces between soil particles. During dry periods, vegetation releases volatile oils that are absorbed into clay minerals and porous rocks. When rain arrives, these trapped oils are released alongside geosmin, contributing a lighter, slightly sweet note to the petrichor blend. The full smell of rain on dry earth is a mixture. They filmed individual raindrops hitting porous surfaces and discovered something no one had predicted. When a raindrop strikes dry soil, it does not simply soak in. At the moment of impact, the drop traps a thin layer of air between its leading edge and the ground surface. [07:08] organic compounds from the surface into the air column above the ground.
