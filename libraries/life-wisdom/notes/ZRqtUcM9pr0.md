# Subtle sabotage: What your face looks like while you're listening | Vanessa Van Edwards

## Central claim

**Being an engaged listener requires actively signaling that engagement through specific,
learnable nonverbal behaviors ("listening loudly") — leaning, the slow triple nod, the head tilt —
while managing two common saboteurs: unconscious self-soothing gestures, and a neutral facial
expression that unintentionally reads as a negative emotion.** `[00:00]` "Listening is an active
physical emotional behavior, not just hearing words... for someone to truly feel heard... you have
to make sure that they hear your listening."

## Listening cues: lean, nod, tilt

`[01:00]`–`[04:02]` **Leaning** in while someone speaks functions as "a nonverbal bold or
highlighter." The **slow triple nod** ("mhm, mhm, mhm") is cited as producing a specific measured
effect: **"research has found that when you do a slow triple nod, the other person speaks 67%
longer"** — but overuse ("bobble heading") is warned against as reading as excessive warmth at the
cost of perceived competence, and nod *speed* matters: a slow nod signals engagement, a fast one
signals impatience. The **head tilt** is described as a cross-cultural signal to "tell me more,"
useful on a muted video call, and specifically noted to soften the delivery of bad news. A **chin
jut**, by contrast, is flagged as a sign of aggression or invasiveness to avoid.

## Two barriers to looking like you're listening

`[05:04]`–`[09:06]` The first barrier is the body: **self-soothing (pacification) gestures** —
rubbing an arm, cracking knuckles — traced to infant comfort behaviors (a pacifier, a parent's
back-rub) that persist into adulthood under stress. These are described as counterproductive and
contagious: **"self-soothing gestures don't actually calm you down, and they are a barrier to
listening... they can be contagious."** The suggested fix isn't suppression (explicitly called
near-impossible) but **displacement** — redirecting the anxious energy into a small, low-visibility
action like pinching thumb and forefinger together, or gripping a pen or mug. The second barrier is
the face: **"resting bothered face"** (RBF) — a neutral expression that happens to look sad, tired,
or angry — addressed not by trying to fix the resting expression (called very hard to do) but by
adding extra verbal warmth cues, especially in a first impression, to counteract it.

## Listening to remember, and capitalization

`[11:07]`–`[12:08]` Two specific listening mindsets are offered: **"listening to remember"** —
tracking specific details worth bringing up in a future conversation, offered as a cure for
disengagement or boredom while listening — and **capitalization**, building on or adding to what
someone shares emotionally rather than only acknowledging it. A stated warning sign that a listener
has gone off track: the other person stops asking follow-up questions back, which — if it never
happens even with active listening — is named as a possible sign of a **"conversational
narcissist,"** someone uninterested in reciprocal exchange.

## Signals and internal states addressed

- **Being repeatedly asked "are you okay?" while simply listening, feeling neutral** — named as a
  likely case of "resting bothered face," not something wrong with the listener's actual emotional
  state. `[08:06]`
- **Unconsciously rubbing an arm, cracking knuckles, or fidgeting when nervous** — named as a
  self-soothing gesture that undermines rather than helps composure, and can transmit the
  nervousness to the other person. `[05:04]`
- **Feeling bored or disengaged while someone else is talking** — addressed with the "listening to
  remember" reframe, aimed at re-engaging attention with a concrete purpose. `[11:07]`
- **Never getting asked a question back despite actively listening to someone** — named directly as
  a potential red flag ("conversational narcissist") about that relationship. `[13:10]`

## Glossary terms introduced

- **Self-soothing (pacification) gesture** — a comfort movement (arm-rubbing, knuckle-cracking)
  rooted in infant soothing behaviors, described as often counterproductive and contagious in
  adults. `[05:04]`
- **Resting bothered face (RBF)** — a neutral facial expression that unintentionally signals a
  negative emotion. `[08:06]`
- **Capitalization** — building on or adding to what someone shares, rather than only
  acknowledging it. `[12:08]`
- **Conversational narcissist** — someone who never reciprocates a conversation with questions of
  their own. `[13:10]`

## Analogies worth reusing

A lean or a nod described as "a nonverbal bold or highlighter" — used consistently as the framing
device for how listening cues emphasize specific parts of what someone says. `[01:00]`–`[02:01]`

## Source-stated confidence

Presented by a named behavioral researcher citing a specific measured research finding (67% longer
speech following a slow triple nod) without naming the underlying study — treated as the speaker's
professional synthesis of behavioral research, not independently verified here. The claim that
universal facial expressions exist is explicitly hedged by the source itself: **"research is very
interesting on universal facial expressions... the jury is still out on how much of our expressions
are universal."**
