# Plain-English Glossary

Terms introduced across this library's notes, defined in plain English. Alphabetical. No citations
here — see the individual note for the source of each term.

**Accountability** (as distinguished from regret) — demonstrated, consistent follow-through on
acknowledging harm, as opposed to a verbal apology alone.

**Acknowledge, validate, permit (AVP)** — a three-step sequence for regulating one's own emotion.

**Alignment** (as distinguished from loneliness) — the steady sense of knowing who you are and what
you stand for, even when no one else is watching.

**Approval addiction / approval detox** — a compulsive, identity-level dependence on external
validation, and the deliberate, incremental process of reducing reliance on it.

**Attachment theory** — the framework describing how early caregiver relationships shape adult
relational patterns.

**Authority without aggression** — retaining decision-making power without relying on force or
punishment.

**Avoidant attachment** — a pattern of withdrawing from intimacy once a relationship becomes
emotionally significant, rooted in fear rather than disinterest.

**Bid for connection** — an attempt (verbal or physical) to get a partner's attention or interest.

**Boundary** (as distinct from a request) — something you tell someone you will do yourself,
requiring nothing of the other person, as opposed to demanding they change their behavior.

**Capitalization** — building on or adding to what someone shares, rather than only acknowledging
it.

**Clarity** — the capacity to distinguish the proper direction and decisions for oneself, without
needing to believe anyone or anything else.

**Coherence** — a state where thoughts, emotions, and behaviors are in harmony, claimed to be
measurable via heart rate variability and cortisol stability.

**Competitive victimhood** — groups or individuals contesting for recognized victim status because
it excuses their own conduct toward an opposing side.

**Compliment vs. complete** — the distinction between what a healthy partner adds to an already
whole life, versus what a partner is wrongly expected to supply.

**Conversational narcissist** — someone who never reciprocates a conversation with questions of
their own.

**Cue cycle** — the three-step decode/internalize/encode process by which a social signal produces
a reciprocal signal in response.

**Deeply feeling kids (DFKs)** — children who feel shame alongside vulnerability and are "more
porous" to their environment, producing more intense emotional reactions.

**Defense mechanisms** — tools the mind uses to shield itself from accurate self-knowledge, not
because the knowledge is false but because it's difficult to absorb.

**Detached analysis** (of criticism) — the habit of stepping back to evaluate whether feedback
carries real value before absorbing or discarding it.

**Dysregulation / co-regulation / regulation** — a three-stage model of emotional development:
emotions exceeding current skills, borrowing a caregiver's calm, and eventual internalized
self-control.

**Emotion regulation** — managing (not banishing) emotions, covering both expression and
"taming" as equally legitimate strategies.

**Emotional detachment** — controlling and owning one's own emotional reactions rather than being
controlled by them, explicitly distinguished from coldness, numbness, or indifference.

**Emotional granularity** — the finding that more precise emotional labels produce a stronger
calming effect than vague ones.

**Emotional Soylent** — a term for AI companionship: nourishing and filling as a backup, but
lacking the richness of real human connection.

**"Expressing and taming"** — a two-stage model for processing difficult emotion: first letting the
feeling out, then actively countering the mood afterward.

**Factory settings** — the relational blueprint a child's earliest attachment relationship
establishes.

**The five hindrances** — a named Buddhist framework: sensual desire, ill will, sloth and torpor,
restlessness, and skeptical doubt — the specific mental states said to obstruct clarity.

**Fusiform face area** — the brain region cited as responsible for identifying facial expressions,
described as trainable with practice.

**Gates with locks you control** (as distinguished from walls) — selective, earned access to one's
inner life, as opposed to blanket defensiveness.

**"Good inside"** — the foundational premise that children (and adults) are inherently good, used
to separate identity from behavior.

**Heart-brain coherence** — a claimed ordered, measurable heart-rhythm state produced by
intentional, emotionally-felt self-talk.

**Hopeful monster theory** — the idea that most personal change should be incremental, but a large,
risky jump can be worthwhile when the status quo is already failing entirely.

**Individuation** — the process of integrating "light" and "shadow" (conscious and repressed) parts
of the self into wholeness.

**Inner stillness** — the stated capacity to remain calm and centered in chaotic situations, tied
to responding rather than reacting.

**Internal locus of control** — the belief and lived reality that a person's outcomes are governed
by their own internal stability, not by surrounding chaos.

**Invisible growth** — private, unwitnessed effort and discipline claimed to be the real substance
behind visible confidence later.

**Isolation as a mirror** — solitude as something that reflects a person's unfiltered self back at
them, without flattery or excuse.

**Limbic resonance** — named alongside mirror neurons as the mechanism by which one person's
internal state is picked up by others without words.

**Limerence** — the early, chemically-driven infatuation phase of a relationship, distinguished
from the sustained "choosing love" phase that follows it.

**Looping for understanding** — explicitly restating what you heard someone say about their
identity or experience back to them, to demonstrate active processing.

**Love maps** — the depth of mutual knowledge partners actively build about each other's inner
world.

**Madman theory** — the negotiating advantage of appearing willing to make irrationally extreme
threats.

**"Marriage number two"** — a relationship deliberately rebuilt after a betrayal, using new
communication patterns rather than reverting to the pre-betrayal relationship.

**The matching principle** — the requirement that both parties in a conversation be having the
same type (practical, emotional, or social) at the same time for real communication to occur.

**Mattering** — the sense that one's presence and contributions are noticed and appreciated by
others, distinct from closeness.

**MDMA-assisted psychotherapy** — a structured, multi-session therapeutic protocol combining MDMA
administration with active psychotherapy during the drug state.

**Moral typecasting** — sorting people into a binary of villain or victim, obscuring that most real
people and situations are neither or both.

**Need for chaos scale** — a measured personality trait capturing desire to destroy or upend
functioning or beautiful things.

**Neuroplasticity** — the brain's capacity to reorganize its neural pathways in response to new
thought or behavior patterns.

**Non-identification** — observing a mental state as one aspect of oneself rather than as the whole
of oneself.

**Palm flash / happy hello / fronting / blocking** — specific named nonverbal cues affecting how
quickly warmth and trust are established in a first impression.

**Presence** — the combination of perceived warmth and perceived competence signaled in a first
impression.

**Protector part** — an emotional-suppression pattern that developed adaptively in childhood and
can persist maladaptively into adulthood.

**Punctuator** — a habitual, largely unconscious nonverbal tic a person uses regardless of the
emotion they're actually feeling.

**Question inflection** — rising, question-like intonation used on a statement rather than an
actual question, correlated with uncertainty or deception.

**Reactance (theory)** — doing something specifically because one was instructed not to (or vice
versa), read as an assertion of autonomy.

**The reborn** — a claimed birth-month archetype (March), defined by renewal and emotional
intensity.

**Repetition compulsion** — the theory that people unconsciously re-create painful past dynamics in
an attempt to master them with adult resources.

**Resting bothered face (RBF)** — a neutral facial expression that unintentionally signals a
negative emotion.

**Romanticism** — the historical shift, beginning in the late 18th century, toward choosing
partners by individual feeling or instinct rather than family or social arrangement.

**Sarcopenia** — the gradual decline in muscle mass and function with age.

**The second look** — noticing that someone glanced back after already passing by.

**Self-regulating** — the state reached once attention has been redirected inward and mind, body,
and nervous system "begin working in sync."

**Self-soothing (pacification) gesture** — a comfort movement rooted in infant soothing behaviors,
described as often counterproductive and contagious in adults.

**Shadow** — the parts of the self a person denies, said to become part of an unconscious "shadow"
that must eventually be confronted.

**Shadow work** — the practice of facing and integrating the repressed, unconscious self rather
than only pursuing external goals.

**Social ("who are we") conversation** — a conversation centered on identity, where acknowledgment
(not a solution or empathy) is what's being sought.

**Soft startup** — raising a difficult topic by describing one's own feeling and a positive need,
rather than describing what the other person did wrong.

**Solitude epidemic** — a reframing of the "loneliness epidemic": increased time alone, not
necessarily increased loneliness.

**Soul contract** — a promise, framed as a declaration of how much a person values truth and
integrity.

**The space between stimulus and response** — the described pause where a person retains the
choice not to react.

**State management** — staying consciously aware of one's emotional state throughout a practice or
protocol.

**Sturdy leadership** — the capacity to hold one's own authority and values while still empathizing
with someone else's distress.

**Supercommunicator** — someone who consistently connects with almost anyone through conversation,
built from a small number of practiced habits rather than innate talent.

**A temple, not a confession booth** — a description for how a person should regard their own
inner life in old age: something with controlled access, not open to whoever passes by.

**The three C's (context, culture, clusters)** — a framework for interpreting social cues
accurately, never relying on a single signal alone.

**Transitional months** — months (March, September) that fall at seasonal boundaries, claimed to
confer psychological duality on people born within them.

**Turning toward / turning away / turning against** — the three possible responses to a bid for
connection.

**Untouchable** — a person who no longer allows anyone else's opinion or absence to determine their
mood or self-worth.

**Unseen competition** — a peer whose apparent support for you is conditional on you not
surpassing them.

**The wise teacher** — a claimed birth-month archetype (December), defined by philosophical depth
and visionary thinking.
