# 06 — Open Questions

Claims in this collection stated without a named source, flagged by their own note as unverifiable,
or otherwise worth treating with real caution. This library has an unusually large amount of
material worth flagging here, concentrated in its motivational-narration cluster (see
`00_README.md` and `05_CROSS_SOURCE_COMPARISON.md`) — the point of this file is to keep the
derived-versus-stated distinction visible, not to discard the content.

---

## Attribution problems specific to the "Shi Heng Yi Wisdom" cluster

- The central named figure behind roughly half this library's notes is rendered inconsistently
  across (and within) individual videos as "Shi Heng Yi," "Shiheni," and "Shihangi" — in at least one
  video, two different spellings appear within the same transcript, a stronger signal of an
  unedited or auto-generated script than of a deliberate citation practice.
- Several videos attribute a short aphorism to this figure by name but never quote or elaborate a
  specific, traceable teaching, text, or date — the name functions as a general credibility marker
  rather than an actual citation in most instances.
- At least one video is narrated in the first person by a speaker introducing himself as this
  figure directly, while every other video in the same cluster references him only in the third
  person — a structural inconsistency worth noting for anyone trying to establish this channel's
  actual voice or authorship.
- One quoted line attributed to this figure ("who looks outside dreams, who looks inside awakens")
  is a widely circulated aphorism attributed in many other contexts to Carl Jung — this collection
  does not resolve which attribution, if either, is correct.
- One video contains an internal transcript duplication (the same ~8 minutes of content repeats
  almost verbatim partway through a video that does not appear to be a compilation), and a separate
  video's transcript becomes textually incoherent for its final several minutes, interleaving
  unrelated nature-documentary content — both flagged as likely caption/production artifacts rather
  than intentional content.

## Claims that borrow scientific vocabulary without scientific support

- A three-day self-talk protocol claims measurable changes to gene expression and a personal
  "quantum field," describing this explicitly as "quantum mechanics in action" — no study,
  researcher, or institution is named anywhere in the source.
- A separate video makes a similar move with "quantum alignment" to describe an emotional/
  psychological shift.
- A birth-month "archetype" framework uses academic-psychology vocabulary ("archetypes," "the
  collective unconscious") to support an unfalsifiable claim structurally similar to astrology, with
  no actual psychological source named.
- A vitamin D and muscle-loss video repeatedly invokes unnamed "studies" and "research" for specific
  claims (a two-week improvement timeline, the existence and function of vitamin D receptors on
  muscle cells) without naming a single study, journal, or researcher.

## Claims stated without a named source in the research-backed cluster too

- A specific claim that self-affirmation techniques (mirror work, visualizations) work better for
  people who already feel good about themselves than for people who don't is attributed to
  unnamed research.
- Specific procrastination statistics in this project's `addiction` library's Chris Bailey notes
  (cross-referenced here since the same speaker also appears in that library) are attributed to an
  unnamed "procrastination researcher."

## Unverifiable personal narratives presented as evidentiary basis

- One video's entire argument rests on an unverifiable claim of personal experience ("I've spoken
  to hundreds of people... one regret echoes louder than all others") followed by five short,
  named illustrative stories with no indication of whether they are real accounts or composites.
- Claims about universal male or female psychology and behavior (what "most men fear," what a
  specific gendered behavior "really means") are made throughout the motivational-narration cluster
  as generalized fact with no survey, study, or named source.

## A genuine, if thin, religious/contemplative source, for contrast

Not every low-citation video in this cluster is equivalent: one video presents a named Buddhist
framework in the first person, explicitly credited to a specific tradition and the speaker's own
described training — a different, more traceable kind of authority (personal account within a named
lineage) than the unattributed psychological or physics-adjacent claims elsewhere in this cluster,
even though it too carries no external, independently verifiable citation.
