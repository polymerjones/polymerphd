# Makeup and Uveitis

## Central claim

**Eye makeup doesn't cause or worsen uveitis, but it does carry an ordinary infection risk that
becomes higher-stakes for a patient who's had ocular surgery, because any breach of the eye surface
is now a route to a much more serious complication.** Answered directly: **"can makeup cause
uveitis? No. Uveitis is not an infection and is not caused or exacerbated by wearing eye
makeup"** `[¶1]` — but: **"those of us who've had a trabeculectomy run the risk of blebitis"**
`[¶1]` if an infection reaches the eye through a makeup-hygiene lapse.

## When she doesn't wear eye makeup

`[¶1]`

Three explicit rules, stated as her own practice: **"1) I don't wear eye makeup to my ophthalmology
appointments. 2) I don't wear makeup after eye surgery until my eyes have healed and I've had
follow-up appointments. 3) I don't wear eye makeup after injections until my course of associated
antibiotic drops is complete."**

## Ingredient screening, "clean" makeup

`[¶1]`

Diagnosis changed her buying habits: **"I used to just purchase what was trendy and in cute
packaging. Now I research extensively before buying any of my cosmetics."** Specific ingredients
avoided: **parabens and phthalates** (endocrine disruptors); **TEA and DEA**, which **"break down
into nitrogen, which can form nitrosamines... carcinogenic (may cause cancer), according to the
Environmental Working Group"**; and **talc**, since **"some talc contains carcinogenic asbestos,
although it's required by law to be asbestos-free"** and can itself irritate eyes. She also gives
concrete replacement intervals: **"Throw out mascara every 3 months... Toss eyeliner once a year
and eye shadow at least every two years."**

## Symptoms and body signals addressed

Not directly addressed as uveitis symptoms — this post is about a secondary infection-risk pathway
(cosmetics hygiene) rather than the disease itself.

## Glossary terms introduced

- **Blebitis** — infection of a surgical bleb, named here as the specific risk elevated by an eye
  makeup/hygiene lapse in a post-trabeculectomy patient `[¶1]`.

## Analogies worth reusing

None stated.

## Source-stated confidence

The "makeup doesn't cause uveitis" claim is stated flatly and unhedged. The carcinogenicity claims
about TEA/DEA/talc are attributed to a named source (the Environmental Working Group) rather than
presented as the author's own toxicological judgment, and the talc/asbestos claim is stated
carefully ("some talc contains... although it's required by law to be asbestos-free").

## Conflicts with other sources

No conflicts. `treatment-complications` defines blebitis's parent condition (the bleb itself, from
trabeculectomy) in more clinical depth; `diffusing-the-bomb`, a later post, describes an actual
bleb-leak crisis, giving concrete stakes to the caution raised here.
