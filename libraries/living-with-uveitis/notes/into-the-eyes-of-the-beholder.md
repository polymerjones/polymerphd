# Into the eyes of the beholder

## Central claim

**Without imaging technology, monitoring posterior uveitis would be essentially impossible for a
patient who, as she's said elsewhere, usually can't feel her own flares — this post is a
first-person walkthrough of the specific tests that make that monitoring possible.** Framed
directly: **"One thing about posterior uveitis is that without microscope and imaging technology,
diagnosing and managing the disease would be nearly impossible. It's never lost on me (or my
insurance bill) how these advanced cameras let the outside world in on what is going on in the back
of my eyes"** `[¶1]`.

## The three tests, from the patient's side

`[¶1]`

**Fundus photography**: a camera-equipped microscope photographing the dilated retina; its value
is explicitly comparative rather than diagnostic-in-the-moment: **"Photographs can be used for
comparisons from visit to visit and year to year which enables doctors to notice even minute
differences... Photographs also leave less room for details lost to memory, interpretation and
error in note-taking."** **Angiography** (IVFA and ICG): dye injected into an arm vein, then
photographed; she notes the practical side effect: **"Both dyes are highly concentrated and will
take about a day to pass through your system (i.e. expect neon urine)."** IVFA is **"not
recommended while pregnant."** **Optical Coherence Tomography (OCT)**: light-wave cross-sections of
retinal layers; she compares it favorably to the other two on comfort grounds: **"OCT is
non-invasive, and in my opinion, is much easier on the eyes due to the lack of flash and the fact
that you can blink at any time during the exam."**

## A specific clinic upgrade, and its effect

`[¶1]`

She credits a concrete facility change with reduced discomfort: **"The Retina Center recently
opened in a new location with new imaging equipment and the difference is night and day in terms of
eye fatigue from having photos taken. The flash is so much less bright."**

## Glossary terms introduced

- **Angiography** — imaging the retinal vessels using fluorescent dye, defined here as literally
  "to write or record vessels," with IVFA (sodium fluorescein) and ICG (indocyanine green)
  distinguished by which layer they best highlight `[¶1]`.
