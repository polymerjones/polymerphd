# Immunosuppressed in the Pacific Northwest

## Central claim

**Written at the start of the COVID-19 pandemic (Washington State, March 2020), this post treats
being immunosuppressed for uveitis as a specific, actionable risk category — not just advice to
"be careful" but a concrete plan across avoidance, medication, diet, sleep, and lifestyle.** Framed
directly: **"Since many uveitis patients are on treatments that work by suppressing the immune
system, diligence when it comes to health and not getting sick is never far from our minds...
This is what I'm doing to protect myself and my family"** `[¶1]`.

## Medication timing collided with the pandemic

`[¶1]`

The post is candid that her disease didn't wait for a convenient moment: **"Recently, my uveitis
has become active once again and I started CellCept (mycophenalate) and a pulse of prednisone,
both of which suppress my immune system. The timing.. not so great."** An Ozurdex injection was
added specifically **"so that I can start to taper down the prednisone while the CellCept ramps up
(hopefully) to full efficacy."** A practical supply-chain tip: **"my insurance will cover a 90-day
supply online... whereas only a 30-day supply at my local pharmacy is covered"** — worth stockpiling
before shortages.

## Diet, sleep, and lifestyle adjustments

`[¶1]`

A specific, sourced sleep claim: **"There have even been studies that melatonin may be beneficial
in reducing inflammation associated with uveitis"** — she takes it "an hour before bed every other
night" while on higher-dose prednisone. Diet advice leans on detox-support foods (cruciferous
vegetables, garlic, green tea) and an explicit caution: **"Sugar and refined grains that turn into
sugar can suppress your immune system."** She's candid about the difficulty of the sleep advice
itself given young children and a pandemic: **"Telling someone to get adequate sleep while on
prednisone during a pandemic is a bit like telling them to 'sleep when the baby sleeps.'"**

("Melatonin as a Therapeutic Resource for Inflammatory Visual Diseases (2017)"), though phrased
cautiously ("studies that melatonin *may* be beneficial"). Everything else in the post is stated as
personal choice ("This is what I'm doing"), not general medical advice.
