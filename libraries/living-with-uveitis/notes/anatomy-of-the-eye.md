# Anatomy of the Eye

## Central claim

**This page exists to let a reader map uveitis's location-based classification (from `uveitis-overview`) onto actual eye structures** — it opens by framing itself as scaffolding for
understanding, not new clinical content: **"Being familiar with the anatomy of the eye can make it
easier to understand what is happening with uveitis"** `[¶1]`.

## Structures, each tied to its uveitis subtype

`[¶1]`

The page's organizing move is pairing each structure with the uveitis category that affects it:
**"Ciliary Body: the part of the eye that connects the iris to the choroid... This is the part of
the eye impacted by intermediate uveitis."** **"Choroid: a thin, spongy network of blood vessels...
This is one of the parts of the eye impacted by posterior uveitis (choroiditis)."** **"Iris: the
colored circle at the front of the eye... This is the part of the eye impacted by anterior
uveitis."** The retina is described functionally — **"the layer of cells on the back, inside part
of the eye that converts light into electrical signals sent to the brain"** — with its functional
center named: **"The functional center of the retina is called the macula."** The **uvea** itself
is defined as the umbrella structure: **"the pigmented layer of the eye, lying beneath the sclera
and cornea, and includes the iris, choroid, and ciliary body"** — the anatomic basis for why
"uveitis" covers such different-seeming conditions.

## Glossary terms introduced

- **Ciliary body**, **choroid**, **iris**, **lens**, **optic nerve** (with **optic papilla/optic
  disc** as the point it exits the eye), **pupil**, **retina** (with **macula** as its functional
  center), **sclera**, **uvea**, **vitreous** — each defined in one or two sentences and, where
  applicable, tied to the uveitis subtype affecting it `[¶1]`.
