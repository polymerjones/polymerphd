# Tools, Tests & Terminology

## Central claim

**A uveitis patient will repeatedly encounter a specific, learnable set of imaging and pressure
tests, and this page exists because doctors use their names assuming familiarity the patient
usually doesn't have** — stated directly: **"it can be overwhelming and confusing when doctors and
technicians start using words and terms that are unfamiliar... This page contains definitions of
tools, tests and terminology that I've come across"** `[¶1]`.

## Tests, by what they measure

`[¶1]`

Imaging tests are distinguished from pressure tests. Imaging: the **Amsler Grid** ("detect vision
problems resulting from damage to the macula... or the optic nerve"), **Fluorescein Angiography**
("a fluorescent dye... injected into the bloodstream... highlights the blood vessels in the back of
the eye"; **"Not recommended during pregnancy"**), **Fundus photography** ("high contrast,
high-resolution imaging of the back... interior portion of the eye"), **Indocyanine Green (ICG)
Angiography** ("fluoresces in the infrared spectrum and highlights the choroid layer"), and
**Optical Coherence Tomography (OCT)** ("uses light waves to take cross-section pictures of the
retina"). Pressure and field tests: **Pachymetry** (cornea thickness, which **"has the potential to
influence Interocular Pressure"**), **Perimetry** / the **Humphrey** and **Goldmann Visual Field**
tests (mapping blind spots), the **Snellen Eye Chart** (visual acuity), and **Tonometry** (measures
intraocular pressure; **"The normal range for pressure is 12-21 mm Hg"**, and **"the patient should
make an effort to breathe normally during this test as holding breathe can influence the IOP"**).

## Diagnostic terminology

`[¶1]`

Terms tied specifically to uveitis diagnosis: **Anterior Chamber Flare** ("inflammation in the
front of the eye causes the breakdown of vessels in the blood-aqueous barrier... creating cloudiness
(flare) when a light is shone in the eye... a diagnostic indicator of uveitis"), **Keratic
precipitates** ("clumps of white blood cells deposited on the corneal epithelium... start out white
and round and become irregular and faded with age... large yellow KP are formed and termed
mutton-fat KP"), the **HLA-B27 Gene** ("strongly associated with autoimmune diseases including
anterior uveitis"), and **Synechiae** ("adhesions formed between adjacent structures within the
eye usually as a result of inflammation... a diagnostic indicator of uveitis").

## Symptoms and body signals addressed

- **Dyschromatopsia** (color vision deficiency), **Metamorphopsia** (straight lines look curved —
  "caused by problems with the eye's retina, and, in particular, the macula"), **Photopsia**
  (perceived flashes of light), and **Scotoma** (a blind spot) are each named as visual symptoms a
  patient might report and have a technical name for `[¶1]`.

## Glossary terms introduced

- **Anterior Chamber Flare**, **Blood-Retina Barrier (BRB)**, **Exudate**, **Granulomatous**
  uveitis, **HLA-B27 Gene**, **Keratic precipitates** (with **mutton-fat KP** as the severe form),
  **Retinal Pigment Epithelium (RPE)**, **Retinal striae**, **Retinal vasculitis**, **Synechiae**
  (anterior vs. posterior) `[¶1]`.

## Analogies worth reusing

None stated — terms are defined directly rather than through comparison.

## Source-stated confidence

Definitional throughout; the one caveated claim is diagnostic-test safety, not disease mechanism:
Fluorescein Angiography is flagged **"Not recommended during pregnancy"** without further
elaboration `[¶1]`.

## Conflicts with other sources

diagnostic-indicator list, giving the mechanism behind terms that page names only briefly (e.g.
