# Prednisone: The Good, the Bad, & the Ugly (Part I)

## Central claim

**Prednisone is described as simultaneously the drug that reliably controls her uveitis and the
one with the widest, best-documented list of side effects across nearly every body system — this
post is a systematic side-effect survey grounded in over a decade of personal dosing history.**
Framed directly: **"The prednisone did its job; my uveitis was brought to a screeching halt. But I
couldn't stay on the high dosage forever... due to all of the side effects"** `[¶1]`, and: **"I now
am aware of them, long and short term, and what I can do to lessen them"** `[¶1]`.

## Mechanism: mimicking the body's own stress hormone

`[¶1]`

**"Prednisone is a synthetic glucosteroid... closely related to and mimics cortisol, which is a
hormone naturally produced by the adrenal gland."** The relevant potency figure is stated
precisely: **"Prednisone... is four to five times as potent as cortisol. Therefore, roughly 5 mg of
prednisone is equivalent to the body's daily output of cortisol."** Mechanistically, corticosteroids
block **"substances that create inflammatory actions called prostaglandins... and white blood
cells... In this way, corticosteroids suppress the immune system."**

## Side effects, by body system

`[¶1]`

**Adrenocortical insufficiency**: **"since you're taking prednisone, the body senses the level of
steroid is 'high enough' and stops making as much"** — the reason abrupt cessation is dangerous.
**Immunosuppression**: increased susceptibility "from persistent athlete's foot and candida" up to
"serious and potentially fatal infections like chicken pox and measles"; live-virus vaccines are
contraindicated at immunosuppressive doses. **Musculoskeletal**: **"The American College of
Rheumatology... considers patients receiving... greater than 2.5 mg of prednisone daily for three
months or longer to be at risk for bone loss"** — her own 2018 DEXA scan found osteopenia in her
hips, addressed since with increased calcium, vitamin D, and weight training. **Ocular**:
**"posterior subcapsular and nuclear cataracts and/or increased intraocular pressure"** — the same
complications named in `treatment-complications`. **Nervous system**: insomnia, mood swings,
depression — she notes a side effect she says isn't widely documented: **"feeling warm all of the
time, which can also impact mood and sleeping."** **Endocrine**: decreased glucose tolerance,
hyperglycemia, sodium/fluid retention producing "moon face." **Dermatologic**: acne (**"prednisone
acne is real"**) and thinned, more easily bruised skin.

## Symptoms and body signals addressed

- Rapid weight gain and "moon face" at high doses `[¶1]`.
- Joint/knee pain severe enough to disrupt sleep at 60mg `[¶1]`.
- Chest and neck acne at high doses `[¶1]`.
- Feeling persistently warm, described as an under-documented side effect `[¶1]`.

## Glossary terms introduced

- **Adrenocortical insufficiency** — reduced natural cortisol production caused by sustained
  external steroid supplementation `[¶1]`.
- **"Steroid-sparing" dose** — below 7.5mg, the threshold under which most prednisone side effects
  aren't typically experienced, per the post's cited source `[¶1]`.

Dosing thresholds (the 40mg "high dose" line, the 2.5mg/three-month bone-loss risk threshold) are

outside the literature: **"another side effect I've noticed, but don't generally read in the
