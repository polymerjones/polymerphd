# Stick a needle in my eye: intravitreal steroid implant injection tutorial

## Central claim

**An intravitreal implant injection delivers steroid directly to the eye so the rest of the body
is spared high systemic doses — but "sparing the body" means a real, repeated, invasive procedure
that this post walks through in granular, practical detail from eight rounds of personal
experience.** Framed directly: **"It's a shot right at the target (localized) and allows the rest
of the body to be spared side effects of high doses of steroids"** `[¶1]`, and: **"I've had eight
intravitreal injections. Five in the left eye (Ozurdex and Iluvien) and three in the right (Iluvien,
Ozurdex and, most recently, Yutiq). This is my experience and these are my tips"** `[¶1]`.

## The three implants, compared

`[¶1]`

**Ozurdex**: 0.7mg dexamethasone, 6mm × .46mm, injected via 22-gauge syringe, **"projected to
disperse steroids for around six months with a peak concentration at two months,"** FDA-approved
2009. **Iluvien**: .19mg fluocinolone acetonide, 25-gauge syringe, ~36 months, indicated for
diabetic macular edema, approved 2014. **Yutiq**: .18mg fluocinolone acetonide with an initial
.25mg dispersal, 3.5mm, 25-gauge syringe, ~36 months, approved 2018 specifically for **"chronic
non-infectious uveitis affecting the posterior segment of the eye."** Her own comparison of the
physical sensation: **"Ozurdex has a definite 'click.' Yutiq seemed to require more pressure than
Iluvien in my experience."**

## The procedure and coping tips, step by step

`[¶1]`

Preparation: ask for **"a prescription for valium or some type of relaxant... if you're anxious,"**
and arrange a ride home. The worst part, in her stated opinion: **"the eye speculum. This is the
metal retractor that holds your eye open"** — a smaller or reshaped speculum, or a pediatric size
for a smaller face, is offered as a concrete fix. During the injection: **"tensing up and increased
blinking will make the speculum more uncomfortable"**; afterward, **"putting an ice pack on the eye
repeatedly for about 20 minutes at a time significantly decreased the bleeding and swelling for
me."** A specific cost figure is given: **"Bills for the injection and the visit run about $13,000
when billed through insurance in my experience."**

## Dealing with a visibly bloodshot eye afterward

`[¶1]`

One injection bled badly enough to spread through her entire eye, and the social fallout was
unexpected: **"I was unprepared for all of the stares and flat out rude comments I received... people that I had never met made [references] to physical or domestic abuse."** Her stated approach:
**"I don't even waste my breath on a response to comments or questions that are off-color or
cruel."**

## Symptoms and body signals addressed

- Post-injection bleeding (hemorrhage) at the injection site is described as likely: **"More than
  likely, your injection site will bleed"** `[¶1]`.
- Sensation during injection ranges from a "click" to more sustained pressure, varying by implant
  `[¶1]`.

## Glossary terms introduced

- **Intravitreal steroid implant injection** — implanting corticosteroid directly into the
  vitreous, first FDA-approved in 2009, described here mechanistically and procedurally `[¶1]`.

## Analogies worth reusing

- **"Go to your 'happy place'"** — her own coping instruction for tolerating the needle-numbing
  step, paired with deep breathing `[¶1]`.

## Source-stated confidence

The dosing, duration, and FDA-approval figures for each implant are stated as manufacturer/product
facts; the comfort tips and social-reaction account are explicitly personal ("in my experience,"
repeated multiple times) rather than generalized claims about how all patients will react.
