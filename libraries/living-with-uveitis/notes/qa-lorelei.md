# Q & A with Lorelei

## Central claim

**This is a guest perspective, not the site author's own words: a first-person interview with a
13-year-old whose anterior uveitis is a direct consequence of Juvenile Idiopathic Arthritis (JIA),
diagnosed at age three — a markedly different course than the author's own adult, idiopathic
onset.** Framed by the introduction: **"Lorelei has anterior uveitis, associated with Juvenile
Idiopathic Arthritis (JIA)"** `[¶1]`.

## Diagnosis and treatment, in her own words

`[¶1]`

Diagnosed at three after her parents mistook the sign for pink eye: **"my parents had noticed that
my eye was red and inflamed. They took me to the doctor for what they thought was pink eye, but
when the antibiotics for pink eye didn't work... they finally decided on sending me to a child
ophthalmologist."** Early treatment included two surgeries — cataract removal ("with uveitis can
sometimes cause cataracts") and a second surgery to remove resulting scar tissue. Current treatment:
**"I take Humira which is an immunosuppressant which keeps me from having flare-ups... a
bi-weekly injection that I can give myself at home. I also take methotrexate which is a
chemotherapy drug which stops me from having reactions to some of the other medications."** Past
treatment (Remicade) was harder logistically: **"you have to take intravenously so it was really
hard to go to the hospital and miss so much school."**

## No subjective sign of her own flares

`[¶1]`

Directly asked whether she can tell when she's flaring, she answers no: **"I've never really been
able to tell when they were happening, my JIA... has only affected my eyes so for me there is
really no tell-tale sign that I'm having a flare-up."** She also states the disease has had
minimal functional impact: **"Honestly I've been really lucky it really doesn't affect my daily
life. I can still play sports and do everything someone without uveitis would."**

## Symptoms and body signals addressed

- No felt symptoms of flare activity in her own experience `[¶1]`.
- Initial presenting sign (in early childhood, reported by a parent, not the patient): a visibly
  red and inflamed eye `[¶1]`.

## Glossary terms introduced

- **Juvenile Idiopathic Arthritis (JIA)** — named here as her uveitis's underlying cause; she notes
  **"JIA is actually more common than childhood type 1 diabetes"** `[¶1]`.
