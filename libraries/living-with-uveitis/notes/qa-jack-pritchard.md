# Q & A with Jack Pritchard

## Central claim

**A guest perspective from a 25-year-old web developer with chronic panuveitis in both eyes,
associated with sarcoidosis — notable for naming a specific social friction the site author's own
posts (`what-you-dont-see`) also describe: being young makes an invisible disease even less
believable to other people.** Stated directly: **"As someone who is 25 years old, nobody makes the
assumption that I am suffering from an autoimmune disease which makes it hard to see. Most people
can afford sympathy to older individuals as it's assumed they may have health problems, but younger
individuals are assumed to be in good health"** `[¶1]`.

## Diagnosis and treatment escalation

`[¶1]`

Onset was gradual and easy to misattribute at first: **"flashes of lights in the corner of my eyes,
associated with weight lifting. Next I lost vision in my eyes for a week when on holiday. Then
finally I arrived in eye casualty in October 2018 to be given an official diagnosis."** Treatment
escalated to **"double eye surgery in my right eye. One surgery for cataracts to replace the lens...
and another at the same time to implant a Xen implant or 'shunt' to reduce the pressure,"**
described as decisive: **"The surgery has saved my life, and restored peace in my mind, as prior to
surgery I was in the most difficult flare up of my life."**

## Turning the diagnosis into a professional mission

`[¶1]`

As a web developer, he's using his diagnosis to change his own field: **"I am currently on a
journey to make the world wide web a little bit more accessible to those with Uveitis... I have
been thinking of different ways I can help, even if small. To help get a better understanding on
how I can help, or what I should build, I am speaking with others that have Uveitis or visually
impairing conditions."** He also names a specific communication gap with doctors: **"sometimes
doctors can easily end up getting highly technical in their speech and that's when I turn to online
resources where others have put it in simpler terms."**

## Symptoms and body signals addressed

- Flashes of light associated specifically with weightlifting/exertion, preceding formal diagnosis
  `[¶1]`.
- A week of vision loss `[¶1]`.
- Light sensitivity severe enough to require altering his home computer setup, and difficulty
  reading screens during flares `[¶1]`.

## Glossary terms introduced

- **Xen implant/shunt** — a device implanted alongside cataract surgery to reduce eye pressure,
  distinct from the Ahmed valve and trabeculectomy described in `treatment-complications` `[¶1]`.
