# What you don't see: an open letter from a uveitis patient

## Central claim

**The vision loss from chronic uveitis is permanent in a way most vision problems aren't, and
almost none of what it actually costs is visible to anyone looking at her.** Framed directly:
**"Even if your eyesight starts to go it can usually be fixed through corrective lenses or surgery.
For me, and those with chronic pan or posterior uveitis, once it goes, it's gone. A blur, a
washout, a 'blind spot,' never to return"** `[¶1]`.

## What's actually invisible

`[¶1]`

The post is structured as a repeated "you don't see" — cataloging what's hidden behind a normal
appearance: **"You would never guess that I've sat through countless dilations, pressure checks,
optic photography, Optical coherence tomography (OCT)... You'd never get close enough to notice the
tiny blue mark from the steroid implant... Or guess that I have two stints to reduce eye pressure
and artificial lenses in both eyes."** It extends to family life — **"you don't know that for a
while I was on immunosuppressants and high doses of steroids that were contra-indicative to
pregnancy; that I thought I would have to choose between vision and having children"** — and to
daily function: **"That busy stores overwhelm my lack of vision. That I'm not as adventurous or
confident as I once was."**

## The psychological task, named directly

`[¶1]`

She names managing the emotional side as a distinct, parallel job to medical treatment: **"I've had
to make the decision that just as I am managing the physical aspects of this disease, I must also
confront the psychological. I've learned to allow myself stages of grief and acceptance. When the
anger comes, I feel it, but I know I can't live there."** The post closes on a reframe away from
victimhood: **"Uveitis is a part of my life but I determine how big of a role it plays to some
degree... I see someone who knows what it is to see."**

## Symptoms and body signals addressed

- Permanent loss of a portion of the visual field, described as "a decent portion of the right
  side of my right eye" `[¶1]`.
- Living "in somewhat of a fog," with busy/visually cluttered environments (stores) specifically
  overwhelming `[¶1]`.

## Analogies worth reusing

- **"When you look at me you don't see..."** — the post's repeated structural device contrasting a
  normal appearance against an invisible chronic-illness burden; reusable for any note discussing
  the psychological weight of a condition with no visible marker `[¶1]`.
