# Inflammation & Immune Response

## Central claim

**In uveitis, a normally protective and self-limiting immune process has become chronic — the
same machinery that fights infection is instead running continuously against the eye (and
potentially other tissue).** Stated directly: **"Uveitis patients may have too much of this immune
system response process going on in the eye (and potentially elsewhere in the body); the
inflammation has become chronic and is no longer an acute response to an injury or invader"**
`[¶1]`.

## The Th1/Th2 balance

`[¶1]`

Inflammation itself is framed as adaptive, not pathological: **"Inflammation in itself is not a
'bad' thing. Inflammation is how the body protects us from infection with foreign organisms... The
immune system response is the first step in the healing process."** The mechanism named: **"Helper
T cells" (Th) are one kind of T lymphocyte. Th cells develop into two types, Th1 and Th2"** — Th1
generates a pro-inflammatory response against intracellular parasites, bacteria, and viruses and
**"suppresses Th2 cells"**; Th2 generates an anti-inflammatory antibody response and **"suppresses
Th1 cells"**. The two subtypes hold each other in check under normal conditions.

## Autoimmune versus autoinflammatory disease

`[¶1]`

The page draws a specific, load-bearing distinction. **Autoimmune disease**: **"occurs in the
adaptive immune system when a response against a self-antigen(s) involving T cells, B cells, or
auto-antibodies induces injury systemically or against a particular organ or tissue... The immune
response of the body no longer recognizes what is tolerable or not, self or non-self."**
**Autoinflammatory disease**, by contrast: **"typically occurs when there is a genetic mutation in
the innate immune system. In this case there is no pathogen, however the body is reacting as if
one were present."** A quoted passage from Dr. Mark Hyman frames the everyday version of this:
**"Your body is fighting something — an infection, a toxin, an allergen, a food or the stress
response — and somehow it redirects its hostile attack on your joints, your brain, your thyroid,
your gut, your skin, or sometimes your whole body."**

## Glossary terms introduced

- **Th1 / Th2 cells** — the two helper-T-cell subtypes whose mutual suppression the page describes
  as the normal regulatory mechanism inflammation relies on `[¶1]`.
- **Self-antigen** — "a molecule or organism that acts like an invader (antigen)... but is
  considered tolerable by the body" `[¶1]`.
- **Auto-antibody** — "one that mistakenly targets and reacts with the body's own tissues or
  organs" `[¶1]`.
- **Autoinflammatory disease** — disease driven by an innate-immune-system genetic mutation, with
  no actual pathogen present, distinguished here explicitly from autoimmune disease `[¶1]`.

## Analogies worth reusing

- **The quoted "redirected attack" framing** — "your body is fighting something... and somehow it
  redirects its hostile attack on your joints, your brain, your thyroid" — a way of describing
  autoimmunity as misdirected defense rather than a broken system per se `[¶1]`.

## Source-stated confidence

The Th1/Th2 mechanism and the autoimmune/autoinflammatory distinction are presented as
established immunology, stated plainly without hedging. The "redirected attack" framing is
explicitly attributed as a quotation (Dr. Mark Hyman) rather than presented as the page's own
clinical claim.
