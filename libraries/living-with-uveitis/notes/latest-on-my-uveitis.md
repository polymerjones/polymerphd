# The latest on my uveitis

## Central claim

**A year of monthly visits and escalating treatment (Cellcept, then Humira, then a series of
Avastin injections) got her inflammation "under control" by her own account, but simultaneously
surfaced a new complication — choroidal neovascularization — showing control of one problem
doesn't mean the absence of new ones.** Stated directly: **"Thus, the good news is, my
inflammation seems to be under control. However, in July my optical coherence topography (OCT)...
showed a bit of fluid in the retina, and there was a new hemorrhage"** `[¶1]`.

## The medication escalation, and its practical costs

`[¶1]`

Cellcept alone at 1500mg twice daily plus 20mg prednisone **"was still not controlling my
inflammation. So, we introduced Humira"** — a biweekly self-injected biologic blocking TNF-alpha.
She notes a mundane logistical friction of biologic therapy: **"it is mailed in a large styrofoam
box every month (with ice packs to keep it cold), which I feel guilty about throwing away.**"
Cumulative side effects across three simultaneous medications are reported without trying to
isolate which drug caused what: **"I have noticed I am bruising super easily, am more dried out
than before and my hair is thinning a bit... it's hard to isolate what's causing what."**

## A new diagnosis: choroidal neovascularization

`[¶1]`

Imaging (FA, ICGA, another OCT) led to a CNV diagnosis, explained mechanistically: **"the retina may
produce too much vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF) which leads to the production of new
blood vessels. The blood vessels grow from the choroid into the retina. However, unlike normal
vessels, these vessels are leaky."** A specific prevalence figure is cited for her exact underlying
diagnosis: **"CNV is commonly associated with Multifocal Choroiditis, which is what I have, and is
found in 32-46% of patients (Neri, Piergiorgi, et al., 2009).**" Treatment is Avastin, **"an
intravitreal injection... [that] works because it blocks VEGF,"** given in a series of three, 4–6
weeks apart.

## Bone density: a measured improvement

`[¶1]`

Following a 2018 DEXA scan showing "minor osteopenia in my lumbar spine and left hip," a follow-up
scan two years later showed reversal: **"both areas of osteopenia are now in the 'normal/ expected
range for age.' My bone mineral density increased 3% in the lumbar spine and 1.5% in the left hip.
Diet and exercise do work!"**

## Symptoms and body signals addressed

- New fluid in the retina and a new hemorrhage on fundus photography, discovered on routine
  imaging rather than felt symptomatically `[¶1]`.
- Recurrence of ptosis (drooping eyelid), previously seen after Retisert surgery, noticed again on
  video calls `[¶1]`.
- Easy bruising, dryness, and hair thinning as cumulative side effects across three concurrent
  immunosuppressive medications `[¶1]`.

## Glossary terms introduced

- **Choroidal neovascularization membrane (CNVM) / CNV** — new, leaky blood vessel growth from the
  choroid into the retina, explained here with its VEGF mechanism `[¶1]`.
- **DEXA (Dual-energy X-ray Absorptiometry) Scan** — measures bone mineral density; used here to
  track osteopenia over time `[¶1]`.

## Analogies worth reusing

- **"Just when I feel like I've about filled up my punch card of uveitis complications, I discover
  I have another!"** — a reusable, wry way of describing cumulative complication burden `[¶1]`.

## Source-stated confidence

The CNV prevalence figure (32–46% in Multifocal Choroiditis patients) is directly attributed to a
named study (Neri et al., 2009) rather than stated as general knowledge. The bone-density
improvement is reported as a specific, scan-confirmed measurement, not a subjective impression.
