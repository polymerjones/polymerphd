# Healthy Vision

## Central claim

**Lifestyle choices don't replace uveitis treatment, but they're framed as a second lever the
patient can pull toward the same goal: keeping inflammation and immune response under control.**
Stated directly up front: **"Uveitis is a serious condition for which one must seek medical care.
That said, in addition to following a treatment plan, lifestyle choices have an impact on overall
health, not to mention eye health and vision. The goal is to keep inflammation and immune response
under control"** `[¶1]`.

## Diet

`[¶1]`

The page reframes the usual "anti-inflammatory diet" advice away from restriction: **"Instead of
focusing on what not to eat while cutting the inflammatory foods, it's often helpful to determine
which foods to include in your diet (i.e. food as medicine)."** It also gives a specific,
sourced dosage table — **"The current daily recommendations for healthy eye nutrients, as
suggested by the American Academy of Ophthalmology (AAO)... are: 500 milligrams (mg) of vitamin C
- 400 international units of vitamin E - 10 mg lutein - 2 mg zeaxanthin - 80 mg of zinc oxide - 2
mg of copper oxide."**

## Stress reduction

`[¶1]`

A specific research claim anchors this section: **"a recent study in the Journal of American
Medical Association Ophthalmology correlates higher stress (via perceived stress and cortisol
levels) with active uveitis."** The page is careful to flag this as not fully settled —
**"Although previous studies have come to varied conclusions"** — before listing concrete
practices: exercise, meditation, journaling, getting outside, and other low-cost interventions.

## UV protection

`[¶1]`

Three concrete rules: **"Wear sunglasses with 100% UVA and UVB protection whenever outside. Choose
amber-colored lenses to neutralize blue light. Wear goggles in pools and hot tubs."**

## Symptoms and body signals addressed

## Analogies worth reusing

- **"Food as medicine"** — the page's own reframing device for diet advice, emphasizing addition
  over restriction `[¶1]`.

## Source-stated confidence

The stress/cortisol link is explicitly hedged rather than stated as settled: **"Although previous
studies have come to varied conclusions, a recent study... correlates higher stress... with active
uveitis"** — correlation, not causation, and against a background of mixed prior findings. The AAO
nutrient doses, by contrast, are presented as a specific, unhedged recommendation from a named
authority.
