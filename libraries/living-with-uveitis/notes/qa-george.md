# Q & A with George of "A Flower in their Eyes"

## Central claim

**A guest perspective from a 19-year-old who shares the site author's own diagnosis of Multifocal
Choroiditis, but with panuveitis rather than posterior-only involvement, diagnosed suddenly during
her final year of high school — and whose response was building the public awareness she
couldn't find for herself.** Framed by the introduction: **"She's got my same diagnosis of
'multi-focal choroiditis,' but with panuveitis, which means that her inflammation is all throughout
her eye"** `[¶1]`.

## A fast, disorienting diagnosis

`[¶1]`

Onset was sudden and initially dismissed: **"I started to notice gray spots in my vision... My
optometrist... decided to send me to the emergency room"** on suspicion of retinal detachment or
fluid behind the retina — but **"the emergency room sent me back home telling me there was nothing
they could do"** despite extreme light sensitivity a week later. Her own read: **"I went home with
fear and pain. This was when I knew there was something wrong."**

## Disrupted plans, and building representation instead

`[¶1]`

Diagnosis derailed specific life plans: **"I had wanted to attend my high school and go to all the
senior events but I sadly couldn't due to the photophobia... I had also hoped to go to Community
College in the hopes of going to Forensics or Liberal art. This was not possible since my M.C.P was
still out of control."** She names the compounding difficulty directly: **"The Hardest part of all
of this has been accepting that there is a possibility of losing my vision at any time. What has
also been hard is the lack of support and awareness there is for Uveitis."** Her response was to
create an Instagram account (@theflowerintheireyes): **"There is sadly a lack of representation and
awareness about Uveitis. It took me almost a year to find a community... I would not want anyone
else to feel as alone and hopeless as I did."**

## Symptoms and body signals addressed

- Gray spots in vision as the presenting sign `[¶1]`.
- Extreme light sensitivity (photophobia), severe enough to require independent study rather than
  in-person senior year `[¶1]`.

## Glossary terms introduced

None new beyond terms already covered elsewhere (Multifocal Choroiditis and Panuveitis are named
in `uveitis-overview`'s location-based classification).

## Analogies worth reusing

- **The account's own name, "A Flower in their Eyes"** — inspired by her mother's description of
  her deformed pupils on first diagnosis: "one looked like a snowman while the other looked like a
  flower" `[¶1]`.

## Source-stated confidence

Entirely a guest's first-person account; no external research is cited within it.

## Conflicts with other sources

No conflicts, but a notable point of comparison: George's diagnosis (Multifocal Choroiditis with
Panuveitis) overlaps with the site author's own (Multifocal Choroiditis, described as posterior in
`latest-on-my-uveitis`), illustrating how the same underlying diagnosis can present with different
anatomic extent per `uveitis-overview`'s classification scheme. Alongside `qa-lorelei` and
`qa-jack-pritchard`, this interview reinforces "lack of awareness" as a recurring, independently-
voiced theme across multiple patients, not just the site author.
