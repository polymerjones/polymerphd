# Women and uveitis / my pregnancy journey

## Central claim

**Women make up the majority of uveitis patients, and the leading explanations are hormonal and
genetic rather than incidental — a fact this post treats as backdrop to her own decade-long,
two-pregnancy experience of managing the disease around childbearing.** Stated directly: **"Women
make up the majority of uveitis patients, young and old (Faia and Drenser, 2017)... In the United
States... non-infectious uveitis is more prevalent (80-90% of cases) (Yeh and Shantha, 2016) and is
most often associated with autoimmunity which occurs far more often in women (Fairweather and Rose,
2007)"** `[¶1]`.

## Two proposed mechanisms for female prevalence

`[¶1]`

**X-chromosome inactivation**: **"Since women have two X chromosomes and don't need two copies of
each gene, one of each gene is supposed to be deactivated; when this doesn't happen in an evenly
distributed method, instances of auto-immune disease are more common (Ørstavik, 2017)."**
**Hormonal cycling**: **"Estrogen and progesterone promote the anti-inflammatory response and
therefore suppress pro-inflammatory cytokines"** — and a specific cited study found **"a
significant increase in the incidence of Acute Anterior Uveitis arising late in the menstrual
period,"** postulating that **"the withdrawal of either or both [o]estrogen and progesterone
provokes the onset of inflammation" (Sanghvi, Aziz, & Jones, 2004)**. The same hormonal shift cuts
both ways in pregnancy: rising estrogen/progesterone suppress the Th1 response, which **"can worsen
symptoms in auto-immune diseases associated with Th2 excess (such as lupus) and relieve
auto-immune diseases associated with excess Th1 response"** — and non-infectious uveitis is placed
in the latter, potentially-relieved category (Yeh and Shantha, 2016).

## Her own diagnosis and two pregnancies

`[¶1]`

Onset came suddenly at 27: **"the lights were reflecting oddly to me, like there was a glare or a
smear over my lens"** despite prior LASIK; an optometrist's response — **"Is your dad with you?"**
— signaled how serious it was. To get off high-dose prednisone before trying to conceive, her
doctor prescribed steroid-sparing drugs, but **"a major side effect for me was that I was unable to
get pregnant on them."** A 2012 RETISERT implant and new lens allowed weaning off systemic
medication; a naturopath later adjusted her thyroid medication and progesterone, and **"within a
month I was pregnant."** She raises an open question about whether prednisone itself affects
thyroid levels enough to matter, citing Haugen (2009): **"Glucocorticoids such as prednisone can
lower serum Thyroid Stimulating Hormone (TSH) levels... however do not induce clinically evident
central hypothyroidism even after prolonged high dose use."**

## Symptoms and body signals addressed

- Sudden visual glare/smear as the first sign, despite prior corrective surgery `[¶1]`.
- Rising intraocular pressure during pregnancy from scar tissue blocking a prior shunt, severe
  enough to require a trabeculectomy five months after her first son's birth `[¶1]`.

## Glossary terms introduced

- **RETISERT implant** — "a time-release steroid implant in the eye," introduced here in the
  context of enabling pregnancy by replacing systemic medication `[¶1]`.
- **Ahmed valve** — implanted a year later to reduce intraocular pressure caused by the RETISERT's
  own steroid `[¶1]`.

## Source-stated confidence

The X-chromosome and hormonal-cycling mechanisms are each attributed to a specific named study
rather than stated as settled fact, and one causal question is left explicitly open: **"Does
prednisone cause enough of a suppression that patients may need micrograms of levothyroxine... or
was this a separate issue with my body? Yet another unsolved hormone mystery."**
