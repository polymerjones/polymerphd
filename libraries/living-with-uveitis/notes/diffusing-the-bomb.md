# Diffusing the bomb

## Central claim

**A bleb — the drainage reservoir created by a trabeculectomy she'd had six years earlier — began
leaking "briskly," triggering roughly a month of escalating intervention (bandage contact lens,
pressure and antibiotic drops, near-hospitalization-level pain) before healing on its own.**
Framed directly at the crisis point: **"my uveitis specialist confirmed that the bleb in my right
eye was leaking 'briskly.' I knew it and hadn't wanted to believe it"** `[¶1]`.

## What a bleb leak actually risks

`[¶1]`

Two specific, named dangers: **"Infection: a leak means there's an opening in the eye. If fluid can
get out, bacteria and other organisms can get in and cause infection such as blebitis,
endophthalmitis, etc."** and **"Hypotony: Normal eye pressure range is 12-22 mmHg. Anything below
this is deemed 'hypotony'"** — she notes patients who've had pressure-reducing surgery **"may live
in the hypotonous range"** without issue, until it causes **"shallow anterior chamber."** Her own
pressure dropped to **"2mmHg during one of the appointments in December,"** far below the normal
range.

## The bandage contact lens ordeal

`[¶1]`

The lens intended to reduce irritation instead caused unexpected pain: **"I woke with severe
burning and pulsating pain in my right eye. I couldn't get it to stop... My husband found me
sobbing in the bathroom sometime around 2:30 AM"** — leading to an ER visit and multiple lens-size
changes before one was tolerated. She's explicit this isn't advice: **"do NOT try this at home"**
after describing attempting to reinsert the lens herself. Autologous blood injection — **"he would
take blood from my arm, and inject it into the bleb to stimulate healing"** — was named as the next
option if the leak didn't resolve on its own, with surgical repair as the last resort; neither was
ultimately needed.

## Symptoms and body signals addressed

- A sudden burst sensation in the eye described as comparable to "water breaking" late in
  pregnancy `[¶1]`.
- Severe burning and pulsating pain specifically from the bandage contact lens itself, distinct
  from the underlying leak `[¶1]`.
- Progressive eye dryness during healing, interpreted (correctly, in this case) as a positive sign
  of the leak closing `[¶1]`.

## Glossary terms introduced

- **Bleb leak** and **hypotony** — defined here with a specific numeric threshold (below 12 mmHg)
  and its downstream risk (shallow anterior chamber) `[¶1]`.
- **Autologous blood injection** — a bleb-leak treatment using the patient's own blood to stimulate
  healing, positioned between drops/contact lens and surgical repair `[¶1]`.

## Analogies worth reusing

- **"Diffusing the bomb"** — the post's own title and closing image for slowly defusing a crisis
  through patience rather than a single decisive fix `[¶1]`.
- **"Ticking Time Bomb"** — the (real) title of an online article she found while researching her
  own symptoms at 2am, used here as an example of how frightening unfiltered patient searching can
  be `[¶1]`.

## Source-stated confidence

The pressure thresholds (12–22 mmHg normal range, hypotony below that) are stated as established
clinical fact. Her guess about the leak's cause — **"the guess was that it was punctured, or just
repeatedly scraped by, the eyelid holder"** — is explicitly flagged as speculation, not a
diagnosed mechanism.
