# Living With Uveitis — Knowledge Base Overview

This package synthesizes 29 sources from livingwithuveitis.com, a personal site written by one
uveitis patient (Wendy) over 2019–2024: eight educational reference pages covering uveitis basics,
eye anatomy, diagnostic tools, inflammation mechanism, and treatment options; eighteen first-person
blog posts documenting her own diagnosis, flares, procedures, and medication history in real time;
and three guest Q&A interviews with other uveitis patients she connected with through the site.

**This is a patient-voice corpus, not a clinical reference.** The reference pages cite published
research the author found and read herself, but the bulk of the material — the blog posts — is
lived experience: what a specific treatment felt like, what a doctor said in a specific
appointment, what worked for her and what didn't. Where a claim traces to cited research, the notes
preserve the citation; where it's personal experience, the notes preserve that too, and keep the
two visibly distinct rather than blending them into one authoritative voice. Two site pages
(`covid-19-uveitis`, `research-links`) were fetched but excluded from notes — see
`deferred_sources.txt` — because they are bare link lists with no first-person claims of their
own to cite.

## What's in each file

- **`01_CORE_PRINCIPLES.md`** — the recurring ideas across this collection: how uveitis is
  classified, why it resists the patient's own pattern-matching, and the treatment ladder from eye
  drops to biologics.
- **`07_PLAIN_ENGLISH_GLOSSARY.md`** — every technical term introduced across the collection,
  defined in plain English, alphabetical, no citations.
- **`09_SOURCE_CATALOG.md`** — auto-generated index of every source; do not hand-edit.

## A note on scope

This collection is a single patient's account plus three guest interviews — it is not a
population-level epidemiological survey or a clinical practice guideline, even where it cites one.
Treatment details (dosing, side effects, procedure experience) reflect one person's course of
non-infectious posterior/panuveitis and multifocal choroiditis; a reader with a different uveitis
subtype (e.g. purely anterior, or infectious) would find less of this collection directly
applicable, though the classification and mechanism pages (`uveitis-overview`, `anatomy-of-the-eye`,
`inflammation`) are general.
