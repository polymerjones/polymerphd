# Plain-English Glossary

Terms introduced across this library's sources, defined in plain English. Alphabetical. No
citations here — see the individual source for the origin of each term.

**Adrenal crisis** — a rare, potentially fatal reaction to a lack of steroid hormone in the body,
most often a risk during abrupt steroid withdrawal after long-term use.

**Adrenocortical insufficiency** — the body's own cortisol production winding down because
supplemented steroid (like prednisone) has been telling the adrenal glands they aren't needed; the
reason stopping steroids abruptly is dangerous.

**Ahmed Glaucoma Valve** — a silicone drainage valve with a flow restrictor, implanted to reduce
eye pressure while preventing pressure from dropping too low right after surgery.

**Angiography (ocular)** — imaging the blood vessels in the eye using a fluorescent dye injected
into an arm vein; IVFA uses sodium fluorescein (yellow-green), ICG uses indocyanine green
(infrared, better for viewing the choroid).

**Anterior Chamber Flare** — cloudiness seen in the front of the eye when inflammation breaks down
the blood-aqueous barrier, letting cells and protein leak into the fluid there; a diagnostic sign
of uveitis.

**Antimetabolite** — a drug that interferes with a cell's normal metabolic processes, slowing the
immune system by blocking cell survival or multiplication (e.g. methotrexate, mycophenolate).

**Aqueous Shunt Implantation** — inserting a small tube to keep a drainage opening (created by a
trabeculectomy) from scarring shut.

**Auto-antibody** — an antibody that mistakenly targets and attacks the body's own tissues.

**Autoinflammatory disease** — disease driven by a genetic mutation in the innate immune system,
where the body reacts as though a pathogen is present even though none actually is — distinct from
autoimmune disease, which involves the adaptive immune system misidentifying self as foreign.

**Autologous blood injection** — a bleb-leak treatment where blood drawn from the patient's own arm
is injected into the leaking bleb to stimulate healing.

**Baerveldt / Molteno Glaucoma Implant** — pressure-reducing drainage implants without a built-in
valve; must be temporarily blocked with a stitch at surgery to prevent excessive early drainage.

**Bleb** — a small reservoir under the eye's surface, created by a trabeculectomy, that lets fluid
drain out of the eye in a controlled way. A **bleb leak** is a breach in this reservoir; risks
include infection (blebitis) and hypotony.

**Blebitis** — infection of a surgical bleb, an elevated risk for anyone with a history of
trabeculectomy.

**Blood-Retina Barrier (BRB)** — a two-layer barrier that selectively controls what can diffuse
into the retina, nourishing and protecting it.

**Calcineurin inhibitor** — a drug that blocks the enzyme calcineurin, which activates T-cells,
lowering immune activity (e.g. cyclosporine, tacrolimus).

**Choroid** — a network of blood vessels beneath the retina that supplies it with nutrients;
inflammation here is posterior uveitis (choroiditis).

**Choroidal Neovascularization (CNV)** — growth of new, abnormal, leaky blood vessels from the
choroid into the retina, a complication that can cause vision loss; when scarring follows, it's
called subretinal fibrosis.

**Ciliary body** — the eye structure connecting the iris to the choroid; controls lens shape for
focusing and is the part affected by intermediate uveitis.

**DEXA (Dual-energy X-ray Absorptiometry) Scan** — a scan measuring bone mineral density, used to
track bone loss risk from long-term steroid use.

**Dyschromatopsia** — deficient or inaccurate color vision.

**Exudate** — fluid, cells, and protein that leak out of blood vessels into nearby tissue.

**Granulomatous uveitis** — a form of anterior uveitis with a grainy appearance from clumped
keratic precipitates.

**HLA-B27 Gene** — a genetic marker strongly associated with autoimmune diseases including
anterior uveitis, though the underlying mechanism isn't established.

**Hypotony** — abnormally low eye pressure (below roughly 12 mmHg), which can distort the eye's
internal structure if it persists.

**Idiopathic** — no identifiable cause found; true for at least a third of uveitis cases.

**Immunomodulatory Treatment (IMT)** — treatment that works by activating or suppressing the
immune system; in uveitis, this generally means suppressing it.

**Intravitreal steroid implant injection** — injecting a slow-release corticosteroid implant
directly into the vitreous to treat inflammation locally rather than systemically.

**Iris** — the colored ring at the front of the eye that controls pupil size; the part affected by
anterior uveitis.

**Juvenile Idiopathic Arthritis (JIA)** — a childhood autoimmune arthritis that can cause uveitis
as an associated condition, more common than childhood type 1 diabetes.

**Keratic precipitates (KP)** — clumps of white blood cells deposited on the inner cornea, a
diagnostic sign of inflammation; large clumped ones are called "mutton-fat KP."

**Lens** — the transparent structure that focuses light into the eye; the site of cataract
formation.

**Metamorphopsia** — a visual distortion where straight lines appear curved, caused by retinal
(often macular) problems.

**Optic nerve** — the bundle of fibers carrying signals from retina to brain; where it exits the
eye is called the optic papilla or optic disc.

**Panuveitis** — inflammation across all areas of the eye (anterior, intermediate, and posterior
combined).

**Photopsia** — perceived flashes of light in the field of vision.

**Pupil** — the dark opening in the center of the iris that regulates how much light enters the
eye.

**Retina** — the layer of light-sensing cells at the back of the eye; its functional center is
called the macula.

**Retinal Pigment Epithelium (RPE)** — the pigmented cell layer between the retina and choroid
that nourishes retinal cells.

**Retinal striae** — concentric surface lines on the retina, appearing after retinal reattachment
(spontaneous or surgical).

**Retinal vasculitis** — inflammation of retinal blood vessels accompanied by evidence of ocular
inflammation and vascular change.

**RETISERT implant** — a surgically implanted, longer-duration time-release steroid implant for
the eye.

**Sclera** — the white outer layer of the eyeball.

**Scotoma** — a partial loss of vision or blind spot within an otherwise normal visual field.

**Self-antigen** — a molecule the body could treat as foreign but normally tolerates.

**"Steroid-sparing" dose** — a low prednisone dose (below roughly 7.5mg) at which most patients
don't experience prednisone's characteristic side effects.

**SUN (Standardization of Uveitis Nomenclature)** — a 2004 specialist working group that
established the standard duration- and location-based classification of uveitis still used today.

**Synechiae** — adhesions between adjacent eye structures caused by inflammation (e.g. the iris
sticking to the cornea or lens); a diagnostic sign of uveitis.

**Tear film** — the eye's own three-layer protective fluid coating, which chlorinated pool water
can wash away, temporarily removing a defense against infection.

**Th1 / Th2 cells** — two types of helper T-cell that normally hold each other's activity in
check; Th1 drives pro-inflammatory responses, Th2 drives anti-inflammatory antibody responses.

**TNF (Tumor Necrosis Factor) inhibitor** — a biologic drug that blocks excess TNF-alpha protein
believed to drive inflammation in non-infectious uveitis (e.g. adalimumab/Humira).

**Trabeculectomy** — surgery that lowers eye pressure by removing a piece of the trabecular
meshwork so fluid can drain to a bleb under the eye's surface.

**Uvea** — the pigmented middle layer of the eye (iris, choroid, and ciliary body together),
lying beneath the sclera and cornea; uveitis is inflammation here.

**Vitreous** — the gel-like, fluid-filled space inside the eye that gives it its shape.

**Xen implant/shunt** — a small device implanted to reduce eye pressure, often alongside cataract
surgery.
