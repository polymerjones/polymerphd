# 06 — Open Questions

Claims in this collection worth treating with more caution than the collection's more directly
sourced material, and places where this collection's own coverage is thin or internally
inconsistent. Nothing here is resolved — the point is to keep the derived-versus-stated distinction
visible.

---

## Statistics and research claims cited without a named source

Several specific, quotable statistics appear across this collection attributed only vaguely or not
at all: a claim that the average person loses over 13 years of life to activities producing zero
lasting value; a claim that talking about a goal produces a neurological reward comparable to
achieving it, reducing the urgency of actual progress; and a claim that roughly half of newly learned
material is forgotten within a day without review (the "forgetting curve"). Some cited research is
named specifically (Robert Emmons on gratitude, Matthew Walker on sleep, Alan Luks on the "helper's
high," Robert Waldinger on relationship quality, Herbert Simon on bounded rationality, Josh Waitzkin
on skill acquisition) and reads as more directly traceable; claims without a named source are
preserved here as stated, not independently verified.

## Anonymous authorship, and what that means for confidence

The large majority of this collection's videos have no identified individual narrator or author —
unlike the smaller named-creator cluster, there's no professional identity, credential, or track
record to weigh the content against. Treat this cluster's claims as popularized synthesis of
widely-circulated self-help and philosophical ideas, not as a specific expert's stated professional
opinion.

## Sensitive material flagged by a source as needing unusual care

At least one note in this collection explicitly flags its own most sensitive claim (that ongoing
grief can, at some point, become a person's last remaining felt connection to someone they lost) as
needing "the most care in the saying" — a rare moment of a source hedging its own emotionally
weighty content directly rather than stating it flatly. Worth preserving that hedge exactly rather
than smoothing it into a flatter claim.

## Citation problems documented in this library

See `05_CROSS_SOURCE_COMPARISON.md` for specific, checkable citation issues within this collection
(a misattributed quotation, an inconsistently transliterated Greek term, reused quotations across
many separate videos) — these affect confidence in the larger cluster's citation precision
specifically, not necessarily the underlying practical advice.

## Coverage gaps

This collection is oriented almost entirely toward individual practice and interpretation — it does
not address group or institutional applications of Stoic philosophy, nor does it engage with
academic Stoicism scholarship in any depth beyond the smaller named-creator cluster. A user looking
for rigorous philosophical scholarship on Stoicism, as opposed to popularized practical technique,
would be better served by the Pigliucci material specifically than by this collection's larger
cluster.
