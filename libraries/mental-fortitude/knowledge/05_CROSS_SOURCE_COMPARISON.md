# 05 — Cross-Source Comparison

This library's two content clusters — a large anonymous-narrator listicle channel, and a small set
of individually-credited long-form videos — differ sharply in citation practice. This file documents
that difference, plus citation-quality issues visible within the larger cluster itself.

---

## The "Stoic" tag frequently doesn't reflect the actual citation makeup

A large number of individual notes in this collection explicitly flag their own video's citation
mix, and the pattern is consistent: many videos tagged "STOICISM" cite as many or more non-Stoic
figures as genuine Stoic ones — Jung, Nietzsche, Rousseau, Kant, Mill, Kierkegaard, Wittgenstein,
William James, Machiavelli, Sun Tzu, Lao Tzu, Rumi, Aristotle, Socrates, Spinoza, Adler, Frankl, and
occasionally explicitly multi-faith religious framing ("not a Christian message... not Muslim...
Hindu or Jewish or Buddhist"). At least one video in this collection cites zero Stoic philosophers at
all despite carrying the "STOICISM" title tag. This isn't unique to a handful of videos — it's
common enough across the corpus to treat as a structural feature of this channel's content, not an
occasional lapse.

## A recognizable hedging pattern in the listicle cluster

Several notes flag the same specific rhetorical habit: a non-Stoic philosopher's view is introduced
with softening language ("his thinking may suggest," "her work indicates") rather than presented as
a direct quotation or citation, distinguishable from how the same videos treat their genuine Stoic
citations, which are typically presented as direct quotes. This suggests the channel itself
distinguishes, at some level, between material it can attribute with confidence and material it's
paraphrasing more loosely.

## Documented citation-quality issues within the corpus

A small number of specific, checkable problems appear across individual notes in this collection: a
Seneca quotation used in one video is attributed to Marcus Aurelius in a different video; a single
Greek philosophical term (most likely "prohairesis") is auto-caption-transliterated at least three
different ways across different videos, none confirmed as correct; and the same core set of
Marcus Aurelius, Epictetus, and Seneca quotations recur verbatim across many separate videos in this
collection, suggesting a shared or reused script/quote bank behind the channel's content rather than
each video representing independent research.

## The named-creator cluster's different register

The smaller cluster of individually-credited videos (Pigliucci, O'Connor, Shipper, LePera) is
consistently more precise about sourcing: named academic terms, explicit historical lineages (e.g.
Epictetus's teaching traced through medieval Christian monastic use into the modern serenity prayer),
and an explicit first-person acknowledgment when a claim is the speaker's own extrapolation rather
than settled fact. This collection's most reliably-sourced material concentrates in this smaller
cluster; the larger listicle cluster is better treated as popularized, sometimes loosely-attributed
synthesis than as a precise citation record.

## Where the two clusters converge despite their different registers

Despite the difference in rigor, both clusters converge on several of the same underlying claims
documented in `01_CORE_PRINCIPLES.md` — particularly that emotional reactivity is best addressed by
inserting a pause before response (framed as the "discipline of assent" in the Stoic-branded
cluster, and as recognizing an automatic nervous-system or conditioned pattern in the named-creator
cluster), and that inherited, childhood-formed patterns explain much of adult reactivity that feels,
subjectively, like an innate personality trait.
