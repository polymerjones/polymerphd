# 03 — Daily Practices

A large share of this collection is built around a morning or evening routine format. Practices
gathered here by when they're meant to be used, not by which video they came from.

---

## Before getting out of bed / immediately on waking

- Deciding a first thought or emotional posture deliberately, before checking a phone or engaging
  any external demand.
- Silently stating a short, chosen intention for how the day will be approached.
- Treating the first minutes after waking as unusually impressionable — several sources use the
  image of wet cement, or a glass of disturbed water needing to settle — and protecting them
  specifically from reactive input.

## In the first hour

- Reserving a fixed period (commonly framed as the "first hour") for self-directed activity —
  movement, reading, silence, journaling — before checking messages or engaging others' demands.
- A short period (a few minutes to fifteen) of sitting in complete silence with no phone or music.
- Naming, specifically, one thing genuinely glad to exist, rather than a general positive mood.
- Naming a realistic specific difficulty the day might bring and deciding in advance who you want to
  be if it happens (a version of premeditatio malorum, see `02_STOIC_TECHNIQUES_AND_PHILOSOPHY.md`).
- Sorting the day's goals, tasks, and relationships into what's genuinely within your control and
  what isn't.
- Light physical movement (a short walk, simple bodyweight exercise) framed around felt experience
  and capacity to endure difficulty, not appearance or formal exercise.

## During the day, at the moment of a triggering event

- Pausing for a few seconds before responding to a provocation, and noticing the physical sensation
  of the reaction (tightness, heat, quickened breath) before acting on it.
- Slowing speech and lowering the voice specifically when tension rises, rather than speeding up or
  raising it.
- Silently renaming an automatic reaction as an interpretation rather than as settled fact — "an
  impression has arisen that tells me X," not "X is true."
- Asking whether a rising feeling is concern (actionable, worth engaging) or worry (not actionable,
  self-generated), and directing energy only toward the former.
- Testing whether a reaction or urge is approval-driven by asking whether the same choice would be
  made if no one would ever find out about it.

## At day's end / before sleep

- A brief, honest, non-punitive review of the day: what went wrong, what went well, what tomorrow
  needs that today didn't get.
- Writing down one true, specific thing done that day worth self-respect.
- Recalling one specific good moment from the day in sensory detail, rather than a general "good
  day" feeling.
- Sorting the day's remaining emotional weight into what's genuinely yours to carry and what was
  never yours, and deliberately releasing the latter before sleep.
- Deciding, the night before, who you want to be the next day — patient, honest, brave — as a
  deliberate choice made in advance rather than a reaction generated in the moment.

## Governing principles across all of the above

- Nearly every source is explicit that these practices are cumulative and repeated, not a one-time
  fix — several state directly that a single application will not produce lasting change, only
  consistent daily repetition will.
- Multiple sources warn against a specific misreading: an early sense of relief, calm, or reduced
  intensity is not evidence the underlying issue is resolved, and shouldn't be treated as permission
  to stop practicing.
- Several sources frame these routines as effective specifically because they're practiced when
  stakes are low (an easy, ordinary day), which is what makes the same capacity available later when
  stakes are genuinely high.
