# Plain-English Glossary

Terms introduced across this library's notes, defined in plain English. Alphabetical. No citations
here — see the individual note for the source of each term. This is a substantial first synthesis
pass across a 113-note library — later notes may introduce further terms not yet reflected here.

**Act as if** (Alfred Adler, as paraphrased) — deliberately acting as though a limiting belief is
already false and letting accumulated small evidence outvote the old belief.

**Akrasia** (Aristotle, as paraphrased) — acting against one's own better judgment, reframed in this
collection as a failure of integrated character rather than simple weak willpower.

**Amor fati** — a Stoic practice of not merely tolerating but affirming whatever occurs.

**Amour propre** (Rousseau, as paraphrased) — self-love made contingent on others' opinions,
distinct from self-respect.

**Ataraxia** — a state of serene calm resulting from not being controlled by external circumstances.

**Bounded rationality** (Herbert Simon, as paraphrased) — the claim that decision quality degrades
past a certain number of available options, rather than continuing to improve with more choice.

**The discipline of assent** — the deliberate, examined choice to let an automatic impression become
belief and then action, distinguished from the impression's automatic, involuntary arrival.

**Desirable difficulty** — a learning challenge that feels harder in the moment but produces better
long-term retention than an easier equivalent.

**The dichotomy of control** — sorting anything a person faces into what is genuinely within their
power (judgment, effort, choice) and what is not, directing effort only toward the former.

**Emotional contagion** — moods and emotional states transferring between people largely
involuntarily, through tone and expression.

**Extinction burst** — a manipulator's escalation in intensity once a previously effective tactic
stops producing a response, explained as desperation rather than growing power.

**The forgetting curve** — the claim that roughly half of newly learned material is forgotten within
a day without deliberate review.

**Hegemonikon** — the Stoic term for the mind's ruling, interpreting faculty, described as
especially vulnerable in the first minutes after waking.

**The helper's high** (Alan Luks) — a small physiological lift from performing an act of kindness,
compared to the effect of light exercise.

**Hesychia** — inner quiet as an ordered, clear mind, distinguished from an empty or merely detached
one.

**Investment in loss** — deliberately practicing in front of more skilled people so weaknesses
become visible and correctable early, rather than concealed.

**Logotherapy** (Viktor Frankl) — the claim that a person can endure almost any hardship given a
sufficient reason why; applied in this collection to purpose as what displaces compulsive craving.

**Ma** — a Japanese concept of meaningful negative space between words, applied to deliberate silence
after stating a position.

**Memento mori** — holding the fact of one's own mortality as a daily, felt reference point, used to
clarify which present activities will matter in retrospect.

**Neuroplasticity** (Norman Doidge, as paraphrased) — the brain's capacity to physically rewire
itself with each repeated choice, used to argue a craving or reactive pattern is not permanent.

**Phantasia** — the automatic, pre-conscious mental impression that arrives before conscious
evaluation, distinguished from the interpretation built on top of it.

**Premeditatio malorum** — briefly and deliberately rehearsing a specific anticipated difficulty in
advance, distinguished from extended anxious rehearsal.

**Prohairesis** — the Stoic faculty of judgment and choice, named as the one thing that cannot be
taken by external circumstance. Auto-generated captions across this collection render this term
inconsistently ("prohiresis," "prohesis," "prohyises"); no single spelling is confirmed correct.

**Prosoche** — self-attention practiced as curious, non-judgmental self-observation rather than
self-criticism.

**Rumination** — a named psychological pattern of replaying past pain, linked in cited research to
anxiety and depression, distinguished from productive "processing."

**Savoring** (Fred Bryant) — deliberately slowing down to register a good moment while it's
happening, using specific sensory detail rather than vague general presence.

**Self-handicapping** — unconsciously lowering expectations or pre-emptively self-criticizing so
that a future failure will not feel as significant.

**The Stockdale Paradox** — surviving hardship by refusing false near-term hope while holding a
stubborn long-term belief in eventual success.
