# 10 Stoic Rules of a High Value Man - A CODE FOR MEN WHO BUILD | STOICISM

## Central claim

**A felt sense of being adrift or unfulfilled is attributed not to a lack of talent or opportunity but to the absence of a personal code — a set of unbreakable standards for what one accepts, refuses, and pursues.** Stated directly: **"Nobody is going to save you... What is missing is a code. A set of rules that a man lives by. Not because someone is watching, but because of who he has decided to be"** `[00:01]`, `[02:02]`.

## One: never chase, never beg for love

`[02:02]`–`[04:06]` — **Marcus Aurelius** is quoted: **"It is not death that a man should fear, but he should fear never beginning to live"** `[02:02]`, and: **"Never esteem anything as of advantage to you that will make you break your word or lose your self-respect"** `[04:06]`. The claim: chasing communicates one thing regardless of intention — **"I do not believe I am enough. And if you do not believe you are enough, why would anyone else?"** `[04:06]`.

## Two: wealth is quiet, desperation is loud

`[04:06]`–`[07:11]` — **Epictetus** is quoted: **"Wealth consists not in having great possessions, but in having few wants"** `[06:11]`. Illustrated with two contrasting restaurant patrons — one performing spending loudly, the other quietly self-contained — with the claim: **"Approval bought with money is not respect. It is rent. It expires the moment you stop paying"** `[06:11]`.

## Three: zero tolerance for disrespect

`[07:11]`–`[09:14]` — **Marcus Aurelius** is quoted: **"If it is not right, do not do it. If it is not true, do not say it"** `[08:14]`. The mechanism claim: **"You do not tolerate it once and it respects your patience. You tolerate it once and it takes that as permission"** `[08:14]`. Explicitly distinguished from aggression: "Zero tolerance does not mean... explosions of anger... It means calm, clear, unmoving boundaries" `[08:14]`.

## Four: never beg — open the door and keep walking

`[09:14]`–`[11:19]` — **Marcus Aurelius** is quoted: **"Make the best use of what is in your power and take the rest as it happens"** `[10:17]`. Explicitly distinguished from suppression: **"A high-value man feels the pain. He allows himself to feel it fully. He does not perform stoicism"** `[10:17]`.

## Five: a peaceful life is worth more than any game — stay away from manipulators

`[11:19]`–`[13:20]` — **Marcus Aurelius** is quoted: **"The impediment to action advances action. What stands in the way becomes the way"** `[12:20]`. The source itemizes manipulation's cost specifically: lost clarity ("you lose trust in your own perception"), lost peace, and lost time — "the hours spent replaying conversations... you will never get them back" `[12:20]`.

## Six: self-respect means closing doors that hurt you

`[13:20]`–`[15:24]` — **Epictetus** is quoted: **"He is a wise man who does not grieve for the things which he has not, but rejoices for those which he has"** `[14:22]`. A specific diagnostic question is offered before returning to a past relationship: "did she come back because she genuinely chose you or because she had nowhere else to go?" `[13:20]`.

## Seven: solve problems, don't wait for rescue

`[15:24]`–`[17:25]` — **Marcus Aurelius** is quoted: **"You have power over your mind, not outside events. Realize this and you will find strength"** `[16:24]`. Prescribed question when facing a problem: **"'What can I do?' Not 'Who will help me?'... Not 'When will things get easier?'"** `[16:24]`.

## Eight: never compete for love

`[17:25]`–`[19:26]` — **Epictetus** is quoted: **"Seek not that the things which happen should happen as you wish, but wish the things which happen to be as they are"** `[18:26]`. Explicitly distinguished from competitiveness generally: "A high-value man competes fiercely in his work, in his growth... But love is not one of those areas" `[17:25]`.

## Nine: build your own — rely on no one, be independent

`[19:26]`–`[21:28]` — **Marcus Aurelius** is quoted: **"Never let the future disturb you. You will meet it if you have to with the same weapons of reason which today arm you against the present"** `[19:26]`. Independence is explicitly distinguished from isolation: **"A high-value man is not a man who needs no one. He is a man who needs no one to do for him what he can do for himself"** `[19:26]`.

## Ten: anything that disturbs your peace steals your power

`[21:28]`–`[24:31]` — **Epictetus's** "make the best use of what is in your power" is reused from principle four `[23:30]`. Peace is explicitly reframed as a resource: **"A high-value man treats his peace the way a wealthy man treats his money. He does not give it to everything that asks for it"** `[23:30]`.

## Signals and internal states addressed

- **A quiet, persistent feeling that "something is off" despite adequate talent or opportunity** — attributed to an absent personal standard rather than a personal deficiency `[00:01]`.
- **Sending late-night messages or apologizing for things not one's fault after being ignored** — named directly as forms of begging communicated through behavior, not just words `[03:05]`.
- **Rationalizing a return to a past relationship** ("she has changed," "the timing was wrong before") — met with a specific diagnostic question rather than dismissed outright `[13:20]`.
- **A tiredness that "sleep does not fix"** — attributed to "living below yourself for too long... performing strength you do not feel" `[27:33]`.
- **Feeling drained after a specific recurring relationship, habit, or environment** — treated as actionable signal rather than something to tolerate as ordinary difficulty `[22:29]`.

## Analogies worth reusing

- **Approval bought with money as rent rather than ownership** — used to describe the temporary, expiring nature of respect purchased through visible spending `[06:11]`.
- **Peace treated like money to be invested carefully** — used to reframe protecting one's attention and environment as active stewardship rather than passive luck `[23:30]`.
- **Recycling pain rather than protecting the heart** — used to describe returning to a relationship without requiring genuine accountability or change `[14:22]`.
