# Repeat These 10 Words Every Morning - Rewires Your Mind | STOICISM

## Central claim

**The first few minutes after waking are the highest-leverage, most exploitable window of the day, and the specific words a person says to themselves in that window function as instructions that load before everything else runs** `[01:06]`. The video's stated mechanism: **"The words you say to yourself in the morning are not just words. They are instructions. They load into your mind before the day starts running on it"** `[01:06]`, and they **"determine what you notice, how you respond, what you believe is possible"** `[02:07]`. It offers ten scripted affirmations, each built on a specific quotation from Marcus Aurelius, Seneca, or Epictetus and paired with a specific daily failure mode.

## Ten morning affirmations

`[04:11]`–`[09:19]` — **One, "Today I choose my response, not my circumstances."** Epictetus, who **"was enslaved"** and **"had less control over his external circumstances than almost anyone,"** is quoted: **"it is not what happens to you but how you react to it that matters"** `[05:14]`. Marcus Aurelius is added: **"You have power over your mind, not outside events. Realize this and you will find strength"** `[07:16]`.

`[09:19]`–`[14:29]` — **Two, "I am not behind. I am exactly where my journey has brought me."** Answers the feeling of being "late" against an unconsciously assembled timeline by arguing the comparison is against **"a fantasy that never had to survive contact with reality"** `[10:20]`. Marcus Aurelius's **"confine yourself to the present"** is reframed **"not as a spiritual instruction to ignore the future"** but as **"a practical recognition that the present is the only place where anything actually gets built"** `[11:22]`.

`[14:29]`–`[19:35]` — **Three, "My peace is not for sale. Not today. Not for anyone."** Seneca is quoted: **"True happiness is to enjoy the present without anxious dependence upon the future... to rest satisfied with what we have"** `[16:30]`. The source names the mechanism directly: **"The entire attention economy is built on the premise that a peaceful, settled, genuinely content human being is useless to them because a peaceful human being does not click as much"** `[17:30]`.

`[19:35]`–`[24:42]` — **Four, "I release what I cannot control and pour myself into what I can."** States the dichotomy of control via Epictetus: **"Of things, some are in our power and others are not. In our power, our opinion, motivation, desire, aversion, not in our power, our body, reputation, command"** `[21:37]`. The Marcus Aurelius "power over your mind" line is repeated from affirmation one `[23:40]`.

`[24:42]`–`[29:48]` — **Five, "I am becoming... more of who I am meant to be."** Argues growth in "wisdom and resilience" is invisible while occurring, unlike a bank balance or a scale `[25:42]`. Seneca: **"It is not that I am brave. It is that I have decided what is worth my courage"** `[26:42]`.

`[29:48]`–`[34:52]` — **Six, "I do not need their approval to know my own worth."** Traces approval-seeking to a childhood survival strategy that **"never got the update"** `[30:49]`. Epictetus: **"Never say about anything that I have lost it, but that I have returned it"** `[31:50]`, applied here to returning to an unperformed self.

`[34:52]`–`[38:56]` — **Seven, "Difficult does not mean impossible. Hard does not mean wrong."** Names a specific cognitive trap — treating difficulty itself as a stop signal `[34:52]` — and answers it with Marcus Aurelius: **"the impediment to action advances action... What stands in the way becomes the way"** `[36:55]`.

`[38:56]`–`[44:03]` — **Eight, "I give my best to this day and I let the rest go."** Separates effort from outcome explicitly: **"outcomes are not determined solely by your effort"** but by **"approximately a thousand other factors that were never in your hands"** `[39:59]`. Seneca: **"Reclaim yourself for you were being robbed"** `[41:00]`, here applied to self-regard rather than time.

`[44:03]`–`[49:09]` — **Nine, "I choose who gets my energy today. Not by accident, by decision."** Epictetus: **"Make the best use of what is in your power and take the rest as it happens"** `[46:04]`. Explicitly distinguished from selfishness: **"The selfish person withholds energy from others to preserve it for their own comfort. The intentional person allocates energy toward what genuinely deserves it"** `[46:04]`.

`[49:09]`–`[54:15]` — **Ten, "This is my life. I am living it forward starting right now."** Names a passive, "gray" drift as the default failure mode `[50:10]`, and quotes Marcus Aurelius: **"Waste no more time arguing about what a good person should be. Be one"** `[51:12]`.

## Closing: why repetition, not one-time insight

`[54:15]`–`[58:17]` — The video closes by predicting its own forgetting: **"You are going to forget most of this by Thursday... the noise of the ordinary day is louder than any insight"** `[55:15]`. It states an explicit, unsourced dosing claim: **"One morning of this changes your mood. 30 mornings of this changes your default. 300 mornings of this changes who you are"** `[56:16]`. Marcus Aurelius's own journaling practice is offered as precedent — writing "the same principles to himself repeatedly across years" because "the gap between them closes only through daily practice" `[56:16]`. Seneca closes it: **"Every day acquire something that will fortify you against poverty, against death, against other misfortunes"** `[56:16]`.

## Signals and internal states addressed

- **Reflexive morning phone-checking and an anxious, comparison-driven scroll before getting out of bed** — named as the day's first, avoidable loss `[00:05]`–`[01:06]`.
- **A persistent, low-grade sense of being "behind" a life timeline relative to peers** — reframed as comparison against "a fantasy that never had to survive contact with reality" `[10:20]`.
- **Guilt or anxiety about disengaging from news, conflict, or other people's urgency** — reframed as protecting a finite, valuable resource rather than irresponsibility `[15:30]`–`[17:30]`.
- **Discouragement from not being able to see one's own growth or change** — addressed directly by contrasting invisible internal growth with visible external metrics `[25:42]`.
- **Chronic approval-seeking and calibrating one's own worth against other people's reactions** — traced to a childhood survival mechanism that "never got the update" for adult life `[30:49]`.
- **Treating the difficulty of a path as evidence the path is wrong** — named explicitly as one of the brain's "favorite lies" `[34:52]`.
- **Self-blame or rumination over outcomes that did not depend solely on one's own effort** — addressed by decoupling effort from outcome `[39:59]`–`[41:00]`.
- **Feeling depleted by day's end without having accomplished what actually mattered** — attributed to energy being claimed "by accident" rather than by decision `[44:03]`–`[46:04]`.
- **A diffuse sense that life is "happening to you" rather than being built by you** — named directly as a "posture" rather than a permanent condition `[50:10]`.

## Glossary terms introduced

- **The operating system (of the day)** — the video's framing for how the first minutes after waking load instructions that shape what the rest of the day notices and believes possible `[01:06]`.
- **The gap** — the small, trainable space between a triggering event and a person's reaction to it, distinct from an automatic, unexamined reflex `[05:14]`.

## Analogies worth reusing

- **The morning as the most valuable, most exploitable "real estate" in the day** — used to argue for protecting the first minutes specifically `[01:06]`.
- **Two lists — "in your power" and "not in your power"** — the dichotomy of control made into a literal budgeting exercise for finite daily energy `[21:37]`–`[22:37]`.
- **The attention economy as a business model that profits from unsettled, anxious, comparison-driven attention** — used to explain why peace has to be actively defended `[17:30]`.
- **A blank page and a pen** — the day framed as the material the future is actually built from, not a rehearsal for it `[52:14]`.

## Source-stated confidence

Built entirely on named quotations attributed to Marcus Aurelius, Seneca, and Epictetus, presented as established fact with no citation, translation source, or primary text given for any of them. The video also makes an unsourced claim about "modern psychology" supporting its ten affirmations `[03:09]` without naming any research, and a specific, unsourced dosing claim about how many mornings of repetition are required to "change who you are" `[56:16]`. The register throughout is motivational and prescriptive rather than evidentiary.
