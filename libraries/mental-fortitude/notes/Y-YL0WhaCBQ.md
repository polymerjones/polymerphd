# 10 Things You Must Do Alone At Least Once in Your Life | STOICISM

## Central claim

**Ten specific, deliberately chosen solitary practices — not solitude as a general idea — convert a person's relationship with being alone from a source of dependency into a source of sufficiency, so that relationships afterward are chosen rather than needed.** Stated directly: **"Solitude is not the absence of people. It is the presence of yourself, undiluted, unperformed, unwatched"** `[00:06]`. The source is explicit that this is not a case for isolation: **"This isn't about becoming a hermit or rejecting connection. Deep relationships still matter, arguably more once this work is done, because they stop being a substitute for a missing sense of self and start being an addition to one that already stands on its own"** `[02:09]`.

## The imagined audience and the cost of avoidance

`[03:09]`–`[05:10]` — On the restaurant practice specifically, the source names the actual obstacle: not the food, but **"the story running underneath it. The fear that being alone in public reads as being unwanted. That story is almost entirely invented, built from imagined judgments that rarely exist outside your own head"** `[04:09]`. The source claims this fear is self-perpetuating precisely because it is never tested: **"Most people rehearse this fear for years without ever testing it... the fear never gets to expire because it never gets challenged"** `[05:10]`.

## Escape versus construction

`[08:12]`–`[10:15]` — On relocating, the source draws a distinction it treats as load-bearing: **"This is not escape. Escape runs from something and it usually brings the same unresolved patterns along for the ride. This is construction instead, the deliberate act of rebuilding a life from the ground up"** `[09:13]`. The discomfort of the first weeks in a new place is explicitly reframed rather than treated as a sign of failure: **"That discomfort is not a sign something has gone wrong. It is the exact discomfort of construction, the space between the old identity being dismantled and the new one not yet finished"** `[10:15]`.

## Signals and internal states addressed

- **The fear that being seen alone in public reads as being unwanted** — named as an invented story rather than an accurate read of how much others notice `[04:09]`.
- **A specific loneliness in the first weeks of an unfamiliar place**, before new routines settle in — framed as evidence of construction rather than a mistake `[10:15]`.
- **Silence that initially feels like "a room with the lights suddenly turned up too bright"** rather than peace, before it turns into a felt sense of company by the second day `[11:17]`, `[12:19]`.
- **The body's "stop" signal mid-challenge**, described as usually "the mind negotiating for an easier version of the day" rather than a real danger signal `[17:25]`.
- **Fear of choosing wrong feeling heavier when made alone** than when a decision is shared or diffused across advisers `[22:34]`.

## Glossary terms introduced

- **Solitude vs. loneliness** — solitude as "the presence of yourself," treated by the source as the opposite of the absence-of-people definition of loneliness `[00:06]`.
- **Escape vs. construction** — running from an old life versus deliberately rebuilding one from zero `[09:13]`.

## Analogies worth reusing

- **A single chair at a restaurant table, a mountain trail with one set of footprints** — opening images of solitude the world reads as loss `[00:06]`.
- **"The room, in reality, is far less interested in you than the anxious mind assumes"** — the imagined audience versus the actual one `[04:09]`.
- **The gap between an old identity dismantled and a new one not yet finished** — the specific discomfort of relocating `[10:15]`.

## Source-stated confidence

This is unattributed self-help assertion delivered through a heavy layer of Stoic and Roman-figure quotations (Epictetus, Zeno of Citium, Cato the Younger, Musonius Rufus, Marcus Aurelius, Chrysippus, Seneca) used as rhetorical support rather than cited argument — the video names no book, letter, or specific text for any quotation, and no modern study or named researcher appears anywhere. Claims about "most people" (rehearsing fear for years, discovering the same thing by Sunday evening, and so on) are asserted as general patterns without any survey, statistic, or source. The practices themselves are presented as confident prescription, not hedged as opinion.

## Conflicts with other sources

`bIrLhwGnggA` ("Stand Alone. Think Alone. Conquer Alone.") argues the identical thesis — that voluntary solitude builds a strength company-dependent people never develop — but does so as pure abstract exhortation across six headings ("stand alone," "think alone," "train alone," "suffer alone," "believe alone," "conquer alone") with essentially no concrete practices, where this video is a checklist of ten specific one-time acts. `iNocwxM9LOA` ("Watch This If You're Ready to Change Your Life") makes the same loneliness/solitude distinction in its sixth of seven "changes": **"loneliness and solitude are not the same. Loneliness is feeling empty. Solitude is choosing space to become stronger"** `iNocwxM9LOA`[20:26] — a close paraphrase of this video's own opening line, **"Solitude is not the absence of people. It is the presence of yourself"** `[00:06]`. None of the three share an identical opening line or verbatim passage, so this is thematic convergence across the channel's back catalog on solitude rather than a templated duplicate — the same Stoic claim (self-sufficiency as the antidote to dependency on others' moods) recurring with different structure and different supporting quotations in each.
