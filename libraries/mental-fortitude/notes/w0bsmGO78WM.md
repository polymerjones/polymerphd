# Your identity, habits, and patterns were designed by childhood wounds | Nicole LePera

## Central claim

**Habits, reflexive reactions, and even traits believed to be core personality are, per LePera,
implicit emotional memories formed in childhood before language or logic existed — survival
adaptations that a person has fused with their identity, mistaking "who we've had to become" for
"who we are."** `[01:01]` "The inner child shows up not only in our reactions, but it shows up in
our adulthood as our habits and patterns and even our identities. We become so merged with the
habits and patterns that we begin to believe that they are just who we are, and we lose sight that
they are who we've had to become."

## Traits reframed as adaptations, not temperament

`[01:01]`–`[02:01]` Three specific examples: **"the overachieving child... might feel like we're
just driven, when in reality, continuing to seek success came from a childhood where love was
conditional"**; independence that "can feel like a choice when in reality it was a protective
stance when we couldn't depend on people around us"; and sensitivity that "feels like our
temperament, but really it was born out of an environment that was so unpredictable that we had to
develop a vigilance." Reflexive coping in the present — reaching for a phone, food, or substances
under stress — is named the same way: **"the nervous system or the body's best attempt at finding
our way to safety... in absence of that."**

## Reaction size as a diagnostic, and the biology behind it

`[03:01]`–`[04:03]` LePera offers a specific marker for distinguishing an inner-child reaction from
an adult one: **"the size of their reaction. When it's really big, overwhelming, all consuming,
all or nothing, that's usually a sign that it's from an older time."** The physiological account:
**"emotional flooding, where physiologically we are actually having that big of a reaction... our
amygdala... is over activated, while the prefrontal cortex, the very logical grounding part of our
brain, becomes under activated."** The stated implication is that shaming a disproportionate
reaction ("just calm down") misses that in the person's own body, "it does feel as big as it is
that we're experiencing it because it is big."

## How attachment style forms

`[06:07]` A single governing question is proposed for how a child's attachment style develops:
**"we observe and notice what happens when we reach for connection."** Consistently available
connection produces security; unpredictable or overwhelming connection produces clinging/anxious
attachment; unavailable connection produces avoidance — "I don't want it or need it."

## Two reconnection practices

`[11:12]`–`[13:16]` Two concrete exercises are offered: looking at an old childhood photograph,
which LePera says automatically increases compassion by making the child's vulnerability and small
size hard to dismiss as "too dramatic"; and a guided sensory-recall exercise — picturing a
childhood room, noticing posture and facial expression in the memory, and asking directly, **"what
did I need? Did I need attention? Did I need safety? Did I need protection?"**

## Signals and internal states addressed

- **A reaction that feels disproportionately large, all-or-nothing, or "not like you" relative to
  the actual trigger** — named directly as the marker of an inner-child reaction rather than an
  adult one. `[03:01]`
- **Reflexively reaching for a phone, food, or a substance when stressed, and mistaking it for a
  personality trait or a willpower failure** — reframed as the nervous system's learned survival
  strategy for finding safety or connection. `[02:01]`
- **Feeling convinced "this is just who I am" about a long-standing habit or pattern** — named as
  the sign of fusion between identity and an old adaptation, not evidence the pattern is
  unchangeable. `[08:09]`

## Glossary terms introduced

- **Implicit emotional memory** — body-based learning formed "before we had logic, before we had
  language," which runs "behind the scenes dictating how we feel" independent of conscious,
  logical knowledge. `[00:00]`
- **Emotional flooding** — a physiological state (elevated cortisol, amygdala over-activation,
  prefrontal cortex under-activation) that produces reactions matching the size of the emotion
  being felt, not the size of the actual present-moment trigger. `[04:03]`
