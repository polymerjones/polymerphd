# Restoration Series 1 - Primary

## Central claim

**This is a full guided restoration practice — moving through lying, ground-sitting, kneeling,
squatting, and standing positions — explicitly framed as reconnecting a practitioner to
"elementary positions and transitions that we've lost in our modern lives," prioritizing breath
and awareness-led positioning over forcing range of motion.** `[00:02]` "This natural movement
primary series is a dedicated sequence of movements that reconnects us to the elementary positions
and transitions that we've lost in our modern lives, from lying to floor sitting to standing and
even breathing... this practice is for rebuilding and maintaining fundamental levels of mobility
and body control for the beginner and advanced natural movement practitioner alike."

## Opening position and breath

`[00:02]`–`[02:03]` The practice opens lying on the back, working toward a named alignment position
(heard in the transcript as "the move notot position" — likely a caption mishearing of a specific
named term this source uses, reproduced here as heard since the correct term can't be confirmed):
midback and the back of the neck touching the ground, chin tucked, shoulders rolled so the thumbs
point toward the ceiling, held for 10 breaths and then 5 more with the arms swept overhead.
Breathing is directed specifically into "the three-dimensional space right where your ribs meet
your upper belly," with an instruction to keep breath "out of the collar bones and neck."

## Ground-sitting and the kneeling-to-squat progression

`[08:16]`–`[15:26]` A sequence of ground-sitting positions (cross-legged, side-bent, bent, long
sit) is built around one consistent target: "sit equally into both hip bones," with hips stacked
under the ribs rather than tucked. This transitions through a "tripod transition" (posting one arm,
rotating the elbow to point with the fingers, pivoting on the ball of the foot) into a kneeling
progression — deep kneeling, tall kneeling (checking that "the iliac crest is roughly in line with
your pubic bone"), tall half-kneeling with a stated counter-rotation cue (front knee rolling away
from the midline while the back knee spreads the opposite way), and finally a deep squat.

## The wall-alignment exercise

`[18:32]`–`[19:32]` Standing against a wall, hips first, then the space below the shoulder blades,
then the back of the head, chin tucked — held for 10 breaths, then extended overhead reach while
maintaining midback and head contact. The source is explicit about what this position represents:
**"this is what an ideal standing position would be if we weren't always forward flexed all the
time."**

## Symptoms and body signals addressed

None in the sense of injury or illness — the video addresses habitual postural patterns (forward
flexion, tension held in the neck/shoulders during breathing) as things the practice is designed to
counteract, rather than symptoms of a diagnosed condition.

## Analogies worth reusing

Growing "longer, taller, wider" through visualization rather than force, as the opening cue for
finding length in the spine while lying down. `[00:02]`
