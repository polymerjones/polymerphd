# The Next Evolution of MovNat: A Conversation with Erwan Le Corre & Brian Betancourt

## Central claim

**Erwan Le Corre frames MovNat as a systematic training method (not unstructured outdoor
activity) built on the premise that natural movement — not specialized sport — is the ancestral
foundation all human physical capability is derived from, and that movement requiring real
problem-solving produces different physiological/neurological effects than repetitive, "mindless"
exercise.** `[11:13]` "It is none of those disciplines, sports, protocols, programs [that] enable
natural movement. It's the other way around. Natural movement is what makes any sports, any
physical activity, any traditional physical discipline possible."

## Why MovNat exists: technique and conditioning applied to natural movement

`[01:01]`–`[03:05]` Le Corre's stated founding rationale: natural movement training didn't exist in
a structured form ("it was missing... very very few people" trained that way), so he built a
method modeled on how martial arts are actually learned — through progressions with trained
teachers, not improvisation — applied to "running, jumping, landing, hanging, climbing, lifting and
carrying, throwing, catching."

## Movement, lactate, and BDNF: a stated hypothesis, flagged as unsettled

`[08:11]`–`[10:12]` Le Corre traces a chain of reasoning from lactate research (Brooks's lactate
shuttle) — lactate as an actively used fuel crossing the blood-brain barrier, possibly a preferred
fuel for axons — toward a speculative conclusion he states outright is not yet established: "this
is not a substantiated theory... I imagine it'll come out and say, oh, lactate is the precursor to
your happiness." He separately cites brain-derived neurotrophic factor (BDNF) as a known
exercise-triggered effect, but stresses that "not any exercise is equal": only movement demanding
active engagement with "the here and now of the movement situation" is claimed to raise BDNF
production more than movement that is "just physical activity" — which he's careful to note is not
itself a bad thing.

## Organizational context (secondary to the physiology claims)

`[13:15]`–`[23:28]` The latter half of the conversation covers Brian Betancourt's hiring as
performance director and an announcement ("MovNat 2.0") of a planned business-support ecosystem for
certified MovNat trainers — content about the organization's plans rather than a claim about
natural movement or physiology, included here for completeness but not treated as a first-aid- or
practice-level claim in its own right.

## Glossary terms introduced

- **Lactate shuttle** — referenced (attributed to researcher Brooks) as the mechanism by which
  lactate, rather than being a mere waste product, is produced and used across muscle, skin, and
  organ tissue and can cross the blood-brain barrier. `[08:11]`
- **Brain-derived neurotrophic factor (BDNF)** — described as a substance that helps "rejuvenate
  the brain," with its production said to depend on the quality (not just quantity) of exercise.
  `[09:11]`

## Analogies worth reusing

- "Have you ever seen an out-of-shape fish or an out-of-shape eagle?" — the line credited with
  first hooking Brian Betancourt on natural movement, used to argue that fitness is native to
  wild animals without gyms or programs. `[06:08]`
- Natural movement training compared to how a martial art is actually learned — through structured
  progressions with trained teachers, not by "going in the street" and figuring it out. `[02:03]`

## Source-stated confidence

Explicitly mixed within the same conversation: Le Corre flags the lactate-to-happiness chain of
reasoning as his own unsubstantiated extrapolation, while treating BDNF's link to exercise as
established. The broader "natural movement underlies all sport" claim and the insecurity/movement
link are presented as personal conviction, not cited research.
