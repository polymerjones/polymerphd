# Behind The Scenes with Erwan Le Corre - "The Workout The World Forgot" - 15 Years of MovNat

## Central claim

**Erwan Le Corre describes making "The Workout The World Forgot" specifically to showcase natural,
evolutionary, ancestral movement — practiced outdoors and barefoot rather than in a gym — as
something he frames as every person's "birthright," suppressed by modern life rather than actually
lost.** `[06:11]` "It's about exercising, but exercising in nature and also based on completely
natural, evolutionary, ancestral movements... how about we move the way we're meant to move, we
move and exercise the way we're designed to exercise, for practical reasons, in environments that
are natural."

## How the video reached a mainstream audience

`[01:01]`–`[03:05]` Le Corre made an early, "very amateur" version of the video in Corsica in 2007,
before natural movement "was even really defined." He showed it to an online contact, "Barefoot"
Ted McDonald, who in turn showed it to freelance journalist Christopher McDougall (later the author
of *Born to Run*). McDougall pursued an 11-page, 16-photo feature article on Le Corre's approach in
Men's Health — described as unusual attention for someone with, in Le Corre's words, "no form of
recognition" or credentials at the time. McDougall is also credited with suggesting the video's
title, though the magazine ultimately used a different title for the article itself.

## "Entirely our birthright" — the video's stated purpose

`[07:12]`–`[09:17]` Le Corre states the video's goal was not to impress but to give people
"permission to go outside and to jump and land and crawl and roll and balance and vault," naming
social conventions ("never the right place, never the right time... you're going to get dirty") as
the barriers keeping people from movements he calls "entirely our birthright." He also describes a
recurring reaction from early workshop attendees: movements that look effortless on video turn out
to be much harder in practice, which he frames as revealing that looking easy is itself evidence of
trained technique, not an absence of real skill.

## An unreported detail: filming through an ankle injury

`[10:19]`–`[11:20]` Le Corre discloses that he sprained his ankle badly ten days before the video
shoot and had no option to postpone since the videographer had already traveled from Oregon —
performing all the planned movements, including a barefoot rock-face slide, on a still-swollen
ankle. He states this is visible only "if you were to watch the video in slow-mo."
