# MovNat Training Session: Breath Work Session

## Central claim

**Breath control — built on three pillars of diaphragmatic, nasal, and slow breathing — is trained
as a skill in its own right, practiced first through maximal inhale/exhale drills across multiple
body positions (seated, squat, prone, spinal flexion) and then deliberately maintained during free,
dynamic movement rather than being abandoned once movement becomes more intense.** `[11:51]`
"Instead of just sitting or squatting or assuming a position, I'm actually moving... I am first and
foremost paying attention to keeping my breathing nasal, diaphragmatic, and slow."

## Why the same sequence is repeated across different positions

`[04:06]`–`[07:25]` The same three-part breathing sequence (deep exhale, diaphragm-only maximum
inhale, diaphragm-plus-upper-respiratory maximum inhale) is performed seated, in a deep squat, and
prone with arms locked out, with the stated reason being that each position is "going to challenge
my diaphragm and my respiratory muscles differently because of the differences in structural
alignment or organization." A further variation — full spinal flexion, knees and feet touching the
ground behind the head — is used specifically because that position challenges "not just the
diaphragm but also all the upper respiratory muscles."

## Applying breath control to movement

`[11:51]`–`[12:51]` The final exercise removes the fixed position entirely: **"you are free to
explore any movement you want which you would normally do maybe without really paying attention to
your breathing... as long as you are not really doing anything intense, there is no need for you to
open your mouth or to breathe faster or to use your upper respiratory muscles."** The stated goal
is building awareness and control over breath during natural movement, not just during a static
drill.
