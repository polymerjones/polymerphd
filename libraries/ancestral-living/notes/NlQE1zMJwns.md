# WHAT TRADITIONAL SPORTS COULDN'T TEACH ME

## Central claim

**Gaspard Nouaille describes discovering the "natural method" of physical education — after a
childhood and youth built entirely around conventional sport (tennis, running, football, rugby,
etc.) — as "a revelation," and frames its value over conventional sport/fitness training as
reconnecting movement to a real purpose, taught through storytelling and group engagement rather
than isolated repetitions.** `[02:01]` "It's been quite a revelation for me because I didn't know
that natural movement at all at that time. I only did sports, and well, it's quite different,
actually."

## From conventional sport to the natural method to MovNat

`[01:00]`–`[02:01]` Gaspard's background is "a background of conventional sport" (tennis, climbing,
running, athleticism, handball, football, rugby, volleyball). In 2020, running physical education
at a Montessori school in France, he found conventional sport "not enough" and sought out a family
teaching Georges Hébert's natural method — a different lineage from Hébert's own descendants,
per the transcript. `[04:03]` He later discovered MovNat in 2023 at a Biarritz training and
completed a Level 3 training in Vienna in 2024, describing MovNat as adding "a lot of really
interesting tools and skills" to adapt the roughly hundred-year-old natural method "to the actual
needs" of modern people.

## Why natural movement feels "odd," and conventional fitness's missing "why"

`[08:06]`–`[09:08]` Asked about cultural resistance to natural movement, Gaspard says it can seem
"a bit odd" because "it's not in our culture." The interviewer extends this with his own
experience training without machines: "people always looking at me like, 'What the hell is this
guy doing?'" He traces the resistance partly to a purpose-disconnection built into conventional
fitness: **"When you look at like traditional functional fitness, it's like I'm deadlifting... Why?
I'm deadlifting to get stronger. It's never I'm deadlifting to be able to take something from point
A to point B."**

## Storytelling, group training, and get-ups as an entry point

`[10:08]` For people unused to being outdoors, Gaspard starts indoors, working on the ground
"because they are not used to get up and sit down anymore" — calling MovNat's ground get-up
exercises "a really good addition" to the natural method. `[12:10]`–`[13:11]` For groups, the
natural method deliberately uses storytelling ("we have to face a danger, we have to pass through
an obstacle, we have to climb into a tree") to create a shared mood that gets participants
attempting movements without consciously deciding to: "they were not thinking they could do it, but
they just do it... they are just like, 'Whoa, what just happened?'"

## Favorite technique families: throwing and balancing

`[14:12]`–`[15:12]` Asked for a favorite thing to teach, Gaspard names two "underestimated"
families: throwing and balancing. The interviewer extends the balance point with his own framing:
apparent simplicity ("stand on one leg... that's easy") gives way to real difficulty, and losing
balance is tied to losing focus on unrelated thoughts — "the next thing you know they're thinking
about the meeting they have to have at 3:00 and then they start to fall."

## Symptoms and body signals addressed

None in the sense of physical symptoms — the conversation addresses training philosophy,
motivation, and pedagogy rather than a specific injury or health condition.

## Glossary terms introduced

- **Natural method** — the roughly hundred-year-old French physical education tradition based on
  Georges Hébert's work, distinct from and older than MovNat. `[02:01]`
- **Emulation** — the term used in the transcript (offered by the interviewer, agreed to by
  Gaspard) for the peer-engagement effect of training natural movement in a large group. `[12:10]`

## Analogies worth reusing

- Deadlifting "to get stronger" versus deadlifting "to be able to take something from point A to
  point B" — used to illustrate conventional fitness's disassociation from movement's real
  purpose. `[09:08]`
- Standing on one leg looking "easy" until attempted, used as a way to show how balance work
  exposes mental distraction rather than only physical weakness. `[15:12]`
