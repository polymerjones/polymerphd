# Ed St. Martin on Teaching Natural Movement at MoveThru

## Central claim

**Ed St. Martin frames the core value of teaching natural movement to older adults as restoring
functional confidence — the ability to get up off the ground, bend over, or move without fear —
more than building fitness for its own sake, illustrated through repeated case studies of students
regaining specific abilities they'd become afraid they'd lost.** `[06:07]` "The moment that you
take somebody through this natural movement journey and they discover how capable they are... it's
transformative across the board. They [have] more confidence, they feel stronger, they feel
safer."

## Case study: overcoming fear of the floor

`[08:09]`–`[09:09]` A student in her mid-60s, injured years earlier, told Ed her biggest fear was
being on the floor and unable to get back up, driven by wanting to play safely with her
grandchildren. Ed's approach was graded exposure — starting on an elevated bench before progressing
to the floor — resulting in a specific described moment: **"when she got up and she saw that she
was still capable of getting up... that fear just melted away."**

## Case study: an 82-year-old doing planks, a 78-year-old returning to kayaking after two hip replacements

`[04:03]`–`[05:06]` An 82-year-old student worked on her core after developing back pain while
cooking, progressing to a plank on a balance board — Ed reports she bought her own balance board
afterward to continue at home. `[09:09]`–`[10:11]` Separately, a 78-year-old "extreme kayaker"
used pre-habilitation training ahead of a hip replacement and returned to kayaking within months of
her second surgery, described by Ed as "one of the most rewarding things in this profession" —
helping people get back to activities they'd defined for themselves, not just generic fitness
goals.

## Symptoms and body signals addressed

Age-related loss of the ability to rise from the floor unaided, general muscle-strength decline
with age, and task-specific pain (e.g. back soreness while cooking) addressed through targeted
strength work rather than general exercise.

## Glossary terms introduced

None beyond "contralateral," used in its ordinary anatomical sense (opposite-side coordination
during crawling) rather than as a newly defined term.

## Analogies worth reusing

Skeletal "deformity" reframed as a teaching asset rather than a pure limitation — Ed's own
irregular body used as the basis for understanding what does and doesn't work for other bodies.
