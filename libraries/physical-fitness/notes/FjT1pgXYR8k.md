# My Darkest Steroid Experiences

## Central claim

**Dr. Mike Israetel states plainly that steroids work as advertised for muscle and strength, but
argues — through three personal stories — that their side-effect profile is unpredictable,
individual-dependent, and can be severe even for someone experienced, ranging from physical
(chronic injection-site swelling) to acute psychological (sudden certainty of imminent death) to
relational (androgen-driven hostility toward people he cares about).** `[01:00]` "steroids get you
much more jacked, that's for sure... [but] that story of drugs making you feel good... it's true
that they have awesome effects on your physique, [but] there is sometimes a plethora of dark sides
that are discussed — we won't skip that part here." `[24:28]` "responses are individual — some
individuals get some side effects, some get others, some don't... you never can tell until you use
it."

## Story one: chronic sterile injection-site swelling (03:02–08:08)

Over roughly a decade of use, Israetel describes recurring severe swelling at injection sites that
repeatedly presented like infection but was not: `[05:04]` "it turns out I never had an infection...
I couldn't bend my leg but a few degrees without excruciating pain for a week and a half." One
episode required a hospital procedure: `[08:08]` "I've been to the hospital before for some quad
shots where they [flayed] open my quad and they drained... a crapload of fluid... it turns out...
actually a sterile abscess." He names the specific change that reduced the problem: `[07:06]`
"the only thing that eventually made a change for me... was I ended up switching to insulin
syringes, which are teeny tiny and disrupt the area much, much less," alongside a switch to a
higher-quality compound source. He also names a related systemic reaction: `[08:08]` "you guys
[should] look up... something called trend flu, test flu — it's when you have a fever, malaise,
headache... and your whole body's weak... and whatever muscle group you injected is nonfunctional."

## Story two: an acute death-anxiety reaction to a trenbolone introduction dose (09:10–14:13)

Starting trenbolone at what he was told was a conservative 75 mg dose, Israetel describes an
intense, content-free panic episode the following day: `[10:10]` "I felt sure in every emotional
way but in zero logical ways that my death was inevitable and going to happen real soon... my
heart rate was totally fine, I was in perfectly good health outside of the trend, but it doesn't
kill you instantly, not even close, and I just knew that I was going to die." He states the
resolution was dose reduction, on a friend's advice, to roughly a quarter of the "conservative"
starting dose: `[11:11]`–`[12:13]` "why don't you go to a quarter of the dose... 18.75 [milligrams]
... felt way better, body started to change, trend is real." He generalizes this into an explicit
recommendation: `[12:13]` "whatever amount of steroids people tell you to do, it's always a good
idea to do like way less than that... if you go high... it's just a really nasty experience." He
separately notes this same low-dose approach later converged with a trend in coaching practice:
`[13:13]` "a lot of the modern prep coaching... [uses] much less trend just in the last month
into a show... to have that extra edge... without" the heavier side-effect load.

## Story three: androgen-driven hostility toward his wife during a 2024 competition prep (14:13–22:26)

Describing a high-dose cycle during 2024 bodybuilding competition preparation, Israetel reports
uncharacteristic, involuntary hostile thoughts during arguments with his wife: `[18:18]` "you know
enough testosterone and trend, all that stuff just pushes on your amygdala to squirt out really
nasty kind of neural priming... it happens in your thoughts, it happens in the things you say."
He states he suppressed rather than voiced these thoughts: `[19:21]`–`[20:22]` "some of the other
thoughts that came to mind were just like... why do I have to put up with this, I could just
leave, we could just get a divorce... every thought [was] some version of nasty." He explicitly
separates the feeling from endorsed belief: `[21:24]` "not everything you feel in the moment is
truly revealed wisdom about how you intend to be a person out in the world... when people say
things like 'always trust your instincts,' that's probably some of the worst advice you can get."

## An explicit high-variance framework for interpreting all three stories (22:26–31:34)

Israetel is explicit that his own experiences should not be read as representative: `[22:26]`
"am I unique[?] does this kind of stuff only happen to me? It doesn't only happen to me, but I am
very unique... I have a real nasty streak." He states response variance spans a full spectrum
independent of bloodwork: `[24:28]` "some people get test flu, their bones will hurt, but their
injection sites are hunky dory... for me my blood work on steroids was always phenomenal, but my
psychological sides were like a gigantic walking nightmare." He also flags a specific bias: users
may not recognize their own altered state in the moment, drawing a direct comparison: `[29:32]`
"just the same way [as] a lot of drunk people... only later, sometimes when they come off steroids
or go to lower doses, do they look back and... realize... in retrospect that is crazy."

## A stated future alternative: non-androgenic muscle-growth drugs (31:34–34:38)

Israetel closes by naming a specific drug class in development — a myostatin inhibitor plus
activin inhibitor combination, attributed to Regeneron Pharmaceuticals — as a lower-side-effect
alternative he expects within a few years: `[31:34]`–`[32:36]` "a myostatin inhibitor and an
activin inhibitor... those two compounds [are] radical muscle growth-blocking pathways... clamp
down crazy muscle growth all the time... these inhibitor drugs... give you radical muscle growth...
comparable to steroids or, in most cases... leave steroids way behind." He states this is his
basis for a generational recommendation: `[33:37]` "if you're a younger person, just wait... in a
few years or less we'll have these... non-side-effect-having, pure muscle growth drugs."

## Form cues and body signals addressed

- **Injection-site swelling with redness and pain that is not infection is named as a recurring,
  identifiable pattern (a sterile inflammatory/immune response), distinct from a true bacterial
  infection** requiring antibiotics: `[05:04]`–`[08:08]`.
- **"Trend flu" / "test flu" is named as a specific systemic symptom cluster** (fever, malaise,
  headache, whole-body weakness, temporary loss of function in the injected muscle group) distinct
  from local injection-site reactions: `[08:08]`.
- **A sudden, context-free sense of impending death with normal vital signs is named as a
  trenbolone-associated acute anxiety reaction**, not a cardiac event — Israetel explicitly notes
  his heart rate was normal throughout: `[10:10]`.

## Glossary terms introduced

- **"Trend flu" / "test flu"** — a flu-like systemic reaction to steroid injection Israetel
  describes but does not claim to have coined `[08:08]`.
- **Myostatin inhibitor / activin inhibitor** — a drug class in clinical trials described as
  blocking natural muscle-growth-limiting pathways, distinct from anabolic steroids `[32:36]`.

## Analogies worth reusing

- **The drunk-person overconfidence analogy** — used to explain why some steroid users cannot
  perceive their own behavioral changes while using: `[29:32]`.
- **"Editor, not author, of your thoughts"** — Israetel's framing for how androgen-driven hostile
  thoughts arise involuntarily but can still be filtered before being spoken: `[18:18]`.

## Source-stated confidence

- **Israetel explicitly and repeatedly frames his experiences as individual rather than
  representative**: `[22:26]`, `[24:28]`, `[30:34]` "I'm not saying any of these things that I
  described are going to happen to you."
- **He explicitly separates a genuine physiological/psychological drug effect from a value
  judgment about the underlying feeling's validity**: `[21:24]` "not everything you feel in the
  moment is truly revealed wisdom."
- **The future-drug section is explicitly framed as anticipated rather than available**: `[33:37]`
  "FDA [approval] at the latest, for the love of God, 2028, probably sooner... they're almost
  here," acknowledging the drugs are still in trials at time of recording.

## Conflicts with other sources

Converges with `j9avZn6B0OE` and `RhXAcvLdBbk` on trenbolone's psychological risk (anxiety,
hostility/aggression, relationship strain) from an independent first-person account rather than a

about maximizing early doses) with a parallel real-time example — Israetel's friend's advice to

myostatin/activin-inhibitor drugs as an alternative to anabolic steroids.
