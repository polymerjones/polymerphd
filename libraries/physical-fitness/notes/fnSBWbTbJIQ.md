# Arm Day w/ Larry Wheels | Powerlifter Meets Bodybuilder

## Central claim

**Seth explicitly frames arm day around deliberately partial repetitions and pump over full range
of motion or heavy load, and both he and Larry independently describe visualizing a competition
pose while performing an otherwise ordinary cable exercise as their cueing method.** `[24:32]` "I'm
all for quarter reps, half reps, three-quarter reps, because I want to continue to push as much
blood [and] stretch the muscle fascia as much as possible." `[26:40]` "whenever I'm doing anything...
I equate back [to a] pose — whenever I'm doing some type of cable pulling... I angle myself, think
like a rear-double-bicep shot, and pull down on my bicep."

## Partial reps as a deliberate choice, not a fault (24:32–25:38)

Seth states this directly as a philosophy applied broadly across bodybuilding, not just this
session: `[24:32]`–`[25:38]` "the whole concept with bodybuilding... I'm all for quarter reps, half
reps, three-quarter reps... only do some [reps] to right here, just [to] pump." He frames this as
inseparable from bodybuilding's lack of a fixed rulebook: `[23:31]` "bodybuilding is the one sport
where there are no rules... give me everything you got."

## Posing as a training cue (26:40–27:42)

Both lifters describe the same technique independently — using a specific competition pose as the
mental target for an exercise's peak contraction, rather than a joint angle or rep count: `[26:40]`
"I like this machine a lot because whenever I do anything, my exercise, I equate back [to a]
pose... like a rear-double-bicep shot." He's explicit this isn't derived from outside study:
`[26:40]`–`[27:42]` "every single scientific anything... needed a bro to study to get there, somebody
had to come up with something."

## Top set / back-off set with a slowed eccentric (35:59–37:00)

Larry describes his current bodybuilding coach's programming philosophy, distinct from his own
powerlifting background: `[35:59]` "top set, drop set, top set, drop set... the most [change] was
really made [by] slowing down the reps... on the eccentric." This mirrors, in different words, the
top-set/potentiation structure documented elsewhere in this library.

## Overhead tricep extension: cable over heavy dumbbell (37:00–38:02)

Larry states a direct preference shift away from heavy dumbbell overhead extensions specifically
because of injury risk at very heavy loads: `[37:00]`–`[38:02]` "for years I've tried [it] the heavy
way... wasting heavy up as quickly as possible... a 180, 200 lb dumbbell, and getting it up there —
such a hassle, risk [of] injury... but this, I get a good pump on my [tricep] with[out that risk]."
He also states a general principle for non-compound isolation work: `[38:02]` "when I do anything
that's not a compound... I don't really care [how] much weight... I'm [just] trying to get [more]
mass[,] thickness, [not weight on the bar]."

## Posing to manage physique asymmetry (39:04–40:06)

Larry names a specific, learned posing skill distinct from training technique — hiding a
size/strength asymmetry between limbs through stance and positioning: `[39:04]`–`[40:06]` "my years
of powerlifting left [me] with a couple of permanent injuries, [a] torn bicep, I have a torn
quad... if one quad[']s doesn't hit the same way as the other... why don't I just position it this
way [so] the side [that] looks really good [shows]... I learned a lot of that stuff from Jay [Cutler]
and from Dorian [Yates]... the judges only see as much as you show them."

## Form cues and body signals addressed

- **Partial-rep "pushing blood" is stated as a deliberate technique, not sloppy form**, tied to a
  specific goal (stretching the muscle fascia, maximizing pump): `[24:32]`.
- **Heavy dumbbell overhead tricep extensions are named as carrying elevated injury risk at very
  heavy loads**, with a cable alternative offered as a lower-risk substitute for the same target:
  `[37:00]`–`[38:02]`.
- **A permanent injury (torn bicep, torn quad) is disclosed directly as a limiting factor**, framed
  as something to manage through posing rather than deny: `[39:04]`.

## Glossary terms introduced

- **The Cutler stomp** — a stance/positioning technique (named after Jay Cutler) used to angle the
  body so a smaller or asymmetrical muscle (e.g., a quad) is turned away from the judges/camera
  `[40:06]`.

## Analogies worth reusing

- **"Bodybuilding's dessert of the week"** `[15:21]` — Seth's framing for arm day as the fun,
  low-cardiovascular-stress session compared to back or leg day.

## Source-stated confidence

Both lifters are explicit that technique choices here are personal and experience-based rather
than derived from formal study:

- **The posing-as-cue technique is explicitly attributed to "bro science" rather than research**:
  `[26:40]`–`[27:42]` "every single scientific anything... needed a bro to study to get there."
- **The cable-over-dumbbell tricep preference is stated as Larry's own experience-driven change**,
  not a general injury-prevention rule for all lifters: `[37:00]` "for years I've tried it the heavy
  way... but this..."
