# Bulk Back

## Central claim

**Sagi Kalev frames back size as producing the V-taper indirectly — visibly wide lats making the
waist look smaller by contrast — the same mechanism named for shoulders in `build-shoulders`[00:00],
here stated for the back instead.** `[00:00]` "We're gonna get your lats so huge that you're gonna
see them coming from far away. We're gonna get the V-tip from the top to the bottom. You're gonna
get small wa[ist] just by having big back."

## Warm-up (01:19–03:20)

Shoulder rolls both directions, walk-out-to-plank holds, then light-weight rehearsals of the
session's main lifts: deadlift (`[02:20]`, "chest up, shoulders back. Glutes out. Focus on
breathing. Squeeze your back"), bent-over row (`[02:20]`, "nothing else moving besides the elbows
going up"), and reverse fly (`[02:20]`–`[03:20]`, "don't bend your wrists... working the traps,
rear delts, everything in the back").

## Set 1 — Pull-over / pull-up superset (03:20–10:24)

Dumbbell pull-over on a bench, superset directly with pull-ups, escalating weight across three
rounds (30s → 40s → a drop set from 50 to 30). A specific injury-prevention correction is given
live, not just as a general caution: `[04:20]` "You see he went a little bit too low. That puts a
lot of tension on the neck. We're trying to prevent that. I'm going to stop him right here." Pull-up
form is checked for compensation: `[07:22]` "he's making sure he's using his back and not his
arms... he's not swinging back and forth."

## Set 2 — Wide-grip EZ-bar row, progressive set (10:24–16:39)

The session's standing rule is stated in full before the set begins: `[11:25]` "rule number one,
stay safe. All right? Rule number two, keep your form. Rule number three, you got to have fun all
day long." Three weights run back-to-back with no rest, reps decreasing as weight rises (15 → 12 →
8): `[14:38]` "we start with the heavyweight. We're going to the medium weight. We're going to the
light. Eight, 12, 15" — the same progressive-set structure defined in `bulk-arms`[04:10], here run
in the reverse (heavy-to-light) direction from the start. Form cue on isolation: `[11:25]` "nothing's
moving. I'm just going to push my elbows to my left [back]."

## Set 3 — One-arm row, force set (16:39–19:48)

Named directly in-source as "beast force," matching `bulk-arms`[12:10]'s force-set definition: five
sets of five reps at a fixed weight, held rather than dropped between sets. `[16:39]` "we got five
sets, five reps, back to back... we're not increasing the weight. We're not dropping the weights.
We're holding it. We're hugging it." Run on both arms in sequence with no rest at the side switch.

## Set 4 — Deadlift, single set with drop set (19:48–24:51)

Three escalating rounds (15 → 12 → an 80-to-50 drop set), each rep cued on the squeeze at the top
rather than just the lift: `[20:48]` "down, squeeze. Down, squeeze." Named target muscles: `[20:48]`
"squeeze your shoulders. Right there, the rhombo[ids], traps." Chin position is checked
specifically: `[22:49]`, `[23:49]` "keep your chin down... keep looking straight. Drop your chin
down."

## Set 5 — Reverse fly / plank one-arm row-and-twist superset (24:51–28:53)

Seated reverse fly superset with a plank position bringing one arm to the chest in a twisting row
(a renegade-row-style move), each move run for either 15 reps or 30 seconds depending on the round.
Fly form and a load-correction cue: `[25:51]` "if you are moving your back, you're using too much
weight. You can drop the weights." Plank-row form: `[26:52]` "keep your glutes down. Keep your back
straight. The only thing that's moving is your arm."

## Stretch and cooldown (28:53–29:54)

Standing wrist-grab side-bend, stretching the lat on each side (`[28:53]`, "grab it on your wrist.
Hold it up... lean to the side"), followed by a bench-assisted triceps/lat stretch with an explicit
equipment substitute named: `[28:53]` "if you don't have a bench, watch Pretty Boy." Closes with a
standing shoulder stretch, fingers crossed and extended, chin dropped on a paced breath `[29:54]`.

## Content note: appended promotional material (30:54–end)

The workout itself ends at `[29:54]` ("that was bulk back... see you next time"). Everything from
`[30:54]` to the end of the file is unrelated Beachbody promotional content — P90X and RevAbs
advertisements — not Body Beast programming, not delivered by Sagi Kalev, and not reflected in this

## Form cues and body signals addressed

- **A specific over-range risk is named and actively corrected on camera, not just cautioned
  against generically**: `[04:20]` "that puts a lot of tension on the neck. We're trying to
  prevent that. I'm going to stop him right here."
- **A compensating body part is named and excluded on the pull-up**: `[07:22]` "he's making sure
  he's using his back and not his arms... not swinging back and forth."
- **Excess load is diagnosed by a specific visible tell, not just difficulty**: `[25:51]` "if you
  are moving your back, you're using too much weight. You can drop the weights."
- **Chin position gets a repeated, specific cue on the deadlift**: `[22:49]`, `[23:49]` "keep your
  chin down... drop your chin down."
- **Isolation on the plank row is checked by naming the one part that should move**: `[26:52]`
  "the only thing that's moving is your arm."

## Glossary terms introduced

- **"Beast force" (force set)** — Kalev's own label for the force-set structure (five sets of five,
  held rather than dropped) already defined in `bulk-arms`[12:10], applied here to the one-arm row
  `[19:48]`.
- **Progressive set, reverse direction** — the same structure defined in `bulk-arms`[04:10], here
  explicitly run heavy-to-light rather than light-to-heavy: `[14:38]` "we start with the
  heavyweight. We're going to the medium weight. We're going to the light."

## Analogies worth reusing

- **"Are you going to wait or are you going to run and grab it? ... Get on the bus. Drive it
  hard."** `[15:39]` — an urgency analogy used to push through a set's final reps.
- **"Say thank you for giving me this huge back."** `[27:52]` — reframing a stretch hold as
  gratitude, the same device used in `build-back-and-bis`[49:54] and `beast-cardio`[30:35] for
  different muscle groups.

## Source-stated confidence

An instructional/motivational training-partner video; its evidence is Kalev's technique narration
and two training partners' (Chris and Brian's) visible performance, not citation or measurement:

- **A specific safety correction is demonstrated live on a training partner, not just described
  abstractly**: `[04:20]` the pull-over depth correction is shown as an in-the-moment coaching
  intervention, not a disclaimer read before the set.
- **A load-adjustment safety allowance recurs across sets**: `[15:39]`, `[25:51]` "if you have to
  drop the weight, drop. There's [no] problem. As long as you keep going"; "you can drop the
  weights."
- **Individual load variation is shown directly**: `[21:49]` Kalev and Chris state different
  personal weights on the same deadlift (65 vs. 40) rather than one prescribed number.

## Conflicts with other sources

The safety/form/fun rule at `[11:25]` matches `build-legs`[23:12], `build-chest-and-tris`[28:19],
and `build-shoulders`[09:12] closely enough to confirm it as a fixed program-wide rule. This
session's explicit, filmed correction of an unsafe pull-over range (`[04:20]`) is the most concrete
injury-prevention moment notated so far across Body Beast sessions — worth comparing against
`build-back-and-bis`'s own pull-over description, which notes the same isolation goal
(`build-back-and-bis`[09:18], "trying to isolate the triceps and just the lat") but does not show a
depth correction, suggesting the neck-tension risk may not be universally called out on this
exercise. The "force set" terminology and structure match `bulk-arms`[12:10] exactly, confirming it
as a fixed program-wide definition rather than a one-off. Against RUSHFIT's
`balance-and-agility`[37:01], whose safety line sits at pain rather than form, this session's named
"too low = neck tension" correction is a rare case of a Body Beast session identifying a specific
injury mechanism rather than a general form/pain distinction.
