# Seth Feroce How-To: Dumbbell Pullovers

## Central claim

**Seth positions the dumbbell pullover as a deliberate rib-cage stretch rather than a strength
movement, done across the bench (not along it) specifically to add a hip-drop that a flat-lying
version can't produce.** `[00:06]` "This gives me a chance to open up my rib cage, expand my rib
cage, stretch out my chest, and really get a full range of motion to make me feel like I'm ripping
my rib cage apart — like I'm getting big as [hell]. That's the whole point of this."

## Setup and execution (01:09–03:11)

Shoulder-blade placement on the bench edge is the stated starting reference point: `[01:09]` "I
put my shoulder blades right on the edge of the bench... get a nice stretch, get ready for it." A
named safety caution follows immediately on grip: `[02:09]` "grab the weight, both hands, put it
over top of my head — do not drop this on your forehead, ladies and gentlemen, that would be a
bad thing." Body alignment is specified precisely: `[02:09]` "my hips are straight with my body,
everything is parallel to the ground... my knees, legs are perpendicular with the ground." The
hip-drop is the technique's signature detail: `[02:09]` "whenever I do this I dip my hips down to
stretch even more, to rip my rib cage apart even more... whenever I come up, I also raise my hips
up and thrust forward." Range of motion at the top is capped deliberately: `[03:11]` "you don't
want to bring it too far down, you end up losing control. I bring this... past my face and above
my chest."

## Why this variation, and its stated purpose (00:06–05:15)

Seth places it as an early-to-mid workout exercise and names the physique effect he's after:
`[04:14]` "I usually do this... second, third, fourth exercise of my workout out of five or six
exercises... it gives me the opportunity to stretch, open up, and feel good... this gives you the
illusion to make sure that you['re a] bigger chest, fuller." He credits a named bodybuilder for
the same technique/effect and is explicit that the exercise is chasing an illusion of proportion,
not a raw size claim: `[01:09]` "Jake Cutler, he was known for doing dumbbell pullovers, it
helped him create the illusion of such a huge, wide chest... and then he ended up having a tiny
waist. Us short, wide-waisted white guys, we need to create that illusion." He explicitly
contrasts his across-the-bench version with the more common flat-lying version and states why he
prefers his own: `[05:15]` "you see people end up laying flat... doing them, that's great, that's
fine, you still [hit] the same concept — however, this inhibits me from dipping my hips down, so I
don't get the same range of motion that I would... want."

## Form cues and body signals addressed

- **Weight control is named as the line between the lifter controlling the exercise and the
  exercise controlling the lifter**: `[01:09]` "you control the weight, the weight controls you,
  you end up hurting yourself."
- **A specific injury risk is called out directly**: `[02:09]` dropping the dumbbell on the
  forehead, flagged as the reason for a deliberate two-handed grip going overhead.
- **Bringing the dumbbell too low is named as a loss-of-control point**, not just a range-of-motion
  choice: `[03:11]` "you don't want to bring it too far down, you end up losing control."
- **Breathing is named as a controllable variable tied to outcome**: `[04:14]` "control your
  breathing, the more you control the breathing, the better off you'll be."

## Glossary terms introduced

- **Vacuum pose** — a posed abdominal-vacuum technique Seth references (without demonstrating
  here) as related to the same rib-cage-expansion goal as the pullover `[01:09]`.

## Analogies worth reusing

- **"Ripping my rib cage apart"** `[00:06]`, `[02:09]` — his repeated description of the target
  sensation/stretch, used as the felt-experience cue for correct depth.
- **"Ten different ways to skin a cat"** `[05:15]` — acknowledging his across-the-bench technique
  as one valid variation among several, not the only correct method.

## Source-stated confidence

This is Seth's own coaching preference and personal technique choice, explicitly marked as such
rather than a claimed universal best practice:

- **His own variation is stated as a preference, with the common alternative explicitly validated
  as legitimate**: `[05:15]` the flat-lying version "hits the same concept," his own across-the-
  bench version is presented as his personal choice for a deeper hip-driven stretch, not as
  correcting an error in the flat version.
- **The physique-illusion claim (wide chest, small waist) is attributed to a named bodybuilder's
  reported result, not asserted as a guaranteed outcome**: `[01:09]` "it helped him create the
  illusion" — offered as his stated example, not a universal promise.

## Conflicts with other sources

exercise chosen for stretch/rib-cage expansion rather than load or hypertrophy volume is a
