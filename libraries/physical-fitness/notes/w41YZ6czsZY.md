# THE GREATEST CHEST TRAINING VIDEO EVER! | Hany Rambod & Seth Feroce

## Central claim

**Hany Rambod draws a precise, named distinction between training to fatigue and training to
true failure, and states directly that only failure — followed by added "intensity multipliers"
like partials, forced reps, and negatives — produces the size and fullness increase a lifter is
actually after; fatigue alone, he says, only maintains what's already there.** `[26:47]` "going
through fatigue is basically meaning that you're just getting tired and you continue to keep going
through the reps... you're just basically trying to tap that pencil on top of that dynamite...
versus going in with a hammer and just hitting it and getting that to explode." `[27:49]`–`[28:50]`
"if you're just trying to get to the point where you're just tired, you'll burn calories, you'll
look good... but you're not going to get muscle growth. If you're looking to get 3D, that's not
going to happen."

## Failure, then intensity multipliers (27:49)

The sequence is specific: `[27:49]` "when you go to failure, whether it's a six-to-eight failure or
ten-to-twelve failure, that means you are done. And then when you're done with that full range of
motion, then... we'll do partials, we'll do forced reps, we'll do negatives... those are what I like
to call intensity multipliers."

## The power press: an upper-chest variant that limits shoulder takeover (11:20–12:21)

A specific hand/back position is described with a stated mechanical purpose: `[11:20]`–`[12:21]`
"what we're doing... is a power press, which is the palms facing each other, and you're arching
your back to try to get your shoulders back, so your shoulders aren't going to be able to kick in
during your press... you're arching here, and then... you're creating that C in the back, so that
the shoulders are also back and you're keeping that chest forward." He ties this directly to a
physique goal for wide-shouldered lifters: `[11:20]` "the wider you are, the more you want that
upper-chest thickness, so that you can look thick and have depth... if you don't, [you] actually end
up looking really shallow."

## Static holds inserted mid-set (17:28–18:29)

A named technique for adding time-under-tension without changing the visible rep count: `[17:28]`
"what you're doing is basically... trying to create that static hold[,] basically making sure that
you're getting more time under tension." He explains why it produces shaking: `[18:29]` "the
stabilizing muscles are kicking in and they're starting to fatigue... as you're pressing with the
eccentric/concentric, on the opposite side you're still having to use the muscle." Frequency is
specific, not constant: `[18:29]` "usually at least once a workout... on certain exercises."

## "Ladders": changing angle within one continuous set (37:04–39:07)

Distinct from simply varying exercises between sets: `[37:04]` "we're going to pick three different
points — we're going to go low, medium, and high, to change up the angle so that when we're doing
the actual fly exercise, we're working on different angles throughout the actual set... you're
still doing a [press or fly], however you're going to hit your body parts at different angles, and
all of a sudden it's just going to keep getting more and more full."

## Chin position and breathing: stated mechanisms, not style cues (28:50–34:00)

Chin position is tied to range of motion, not posture alone: `[28:50]` "the reason why you need to
keep that chin up is so that you can be able to get the maximum flexion as you bring your hands
together with that upper chest — lots of times when your chin is down, you're limiting your range
of motion as well." Breathing is stated as a direct oxygen-supply issue, not just a relaxation cue:
`[34:00]` "don't hold your breath, we need oxygen to be able to get a pump and for you to have
endurance... when you're holding your breath, you are going to starve that muscle from... oxygen."

## Hydration as a hard precondition for pump (35:02–36:04)

Hany states this without qualification: `[35:02]`–`[36:04]` "making sure that your water levels and
hydration level is really high is really really important... it doesn't matter how good [your
pre-workout] is if you're dehydrated, because you're not going to be able to really get a pump...
I don't give a [damn] what you take for your pre-workout, you're not going to be pumped."

## Form cues and body signals addressed

- **Fatigue and failure are named as mechanically distinct states**, only one of which is
  growth-producing: `[26:47]`–`[28:50]`.
- **The power press's arched-back, shoulders-pulled-back position specifically prevents shoulder
  takeover on upper-chest presses**: `[11:20]`.
- **Chin position directly changes range of motion/flexion at the top of the movement**, not just
  form aesthetics: `[28:50]`.
- **Breath-holding beyond one or two reps is named as a direct cause of reduced endurance**, via
  oxygen supply to the working muscle: `[34:00]`.
- **Dehydration is stated as an unconditional block on pump**, regardless of supplement quality:
  `[35:02]`–`[36:04]`.

## Glossary terms introduced

- **Power press** — an incline press variant with palms facing each other and the back arched to
  pull the shoulders back, reducing shoulder involvement to emphasize the upper chest `[11:20]`.
- **Intensity multiplier** — Hany's umbrella term for techniques (partials, forced reps, negatives)
  applied after reaching true failure, to extend a set beyond the point full-range reps are
  possible `[27:49]`.
- **Ladders** — changing hand/arm angle (low, mid, high) within a single continuous set, rather
  than between separate sets `[37:04]`.
- **Static hold** — a paused isometric segment inserted mid-set to add time-under-tension and
  recruit stabilizer muscles `[17:28]`.

## Analogies worth reusing

- **"Tapping that pencil on top of the dynamite" vs. "going in with a hammer"** `[26:47]` — Hany's
  stated 20-30-year-old bodybuilding analogy distinguishing fatigue from true failure.
- **"Ordering off the secret menu"** `[42:11]` — Seth's analogy for how "Feroce shredded" became
  informal shorthand among other competitors for an extreme conditioning standard, modeled on an
  In-N-Out Burger secret-menu order.

## Source-stated confidence

Hany is direct about which claims are near-universal training physiology versus athlete-specific
coaching judgment:

- **The fatigue-vs-failure distinction and the oxygen/breathing mechanism are stated as general
  physiological principles**, not specific to Seth: `[26:47]`, `[34:00]` "all athletes of all ages,
  of all levels" tend to hold their breath.
- **Carbohydrate/metabolism recommendations are explicitly scoped by individual metabolism type**,
  not a fixed number for everyone: `[14:22]` "it depends on your metabolism... if your metabolism is
  really fast and you're lean, you can take in about 300 grams of carbs, no problem."
- **The muscle-fiber/genetics discussion is framed as Hany's professional read on a specific
  athlete (Seth)**, not a generalized claim about all lifters: `[04:05]`–`[05:05]`.
