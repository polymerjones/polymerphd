# Seth Feroce How-To: Alternating Dumbbell Curls

## Central claim

**Seth deliberately caps his range of motion on alternating dumbbell curls below a full
top-position curl, crediting the idea to Phil Heath, because going higher shifts tension off the
bicep and onto the shoulder.** `[03:04]` "I actually had a chance to train with Phil Heath... I
noticed it on his bicep training, he had a shorter range of motion than I was using... he said,
'it's a short-range motion, I just get more blood flow into my biceps by doing it this way.'"

## Where the rep stops, and why (02:02–04:06)

The failure mode he names is specific: `[02:02]` "a big thing that I see people do wrong is they'll
bring it all the way up and then they'll feel the weight or the tension come off of their bicep and
get put onto their shoulder... whenever I'm doing these... you won't see me go any higher on this."
He ties the stopping point to his own posing position rather than an abstract joint angle:
`[02:02]` "whenever I go to flex my bicep in a pose... I'm right here, this is where I'm at... 
whenever I come up, that's the pose I hit." He's explicit this is about efficiency, not correctness
in an absolute sense: `[02:02]`–`[03:04]` "it's not so much that it's incorrect, you can do it if
you choose, but you won't be getting as much out of the exercise as possible."

## Load, "rock," and mid-set stretching (01:00–04:06)

Hand placement is named as a first emphasis variable, same as in his other curl videos: `[01:00]`
"pay attention to where you grab — hand placement dead-nut center, all the way back, all the way
forward — either way that you choose to do it, you're going to feel [it] pull differently on your
bicep." As load increases, some body English is treated as an acceptable trade-off rather than a
flaw to eliminate entirely: `[01:00]`, `[04:06]` "is the rock a good thing or a bad thing? A little
bit of both... the stricter the better, but sometimes it's all a give and take" — with the
condition that "I'm still keeping that range of motion exactly the same because I want constant
tension on my muscles." Mid-set stretching is named as a deliberate blood-flow tactic, not
incidental fatigue: `[04:06]` "you'll see me stretch out my arms... I'm looking to allow more blood
flow to go into the muscle group... then you'll see we do a couple extra reps and then all of a
sudden more blood will start rushing."

## Form cues and body signals addressed

- **A specific top-of-rep point is named as where bicep tension is lost to the shoulder**:
  `[02:02]` going higher than his stated stopping point shifts load off-target.
- **"Rock" at heavier weight is graded, not banned outright**: `[01:00]`, `[04:06]` acceptable only
  so long as range of motion stays constant and tension stays on the bicep, distinguished from
  outright walking/momentum which "jeopardize[s] the actual exercise itself."
- **Mid-set stretching is a deliberate blood-flow technique**, not a rest pause: `[04:06]`.

## Glossary terms introduced

- **"Rock"** — Seth's term for the small body-momentum assist that appears as dumbbell curls get
  heavier; treated as a acceptable trade-off only if range of motion and bicep tension stay
  constant `[01:00]`.

## Analogies worth reusing

- **Stopping the curl at "the pose I hit"** `[02:02]` — using his own most-muscular bicep pose as
  the practical reference point for where to stop the concentric rep, rather than a joint-angle
  number.

## Source-stated confidence

The core technique here is attributed explicitly to another named bodybuilder rather than claimed
as Seth's own discovery, and the mechanism is stated as reported, not measured:

- **Attributed directly to Phil Heath's own explanation**: `[03:04]` "he said... 'I just get more
  blood flow into my biceps by doing it this way with a shorter range of motion.' And I was like,
  oh, okay." Seth adopted it after watching and asking, not from independent testing.
- **The blood-flow-equals-growth chain is stated as his personal training philosophy**, not a
  cited mechanism: `[04:06]` "that's my whole philosophy behind training: blood flow equals
  nutrient flow equals roundness, fullness, growth."

## Conflicts with other sources

Against `OEP-Sd4et5A`'s cable-curl note (hand placement changes which part of the bicep is
worked, "one way is not better than the other"), this note adds a related but distinct variable —
range of motion, not just hand placement — as a second lever for keeping tension on the bicep
specifically. The two converge on the same underlying goal (isolate the bicep, keep tension off
the shoulder) via different mechanisms. Against `VfZeOq18YGU`'s trap note and `eY2npBUVU4Y`'s
cable-warm-up section, blood flow as a stated training goal recurs again here — now with a fourth
independent technique (shortened range of motion) offered toward the same end.
