# Arm Day on Low Carbs | Seth Feroce

## Central claim

**Seth deliberately schedules arm day on a low-carbohydrate day specifically because he judges it
a lower-output workout compared to legs, and uses the session to demonstrate what he calls
"instinctive training" — defined explicitly as objective self-assessment, not simply following
whatever feels good.** `[00:00]`–`[01:02]` "on lower carb days I tend to always go after lower
output workouts, like I'm not doing a leg day on low carbs tomorrow." `[07:13]` "that's the
instinctive training... a very advanced technique — but just because I don't like it doesn't mean
I won't do it. It's paying attention to my physique and being very, very objective with myself, and
not blowing smoke up my [own] ass."

## Defining instinctive training precisely (07:13–08:14)

Seth is explicit that this is not "do whatever you feel like": `[07:13]` "the whole time I'm going to
be analyzing my physique, watching how it works, seeing how my arms look as I'm continuing to push
all the blood... and pay attention to what... is going on in the workout — if I like it and it's
working, let's keep pushing; if I'm... not feeling this, I'm going to move on." The objectivity
requirement is stated as the actual difficult part: `[07:13]`–`[08:14]` "just because I don't like
it doesn't mean I won't do it... just pay attention, be very objective... [not] I just really don't
want to do these today, I'm just going to move on and say I didn't [need to]."

## Tricep press-down: wrist rotation and lean as separate levers (06:13–07:13)

A specific wrist action is named for long-head emphasis: `[06:13]` "whenever you're doing them and
you come down and twist your wrist down, you feel your long head — crazy activation on it." Body
lean is a distinct, separate lever for the lateral head: `[06:13]` "we can lean forward and keep
them together and work on the lateral head — still working your triceps, but you're going to feel
a little more in your lateral head."

## Cross-body tricep extension: distance from the pulley (17:47–18:49)

Seth introduces a variable not addressed in this library's other tricep notes: how close the
lifter stands to the cable pulley changes the pull angle even with an identical exercise setup.
`[17:47]`–`[18:49]` "if I stand more upright or I lean into it, still doing cross-body tricep
extensions... it's a different pull on the tricep than if I'm here... closer, it's going to be
different than if I'm further away from the cable or the pulley." He frames the cumulative value
of small adjustments like this directly: `[18:49]` "if we're playing the game of inches... all
those crumbs turn into a big [pile] by the end of the workout."

## Diet-training day matching (00:00–02:03, 09:29–10:29)

Seth states a specific nutrition-periodization rule tied to output demand rather than a fixed
weekly carb schedule: `[01:02]` low-carb days are matched to lower-output training (arms), with a
high-carb day reserved for a higher-demand session (legs, or shoulders the next day). He is
explicit that the resulting look (flatter, less "pushing" muscle fullness) is an expected,
temporary trade-off he's specifically studying: `[09:29]`–`[10:29]` "whenever I start eating some
carbs... everything would start pushing even more... but that's part of the process."

## Form cues and body signals addressed

- **Wrist rotation and body lean are independent levers on the same press-down movement**, one
  targeting the long head, the other the lateral head: `[06:13]`.
- **Distance from the cable pulley changes pull angle**, distinct from grip or lean: `[17:47]`.
- **Objectivity, not just feeling, is the stated standard for instinctive training**: `[07:13]`.
- **Carb timing is matched to a workout's output demand**, not applied uniformly across training
  days: `[01:02]`.

## Glossary terms introduced

- **Instinctive training** (as defined here) — objective, ongoing self-assessment of what's
  actually working versus what's merely preferred, explicitly distinguished from simply avoiding
  disliked exercises `[07:13]`.
- **Pose within a pose** — adjusting limb/body position mid-pose (e.g., flaring the legs while
  hitting a front lat spread) to control how adjacent muscle groups visually transition into one
  another `[11:30]`.

## Analogies worth reusing

- **"Game of inches"** `[18:49]` — used again here, applied to cable-distance-from-pulley rather
  than hand placement, showing the same framing recurs across different mechanical variables.

## Source-stated confidence

Seth is explicit that both the diet-timing choice and the instinctive-training method are personal
experimentation, not settled protocol:

- **The carb/output-matching schedule is stated as his own current plan, open to revision**:
  `[15:34]` "bodybuilding is a marathon, not a sprint... [pros are] still learning and seeing how
  they can get... half a percent better... sometimes they get ten percent worse trying to get [that]
  percent better."
- **Instinctive training is explicitly labeled advanced and conditional on self-honesty**, not a
  shortcut: `[07:13]` "a very advanced technique."
