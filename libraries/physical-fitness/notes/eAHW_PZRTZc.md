# Tips for Building a Bigger Back | Grips, Positioning, & Leverages

## Central claim

**Seth frames this entire session as teaching a training partner to find his own pulling
position through live experimentation and hands-on feedback, rather than handing him one fixed
technique — and explicitly warns against copying a technique seen online without that
individualized verification.** `[06:18]` "You just have to find out what works for you and what
feels good for you. You can't always just go off of you seeing somebody on the internet [and be
like], 'I need to do it like that.' That's how you get hurt, because everyone's different."
`[09:20]` "It's like, no, no, no — you can[not just copy one way]... I don't train back the same
way I train legs, the same way I train chest, the same way I train arms."

## Torso angle and pull height as emphasis variables (02:03–04:06, 21:59–24:01)

On the DY row, torso angle is named directly: `[03:04]` "in order to get that activation in my
lats where I like it, I'm... almost [with my] chest... parallel to the floor... that's how I
personally like it. And then... as I get heavier [I] start to creep up, but I'm still engaging
that same part of my back and lat I want to hit." Pull height relative to the torso is then tested
live as a direct comparison on the same exercise: `[21:59]`–`[23:00]` "I want you to do three reps
to below your navel, so you can feel it... now I want you to do three between your sternum and your
navel... same exercise [feels] different, don't it?" The stated mechanism linking pull height to
elbow position: `[23:00]` "the further you go up, the more naturally your elbows are going to come
out. The further you go down, the more naturally your elbows will go in." Seth states his own
current-session choice explicitly as a sequencing decision, not a universal rule: `[24:01]` "I
won't [pull] down because we just did a ton of lower lat... I like mid, because we're going to do
[upper back] after this."

## Finger pressure and grip as a lats-vs-biceps/upper-back lever (14:42–17:45)

A specific, repeatable technique is named: `[14:42]` "if you go grip it from the side, you'll...
pull with your pinkies down low... I rarely pull with my index finger... I'll pull right here and
you'll feel it there, but then I'll also feel all the way up into my [traps]." The mechanism is
stated directly: `[15:42]` "if you're pulling with your index finger and your middle finger,
you're going to pull more with your biceps and start to engage your upper back much more. You
start pulling with your ring finger and your pinky and... keeping your elbows tucked, you're going
to start pulling way more with your lats." This is explicitly the origin of a grip cue used
elsewhere in this library: `[16:44]` "that's where it came from, me... pulling with fingers... if I
pull with my pinkies, I noticed that I immediately started pulling with my elbows" — directly
addressing why "pull with your elbow" as a verbal cue didn't make sense to him until he found his
own version of it through grip.

## Hands-on feedback as verification (07:18–18:47)

The session's actual teaching method is named directly: `[07:18]` "we're just knocking around ideas
and feelings... you can't see your back, you feel it, we're watching it. So if what you're feeling
is what we're seeing, you got it." Training partners physically touch the lifter's back mid-set to
confirm engagement: `[11:25]` "put your hands here... put your hands there, that way you can feel
everything occurring as you're doing it," with a partner reporting back in real time: `[17:45]`
"'dude, I can feel your lats through your shirt' — it just triggered... 'okay, so what I just did is
semi-correct.'"

## Form cues and body signals addressed

- **Pull height (below the navel vs. between sternum and navel) is a direct, testable lever
  between lower-lat and mid-back emphasis on the same exercise**: `[21:59]`–`[24:01]`.
- **Elbow position follows naturally from pull height rather than being cued independently**:
  `[23:00]` higher pull → elbows out; lower pull → elbows in.
- **Finger pressure (pinky/ring vs. index/middle) is a stated lever between lat and bicep/
  upper-back recruitment**: `[15:42]`.
- **Copying an unverified technique from social media is named directly as an injury risk**:
  `[06:18]` "that's how you get hurt, because everyone's different."

## Glossary terms introduced

- **DY row** — named after Dorian Yates, a chest-supported row machine used here for lower-lat
  focus; distinguished explicitly from a shoulder/upper-back variant done by raising the seat and
  hand position (`[12:26]`–`[13:26]`).
- **Pistol grip (pulling with the pinky/ring fingers)** — Seth's earlier-established cue,
  referenced here as the origin point that later made the "pull with your elbow" verbal cue make
  sense to him `[16:44]`.

## Analogies worth reusing

- **"You feel it, we're watching it"** `[07:18]` — framing why a training partner's real-time
  visual/tactile feedback closes a gap the lifter can't check alone (not being able to see one's
  own back).

## Source-stated confidence

The video is explicit, more so than most notes in this library, that its cues are being verified
live and individually rather than asserted as universal:

- **The whole framing rejects a single correct technique in favor of individualized feedback**:
  `[06:18]`, `[09:20]` "you have to try a bunch of different variations," and copying a technique
  wholesale from someone else "is how you get hurt."
- **The finger-pressure/elbow connection is stated as Seth's own retrospective realization, not
  something he understood in advance**: `[16:44]` "I still didn't get it... that's where it came
  from, me... pulling with fingers."
