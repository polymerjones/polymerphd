# How-To: T-Bar Rows - Feroce Iron Academy

## Central claim

**Seth frames the T-bar row's technique around one failure mode — being pulled forward onto the toes — and builds every cue (foot placement, head position, leg drive) around preventing it, while using grip width to steer emphasis between lats and upper back.** `[00:06]` "This is designed to activate specific parts of your back and lat as you're doing this exercise... similar to a barbell row or any type of exercise where you're going to be picking things up off the ground, start at your feet. The base is the best place to start."

## Grip width changes the target (00:06–01:06)

`[00:06]` "With your close grip you're going to be activating more of your lats... with the wide grip you're still going to activate your lats, but you're also going to hit portions of the upper back — your teres major, your teres minor, your infraspinatus, your rhomboids, [the] top of your traps." The close grip is named as his own priority movement: `[01:06]` "this is something that has helped me build lower lat thickness and overall lat development."

## Base and posture: preventing the forward pull (01:06–03:10)

The core safety/technique cue is repeated and tied directly to a named consequence: `[01:06]` "whenever you pick this weight up, you want to make sure that you have a lot of the weight on your heels, because if you don't, and you put the weight on your toes, this is going to pull you forward — same as if... you put your face down, you tend to lean forward." The standing sequence is given in an explicit order: `[02:08]` "grab the weight, head up, chin up, chest up... we're going to lift with our legs, not with our back — your legs are stronger than the lower back." Elbow position is tied to a specific, graded effect rather than a pass/fail rule: `[02:08]`–`[03:10]` "whenever you keep your elbows in closer to your body, you're going to activate more lat; whenever they come out, you still activate your lats, you're just not optimizing the exercise."

## Grip and progressive loading (03:10–05:12)

Seth names his own grip preference and flags it as personal rather than mandatory: `[03:10]` "I use a false grip, that's whenever I wrap my thumbs around — I do not wrap my thumbs under... it's personal preference." As load increases, a belt is introduced for a stated mechanical reason: `[05:12]` "as you move up in weight, something that you got to stay mindful of is keeping your core tight, your lumbar, your lower back strong, but as you go up in weight sometimes that's not enough — use a belt... that's going to help keep everything in, and it's going to give you a little bit more core strength."

## Form cues and body signals addressed

- **Weight on the heels, not the toes, as the standing safeguard against being pulled forward**: `[01:06]` stated as the direct fix for the forward-lean failure mode.
- **Losing control of the weight is treated as losing control of the muscle, not just a load problem**: `[05:12]` "the second that you lose control of the weight, you lose control of your muscles, and you are no longer in control and you're not optimizing the exercise."
- **Elbow position is a spectrum, not a binary**: `[02:08]`–`[03:10]` closer-in elbows increase lat activation; flared elbows still work the lats, just less optimally.
- **A belt is introduced specifically as load increases**, not used from the first set: `[05:12]` "as I move up in weight, that's whenever I strap on the belt."

## Glossary terms introduced

- **Close grip / wide grip (T-bar row)** — Seth's two named hand-placement variants on the same
  T-bar machine, close grip biased toward lat thickness, wide grip adding upper-back/rhomboid/trap
  involvement `[00:06]`.
- **False grip** — thumbs wrapped around the same side as the fingers rather than underneath the
  bar, Seth's own stated preference `[03:10]`.

## Analogies worth reusing

- **"This is nothing but a science experiment for you"** `[04:11]` — framing trial-and-error
  self-observation as the primary way to learn correct form, ahead of outside instruction.

## Source-stated confidence

This is a hands-on technique tutorial from Seth's own coaching experience, and he explicitly
declines to claim outside scientific backing for it:

- **Explicitly not claimed as evidence-based**: `[04:11]` "I don't need any scientific evidence or
  clinical studies to tell you how you're going to feel when you do this exercise... make that
  connection with your mind and then you'll be able to determine how you should do it."
- **Trial and error is named as the actual mechanism by which lifters learn technique**: `[04:11]`
  "trial and error is how 95% of all bodybuilders get to where they are — actually, no, it's 100%
  trial and error. You got to figure out what works best for you."
