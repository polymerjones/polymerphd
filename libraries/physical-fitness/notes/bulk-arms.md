# Bulk Arms

## Central claim

**Sagi Kalev frames arms as the body part with the most social payoff, distinguishing this
session's ambition ("cannons") from ordinary arm training ("guns") before the first set.**
`[00:00]` "Bulk Arms. The place where it really counts. You want to have big triceps? You want to
have big biceps? This is the right place to be. That's where everybody sees first... this is not
the gun show. This is the cannon show."

## Warm-up (01:10–03:10)

Arm circles both directions, then light-weight rehearsals of the session's exercise families:
straight curls (`[01:10]`, "should be lightweight... keep your chest up"), a wider-grip curl
(`[02:10]`), hammer curls — explicitly named as covering both biceps heads: `[02:10]` "working all
sides of the biceps. The long part. The short part" — and a light two-arm overhead triceps
extension (`[02:10]`–`[03:10]`, "hips back, and your chest up"). Closes with high knees before the
first working set.

## Set 1 — Curl, progressive set (03:10–08:10)

The first in-session definition of a progressive set: three weights run back-to-back with no rest,
reps dropping as weight rises. `[04:10]` "I'm starting with a 10. Straight curls... then I'm moving
right away. Going down with the reps. To 20s. With no rest. 12 reps. And then I'm moving again. To
30s. I'm going to do 8 reps. With no rest. You got it guys. That is progressive set." A second round
reverses the direction — heavy to medium to light — named directly as the same technique run
backward: `[06:10]` "we're going to start with the heaviest weight... and then we're going to the
lighter one... we are progressing up, progressing down."

## Set 2 — Overhead triceps extension, single set with drop set (08:10–12:10)

Seated, weight lowered behind the head and pressed up, elbows fixed: `[08:10]` "nothing's moving.
Beside my arms going up and down. Make sure you keep your elbows in." Three escalating rounds
(12 → 8, closing in a drop set), with an effort framing distinct from the program's usual "failure
is not an option": `[10:10]` "success is my only option."

## Set 3 — EZ-bar curl, force set (12:10–16:10)

Named directly as Kalev's own favorite move in this session, and the first in-session definition of
a force set: `[12:10]` "This is my favorite move in this workout. We got five sets of five reps.
Back. Focusing on the negative. In this move, there's not going up or down with the weight. We're
staying with it." The structure, restated for clarity: `[13:10]` "we got five reps like that. We
don't drop the weights. We hold it for a few. And then we do five more. We got five times to do
that. No breaking down. No dropping. No going up." Emphasis throughout is on the slow eccentric
(lowering) phase rather than the lift itself: `[14:10]` "control it on the way down. Real slow. Let
your muscle fill in it. Squeeze on it. Stretch it."

## Set 4 — Lying triceps extension ("crush"), single set with drop set (16:10–21:10)

Named in-source as "crush[ers]": `[16:10]` "this is called crush[ed]... bring the weight lying
down. Extend it up. Lock your elbows in... your hand's going right to your forehead." An isolation
check follows directly: `[17:10]` "make sure your elbows are not [tucked too] tight. They're not
going anywhere besides squeezing your chest. You want to squeeze the chest with your elbows.
That's why you're not working chest. You're only working the triceps." Three escalating rounds
(15 → 12 → 8+8 drop set).

## Set 5 — Concentrated hammer curl, progressive set (21:10–27:12)

One arm at a time, elbow braced against the top of the knee: `[22:10]` "next to your top of your
knee. And bring your arm all the way up. Without raising your elbow off your knee." Each arm runs
its own full light-medium-heavy progression (15 → 12 → 8) before switching sides, with no rest at
any transition — weight change or side change.

## Set 6 — Triceps kickback, progressive set (28:12–33:12)

Staggered stance, elbow raised and fixed, isolation cued through the small finger rather than the
whole hand: `[28:12]`, `[29:12]` "starting with elbow up. Kick back. Extend... focus on the pinky.
As you get up." Each arm again runs its own light-medium-heavy progression (15 → 12 → 8) with no
rest between weights or sides.

## Ab finisher — weighted-plate crunch ("old school abs") (34:12–35:13)

A single 30-rep set, weight plate held behind the head: `[34:12]` "come as high as you can and
squeeze your abs." Nutrition is folded directly into the coaching cue: `[34:12]` "you got to eat
right. Abs, ma[de] in the kitchen, but they're also made on your workout."

## Stretch and cooldown (35:13–36:13)

Standing side-bend with arms crossed and raised, in-and-out arm extension, then a forward-reaching
chest stretch with a no-force rule stated directly: `[35:13]`–`[36:13]` "don't force it. You just
want to extend this." Closes with a from-the-back triceps/shoulder stretch, breath-paced.

## Content note: appended promotional material (37:13–end)

The workout itself ends at `[36:13]` ("that was amazing. Bulk arms... see you next time"). Everything
from `[37:13]` to the end of the file is unrelated Beachbody promotional content — P90X2, Shakeology,
and P90X advertisements — not Body Beast programming, not delivered as part of the Kalev-led
session, and not reflected in this note's exercises or training-focus facets.

## Form cues and body signals addressed

- **Isolation is checked by naming which joint should stay fixed**: `[09:10]`, `[22:10]` "nothing's
  moving besides my arms"; "without raising your elbow off your knee."
- **A specific compensating muscle is named and excluded**: `[17:10]` "you want to squeeze the chest
  with your elbows. That's why you're not working chest. You're only working the triceps" — a
  correction against elbow flare that would recruit the chest instead of the triceps.
- **The eccentric (lowering) phase is called out as the trained variable in the force set**:
  `[14:10]` "control it on the way down. Real slow. Let your muscle fill in it."
- **A load-adjustment safety allowance recurs across sets**: `[08:10]`, `[21:10]` "you have to drop
  the weights. Drop the weights. Just like that... just write it down."
- **A specific isolation checkpoint (the small finger) is used on the kickback**: `[29:12]` "focus
  on the pinky. Extend your triceps."

## Glossary terms introduced

- **Progressive set** — three weights (light, medium, heavy) run back-to-back with no rest, reps
  decreasing as weight rises; can also run in reverse (heavy to light). First defined in-session
  here, matching the term named without definition in `basics`[01:01] `[04:10]`.
- **Force set** — five sets of five reps at one fixed weight, held rather than dropped between
  sets, with emphasis on a slow negative (lowering) phase rather than a full up/down rep cycle.
  First defined in-session here, matching the term named without definition in `basics`[01:01]
  `[12:10]`.
- **"Crush" (lying triceps extension)** — the source's own shorthand name for a lying triceps
  extension to the forehead `[16:10]`.
- **"Cannons" vs. "guns"** — the source's own escalated term for well-developed arms, used to
  distinguish this session's ambition from ordinary arm training `[00:00]`.

## Analogies worth reusing

- **"That's back in the days when you're still going to kindergarten. Now you're in the next
  phase."** `[10:10]` — used to frame an upcoming drop set as a step up in difficulty from what
  came before.
- **"You train them right. You be nice. Be polite. They'll love you forever."** `[13:10]` — said
  about equipment (a cleaned bar), reused humorously to reinforce the "clean your equipment"
  safety habit.

## Source-stated confidence

An instructional/motivational training-partner video; its evidence is Kalev's technique narration
and two training partners' (John and Nick's) visible performance, not citation or measurement:

- **"Success is my only option" is offered as this session's specific reframing** of the program's
  more common "failure is not an option" phrasing (`[10:10]`; compare `build-legs`[05:04]) — the
  same underlying stance stated in different words rather than a different standard.
- **A completion-optional, load-adjustable safety line recurs at multiple points**: `[08:10]` and
  `[21:10]`,
  `[21:10]` "if you have to drop the weights, you know what? Drop the weights. No problem."
- **Long-term effort is named directly rather than implied**: `[19:10]` "these guys have been
  doing it for a long time. They really, really work hard to get these cannons."

## Conflicts with other sources

This session gives the first in-source definitions of "progressive set" and "force set," two of
the eight set types `basics`[01:01] names without defining — worth checking as more sessions are
notated whether the remaining undefined types ("drawing sets," "combo sets," "multi sets") get
defined elsewhere, and whether `build-back-and-bis`'s "giant set" is one of `basics`'s named types
under a different label (flagged as an open question in `build-back-and-bis`'s own Conflicts
section). The "success is my only option" framing here is a close variant of `build-legs`[05:04]'s
"success is the only option. Failure is not" (restated at `build-legs`[15:05]) — consistent phrasing
across sessions rather than a contradiction. Against RUSHFIT's `balance-and-agility`[37:01], whose safety line
sits at pain rather than form, this session's repeated "drop the weight, just write it down" offer
is again closer to a completion/load-adjustment standard.
