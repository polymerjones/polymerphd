# RUSHFIT Nutrition Guide

## Central claim

**The guide treats nutrition as load-bearing infrastructure for the training program, not
lifestyle advice on the side — explicitly ranked alongside the workouts themselves — and backs
that with an actually calculable method (a personal BMR formula plus an activity multiplier)
rather than generic "eat healthy" guidance.** `[p.4]` "Nutrition is a vital but often overlooked
element of a training program; it's as important to any workout program as exercise, repetitions
and time spent sweating."

## Calculating a personal calorie target (p.6–p.9)

A two-step Basal Metabolic Rate calculation (separate formulas for women and men, using height,
weight, and age) is multiplied by an activity factor tied directly to weekly training frequency:
×1.2 for 1-2x/week, ×1.4 for 2-4x/week, ×1.6 for 4-6x/week `[p.6]`–`[p.7]`. Weight loss is then
reduced to one governing equation: `[p.8]` "calories burned − calories eaten = calorie deficit...
Once you accumulate a 3,500 calorie deficit, you've burned off 1 pound of fat!" A specific floor is
stated against under-eating: `[p.9]` "Your daily caloric intake must be above 1200 calories per
day," paired with a stated healthy pace of 1-2 lbs/week and a table translating four deficit sizes
(450/900/1,100/1,600 calories) directly into pounds of fat lost over the 8-week program.

## Macronutrient basics and pre/post-workout timing (p.10–p.13)

Carbohydrates are explicitly defended against "recent diet fads" that "mistakenly singled out
carbs as bad factors in the diet" `[p.10]`, with the majority recommended from whole-grain sources.
Protein and fat sources are each split into named animal/plant (protein) and saturated "bad" vs.
unsaturated "good" (fat) categories `[p.11]`. The pre- and post-workout macro ratios are stated as
a near-mirror-image swap rather than a fixed ratio used throughout the day:

| | Carbs | Protein | Fat |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Pre-workout** `[p.12]` | 65% | 35% | 10% |
| **Post-workout** `[p.13]` | 35% | 55% | 10% |

Water intake is quantified for both windows too: a half to three-quarters of a litre before a
workout, a half to a full litre after `[p.12]`–`[p.13]`.

## Do's, Don'ts, and Super Foods (p.14–p.19)

The Do's list centers on eating frequency (three meals plus two snacks rather than one or two
large meals), hydration (6-8 glasses/day), fibre, and naming Omega-3/6 fats as immune- and
joint-supportive `[p.14]`–`[p.15]`. The Don'ts list singles out hydrogenated fats by citing outside
authorities directly: `[p.16]` "Studies show that these trans fats are strongly linked to
cardiovascular disease and the World Health Organization and the National Academy of Science are
recommending that trans fats be eliminated from our diets completely" — the guide's only
externally-attributed claim rather than a stated program rule. Sugar is framed in explicitly
addiction terms: `[p.16]` "Sugar is a drug, and we become physically addicted to it." A named list
of "Super Foods" follows (berries, citrus, cruciferous vegetables, eggs, leafy greens, legumes,
nuts/seeds, oats, olive oil, cold-water fish, soy, green tea, tomatoes, turkey, whole grains,
yogurt), each with a specific serving-frequency recommendation `[p.18]`–`[p.19]`.

## Daily structure and label-reading (p.20–p.23)

A meal-by-meal template (breakfast within an hour of waking, most calories before 5pm, dinner 2-3
hours before bed, snacks every 3 hours minimum) is given with concrete example meals for each slot
`[p.20]`–`[p.21]`. Label-reading guidance names specific disguised-ingredient patterns: `[p.23]`
"'Flour' usually means wheat; 'vegetable oil' may mean corn oil, anything that ends in '...ose' is
typically another way of saying 'sugar.'"

## Form cues and body signals addressed

- **Under-eating is named with a specific physiological consequence, not just "bad for you"**:
  `[p.22]` "if you do not eat enough, you may experience symptoms of low blood sugar, such as
  fatigue, irritability and headaches."
- **Under-eating for weight loss is named as self-defeating, not merely unpleasant**: `[p.9]` "If
  you eat too little your body will go into 'storage' mode and actually hold on to excess fat."

## Glossary terms introduced

- **Basal Metabolic Rate (BMR)** — the guide's core calculation: calories needed per day given
  height, weight, age, gender, and activity level `[p.6]`.
- **The calorie equation** — calories burned minus calories eaten equals the calorie deficit
  driving fat loss, with 3,500 deficit calories equal to one pound of fat `[p.8]`.

## Analogies worth reusing

- **"Sugar is a drug, and we become physically addicted to it"** — the guide's framing for its
  strongest Don't `[p.16]`.

## Source-stated confidence

- **The hydrogenated-fat claim is the guide's one explicitly externally-sourced statement**,
  attributed to named bodies (WHO, National Academy of Science) rather than presented as the
  program's own opinion `[p.16]` — a notably different register from the rest of the guide's
  unattributed "Do/Don't" rules.
- **The BMR formula is explicitly flagged as an approximation with a named blind spot**: `[p.6]`
  "The only factor it omits is lean body mass and thus the ratio of muscle-to-fat a body has...
  this equation will be very accurate for most average sized people" — i.e., stated as less
  accurate for atypical body composition, not universally precise.
- **The healthy-pace framing (1-2 lbs/week) is stated as a ceiling implicitly**, paired
  immediately with the under-eating warning, rather than encouraging faster loss `[p.9]`.
