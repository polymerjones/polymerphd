# Build Chest & Tris

## Central claim

**Sagi Kalev calls this Body Beast's foundation session and states its target explicitly as the
whole chest — top to bottom — plus all three triceps heads, tying the payoff directly to a
shirtless-beach visual rather than a strength number.** `[00:00]` "This is the foundation of the
workout, the built workout. Working the chest all around from top to bottom, the triceps, all the
three heads of the triceps... when you go on the beach and you're going to take your shirt off,
that's the first thing that people are going to see."

## Warm-up (01:13–03:15)

Jog, then extended-arm shoulder rolls in both directions (`[01:13]`, "elbows stay to the top, both
sides... nothing else is moving. The only thing you're doing is breathing"), into a graduated
close-grip push-up progression: halfway range first (`[02:13]`, "go halfway down... this is not
about going faster. This is about getting warmed up"), then full range at a wider-than-shoulder
stance with a named target: `[03:15]` "focus on the tricep. And make sure you are squeezing your
chest with the bottom of your palm." A knee-drop regression is offered at both stages: `[02:13]`,
`[03:15]` "drop your knees down" / "drop your knees if you can't finish."

## Set 1 — Flat dumbbell press, single set with drop set (03:15–08:18)

Three escalating-weight rounds (15 → 12 → 8 reps), head supported on a stability ball. Form cue:
`[04:16]` "bring the weights to your side of your chest. Focus on your legs. Everything nice and
tight. And press it to the top... squeeze your legs." Round three is a named drop set: `[06:18]`
"drop into eight. Heavyweight. Going to lighter weight. This is our drop set. No rest." Between
sets, Kalev states the reason for writing weights down as a standing rule rather than a one-off
reminder: `[08:18]` "I'm going to keep saying it and say it like a broken record... that's how next
workout you will know where to start."

## Set 2 — Incline fly / incline press superset (09:18–18:18)

Two exercises with no rest between, escalating together across three rounds (15+15 → 12+12 →
8+8, the last round explicitly a combined drop set and superset). Fly form: `[09:18]` "open your
arm. Keep your chest up. As you come up, pull your chest to the top. Lock on your chest." Press
form, done immediately after with no break: `[10:18]` "Remember, there's no rest on the superset."
Kalev names the muscle-region coverage directly: `[10:18]` "We work in chest. We work in the middle
of the chest. We work in everything. Total package."

## Set 3 — Giant set: flat close-grip press, partial fly, decline push-up (18:18–28:19)

Three exercises run back-to-back with no rest, three rounds at escalating weight (15 → 12 → 8
reps). Partial fly is explicitly a half-range move, tension held rather than released at the
midpoint: `[20:18]` "you go in halfway. Open. Go halfway up. Stop right there. You want to keep the
tension right there on the chest the whole time... if you have to drop the weights, that's okay.
As long as you finish." Decline push-up, feet elevated on the bench: `[21:18]` "make sure you are
squeezing... from the bottom of your hand all the way to your lower chest. Keep your glutes up."
Failure is named directly as a training signal rather than something to avoid: `[22:18]` "That was
failure. That's when you really know when your body is working."

## Set 4 — Lying dumbbell triceps extension, single set with drop set (28:19–33:19)

Named in-source as "line, plate, triceps... triceps extensions" `[28:19]`, done lying back with
weights lowered to the sides of the head and extended up, elbows held in. Kalev states an explicit
priority order at the top of this set: `[28:19]` "Safety number one. Form number two. And the
number one rule is have fun." A negative/positive terminology distinction is introduced: `[31:19]`
"on the way down, that's your negative. On the way up, that's your positive." Round three is a
drop set, defined again in-source: `[31:19]` "a drop set is when you're going back to back and you
drop the weights without rest... bring your heavier weights to the lighter weights back to back."
A completion-over-count framing recurs here: `[30:19]` "if it was too heavy and you couldn't finish
those reps, you can drop the weight down. But finish. We're all about finish."

## Set 5 — One-arm kickback / close-grip push-up superset (33:19–42:19)

Kickback: staggered stance, elbow raised and locked, forearm extended back without dropping the
elbow — `[34:19]` "raise your elbow up. Close to your left. And extend it as high as you can.
Without dropping your elbow" — with the source naming the isolation check directly: `[35:19]` "the
only thing that's moving is your arm going up and down. Focus on the pinky going up." Followed
immediately (no rest, per superset rule restated at `[36:19]`) by close-grip push-ups. Escalates
across three rounds to a combined drop set/superset finish at `[39:19]`–`[41:19]`.

## Set 6 — Bench dip and reverse-crunch finisher (42:19–46:19)

One minute of bench (or chair) dips, with an explicit at-home substitute named up front: `[42:19]`
"you guys don't have a bench at home. You can use a chair. You can use anything that is stable."
Form cue on range and target: `[43:19]` "squeeze right from here. The bottom of your palm going
right to the top of your triceps... it's not about how many you do. It's about doing it right." A
mid-set regression is offered without stopping the clock: `[43:19]` "bring your feet back. Keep it
at 90 degrees right there. And go a shorter move." Immediately followed by one minute of a
reverse-crunch-style ab move (knees rolled to the chest), with a named muscle to avoid substituting
in: `[45:19]` "don't use your hip flexors, which are right here. Go right to the chest... slightly
bend them and roll back up with your chest."

## Stretch and cooldown (46:19–49:19)

Standing torso-rotation chest/shoulder stretch, breath-paced: `[47:19]` "breathe from your nose.
Exhale from your mouth." Overhead triceps stretch, each arm, with an explicit no-force rule:
`[48:19]` "Don't force anything. Just let it be. Feel the stretch." Closes with a standing
shoulder-and-chest stretch: fingers crossed, hands drawn back, chin dropped, shoulders rolled,
paced breathing repeated `[49:19]`.

## Form cues and body signals addressed

- **A completion-over-count rule recurs across multiple sets, not just once**: `[20:18]`, `[30:19]`,
  `[37:19]` "if you have to drop the weights, that's okay. As long as you finish"; "it's not about
  how many you do. It's about how right you do."
- **Failure is explicitly reframed as useful information, not a stopping point to avoid**:
  `[22:18]` "That was failure. That's when you really know when your body is working."
- **A negative/positive vocabulary is introduced for tempo control**: `[31:19]` "on the way down,
  that's your negative. On the way up, that's your positive."
- **Isolation is checked by naming the one thing that should move**: `[35:19]` "the only thing
  that's moving is your arm going up and down."
- **A specific compensating muscle is named and excluded on the ab finisher**: `[45:19]` "don't use
  your hip flexors... go right to the chest."
- **Stretch force is explicitly capped**: `[48:19]` "Don't force anything. Just let it be."

## Glossary terms introduced

- **Negative / positive (tempo)** — the lowering phase of a lift versus the lifting phase, named
  explicitly as distinct halves of a rep `[31:19]`.
- **Partial fly** — a fly variant stopped at half range, with tension deliberately held rather than
  released at the midpoint `[20:18]`.
- **One-arm kickback** — staggered stance, elbow locked and raised, forearm extended straight back
  to isolate the triceps `[34:19]`.

## Analogies worth reusing

- **"That's how we get big... that's how we get bigger... it's better. What you always wanted."**
  `[14:18]` — Kalev's own escalating-repetition motivational phrasing tied directly to load
  progression.
- **"You know what happens? That was Sean triceps" (smoke/smell running gag)** `[36:19]`, `[47:19]`
  — a recurring bit used to mark a set's visible/felt intensity rather than a literal claim.
- **"Barbie" as a contrast term for insufficient effort**: `[11:18]`, `[44:19]` "this is the beast
  workout. Not the girly workout. You hear that Barbie?" — noted here as the source's own framing,
  not adopted terminology.

## Source-stated confidence

An instructional/motivational training-partner video; its evidence is Kalev's technique narration
and two training partners' (Sean and Richard's) visible performance, not citation or measurement:

- **Safety and form are stated as ranked priorities, fun included as a named rule rather than an
  aside**: `[28:19]` "Safety number one. Form number two. And the number one rule is have fun."
- **Individual load and rep variation is shown directly rather than prescribed as one number**:
  `[05:16]`, `[37:19]` Kalev, Sean, and Richard each state different personal weights on the same
  lift (20s/25s/30s; later 8/10 vs. 15/20 on kickbacks).
- **A stretch's depth is explicitly capped by feel, not a fixed hold count**: `[48:19]` "Don't force
  anything. Just let it be. Feel the stretch."

## Conflicts with other sources

Against `build-back-and-bis`, the sessions share the same set-type vocabulary (single, super,
giant, drop) and the same standing rules — "write the weights down," a completion-optional safety
line, and a limited-equipment training-partner substitute — suggesting these are fixed program-wide
conventions rather than session-specific choices; worth confirming once the remaining `build-`/
`bulk-`/`tempo-` sessions are notated. One difference worth tracking: this session states an
explicit three-part priority order — "safety number one, form number two... have fun" (`[28:19]`)
— where `build-back-and-bis`[05:17] states only "keep your ego outside the door. Keep your form,"
without naming fun as a formal rule; whether that third element is stated in every session or only
some is not yet resolved. Against RUSHFIT's `balance-and-agility`[37:01], whose safety line sits at
pain rather than form, this session's "as long as you finish" framing is again closer to a
completion standard than a pain threshold.
