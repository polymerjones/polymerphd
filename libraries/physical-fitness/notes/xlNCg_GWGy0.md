# Intense Arm Workout Seth Feroce | Whiteboard Workout

## Central claim

**Seth structures the entire session around an explicit two-phase philosophy: heavier,
"mass-building" exercises early in the workout while energy is highest, followed by lighter,
isolation/"separation" work later to shape and define what was just built.** `[09:13]`–`[10:14]`
"we're mass building because we have more energy at the beginning of the workout... using heavier
weight with moderate to lower reps." `[16:23]`–`[17:25]` "this is a separation exercise, we're not
building massive arms, we already did that... now we're going to start building the rest of it, we're
going to shape the physique, move more blood."

## Hammer curl grip position along the handle (22:32–23:32)

Extending a variable introduced elsewhere in this library (grip position along a dumbbell), Seth
applies it specifically to hammer curls: `[23:32]` "you can grab down low, you can grab up high, or
you can grab in the middle... if you grab up high, whenever you're pulling, understand that you're
going to get a different pull than... all the way back — then you got to bring it up more, and
you'll feel more action in your forearm... I'm able to get a better squeeze on my bicep as well
whenever I'm up here." He declines to declare one correct answer: `[23:32]` "which one's better?
Well, we're going to know as we progressively use these types of exercises and also experiment with
them."

## Arm-day-specific pre-workout choice (18:28–19:28)

A specific, stated reason for avoiding caffeine on this particular training day: `[18:28]`–`[19:28]`
"on an arm day... no caffeine, zero caffeine pre-workout... because caffeine is a vasoconstrictor —
I want no stimulants in my body so I can get juicier." This is presented as day-specific, not a
blanket rule, tied to the earlier point that arm day requires less overall energy expenditure than
leg or back day.

## A disclosed injury and continued use of a favorite exercise (26:50–27:51)

Seth names a specific exercise (the overhead "French curl") as one that fitness commentators
criticize for joint risk, and discloses his own resulting injury directly rather than omitting it:
`[26:50]`–`[27:51]` "whenever I tore my tricep... I had nightmares of never being able to do this
exercise again... I can [do it again] though, thanks to [medical treatment]." His stated risk
management is the same "stay in control of the weight" principle used elsewhere: `[27:51]` "as
long as you stay in control of the weight, you're going to be good."

## Preacher curls: injury caution restated (11:17–12:17, 31:01–32:02)

Seth repeats a specific warning across two points in the session rather than mentioning it once:
`[11:17]` "you just have to be careful because it's a great way to tear a bicep if you go too
heavy"; `[31:01]` "you have to be mindful during this exercise so you don't tear a bicep — there's so
many failed videos on the internet that I can't stand watching."

## Form cues and body signals addressed

- **Session phase (early/heavy vs. late/isolation) is an explicit structural principle**, not an
  incidental ordering: `[09:13]`–`[10:14]`.
- **Grip position on hammer curls trades bicep squeeze against forearm involvement**, restated as a
  variable to experiment with rather than a fixed answer: `[23:32]`.
- **Caffeine is avoided specifically on arm day as a vasoconstrictor**, a day-specific rather than
  universal pre-workout rule: `[18:28]`–`[19:28]`.
- **A previously torn tricep is disclosed directly** as the cost of a favored but joint-risky
  exercise, with "stay in control of the weight" named as the ongoing safeguard: `[26:50]`–`[27:51]`.
- **Partial reps and negatives are explicitly counted as legitimate work**, not a lesser version of
  a "real" rep: `[25:49]` "partial reps matter, negatives matter... just because it's not a full
  repetition does not mean you're [failing]."

## Analogies worth reusing

- **"Making sweet love to yourself" / mirror eye contact** `[01:01]`, `[23:32]` — his repeated,
  intentionally provocative framing for full mental engagement and self-assessment during a set,
  used consistently enough across this session to read as a genuine coaching device, not just a
  one-off joke.

## Source-stated confidence

Seth is explicit that several claims here are personal preference or self-experimentation, and
discloses a real injury rather than presenting a favorite exercise as risk-free:

- **The hammer-curl grip variable is explicitly left open-ended**, not resolved to one correct
  grip: `[23:32]` "which one's better? ... we're going to know as we... experiment with them."
- **The overhead-extension injury is disclosed as his own lived consequence**, not hidden to avoid
  undercutting his endorsement of the exercise: `[26:50]`–`[27:51]`.
