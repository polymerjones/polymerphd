# What The Hell Is TREN? And Why Is Everyone Taking It?

## Central claim

**Dr. Raynor frames trenbolone as a synthetic anabolic steroid, derived from nandrolone, whose
appeal is a favorable anabolic-to-androgenic ratio relative to testosterone — but argues this
favorable ratio does not make it safe, citing cardiovascular, psychological, and renal effects
severe enough that he states it is "the one you want to stay away from the most."** `[00:00]`
"Tren is the monster steroid of all the steroids you could abuse... Tren is the one you want to
stay away from the most." `[06:09]` "Tren or Trenbolone is a highly effective synthetic anabolic
steroid derived from nandrolone by making specific modifications to its chemical structure...
enhance their anabolic effects while reducing androgenic effects."

## Origin and regulatory status (07:12–09:14)

The video traces trenbolone from veterinary to human use: `[07:12]` "Trenbolone... wasn't ever and
still isn't actually on the market [for humans]... all steroids are illegal for use without a
valid prescription... steroids are schedule three substances." `[08:13]` "Trenbolone was first
synthesized in 1963 and was originally classified as a veterinary androgenic anabolic steroid...
used in the agricultural industry to bulk up cattle." A human formulation (parabolin) was
developed in the 1980s by a French pharmaceutical company for muscle-wasting conditions,
malnutrition, and osteoporosis, but "has since been taken off the market even in France"
`[08:13]`. The video attributes rising availability partly to underground raw-powder supply,
citing the book *Doping in Sport and Fitness*: `[09:14]` "the increase in its usage may actually
be because of the increase in availability of raw powders from underground steroid labs including
those from China in the '80s and '90s."

## Dosing and mechanism claims, with an explicitly flagged conflicting citation (10:16–14:20)

Stated typical dosing: `[10:16]` "the average starting dose of Trenbolone ranges from 50 to 100
mg for beginners and 300 to 500 mg per week for the advanced user," with some users reaching "up
to 800 mg per week." On relative potency, the video presents two sources whose figures it flags as
in tension with each other: `[12:17]` "a review written by F. Newman suggests conflicting
findings, noting that Tren is actually 3 to 5 times more potent than testosterone in terms of its
effects on the androgen receptors. This claim was also echoed by Dr. Thomas O'Conor, otherwise
known as the metabolic doc... 'this drug is 300% stronger androgenic.'" — presented immediately
after describing trenbolone as having "reduced androgenic activity in comparison" to testosterone,
which the narration itself flags: "well, that just notes the complexity involved in evaluating
steroid compounds." Trenbolone is distinguished from SARMs on mechanism despite similar marketing
claims: `[13:17]`–`[14:20]` "Tren is not classified as a sarm... sarms achieve reductions in
androgenic side effects by selectively targeting androgen receptors in [specific] tissue... to
this day complete dissociation of the anabolic effects of an AAS from its androgenic
characteristics has been impossible."

## Side effects, organized by system (14:20–24:35)

The video catalogues effects with an explicit hedge on the estrogen-pathway ones: `[14:20]`
"while Tren balone doesn't directly convert to estrogen, its interaction with the progesterone
receptor can still result in some estrogen-like effects," including gynecomastia and lactation,
described as uncommon: `[15:20]` "while these estrogen-like side effects are uncommon, it's
essential to remember that individual responses to steroids can vary." Androgenic effects listed:
oily skin, acne, seborrheic dermatitis, facial hair overgrowth, scalp hair loss `[16:22]`.
Reproductive effects: hypogonadism, sperm production declining "usually zero within a few months,"
testicular shrinkage of 25–35%, and — specific to trenbolone — hypersexuality alongside erectile
dysfunction that "can even last beyond discontinuation of the drug" `[16:22]`–`[17:23]`.
Cardiovascular and renal effects: left ventricular thickening reducing pumping efficiency,
endothelial dysfunction, raised blood pressure, red blood cell count, and cholesterol, plus kidney
damage via FSGS (focal segmental glomerulosclerosis) "heightened when a user has a genetic
predisposition" `[18:25]`–`[20:28]`. A specific injection-related effect, "trend cough"
(pulmonary oil microembolism), is described with an explicit reassurance hedge: `[20:28]`–
`[21:29]` "this cough attack can be scary, but it may not be as dangerous as it seems... [though]
as a doctor I will say it is something you'd want to avoid if possible."

## Neurological and psychological effects, with an explicit human-evidence hedge (21:29–24:35)

On amyloid-beta plaque buildup, the video states the evidence is animal-only and not confirmed in
humans: `[21:29]` "some animal studies suggest that Tren may increase the production of amyloid
beta plaques in the brain, which are linked to Alzheimer's disease; however, it's unclear if the
same effects occur in humans." On aggression, it cites a named 2023 study — the author's name is garbled in the caption track as
"Timothy and[t]ski," reproduced here as captioned rather than guessed at: `[22:31]` "a 2023 study
conducted by Timothy and[t]ski at all... notes that users displayed extreme propensity towards
psychosocial harms in terms of aggression and violence and also impulsivity regulation issues...
it was the single most powerful contributor to psychological changes for them." The same study is
cited for relationship strain, social withdrawal, and insomnia ("transomnia") among users
`[23:33]`–`[24:35]`.

## Cultural framing: drug-based vs. training-based bodybuilding culture (25:36–33:48)

The video's closing argument shifts from pharmacology to sociology, citing a Washington Post
article (Bonnie Burkowitz and William Nef, as captioned) on the shift from a "classic," narrow-waisted
bodybuilding look to the modern "mass monster" physique since the 1990s, and quoting Brad
Shenfeld, as captioned: `[04:05]`–`[05:08]` "when people go to the zoo they want to see lions and tigers... not
cats and dogs." It states concern about use among minors, citing the Australian National Drug
Strategy Household Survey: `[31:43]` "non-medical anabolic steroid use almost tripled in the 18
years between 2001 and 2019... there are reports that boys as young as 13 are asking about
steroids." It closes on the death of bodybuilder Rich Piana as an example rather than a direct
causal claim: `[27:37]` "there is moderate concern that Tren is directly causing the death of some
of its users, but it's not necessarily that straightforward... an example is the death of
bodybuilder Rich Piana, whose autopsy revealed significant [spleno]megaly and cardiomegaly."

## Form cues and body signals addressed

- **A red-flag symptom cluster for cardiovascular strain is named explicitly**: elevated blood
  pressure, "trend cough" onset within an hour of an oil-based injection, and left-ventricular
  thickening reducing pump efficiency `[19:27]`–`[21:29]`.
- **Reproductive-system shrinkage (testicular, 25–35%) and hypogonadism are named as expected,
  near-universal effects of use, not idiosyncratic reactions** `[16:22]`.
- **Muscle-tendon mismatch is named as a distinct injury mechanism** — rapid muscle growth
  outpacing tendon adaptation — citing muscle physiologist Stuart Phillips: `[24:35]` "muscles so
  big they're literally ripping the body apart... that's when you attempt some diabolically heavy
  lift and you just rip a bicep tendon."

## Glossary terms introduced

- **Trenbolone / "Tren"** — synthetic anabolic steroid derived from nandrolone, explained in
  detail as this video's central subject `[06:09]`.
- **Pulmonary oil microembolism (POME) / "trend cough"** — an acute coughing/breathing reaction to
  oil-based injectable steroids `[20:28]`.
- **FSGS (focal segmental glomerulosclerosis)** — a kidney-scarring condition named as a
  trenbolone-associated renal risk `[18:25]`.
- **SARMs (selective androgen receptor modulators)** — non-steroidal compounds contrasted with
  trenbolone on mechanism `[13:17]`.

## Analogies worth reusing

- **"When people go to the zoo they want to see lions and tigers... not cats and dogs"** — Brad
  Shenfeld's framing, as captioned (quoted via the Washington Post article) for why bodybuilding audiences
  reward extreme size over the older, leaner aesthetic `[05:08]`.
- **"The trend look" / "trend stench" / "trend skin"** — recurring informal shorthand in the video
  for the visibly recognizable side-effect profile of trenbolone use `[16:22]`.
