# Build Legs

## Central claim

**Sagi Kalev calls legs his own favorite body part to train and names all four major leg regions
up front — quads, hamstrings, glutes, calves — framing the session's payoff as reversing
underdeveloped ("chicken") legs specifically, not general lower-body fitness.** `[00:00]` "Build
legs, my favorite body parts. We will use single set, super set, giant sets. We're going to work
the caps, the hamstrings, the glutes, the quads, all around it. You know what? It's time to get rid
of your chicken legs."

## Warm-up (01:04–02:04)

High knees, hip circles ("we got to warm up the hips" `[01:04]`), alternating reverse lunges (front
leg held at 90 degrees, chest open), and bodyweight sumo squats, toes turned out — `[01:04]` "you
want to stretch your glutes. You want to work your thighs." Closes with high knees at increased
pace before weights are picked up.

## Set 1 — Sumo squat, single set with drop set (02:04–05:04)

Weight held under the chin, close to the body, three escalating rounds (15 → 12 → 8, the third an
explicit no-rest drop set). Depth and knee tracking are both named: `[02:04]` "go as low as you
can... make sure you don't knock your knees." The drop set is defined again in-source: `[04:04]`
"Remember, there is no rest on a drop set." A recurring effort framing appears here for the first
time in this session: `[05:04]` "get to that zone. Find that place. Failure is not an option."

## Set 2 — Front lunge / step-up-to-reverse-lunge superset (05:04–16:05)

Alternating front lunges superset directly with a bench step-up into a reverse lunge, no rest
between the two moves, escalating weight across rounds (15 → 12 → 8 per leg). Step-up form: `[06:04]`
"straighten your legs out. Ninety degree. Right there on the opposite legs. And pull your shoulders
up as you're extending your chest." An explicit "too easy" exchange is used to frame intensity:
`[09:04]` "Is it pretty easy? ... It's never too easy. You know what? Because you don't build legs
if it's too easy." A stated safety allowance recurs through this set: `[12:05]` "if you have to stop
and drop the weights, that's okay." One line frames the set's difficulty directly rather than
minimizing it: `[14:05]` "Your body saying no. Your heart saying yes."

## Set 3 — Giant set: squat, Bulgarian split squat, one-leg stiff-leg deadlift (16:05–29:16)

Three exercises run back-to-back with no rest, across four escalating rounds (15 → 12 → 8 reps
twice more at increasing weight). Squat cue on knee tracking: `[17:05]` "make sure your knees don't
buckle. Let them face your big toes." Bulgarian split squat (rear foot elevated on a bench), with
an explicit limited-equipment substitute: `[17:05]` "if you don't have a bench, watch Big Mac right
there." One-leg stiff-leg deadlift, named in-source as "the ski move," with a posture analogy:
`[23:12]` "think about there's a broom going right through you, and it can go forward or
backwards" — used to cue a flat, hinge-only back line. A session-wide rule is stated mid-set:
`[23:12]` "Rule number one, stay safe... you got to have fun. You don't have fun, it's not worth
it." A no-weight regression is offered explicitly for the advanced squat variant: `[14:05]` "if you
have to do it with no weight, you know what? This workout is advanced."

## Set 4 — Calves and finisher ab work (29:16–37:19)

Standing single-leg calf raise, 30 seconds each leg, weight held close to the body: `[30:18]`
"bring your calves all the way. Extend them to the floor as low as you can. But don't touch it. Go
as high as you can." Immediately into seated calf raise, weight balanced on the knee, 30 seconds:
`[31:18]` "the weights are always in control... you want to stretch it. You want to squeeze as high
as you can." Followed by a seated ab move — leaning back, knees rolled to the chest — run for two
30-second rounds: `[33:18]` "you're going to roll your legs. You're going to lean back. And you're
going to bring your knees all the way to your chest." The block closes with a 30-second flat-bench
core roll-out: `[36:19]` "roll it out... it's all about success." Difficulty is acknowledged
directly rather than softened: `[36:19]` "It's not easy. I never said it's going to be easy."

## Stretch and cooldown (37:19–39:19)

Standing hamstring reach, straight leg forward, toes reached for, with a named regression: `[37:19]`
"if you're not flexible, bend that knee. If it's shaking, just hold it." Standing quad stretch,
knee drawn close to the body, explicitly capped by force: `[38:19]` "stretch it whatever you can.
As high as you can. Don't force it." Closes with a two-sided kneeling hip-flexor stretch, hands on
the front knee, leaning back with a paced breath: `[38:19]` "feel the soya's [psoas] right there.
Feel the hip flexors. Take a deep breath."

## Form cues and body signals addressed

- **Knee tracking is named on two separate lifts in this session**: `[17:05]` "let them face your
  big toes" (squat); a similar "don't let your knee go all the way out" cue on the Bulgarian squat
  `[18:07]`.
- **A stop-and-drop safety allowance is repeated across multiple sets**, not stated once:
  `[12:05]`, `[22:10]` "if you have to stop and drop the weights, that's okay"; "if you have to
  drop the weights, that's what you do."
- **"Too easy" is treated as a red flag rather than a compliment**: `[09:04]` "it's never too easy...
  because you don't build legs if it's too easy."
- **A no-weight regression is named explicitly for the giant set's hardest variant**: `[14:05]`
  "if you have to do it with no weight, you know what? This workout is advanced."
- **Calf-raise range is bounded at both ends**: `[30:18]` "extend them to the floor as low as you
  can. But don't touch it. Go as high as you can."
- **Stretch depth is explicitly capped by force, and a shaking limb is a hold-not-push signal**:
  `[37:19]` "if it's shaking, just hold it"; `[38:19]` "don't force it."

## Glossary terms introduced

- **"The ski move" (one-leg stiff-leg deadlift)** — the source's own name for a single-leg
  stiff-leg deadlift, bar or dumbbell brought close to the working leg with a flat back `[19:07]`.
- **Bulgarian squat** — rear foot elevated on a bench, front leg doing the work, named directly in
  the transcript `[17:05]`.
- **"Chicken legs"** — the source's own term for underdeveloped legs, framed as the session's
  starting problem to fix `[00:00]`.

## Analogies worth reusing

- **"Think about there's a broom going right through you"** `[23:12]` — a straight-back posture cue
  for hip-hinge movements, reusable for any stiff-leg or deadlift-pattern exercise.
- **"Your body saying no. Your heart saying yes."** `[14:05]` — framing mid-set difficulty as a
  motivational contrast rather than a stop signal.
- **"Success is the only option. Failure is not."** `[15:05]`, `[36:19]` — repeated verbatim across
  this session as the standard effort framing.

## Source-stated confidence

An instructional/motivational training-partner video; its evidence is Kalev's technique narration
and two training partners' (Richard and Big E/Edwin's) visible performance, not citation or
measurement:

- **Difficulty is stated directly rather than downplayed**: `[12:05]` "This is brutal... This is
  tough. This is hard."; `[36:19]` "It's not easy. I never said it's going to be easy."
- **A stop-and-drop safety allowance is repeated as standing permission, not a one-time
  exception**: `[12:05]`, `[22:10]`, `[30:18]` — offered at three separate points across three
  different exercises.
- **Individual load variation is shown directly**: `[13:05]`, `[21:09]` Richard and Kalev state
  different personal starting/ending weights on the same lifts (15→30, 20→40) rather than one
  prescribed number.

## Conflicts with other sources

Against `build-back-and-bis`[05:17] and `build-chest-and-tris`[28:19], both of which state a
priority-order rule (ego/form, or safety/form/fun) once near the start of a set, this session
states a similar three-part rule ("stay safe... you got to have fun," `[23:12]`) mid-session rather
than at the top — consistent with these being fixed program-wide conventions restated at different
points rather than session-specific. The "success only, failure is not an option" framing at
`[05:04]`/`[15:05]`/`[36:19]` sits in tension with `build-chest-and-tris`[22:18], which reframes
hitting failure as useful information ("That was failure. That's when you really know when your
body is working") rather than something to avoid outright — worth resolving once more sessions are
notated whether "failure" means muscular failure (embraced) or giving up (rejected) in each case,
since this transcript's wording doesn't disambiguate. Against RUSHFIT's `balance-and-agility`[37:01],
whose safety line is pain rather than form, this session's repeated "stop and drop the weight" offer
is again closer to a completion/load-adjustment standard than a pain threshold.
