# My Number 1 Piece Of Advice If You Are Going To Use Steroids That Nobody Will Tell You

## Central claim

**Derek rejects the standard community advice that readiness to use steroids is a function of
age (25, 30, "hormones declining") and argues instead that the real gating factor is whether a
lifter has fully mastered diet and training first — because every person has a finite, depletable
number of viable cycles before hair loss or organ damage forces them to stop.** `[00:13]` "my
biggest piece of advice before you get into a performance enhancement domain... it's not very
standard advice." `[01:15]` "the advice that's usually given is usually misguided... here's my
reason of why you shouldn't start regardless of your age until you know what the [hell] you're
doing in the kitchen in the gym." `[04:19]` "my number one piece of advice is to not start gear
until you know 100 percent what you're doing in the gym and in the kitchen."

## The stated mechanism: two depletable resources (01:15–04:19)

Derek frames long-term steroid use as bounded by exactly two irreversible failure points, not by
willpower or desire: `[01:15]` "you have 100,000 hair follicles on your head and you have one set
of organs so the only two things that are typically gonna completely knock a guy off his blasting
of gear is going bald or having a major health crisis." He states this as close to inevitable for
sustained use: `[02:18]` "one of those two things is gonna hit a stage where it's gonna force you
to either severely pull back your dosages or come off entirely... it's a ticking time bomb."

## Why "wait until 25/30" is the wrong frame (05:23–07:25)

Derek's counter-argument is that chronological age doesn't track competence: most people who
"know how to diet and train" by conventional milestones actually don't have a genuine grasp of
what's optimal for their goal — they're following forum routines (`[05:23]` "doing like illogical
things that they just like read on a forum") rather than working from real understanding. He
estimates the actual timeline to competence: `[06:24]` "even if you're a 20 year old natural and
you have like peak natural test, within two years of doing everything perfectly... you're
probably gonna hit diminishing returns," but adds that most people, including himself
retrospectively, don't actually reach that competence on schedule: `[07:25]` "I probably didn't
even have a full comprehensive understanding of diet [and] training till like a few years ago...
and I had been training for like four years prior to that."

## The regret framing (07:25–09:27)

Derek's personal stated regret is specifically about wasted cycles, not about having used steroids
at all: `[08:26]` "you wasted potential muscle gain on cycles where you had subpar diet and
training because now you're at a point where you can't blast again." He names his own outcome —
hair loss — as the resource that ran out for him: `[07:25]` "in my case it was hair loss and
fortunately... I had the willpower to stop in order to preserve what's on my head." He generalizes
this to a "don't waste the blasts" rule: `[09:27]` "you only have so many blasts in your tank
before you get to a point where you're gonna have to stop, and don't waste the blasts."

## A stated retained-benefit claim (myonuclei) (09:27–10:28)

Derek references a claim — sourced to "other videos" he's made, not elaborated here — that muscle
gained under steroid use is retained via a permanent cellular change (the term is almost certainly
"myonuclei," but the auto-caption track renders it "my own nuclei," reproduced here as captioned):
`[10:28]` "once you gain new my own nuclei in the muscle you can retain that indefinitely, and it
gives people... who aren't on peds anymore... a level of muscular cell structure... that would
otherwise be unattainable to a
natural." This is presented as settled background fact within his own body of content, not argued
or sourced in this video.

## Form cues and body signals addressed

- **Hair loss and major organ/cardiovascular crisis are named as the two involuntary stopping
  points** for sustained steroid use, not personal choice: `[01:15]`, `[02:18]`.
- **"Knowing what you're doing" is explicitly defined as pre-existing, thorough diet and training
  competence** — not a specific age, lifting experience number, or hormonal marker: `[05:23]`.
- No physical exercise technique or form cue is addressed in this video; it is entirely decision
  framework and pharmacology-adjacent commentary.

## Glossary terms introduced

- **"Blast" / "blasting"** — Derek's term for an active cycle of supraphysiological steroid dosing,
  used throughout without definition, assumed familiar to his audience `[03:18]`.
- **"Cruising"** — a lower, TRT-level maintenance dose used between blasts, mentioned but not
  defined in depth here `[03:18]`.
- **"Myonuclei" (muscle memory claim)** — cellular structures Derek claims are permanently added
  during a cycle and retained after stopping use; the auto-caption track renders the word as
  "my own nuclei" `[10:28]`.

## Analogies worth reusing

- **"Ticking time bomb"** — Derek's own recurring phrase for the accumulating, inevitable health
  or hair cost of sustained steroid use `[02:18]`, `[03:18]`.
- **The two-resource framing itself (hair follicles vs. organs)** functions as the video's
  organizing analogy for why steroid use has a hard ceiling regardless of desire `[01:15]`.

## Source-stated confidence

- **Derek explicitly hedges his own competence-timeline estimate as debatable**: `[06:24]` "some
  guys might disagree, but I think..." when proposing the roughly two-year window to reach a
  natural genetic ceiling.
- **He presents the myonuclei retention claim as an established fact from his own prior research**
  rather than arguing it in this video, and does not cite a study on camera here `[10:28]`.
- **The core argument is explicitly framed as personal retrospective regret** ("if I could go back
  in time...") rather than a universal prescription, and he twice concedes it isn't standard
  community advice `[00:13]`, `[07:25]`.
