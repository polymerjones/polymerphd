# 3D Shoulder Workout | Bro Split

## Central claim

**Seth builds an entire shoulder session around raises, rows, and shrugs with zero pressing
movements, in direct response to an acute elbow flare-up earlier that week, and demonstrates that
a complete, effective shoulder workout doesn't require pressing at all.** `[02:07]` "We're going to
do shoulders, no presses today... if you can't do raises — say your shoulder hurts, or something
hurts with presses, whether it's your elbow, your shoulder, something — but you're able to do
raises: front raises, side raises, rear delt raises in different ways, upright rows, some shrugs —
you can get one hell of a shoulder workout."

## An injury governs the day's programming, not a fixed plan (02:07)

The elbow issue is described directly, including his own initial catastrophizing and the eventual
resolution: `[02:07]` "my elbow just all of a sudden went off the rails, and I thought I did
something to it and tore my tricep again... I woke up yesterday morning [and there's] no more
pain... I'm not [pressing] today." He also states a direct rejection of masking the issue with more
supplementation: `[02:07]` "maybe if I just shove a bunch of [peptides] in there... I'm like, Seth,
that's not the answer."

## Drop set with timed rest for blood flow (05:17)

A specific rest interval is stated as deliberate, not incidental: `[05:17]` "in between sets, okay,
like whenever I go from the 65 down to the 55, I'll probably give it like an 8 to 10 second break
in between to let the blood flow to my shoulders... and then as I go, it'll get more and more
difficult, more and more pumped."

## Front raise grip position, restated (09:23)

Consistent with other notes in this library: `[09:23]` "if I grab the dumbbell all the way to the
top... it's going to hit my front delt different than if I pull my hand all the way back... it's
going to pull differently on my body. You can see it happening."

## Face pulls chosen to replicate a specific pose (15:29–17:32)

Seth explains a deliberate departure from the standard high face-pull technique, tied directly to a
competition pose rather than a stated biomechanical advantage: `[15:29]`–`[16:31]` "most people would
have this high and they pull [to] their face — which is effective, okay, it is. But... the way I
like to do them is I'll bend over, keep my head up, and I'll pull here... focus and feel and pull
this rope out with my pinkies... I lock my shoulders and I don't use any lat, I don't use any
bicep." The stated reason: `[16:31]`–`[17:32]` "whenever you set your feet in a rear double bicep, set
your hands above your head and then pull down... so the face pulls that we're doing... [with the]
head slightly up... is the same as you throwing your arms up... hitting that rear double bi[ceps]."

## Form cues and body signals addressed

- **A full shoulder session is deliberately built with zero pressing to work around an acute
  elbow issue**, demonstrating raises/rows/shrugs alone are sufficient: `[02:07]`.
- **Drop-set rest intervals are timed (8-10 seconds) specifically to let blood flow between weight
  decreases**, not an arbitrary pause: `[05:17]`.
- **A standard technique (high face pulls) is explicitly acknowledged as effective, but replaced
  with a variant chosen for posing specificity**, not because the standard version is wrong:
  `[15:29]`.

## Source-stated confidence

Seth explicitly frames the no-press format as a response to a real, unresolved physical concern,
not a planned training variation, and treats his own catastrophizing as something to correct:

- **The initial "tore my tricep again" fear is stated and then explicitly walked back** once pain
  resolved, rather than presented as a confirmed diagnosis: `[02:07]`.
- **The face-pull variation is explicitly not claimed superior to the standard technique** — both
  are acknowledged as effective, with the choice framed as personal/posing-driven: `[15:29]`.
