# Bulk Legs

## Central claim

**Sagi Kalev gives this session's target a running nickname up front — "Quadsila" — and states the
practical consequence directly (new pants needed) rather than only a training-focus statement.**
`[00:00]` "Bulk legs, here you come, Quadsila. That is your new name. That's right, because you're
gonna get big legs. No, you're gonna get huge legs. And you know what? You're gonna have to get new
pants."

## Warm-up (01:05–02:05)

Hip twists and circles, alternating reverse lunges (`[01:05]`, "shoulders back. Chest up.
Breathe"), side-to-side squats, and bodyweight sumo squats, knees tracking the toes `[02:05]`.

## Set 1 — Walking front-and-back lunge, single set (02:05–10:14)

Three escalating rounds (12 → 10 → 8 reps, weight rising each round). A named regression is offered
for balance: `[03:06]` "if you lose your balance and you are new to this exercise, you can pause for
one second and step back." The session's three-part standing rule is stated in full here: `[05:08]`
"we got three major rules here. Safety, number one. Form right with it. And last one, you got to
have fun." A stop-in-place allowance is explicit without ending the set: `[06:09]` "if you have to
stop in the center, that's all right. Take your time, but keep going."

## Set 2 — Squat, progressive set, forward then reversed (10:14–16:18)

Three weights run light-to-heavy with no rest (15 → 12 → 8), then repeated heavy-to-light (8 → 12
→ 15) — the same forward/reverse progressive-set structure seen in `bulk-back`[14:38] and
`bulk-chest`[13:20]. A specific compensation is named and corrected live: `[11:14]` "I want to see
you working your legs. Not your back," and neck/chin movement is flagged as an unwanted signal of
overload: `[12:15]` "I don't want any movement. Nothing with the neck."

## Set 3 — Sumo squat, force set (16:18–18:22)

Matches the force-set structure defined in `bulk-arms`[12:10] and confirmed in `bulk-back`[19:48]
and `bulk-chest`[11:19]: five sets of five, one fixed weight, held rather than dropped between
sets. `[16:18]` "five sets of five reps. Holding the weights. Very little rest... we're not going up
with the weight. We're not going down with the weight."

## Set 4 — EZ-bar-loaded split squat, progressive set (18:22–29:27)

Bar loaded across the back, one stationary lunge/split-squat leg at a time, each leg running its
own full light-medium-heavy-then-reversed progression (15 → 12 → 8, then 8 → 12 → 15) before
switching sides — the longest single block in this session. Form cue on range and load path:
`[19:22]` "look at my knees. Look at my toes... my back of my heel. Squeeze in my glutes." Kalev
names the exercise's reputation directly rather than downplaying it: `[19:22]` "who likes to do
legs? Not a lot of people. Why? Because it's hard."

## Set 5 — Stiff-leg deadlift / cossack squat superset (29:27–36:32)

Two exercises with no rest between them, three escalating rounds, the third a drop set on the
stiff-leg deadlift. Stiff-leg deadlift cue and its stated rationale (not just an instruction):
`[30:27]` "as you come up, you are squeezing from the bottom... that way you take the tension off
the lower back," restated again at `[32:30]` and `[35:32]`. Cossack squat depth is explicitly the
trained variable: `[31:27]` "I want you to stay low. You work in your inner thighs. You work in
your outer thighs... I don't want you to go all the way up."

## Set 6 — Single-leg calf raise and "spell the beast" ab finisher (36:32–40:34)

Fixed at 50 reps per side regardless of weight, with an explicit finish-the-count rule: `[36:32]`
"pick as heavy weight as you can. If you have to drop it, drop it as long as you finish 50." A
named list of equipment substitutes: `[39:32]` "if you need assistance, use a chair. Use a table.
Use a refrigerator." Immediately followed by the same "spell the beast" ab sequence introduced in
`beast-abs`[10:13], run forward then backward.

## Stretch and cooldown (40:34–41:35)

Standing hamstring reach, held rather than counted, explicitly capped by force: `[40:34]` "hold it
as much as you can... don't force it. Just hold it." Bench-assisted quad stretch, with an
acknowledged flexibility difference stated directly rather than smoothed over: `[40:34]` "I'm not
as flexible as you are. That's all right. Whatever you can. As long as you try." Closes with a
kneeling hip-flexor stretch, hands on the front knee, leaning back `[41:35]`.

## Form cues and body signals addressed

- **A specific compensating body part is named and corrected live**: `[11:14]`, `[12:15]` "I want
  to see you working your legs. Not your back"; "I don't want any movement. Nothing with the
  neck."
- **A hip-hinge tempo cue is paired with its own stated mechanical rationale, not left as a bare
  instruction**: `[30:27]`, `[32:30]`, `[35:32]` "as you go down, squeeze the quads. As you go up,
  squeeze the glutes. That way you take the tension off your lower back" — repeated verbatim three
  times across the session.
- **A stop-in-place allowance is explicit and does not end the set**: `[06:09]` "if you have to
  stop in the center, that's all right. Take your time, but keep going."
- **A fixed rep target overrides load selection on the calf-raise finisher**: `[36:32]`, `[39:32]`
  "if you have to drop it, drop it as long as you finish 50."
- **Individual flexibility differences are named directly during a stretch rather than implying a
  single standard**: `[40:34]` "I'm not as flexible as you are. That's all right."

## Glossary terms introduced

- **"Quadsila"** — the source's own nickname for the target of this session, a portmanteau framing
  quad growth as monstrous in scale `[00:00]`.
- **Force set** — confirmed again here matching `bulk-arms`[12:10], `bulk-back`[19:48], and
  `bulk-chest`[11:19] exactly: five sets of five, one weight, held not dropped `[16:18]`.
- **Progressive set, forward and reversed** — confirmed again here matching `bulk-back`[14:38] and
  `bulk-chest`[13:20]: the same three weights run light-to-heavy and then immediately heavy-to-light
  `[10:14]`.

## Analogies worth reusing

- **"Quadsila"** `[00:00]` — reusable shorthand for extreme size framing on any single body part.
- **"That's how winners are made."** `[31:27]` — used specifically to reframe staying low
  (harder, not easier) in the cossack squat as the actual point of the exercise.
- **"It's burning, baby. It's hurting. You know what? It's burning... that you're still alive."**
  `[23:27]` — reframing burning discomfort as evidence of effort rather than a warning sign, stated
  directly rather than implied.

## Source-stated confidence

An instructional/motivational training-partner video; its evidence is Kalev's technique narration
and two training partners' (Eric and Chris/"Smiley"'s) visible performance, not citation or
measurement:

- **A mechanical rationale is given for a tempo cue rather than asserting it as a rule with no
  reason**: `[30:27]` the hip-hinge squeeze sequence is tied explicitly to reducing lower-back
  load, not just "do it this way."
- **A stop-in-place allowance is repeated as standing permission, not a one-time exception**:
  `[06:09]`, `[12:15]` "take your time, but keep going"; a full stop is called for directly when a
  neck-strain risk is seen on camera.
- **Individual load variation is shown directly and interactively**: `[13:16]` Kalev walks Eric
  through his own planned weight sequence (25 → 20 → 15) out loud rather than assigning one number.

## Conflicts with other sources

The three-rule safety/form/fun statement at `[05:08]` matches the wording in `build-legs`[23:12],
`build-chest-and-tris`[28:19], `build-shoulders`[09:12], and `bulk-back`[11:25] closely enough to
confirm it as a fixed, verbatim program-wide rule rather than session-specific phrasing. This
session's repeated, explicit lower-back-tension rationale for the stiff-leg deadlift's squeeze
sequence (`[30:27]`) is a more developed version of the general "squeeze on top" cues given for
similar hip-hinge moves in `build-legs`'s one-leg stiff-leg deadlift (`build-legs`[19:07]) — worth
noting as the clearest stated mechanical reason for a specific cue found across the Body Beast
sessions notated so far, comparable to `bulk-back`[04:20]'s pull-over neck-tension correction and
`bulk-chest`[24:22]'s floor-lift mechanic. Against RUSHFIT's `balance-and-agility`[37:01], whose
safety line sits at pain rather than form, this session's neck-movement correction at `[12:15]` is
again a case of a Body Beast session identifying a specific injury mechanism rather than a general
form/pain distinction.
