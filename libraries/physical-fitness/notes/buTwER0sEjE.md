# Arm Training | My Current Protocol & Split

## Central claim

**Seth's arm day is structured as a straight bicep/tricep superset — four exercises per muscle
group, alternating back and forth rather than finishing one group before starting the other —
and he states this is chosen specifically for pump, not as a claimed superior hypertrophy method.**
`[00:27]` "I do a bicep set, then a tricep... I pick four exercises for biceps, four exercises for
triceps. I go bicep, tricep, bicep, tricep, bicep, tricep, back and forth the whole time, because
you get a sick, nasty pump."

## Exercise choices and their stated purpose (01:29–17:30)

The preacher curl machine is chosen specifically to remove cheating options: `[01:29]` "focus on
moving some weight with full focus on the biceps — there's not... the ability to cheat a whole
lot during this." On tricep press-downs, a specific grip is chosen for a named visual/muscular
target: `[16:29]` "I like the single-arm reverse-grip press-down, and really focus on that lateral
head... even though I can push more weight doing it like a neutral grip or a regular grip, I like
the reverse grip... so that lateral head pop[s]" — verified by checking the mirror, not just felt.

## Training frequency and periodic re-prioritization (17:30–19:33)

A specific split structure is named: `[17:30]`–`[18:33]` "I do two days on, one day off... every
fifth day I've been hitting arms... I'm not on like a seven-day split, I'm on two days on, one day
off for recovery reasons." He also states a deliberate, planned shift in priority once a body part
catches up: `[19:33]` "starting next week I'm going to prioritize back... for the next six weeks...
happy with the arm progress, [but] I think right now I need to transition into making back more of
a priority."

## Distinguishing enjoyment from effectiveness (21:35–23:35)

Seth states a specific self-monitoring principle: track what's working independently of what's
enjoyable. `[21:35]` "just because you really liked it and it was a lot of fun, if it's not
working, don't convince yourself that it is — and vice versa, just because you don't like it and
it is working, don't be like, I don't like it and it's not working... it is working, but I don't
like it." He frames continuous self-assessment (notes on food, pump, how a specific exercise felt)
as the mechanism for catching this: `[22:35]`–`[23:35]` "I keep... feel[ing] everything, like I'm
flexing in the mirror... did I like those reverse-grip machine dips... after I got done I really
like the way my triceps just feel like they're exploding."

## Form cues and body signals addressed

- **Grip choice on press-downs is verified visually (in the mirror), not just felt**, tying a
  specific grip to a specific visible muscle target: `[16:29]`.
- **A plate-loaded preacher curl machine is chosen specifically because it restricts cheating**,
  named as a deliberate strictness choice: `[01:29]`.
- **Training frequency is tied explicitly to recovery**, not maximized for volume: `[18:33]` "two
  days on, one day off, for recovery reasons."

## Analogies worth reusing

- **"Brick by brick, day by day, meal by meal, workout by workout, set by set, rep by rep"**
  `[21:35]` — his framing for the accumulation of small consistent details into meaningful
  long-term progress.

## Source-stated confidence

The bicep/tricep superset structure and single-arm press-down grip choice are both stated as
personal preference tied to a specific stylistic and visual goal, not claimed as objectively
superior programming:

- **The superset structure is justified by feel and appearance, not a claimed physiological
  advantage**: `[00:27]` "you get a sick, nasty pump... you look cool, you feel cool."
- **The reverse-grip press-down choice is explicit that it sacrifices load for a visual target**:
  `[16:29]` "even though I can push more weight doing it [differently]... I like the reverse grip."
