# Back to My Roots | Grungy Shoulder Training

## Central claim

**This session is light on technique instruction relative to most of this library's Seth Feroce
notes — most of the runtime is casual gym camaraderie and singing along to music rather than
coaching — but it does state one specific, repeatable form cue for machine lateral raises: a
slight elbow bend with a relaxed ("limp") wrist to keep tension off the elbow joint.** `[11:59]`
"The reason that I have... a little bit of bend in my arms [is]... it puts too much stress on the
elbows [without it]." `[11:59]` "a limp wrist, that's how I always [do it]... no tension [in the
grip]."

## The lateral-raise cue (10:56–12:59)

Seth walks a training partner through the same cue twice, tying it to a visual mirror check:
`[10:56]`–`[11:59]` "little bend in the arms, 'cause we're going to move a little bit of weight,
'cause we want to grow"; `[11:59]`–`[12:59]` "if you're looking at yourself in the mirror... whenever
you have a little bit of bend, all of a sudden you see how everything kind of round[s]... when I
come down, [the] rear delts start pushing and they start rounding off — that's in my head what I'm
doing."

## Form cues and body signals addressed

- **A slight, deliberate elbow bend on lateral raises is named as a joint-stress reducer**, not
  just a style preference: `[11:59]` "it puts too much stress on the elbows" without it.
- **A relaxed grip ("limp wrist") is paired with the elbow-bend cue** to keep tension off the
  forearm/elbow rather than the target shoulder muscle: `[11:59]`.
