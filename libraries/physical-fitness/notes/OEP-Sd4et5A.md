# Seth Feroce How-To: Cable Curls

## Central claim

**Seth treats the cable curl not as one exercise but as a small family of exercises generated by
changing hand spacing and body lean on the same machine, and repeatedly declines to name a single
best variation.** `[02:05]` "I grab them further apart, do them straight — here I change my grip,
same body position, same foot placement, two different exercises. Take a step back, lean a little
forward, get a good squeeze, change my hand grip — that's four different exercises I just did...
six different exercises, all in the same machine."

## Hand placement and lean as emphasis controls (01:04–02:05)

The core rule he gives is directional, not absolute: `[01:04]` "the closer you put your hands
together, the more outside of the bicep you work — you'll feel it pull. The further apart they
go, the more inside the bicep you work." Body lean is named as a second, independent variable:
`[01:04]` "if I come closer to this and pull up, constant tension; if I take one step back,
position myself a little further tilted back, I'm going to pull differently on my biceps." He is
explicit that neither is superior: `[01:04]` "is one better than the other? Not really — both
still working the biceps, they're just pulling different. One may rush blood faster into the
bicep than the other, so just pay attention and see what you like."

## Using the low-row/lat machine as a curl station (03:08–04:08)

Seth calls out doing curls on a low-row machine as a deliberate, slightly unconventional choice,
distinguishing it from squat-rack curls: `[03:08]` "this is the low row machine here, for back
typically... I'm not [that inconsiderate] for doing curls in the squat rack." A specific mechanical
caution follows from the seated position: `[03:08]`–`[04:08]` "if I lay all the way back, whenever
I go to do this I'm probably going to smack the weights, you'll hear them off of the top — so if I
do lay all the way back, I'll position myself a little bit further down so that I can get a pull."

## Form cues and body signals addressed

- **Hand spacing is a directional cue, not a fixed rule**: `[01:04]` closer hands bias outer
  bicep, wider hands bias inner bicep.
- **Body lean changes the pull independent of hand spacing**: `[01:04]` leaning back versus
  staying upright changes which part of the movement carries tension.
- **A specific equipment-contact hazard on the low-row machine**: `[03:08]`–`[04:08]` reclining too
  far causes the weight stack to strike the top of the machine; correct positioning avoids it.
- **"Pay attention to how you feel" is repeated as the actual test**, not a specific numeric or
  visual target: `[02:05]`, `[04:08]`.

## Glossary terms introduced

## Source-stated confidence

Seth is explicit, repeatedly, that this video describes personal variation and experimentation
rather than a single correct technique:

- **No hand-placement or lean variant is claimed superior**: `[01:04]`, `[02:05]` "is one better
  than the other? Not really" and "one way is not better than the other."
- **The only verification method offered is the lifter's own felt experience**: `[04:08]` "the
  only real way to understand or get a feeling for what I'm talking about here is to go in and do
  them... take the time to actually figure your body out."
