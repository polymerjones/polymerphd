# The Ultimate Dumbbell Shoulder Workout | Seth Feroce

## Central claim

**Seth states his core mental method for every bodybuilding exercise directly: visualize the
specific look or pose you're building toward before and during the set, rather than treating the
exercise as a task to complete.** `[06:32]` "Before you're doing any exercise, you have to visualize
and believe that what you're about to do is going to occur... the whole reason I did a side lateral
was for a specific reason, the whole reason I do a dumbbell press instead of a barbell press is for
a specific reason, to get a better contraction... it was just how my mind worked with it all."
`[07:33]` "everything in bodybuilding was done for a specific purpose, for a specific look, for that
muscle group, for that part of that muscle group — even down to that specific."

## The "set set" worked in full, with a stated reason for the rest interval (09:17–11:18)

Seth gives the most complete worked example of this technique in the library, including the exact
reasoning behind the specific rest duration: `[09:17]` "that last set was a set set — did the
hundred-pound dumbbells for a set of ten, cut the weight in half, and then did double the reps... 50
pound dumbbells for 20 reps... I did 10, took a 10-second break, did 5, [another] 10-second break,
5 more, got my 20 reps in." He explains why a fixed number beats an open "go to failure": `[09:17]`
"I fixated on a number rather than just saying failure, because failure is like, depending on your
mood one day, you might be... killing it, and then the next day you might just kind of dog it... and
still somewhat felt good about yourself, but knew [in] the back of your head" it wasn't a true
effort. The 10-second figure itself is deliberate: `[10:18]` "that gives me enough time to catch my
breath, maybe take a [sip] of water... and also let some blood flow to that area."

## Rear delt: a quantified range-of-motion cap (11:18–12:20)

More precise than this library's other rear-delt notes: `[12:20]` "I stick to about three-quarters,
maybe a little more, of that rep, instead of [going further]... because with the weight in my
hands, the larger... muscle groups are going to take over — a lot of my back, my traps, my
rhomboids." The face pull pairing is restated with the same pose-transition logic seen elsewhere:
`[13:21]` "I lean down like this, and rather than my head all the way down... a behind-the-neck
pull, I'll be like 50% of a face pull... I'm envisioning hitting the rear double bicep shot."

## Tricep warm-up chosen for angle variety (16:23)

Seth states a specific reason for always starting tricep work the same way: `[16:23]` "I warm up
every single tricep workout with rope press-downs — I do it simply because I can do 300 different
variations, and the name of the game is blood flow... I'm going to hit my triceps from every single
angle... so they're nice and round, and I can [then] pinpoint the parts of my tricep I want to hit."

## Skull crusher: weight behind the head for a bigger stretch (19:28)

A specific range-of-motion variant with a stated purpose: `[19:28]` "I love bringing the weight...
behind my head rather than down to my face — [it] gives you a great stretch on your long head...
whenever you're trying to build mass on your triceps." He also names the trade-off on the related
overhead single-dumbbell extension: `[19:28]` "the more weight you do, the more risk of injury
you're running" with two hands on one dumbbell versus other variants.

## One-arm press-down burnout finisher (20:57–21:59)

A specific, detailed finishing protocol: `[20:57]` "one-handed cable press-downs... start with my
left hand, do 10 reps... 10 reps with my right... come back... a little closer step, and do push-
downs... 10 this arm and then 10 this arm... then I come back and do 10 reverse-grip press-downs...
go back and forth, switching up every different variation... until I can't do any more" — using a
light weight (20 lb in this demonstration) to sustain roughly 150 total reps across variations.

## Form cues and body signals addressed

- **A fixed numeric target beats an open "go to failure" because self-assessed failure varies day
  to day**, stated as the explicit reason for the "set set" structure: `[09:17]`.
- **The 10-second rest interval in a "set set" is chosen for three specific stated reasons**:
  recovering breath, hydrating, and letting blood flow back into the muscle: `[10:18]`.
- **Rear delt range of motion is capped at approximately three-quarters of the full rep**, a more
  precise figure than given elsewhere in this library: `[12:20]`.
- **A burnout finisher alternates hand and grip variation rather than repeating one movement to
  failure**, sustaining volume across many angles: `[20:57]`.

## Glossary terms introduced

- None beyond terms already established elsewhere in this library (set set, hug a tree, French
  curl), restated here with additional precision.

## Analogies worth reusing

- **"Becoming the exercise"** `[06:32]` — Seth's own phrase for the mental visualization practice
  underlying his whole training philosophy.

## Source-stated confidence

The visualization philosophy and rest-interval reasoning are both stated as Seth's personal
method, developed through practice rather than derived from outside instruction:

- **The visualization approach is described as simply "how my mind worked"**, not asserted as the
  only valid mental approach to training: `[07:33]`.
- **The specific "three-quarters" rear-delt figure is offered as his own practiced range**, not a
  measured biomechanical optimum: `[12:20]` "I stick to about three-quarters, maybe a little more."
