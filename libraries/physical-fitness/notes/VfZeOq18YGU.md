# Seth Feroce How-To: Traps

## Central claim

**Seth credits a specific elbow-bend technique — not just heavier shrugging — for finally
building traps he says he lacked in earlier years, and uses pump (blood flow) as the in-set signal
that the technique is working.** `[00:01]` "If you look at some older pictures, I never really had
like overwhelming traps, and it was... I believe [it] to be because I just kind of figured out how
to make them fire." `[02:03]` "If you keep your arm straight, you're restricted — you can see my
traps working, but I don't get nearly the blood flow [as] when my elbows slightly bend up and push
into my ears."

## Setup and the elbow-bend technique (01:02–03:04)

Base and grip come first: `[01:02]` "you don't want to be stiff-legged straight... you want to make
sure that you got a little bounce, you got your knees bent, good base, feet planted into the
ground" and hand placement "directly in the dead center." The technique's core move: `[01:02]`
"whenever I go to do my shrugs, I slightly bend my elbows up... whenever I keep my arm straight and
just try and jam them up into my shoulders, I don't get nearly the pump." Between reps, a full
stretch is deliberately reintroduced: `[03:04]` "I want to make sure that my arms hang in the
beginning of every set... you'll see me kind of stretch my head out and stretch my neck out, my
traps, so I can feel that they're pull[ed]." Load is scaled to the point technique holds up: with
lighter weight, `[02:03]`–`[03:04]` he notes he could shrug "without even using my shoulders," and
recommends the elbow-bend cue especially matters as weight increases, moving through "the 80s...
the hundreds, the 120s, the 140s, the 150s."

## Form cues and body signals addressed

- **Pump is treated as a direct diagnostic, not a bonus feeling**: `[00:01]`, `[04:10]` "if I'm not
  getting a pump, that's a pretty good indication that I'm not doing something I should be doing"
  and, closing, "if you're not getting a pump from the exercise that you're doing, it's not
  working the way it should be."
- **Straight-arm shrugging is named as a specific failure mode that still recruits the target
  muscle but underperforms it**: `[02:03]` "you can see my traps working, but I don't get nearly
  the blood flow."
- **Base stance (soft knees, feet planted) is named before any load discussion**, establishing it
  as a precondition rather than a detail: `[01:02]`.
- **Torso angle (arching the back, chest out) is named as a further emphasis variable** on top of
  the elbow-bend cue: `[02:03]` "if I arch my back a little bit more and stick my chest out, I'm
  going to hit [them] from a different angle."

## Glossary terms introduced

- None beyond Seth's descriptive phrasing for the elbow-bend cue ("jam my shoulders into my
  ears") — not offered as a formal named technique in this video.

## Analogies worth reusing

- **"Jam my shoulders into my ears"** `[01:02]`–`[03:04]` — his repeated physical cue for the
  top-of-rep elbow-bend contraction on shrugs.

## Source-stated confidence

This is Seth's own experience-based technique change, explicitly framed as self-taught rather
than instructed or studied:

- **The technique is attributed to his own observation and adaptation, not a coach or study**:
  `[00:01]` "I watched a lot of videos of other people and then I kind of analyze their workouts as
  well, and then I kind of put my own spin on it."
- **The pump-as-signal claim is offered as his personal working heuristic**, not a measured
  physiological threshold: `[00:01]`, `[04:10]` restated twice in almost identical language,
  suggesting it's his standing rule of thumb rather than a one-off remark.
