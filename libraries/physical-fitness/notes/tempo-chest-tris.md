# Tempo Chest Tris

## Central claim

**This session applies the same counted-tempo device introduced in `tempo-back-bis`[00:00] to chest
and triceps, restating the same technical vocabulary before any lift begins.** `[00:00]` "Tempo
chest and tries. Learning how to count slow and fast. Negative, positive, eccentric, concentric.
That's how you're gonna get huge packs. That's how you're gonna work your triceps."

## Warm-up (01:03–03:04)

Arm circles and 90-degree arm rotations, then a graduated push-up progression through three grip
widths — close, medium, and wide — each run at a controlled pace rather than for speed: `[02:04]`
"everything is in control. Today's temp[o], chest and trice[ps]. Everything is in control."

## Set 1 — Flat press, tempo set (03:04–10:13)

Three rounds (15 at 6-down/6-up, 12 at 3-up/6-down, 8 at 3-down/3-up), weight rising each round.
The session's stated rationale for the whole tempo approach: `[05:07]` "that's how you learn to
control the weight and not the weight controlling you because it's all about safety. It's all
about your form and it's all about getting results." Continuous, non-paused motion is named
explicitly as part of correct execution: `[05:07]` "it's a very, very one-motion weight... there's
no pop, pop, pop." Followed by a figure-four bench crunch ab finisher, 10 reps each side, reusing
the "apple underneath your chin" cue from `beast-abs`[01:09] `[10:13]`.

## Set 2 — Incline press, tempo set (11:15–18:20)

Same three-round tempo pattern. A limited-equipment note goes further than usual, naming the
substitute as objectively harder rather than merely equivalent: `[21:22]` "he makes it look easy.
But you know what? It's actually way harder because you have to stabilize it. You have to work
more muscles." Followed by the "cricket crunch" — a reverse-plank, single-leg-resisted crunch,
introduced with a running joke about its age: `[18:20]` "this move... about 100 years ago... this
is a 100 years old exercise." 10 reps each side.

## Set 3 — Incline fly, tempo set (19:21–26:27)

Same three-round tempo pattern. A specific postural target is stated rather than left implicit:
`[22:23]` "we want to stand proud. We want the chest to stick out." A named form correction:
`[22:23]` "your arms are just too loose. They don't do nothing besides grabbing the weight." Followed
by a held-plank finisher using the same 6-up/6-down oscillation as the main lifts rather than a
static hold, 10 reps `[26:27]`–`[28:30]`.

## Set 4 — Lying EZ-bar triceps extension ("crush"), tempo set (29:30–35:45)

The session's three-part rule is restated in full: `[29:30]` "rule number one, safety. Rule number
two, [your] form. Rule number three, [it] gets huge as you have fun." Isolation cue: `[30:36]`
"elbows in... you want to keep it in because we want to work just the triceps. We've done enough
of the chest." A specific comparison to load-independent difficulty is made directly: `[31:37]`
"even if it's not the heaviest weight, just the resistance on going on negative and positive."

## Set 5 — Seated crunch and a kickback/triceps-extension superset (36:45–48:18)

An EZ-bar-weighted seated crunch is run first (10 reps, 3-up/3-down), with an explicit
completion-over-load rule: `[36:45]` "if you have to drop the weights, go lighter, that's all
right... as long as you give me 10 reps." This is followed by a superset pairing a one-arm triceps
kickback with a second triceps movement done seated with legs extended — its exact form is not
fully resolvable from this transcript, which calls it "beast triceps" and later "the beast's force,"
naming it as Kalev's own favorite in this session: `[40:47]` "this is the beast's force. I love
this force. My favorite force." An explicit equipment-substitute list is given for whatever bench
or support this move uses: `[40:47]` "you can use a chair. You can use a table. Or you can sit on
your mama or your grandma."

## Cooldown finisher — plank side-to-side knee-tuck twist (49:28–51:41)

A plank-based twist, knee driven in and held for a 6-count on each side, 10 reps total: `[49:28]`
"we're going to a plank with side, side, twist. Knee underneath. Go in one side. Go on to the other
side. Six, hold. Six, hold."

## Stretch and cooldown (51:41–52:46)

Standing side-turn stretch with breathing explicitly counted rather than just paced: `[51:41]` "one,
two, three, four, five, six" on the turn, capped by a no-force rule: "don't force it." Closes with
a standing cross-hands shoulder stretch, chin dropped, shoulders rolled `[52:46]`.

## Form cues and body signals addressed

- **Continuous, unpaused motion is named as part of correct tempo execution, not just the count
  itself**: `[05:07]` "it's a very, very one-motion weight... there's no pop, pop, pop."
- **A limited-equipment substitute is explicitly named as harder, not just adequate**: `[21:22]`
  "it's actually way harder because you have to stabilize it."
- **A specific postural target (not just a muscle name) defines correct form**: `[22:23]` "we want
  to stand proud. We want the chest to stick out."
- **Isolation is checked by naming what work is already done and should not be repeated**:
  `[30:36]` "we want to work just the triceps. We've done enough of the chest."
- **A completion-over-load rule is repeated at a session-ending set**: `[36:45]` "if you have to
  drop the weights, go lighter, that's all right... as long as you give me 10 reps."

## Glossary terms introduced

- **"Cricket crunch"** — the source's own name for a reverse-plank, single-leg-resisted crunch,
  introduced with a joke about its age `[18:20]`.
- **Figure-four abs** — a bench crunch with one leg crossed over the other, raised leg locked in
  position, reusing the "apple underneath your chin" cue from `beast-abs`[01:09] `[10:13]`.
- **"The beast's force"** — named as Kalev's own favorite move in this session, a seated/braced
  triceps movement whose exact mechanics are not fully resolvable from this transcript `[40:47]`.

## Analogies worth reusing

- **"Squeeze your chest so hard that I can bust a nickel on it."** `[07:12]` — a concrete,
  physical-object cue for maximal contraction, distinct from more abstract "squeeze hard" phrasing
  used elsewhere.
- **"Failure will bring you to that promise land."** `[53:47]` — the session's closing line,
  reframing failure as instrumental rather than something to avoid, in the same spirit as
  `lucky-7`[14:03]'s "he's not a quitter. He's not a failure. He keeps going."

## Conflicts with other sources

This session confirms the numeric tempo device introduced in `tempo-back-bis`[03:00] is a
program-wide technique applied across muscle groups, not specific to back and biceps — the same
three-round structure (6/6 → tightened ratio → 3/3, reps falling as weight rises) appears here
applied to pressing and triceps movements. The rule restatement at `[29:30]` matches
`bulk-legs`[05:08], `bulk-back`[11:25], and other sessions closely enough to confirm it as fixed,
verbatim program-wide phrasing. This session's closing line (`[53:47]`, "failure will bring you to
that promise land") is the clearest short-form statement of the failure-reframing theme also seen
in `lucky-7`[14:03] and `build-legs`[05:04] — worth treating these three as the same underlying
program stance restated across sessions rather than independent claims. Against RUSHFIT's
`balance-and-agility`[37:01], whose safety line sits at pain rather than form, this session's
stated tempo rationale (`[05:07]`, controlling the weight rather than being controlled by it) is a
mechanistic safety argument distinct from RUSHFIT's pain-threshold framing.
