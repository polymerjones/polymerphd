# Total Body

## Central claim

**Sagi Kalev names this session's structure directly as circuit training — alternating lower- and
upper-body exercises within the same session rather than grouping by muscle group — and attributes
three distinct outcomes to that alternation specifically.** `[00:00]` "This is when you do circuit
trading [training]. You work your lower body and upper body. You work your legs, then you work
your back. You work your hamstrings, and then you work your chest... that's how you burn fat,
increase muscle mass, and get more energized."

## Warm-up (01:02–02:08)

Jog, shoulder rolls both directions, and walk-out-to-plank holds — the same warm-up family used
across other Body Beast sessions, run at a lighter dose appropriate to a circuit format.

## Round 1 — band row, push-up, squat, crunch (02:08–06:21)

Four exercises run through in sequence at 15 reps each, no grouping by body part: a prone
resistance-band row (`[02:08]`, "his leg on the ground is lifting his chest off the floor... the
band's going all the way back"), push-ups, a squat run for maximum reps at a chosen weight rather
than a fixed load (`[03:12]`, "I want you to pick as heavy weights you can. So we got 15... if it
was too easy, next round you go up"), and a crunch reusing the "apple underneath your chin" cue
introduced in `beast-abs`[01:09] (`[04:16]`). The sequence repeats a second time with heavier squat
weight and an explicit full-range-of-motion cue on the band row: `[05:17]` "I wanted to make sure
that you are letting the band to stretch, that way you have a full range of motion all the way in
and out."

## Round 2 — incline press, bent-over row, reverse lunge, floor twist (06:21–15:40)

Incline press (`[07:21]`, "controlling it all the way down slow up"), bent-over row with a named
neck-safety cue (`[09:25]`, "drop your chin down right there, buddy. You don't want to strain your
neck"), alternating reverse lunges with a knee-tracking check (`[10:26]`, "make sure your knees are
not passing your toes"), and a floor Russian twist with an explicit rationale connecting the move to
injury prevention: `[11:28]` "we making sure we strengthen the core... that's the connection from
upper body [to] lower body. If you don't have a strong core, you can't connect, you can't get
results, you're gonna get hurt[,] and we don't want that." The full sequence repeats a second time
at increased weight.

## Round 3 — shoulder press, side raise, stiff-leg deadlift, Russian twist (15:40–27:06)

Shoulder press run as a single-single-double combo — the same pattern used for the hammer curl in
`build-back-and-bis`[35:41], here applied to shoulders: `[15:40]` "shoulder press. Single, single,
double." Standing side raise, with a stability rationale for a seated/braced variant: `[18:46]`
"that's why you work the core so you will not fall down." Stiff-leg deadlift, cued the same way as
in `bulk-legs`[30:27]: `[19:49]` "squeeze your quads... come up from the glutes right to the
hamstring." Floor Russian twist. A specific correction ties technique to weight selection: `[20:53]`
"if you are not [twisting under control], then you're using too much weight." The full sequence
repeats a second time.

## Curl-kickback-calf-plank block (27:06–37:35)

Curl with a rotation added on the way down (`[27:06]`, "curl it up. Turn it. Bring it slow down");
one-arm triceps kickback with a wrist-position caution: `[30:11]` "don't let your wrist rotate...
keep it straight"; standing calf raise, described end to end: `[30:11]` "from the bottom to the top.
From your tip toes all the way to your forehead"; and a side plank explicitly tied to a specific
external motivation: `[31:19]` "you want to look good in your baby suit? That's what you do." The
whole four-exercise block repeats a second time on the opposite side/arm.

## Stretch and cooldown (37:35–39:39)

Standing shoulder stretch (`[37:35]`, "we are focusing right here from the back of the shoulder
going to the lat"), a cross-hands chest/shoulder stretch, a standing hamstring reach with a
breath-paced no-force rule (`[38:36]`, "breathe from your nose... don't force it"), and a closing
kneeling hip-flexor stretch `[38:36]`.

## Form cues and body signals addressed

- **A core-strength rationale is stated as connecting upper and lower body, with a named
  consequence for skipping it**: `[11:28]` "if you don't have a strong core, you can't connect,
  you can't get results, you're gonna get hurt."
- **A specific neck-safety cue is given on the bent-over row**: `[09:25]` "you don't want to strain
  your neck. You just want to work their [your] upper body back."
- **Excess load is diagnosed by a specific visible tell (loss of twist control), not just
  difficulty**: `[20:53]` "if you are not [in control], then you're using too much weight" — the
  same diagnostic device used in `bulk-back`[25:51].
- **A rep target is explicitly adjustable both up and down based on difficulty**: `[21:56]` "if you
  didn't finish 15, go lighter. If it was too easy, go heavier."
- **Knee tracking is checked on the reverse lunge**: `[10:26]` "make sure your knees are not
  passing your toes."

## Glossary terms introduced

- **Circuit training** — the source's own name for this session's structure: alternating exercises
  across upper and lower body within one continuous sequence, rather than grouping sets by muscle
  group as in the other Body Beast sessions notated so far `[00:00]`.
- **Curl with a twist** — a bicep curl with an added forearm rotation on the way down, distinct
  from the plain curl and hammer-curl variants used elsewhere `[27:06]`.

## Analogies worth reusing

- **"You want to get that booty out and you want to stretch the hamstring."** `[13:37]` — plain,
  outcome-first phrasing for a stiff-leg-deadlift-family cue.
- **"That's from playing the piano for a long time."** `[27:06]` — a lighthearted analogy for
  ab-work-induced definition ("ripples"), distinct from the program's more common "beast" framing.

## Source-stated confidence

An instructional/motivational training-partner video; its evidence is Kalev's technique narration
and two training partners' (Brian and Nick's) visible performance, not citation or measurement:

- **The session's core claim (fat loss + muscle gain + energy from alternating body parts) is
  stated as a direct consequence of the circuit structure itself**, not attributed to any single
  exercise or measured independently: `[00:00]` the three outcomes are named together as "that's
  how" the structure works, without further evidence given in this source.
- **A completion-over-load rule recurs across the whole session**: `[12:34]`, `[21:56]` "if you
  can't make it, that's all right. Write it down. Drop it. Keep going"; "if you didn't finish 15, go
  lighter."
- **Individual load variation is shown directly**: `[06:21]` Nick and Brian state different personal
  weights on the same lift (40 vs. 35) rather than one prescribed number.

## Conflicts with other sources

This is the second Body Beast session (after `lucky-7`) built around a structural device outside
the eight named set types from `basics`[01:01] — "circuit training" here, the ascending pyramid in
`lucky-7`[03:02] — suggesting the program uses named set types (single, super, giant, drop,
progressive, force, combo, multi) as its default vocabulary but is not limited to them for whole
distinct session formats. The single-single-double combo pattern confirmed here on shoulder press
matches `build-back-and-bis`[35:41]'s hammer-curl example and `bulk-chest`[20:21]'s fly-press
example, reinforcing that "combo set" means "multiple components counted as one rep" generally
rather than one fixed exercise pairing. The core-strength-prevents-injury rationale at `[11:28]`
echoes `bulk-legs`[30:27]'s lower-back-tension rationale and `tempo-back-bis`[33:34]'s
muscle-memory rationale, adding to a small cluster of sessions that state a specific mechanical
reason for a safety cue rather than treating safety as a bare rule. Against RUSHFIT's
`balance-and-agility`[37:01], whose safety line sits at pain rather than form, this session's
"weight is too heavy if you lose twist control" diagnostic (`[20:53]`) is a concrete, observable
substitute for a pain threshold.
