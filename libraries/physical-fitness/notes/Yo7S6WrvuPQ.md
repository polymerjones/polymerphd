# Seth Feroce How-To: Rear Delts

## Central claim

**Seth's core correction is counterintuitive on its face — a shorter range of motion, not a longer
one, on rear delt raises — because the rear delt is small enough that a full range of motion
recruits larger muscles instead.** `[01:02]` "Your rear delts are so small that why in the world do
I need a range of motion that is flapping like a bird? ... whenever I go all the way up, now my
side delts are going to start coming into play, my traps are going to start coming into play, and
even my lats will start coming into play... leaving your rear delts not really getting hit that
hard."

## Range of motion and load (01:02–03:06)

He states a direct trade-off between range of motion and weight: `[01:02]` "with a shorter range of
motion you're going to end up using a little bit heavier weight... whenever you use a lighter
weight, we all have a tendency to have a longer range of motion. We want short range of motion, we
want small bursts." Typical loads named for this: `[02:04]` "seated I hang around like the 40s,
45s, and whenever I'm standing I'll hang around the 70s or the 80s." He verifies the technique by
felt muscle activation rather than a visual range cue: `[02:04]` "this is such a small range of
motion, but I can feel my rear delts firing a whole lot more than as if I was all the way up."

## Hand placement and rotation as a second lever (02:04–04:07)

As in his other shoulder videos, grip position changes the angle of pull: `[02:04]`–`[03:06]` "if I
place my hands dead center... I'm going to hit them, but if I... kick my hands all the way back in
the dumbbell, turn my hands towards me so the palms face me... it's hitting a different part of
your rear delt." He names a specific compensation to watch for when pulling too far back: `[04:07]`
"if I pull all the way back, I just felt my lats fire — I don't want my lats firing during my rear
delt workout, I want all rear delt, all the time, short range of motion."

## Form cues and body signals addressed

- **A felt-activation check substitutes for a visual range-of-motion target**: `[02:04]` "I can
  feel my rear delts firing" is the stated verification, not a specific joint angle.
- **Larger neighboring muscles (lats, side delts, traps) taking over is named as the specific
  failure this shortened range of motion prevents**: `[01:02]`, `[04:07]`.
- **Base and posture on the standing/bent-over variant**: `[04:07]` "slightly bend over, stick your
  ass out, have a good base, make sure your feet are planted into the ground."
- **Hand placement can change mid-set without stopping**: `[04:07]` "there's nothing stopping you
  from... go[ing] from this hand placement to this hand grip while you're doing [the exercise] in
  the same set."

## Glossary terms introduced

- None beyond his descriptive phrase "game of inches" / "game of angles," used loosely rather than
  as a formal coined term `[03:06]`, `[05:09]`.

## Analogies worth reusing

- **"Don't flap like a bird"** `[01:02]`, `[05:09]` — his repeated shorthand for the over-long
  range of motion he's correcting on rear delt raises.

## Source-stated confidence

Seth frames his rear-delt correction as a personally hard-won lesson from years of doing the
exercise wrong, not as an outside-verified technique:

- **He states directly that he lacked rear delt development until changing this technique**:
  `[00:01]` "probably about six, seven years ago I finally started training them right... because I
  was making every common amateur mistake that so many of us make."
- **The load/growth claim is stated as his general shoulder-training philosophy rather than a
  measured result specific to rear delts**: `[05:09]` "it's not like you're going to use a
  ridiculous amount of weight to work a muscle group that big to get it enormous — it wants blood
  and it wants nutrients."

## Conflicts with other sources

Against `ALNyDCkW9y8` (front raises) and the cable-curl/curl notes, this is the first
physical-fitness note where the emphasis-preserving strategy is explicitly a *shorter* range of
motion rather than hand placement or elbow bend alone — worth flagging as a genuinely distinct
technique rather than a restatement, since the other shoulder/arm notes in this set do not
recommend deliberately truncating range of motion.
