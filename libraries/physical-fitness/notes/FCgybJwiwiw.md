# Shoulder Training | Seth Feroce

## Central claim

**Seth gives a precise, correctable form cue on side laterals: the arm must lead the dumbbell
upward, not the other way around, because letting the dumbbell rise ahead of the arm shifts load
onto the shoulder joint instead of the target muscle.** `[02:02]` "Always keeping your arm higher
than the dumbbells — if the dumbbell comes up higher, you're going to put too much negative stress
on your shoulder, on the joints, and it won't be [on the] tension on the muscle. So it's always
[that] your arm is leading it up, don't put the dumbbell up here."

## Side laterals spaced across the whole session (02:02, 04:20)

Rather than doing side laterals once, Seth recommends distributing them throughout the workout:
`[02:02]` "if your shoulders still suck, do them in the beginning, and do them at the end, and do
them in the middle — do them all the time, don't stop doing them." He ties this to alternating with
pressing movements for continuous pump: `[04:20]` "you're doing your side laterals and then moving
into a pressing movement, and then coming back to side laterals, because we want to constantly...
continue to push blood."

## Form cues and body signals addressed

- **A specific, checkable form error is named on side laterals**: letting the dumbbell rise ahead
  of the arm shifts stress from muscle tension onto the shoulder joint `[02:02]`.
- **Side laterals are distributed across a session** (beginning, middle, end) rather than performed
  once, specifically to sustain blood flow to the shoulders: `[02:02]`–`[04:20]`.
- **Progress on a stubborn body part is stated as cumulative, not achievable in one workout**:
  `[05:22]` "this doesn't happen overnight or from one workout... continuously pushing... moving blood
  using the weight, not just moving the weight to move it, but feeling the actual muscles activate."
