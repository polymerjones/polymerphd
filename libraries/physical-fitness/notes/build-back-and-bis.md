# Build Back & Bis

## Central claim

**Sagi Kalev names the specific back muscles this session targets before the first set — rhomboids,
traps, lats — and both heads of the biceps, framing the visible goal as a V-taper physique rather
than a generic "back and arms" day.** `[00:00]` "We work in the back, the rhombus, the traps, the
lats. We work in the short part of the biceps, the long part of the biceps... when you're gonna
have that V shape, that V-tape, when you're gonna see your lats sticking out of your shirt, you're
gonna go, Yes, I did it."

## Warm-up (01:16–03:16)

Light jog, arm circles ("a lot of blood going to the shoulder... we're gonna use them a lot in the
workout" `[01:16]`), walk-out-to-plank holds, then three light-weight movement rehearsals that
mirror the session's main lifts: a light deadlift (`[02:16]`, "shoulders back, chest up"), a light
bent-over row (`[02:16]`–`[03:16]`, "keep the weights parallel to the floor"), and a light reverse
fly (`[03:16]`, "glutes out... don't let nothing else move besides raising your elbows up").

## Set 1 — Deadlift, single set (03:16–08:17)

Escalating weight across rounds, capped with a drop set. Round one, 15 reps, form cue: `[04:16]`
"bend down. Keep your chest up. Shoulders back. Glutes out... come up. Squeeze your rhombus.
Squeeze your traps." A named principle governs load selection: `[05:17]` "keep your ego outside the
door. Keep your form. If you can't do it, drop the weight." Round two drops to 12 reps at heavier
weight; round three is a drop set — 8 reps at 50 lb with no rest, straight into a lighter-weight set
— stated explicitly: `[06:17]` "Remember drop set. Eight. Drop to eight. No rest."

## Set 2 — Pull-over / pull-up superset (08:17–15:18)

Dumbbell pull-over on a stability ball (head and neck supported on the ball, weight brought from
overhead down to the chest and back) superset directly with pull-ups, no rest between the two
exercises. Isolation cue on the pull-over: `[09:18]` "trying to isolate the triceps and just the
lat... he's not going too low to take the tension of the shoulders. All lat." A limited-equipment
substitute is repeated at nearly every exercise in this session: `[09:18]`, `[10:18]`, `[12:18]`,
`[17:21]`, `[20:21]`, `[21:22]`, "if you have limited equipment, watch Nick" (dumbbells in place of
a barbell or an EZ bar). Pull-up reps run 10, then 12 at heavier load, then a final 10-rep set,
each paired with a corresponding pull-over set at increasing weight (20s → 40s).

## Set 3 — Giant set: bent-over EZ-bar row, one-arm row, seated reverse fly (15:18–27:30)

Three exercises run back-to-back with no rest, repeated for four total rounds at escalating weight
(15 reps down to 8 reps as weight increases). Bent-over EZ-bar row form: `[16:18]` "bend down...
bring the bar right underneath your knees and bring it all the way to the middle of your
midsection." One-arm dumbbell row, braced on a bench: `[17:21]` "my arms on the bench. Keep your
chest up. Extend the other leg out." Seated reverse fly: `[19:21]` "sit on the bench. Feet at 90
degrees. Chest leaning forward. Keep it up straight back and extend your arms up... keep your chin
down," with an explicit isolation check: `[23:27]` "I'm not moving my body. Only my arms coming
up... work on the back of the shoulders, going all the way to the traps and the rhomboids."

## Set 4 — Underhand-grip chin-ups, 30-second rounds (27:30–30:33)

Named directly as Kalev's own favorite move in the session: `[27:30]` "Set number 4. This is my
favorite move." Three 30-second max-effort rounds, form emphasis on elbow path rather than pure
rep count: `[27:30]` "bringing his elbows in. We work in the lower lats. Upper lats. We work in the
whole back," with an explicit pace correction: `[28:31]` "it's about getting a big back. It's not
about speeding up. Stay with the form."

## Set 5 — Bicep curl, single set with drop set (30:33–34:38)

Supinating curl, seated on a stability ball for stability challenge, escalating weight across three
rounds (15 → 12 → 8 reps) with the third round a drop set. Named target framing: `[31:34]` "This is
the gun show. We're going to blast it. We're going to build the biceps all the way." Form cue on
elbow position: `[32:36]` "Elbows down. You don't want to raise them up. This is biceps. Anything
else."

## Set 6 — Hammer curl, combo set (34:38–40:45)

A "single, single, double" combo pattern — one rep each arm individually, then one rep both arms
together, counted as a single repetition of the set: `[35:41]` "Single. Single. Double... that was
one rep." The long head of the biceps gets an in-source nickname here: `[35:41]` "this is
everything you work in the long part of the biceps. There you go. We call it the broccoli." Weight
escalates across three rounds (15 → 12 → 8 reps).

## Set 7 — Close-grip EZ-bar curl, single set into drop set (40:45–46:51)

Standing curl, close/reverse grip, chest up, minimal elbow drift: `[41:45]` "elbows back, tight.
Chest up. Slightly bend your knees and come all the way up without raising your elbows." A
completion-optional safety line is stated directly rather than implied: `[41:45]` "no matter what
weight you use, it's okay if you can't finish the reps." Three rounds, escalating weight, closing
in a drop set (12 → 8 reps).

## Set 8 — Cobra, two 30-second rounds (46:51–48:54)

A back/core finisher done prone on the floor rather than with weights: `[46:51]` "lie flat right
there. You want to keep your chest up. Keep your arms out. And you want to open your hands and
your fingers all the way out... we're opening the chest and we're stretching, squeezing on the
lower back." Nutrition is folded directly into the coaching cue between rounds: `[47:51]` "Stay
with it. Take your supplements. Eat what's on the plan."

## Stretch sequence (48:54–50:54)

Standing lat stretch, arm extended overhead, wrist grabbed and pulled into a side bend, breathing
paced explicitly: `[48:54]` "Breathe in. Breathe out." A bench-assisted lat stretch follows, elbow
dropped and hand brought behind the head while leaning into the stretch, with a stated tolerance
rule: `[49:54]` "Stretch it. Feel it. Don't force it. It has to be in the right tolerance." Closes
with a standing shoulder/neck stretch, fingers crossed in front, chin dropped on two paced breaths
`[50:54]`.

## Form cues and body signals addressed

- **"Keep your ego outside the door" is stated as the governing rule for weight selection**:
  `[05:17]` explicitly framed as more important than hitting a target weight.
- **Rep completion is explicitly not required for safety's sake**: `[41:45]` "it's okay if you
  can't finish the reps."
- **A dedicated limited-equipment substitute is named at nearly every exercise**, not just once:
  `[09:18]`, `[10:18]`, `[12:18]`, `[17:21]`, `[20:21]`, `[21:22]` — dumbbells in place of a
  barbell/EZ bar throughout.
- **Isolation is checked by what does *not* move, not just by what does**: `[09:18]` "trying to
  isolate the triceps and just the lat"; `[23:27]` "I'm not moving my body. Only my arms coming
  up."
- **A stretch's depth is capped by tolerance, not by a fixed count**: `[49:54]` "Don't force it. It
  has to be in the right tolerance."
- **Load is explicitly written down as a tracking discipline**: `[20:21]` "make sure you write the
  weights. That's what I used to do all the time until I got it right" — repeated after nearly
  every set.

## Glossary terms introduced

- **Giant set** — three (or more) exercises performed back-to-back with no rest between any of
  them, distinct from a superset's two `[15:18]`.
- **Drop set** — finishing a lift's last round at a reduced weight immediately, with no rest,
  stated explicitly each time it's used: `[06:17]` "Remember drop set. Eight. Drop to eight. No
  rest."
- **Combo set** — one repetition defined as single-arm reps on each side followed by a
  simultaneous both-arms rep, demonstrated on the hammer curl as "single, single, double"
  `[35:41]`.
- **"The broccoli"** — the source's own nickname for a fully flexed, peaked long head of the
  biceps `[35:41]`.
- **Dynamic set training** — reused from `basics`[01:01]; this session is the first to actually
  demonstrate its named set types (single, super, giant, drop, combo) in sequence rather than list
  them.

## Analogies worth reusing

- **"I'm building a house here... that's a high rise"** `[44:48]` — Kalev's own framing of
  progressively heavier drop-set weight as construction, reusable for any escalating-load segment.
- **"That's the smell of getting huge"** `[36:41]` — muscular pump described in sensory rather than
  purely visual terms.
- **John's origin story as a motivational aside**: `[38:45]` "John was a skinny, skinny boy when he
  was young... he said, I'm going to get bigger... and that's what happened" — used to connect a
  visible physique on screen to a stated multi-year effort, not a quick result.

## Source-stated confidence

An instructional/motivational training-partner video; its evidence is the coach's technique
narration and two training partners' (John and Nick) visible performance, not citation or
measurement:

- **Safety is stated as a standing rule repeated at nearly every new exercise, not a one-time
  disclaimer**: `[16:18]`, `[41:45]`, `[44:48]` "always, always make sure you got the safety zone";
  "it's okay if you can't finish the reps"; "now I got to keep it safe."
- **Individual load variation is shown, not asserted**: `[05:17]` Kalev, John, and Nick each name
  different personal weights on the same lift (20s/40s/42s) rather than a single prescribed number.
- **Confidence-building is named as a stated side effect, conditioned on time**: `[44:48]` "you can
  lift this weight. You can do anything you want. That's how you build confidence. But you know
  what? It took me a long time to get here."

## Conflicts with other sources

No other Body Beast strength session has been notated yet to compare set structure against.
Against `basics`, which lists "single sets, supersets, drawing sets, progressive sets, drop sets,
force sets, combo sets, and multi sets" as the full roster (`basics`[01:01]), this session
demonstrates only five of those eight named types (single, super, giant, drop, combo) — worth
checking whether "giant set" as used here is the source's synonym for what `basics` calls a
"multi set," since `basics` does not separately define either term, or whether the remaining
named types (drawing, progressive, force) appear in later Body Beast sessions not yet notated.
Against RUSHFIT's `balance-and-agility`[37:01], whose safety line is drawn at pain rather than
form, this session's safety framing ("it's okay if you can't finish the reps," `[41:45]`) is closer
to a completion-optional standard than a pain-threshold one — a difference worth tracking as more
Body Beast sessions are added.
