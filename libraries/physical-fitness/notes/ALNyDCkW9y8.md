# Seth Feroce How-To: Front Raises

## Central claim

**Seth treats the front raise, like his other shoulder work, as one movement made into many
through hand placement, body angle, and hand rotation, always kept light and capped below eye
level — while explicitly flagging one of his own advanced variants as something he personally
avoids because of a shoulder click.** `[00:01]` "It's all about hand placement and angles, body
position... each hand placement is going to hit your front delt a little bit different — it's
going to pull a little bit different 'cause the weight is distributed differently in your hand."

## Range of motion and hand placement (01:02–03:05)

A hard ceiling on height is stated directly: `[01:02]` "you will never see me go all the way up
here — I believe at that point all of the weight now comes off of the muscle group that I want it
to hit... the top of the dumbbell will never go higher than my eyeball." Elbow position is
slightly bent, not locked: `[01:02]` "my arms are slightly bent, they are not completely straight,
so that I can control the weight a little bit more." Hand placement — center, forward, or back —
is named as a distinct emphasis variable in the same way as his curl videos: `[02:04]`–`[03:05]`
"if your hands were placed... all the way back... you're going to feel it hit your front delt
different than [if] your hands were placed directly in the center."

## An advanced variant he flags as personally risky (03:05–06:09)

He introduces a shoulder-dip technique, explicitly labels it advanced, and then discloses his own
adverse reaction to it: `[03:05]`–`[04:06]` "if you dip your shoulder down and pull up a little bit
more, this is... a little more of an advanced technique... I'm able to get a better squeeze at the
top." But immediately after: `[05:08]` "whenever I do these, every time I do this I can feel my
left shoulder click[ing] — [that's] not a good thing... I don't see a reason to do it." He
generalizes this into a standing rule rather than presenting his own case as unique: `[05:08]`
"whenever you start to change the motion of your hands, if you have bad shoulders or it doesn't
feel like it's doing the right thing 'cause it hurts, it's probably not a good idea to do [it]."

## Form cues and body signals addressed

- **A personal joint-click is named directly and treated as a stop signal for that specific
  variant**: `[05:08]` "I can feel my left shoulder click — [that's] not a good thing... I don't
  see a reason to do it."
- **A general compensation rule closes the video**: `[06:09]` "if you feel another body part take
  over more of the weight than your front delts, stop doing the exercise."
- **Height is capped at eye level as a standing rule, not a one-time cue**: `[01:02]`.
- **Load is kept deliberately light throughout**, tied to the movement's isolation purpose:
  `[03:05]` "you can't really do heavy front delts... we want to rush as much blood into those
  front delts as possible, and the only way to do it is through different angles, different hand
  placement."

## Glossary terms introduced

- **Pronate / supinate (hand rotation)** — named as an additional emphasis-shifting variable on
  front raises alongside hand placement and body angle `[06:09]`.

## Analogies worth reusing

- **"Rather than just stand there and do these shitty, same thing over and over"** `[06:09]` — his
  framing for why varying hand placement/body position across sets of the same exercise beats
  repeating one variant.

## Source-stated confidence

Seth is explicit that the shoulder-dip variant is his own instinctive adoption, not a technique he
verified is safe for other people, and states his own limitation candidly rather than omitting it:

- **The advanced variant is attributed to instinct and feel, not study**: `[04:06]` "I just adopted
  this, you know, from training and just getting a feel, because I'm very instinctive with my
  training — if I'm doing something and I don't feel it, I move on right away."
- **His own adverse response to that variant is disclosed rather than smoothed over**: `[05:08]`
  the shoulder click is stated as his own experience, generalized into a caution for anyone with
  shoulder issues, not withheld to make the technique look uniformly safe.
