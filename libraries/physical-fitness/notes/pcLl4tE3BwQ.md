# Old School Back Workout | Time to Get Huge

## Central claim

**Seth structures this session around a single repeated idea — where you pull to, and how your
elbow is positioned, determines which part of the back gets trained, regardless of which pulling
exercise you're doing — and applies it consistently across pull-downs, rows, and rack pulls.**
`[08:10]` "How you pull, where you pull to, is going to dictate what part of the back it hits
more of."

## Pull-down target point: lower lats vs. upper back (05:08–08:10)

`[06:09]` "Whenever you're doing pull-downs... where you bring the bar and how you position your
body [is] going to play a giant role in what part of the back it hits. If you want to target the
upper back, you're going to bring it right below your chin and into your chest. [To] target the
lower lats... shoot for right... at your nipple line, so that way when it's down in front of you,
you start to engage those lower lats." Hand spacing is a second, named variable: `[07:10]` "you go
out wider, you're not going to have the same type of range of motion than if you go closer... I
personally like to put my hands about a thumb length away from the bend in the bar." He is
explicit that there's no single scientifically mandated technique, only what a given lifter can
feel and repeat: `[07:10]` "is there a right way? I'm sure there's some [study] out there telling
you exactly what you're supposed to do to get big lats — somehow you do it and still don't get big
lats, that's 'cause you're not pulling right and you don't actually feel [it]... you need
one-on-one interaction to make [sure] you're doing it the way that works best for your body."

## One-arm dumbbell/iso rows: posting angle and elbow drift (13:27–17:30)

Seth states a personal dislike for head-on-bench rowing, framed as a fit issue for him
specifically rather than a flaw in the technique: `[13:27]` "I personally don't like those, because
whenever I do it I cannot connect with my back... I've tried to do them numerous times and it just
don't work for me." Posting height and angle change loading on adjacent joints, not just back
emphasis: `[14:27]` "whenever you start moving some serious weight, if you're this far on here and
you're yanking, dude, [a] lot of stress is going on my elbow and my tricep." Within a single set,
elbow position is allowed to drift as a deliberate fatigue-management tactic: `[15:27]` "you'll
watch my elbow begin to come out, and as I'm pulling up I'm able to squeeze [my] mid-back, and I
can still feel my lower lat... I can tuck my elbow in and I'll be like, oh, there it is, that lower
lat, and I can pump out five more reps."

## Rack pulls: an intermediate/advanced substitute, not a deadlift replacement (23:35–28:44)

Seth is explicit about the exercise's hierarchy and risk profile: `[23:35]` "I would consider rack
pulls to be more of an intermediate to advanced exercise. I think deadlifts and pull-ups will be
king — they're king because they're basic compound movements." The specific reason he substitutes
rack pulls here is to remove ground-level core engagement from the equation: `[24:37]` "when I'm on
the ground, the first thing you're going to do is... engage your core, and most bodybuilders have
weak cores... meanwhile we're taking a bunch of gear and we're stronger than [our core is] — and
that's how we end up hurting ourselves." Grip and bar position are named specifically: `[24:37]`
"I do [a] double overhand [grip] because I want my back to get even... I don't want to do an
over-under grip"; `[27:42]` "I prefer the bar right below my kneecaps." He explicitly warns against
yanking under heavy load, naming specific injuries: `[26:39]` "you'll see people yank back and I'm
like, why you yanking back? You're risking injury — you're going to... tear a bicep, you're going to
tear a... [pec], you could tear... your shoulder labrum, anything, your rotator cuff." He also states
his own programming frequency rather than a universal rule: `[27:42]` "I don't got to do deadlifts
every week... I do rack pulls every other week — I don't need to, I already built that foundation
years ago."

## A cueing disagreement that isn't a technique disagreement (16:29–17:30)

Seth and Tai explicitly disagree about the phrase "pull with your elbow" while agreeing on the
underlying mechanism: `[16:29]` "what he just said never made sense in my head... people [say]
'pull with your elbow,' I could never grasp that concept... I'm using my hands." Tai's response
names the same rule in different words: `[16:29]` "for me it's like, the closer your elbow's to
your side, the more low lat, and [as] he go[es] up, you hit mid[back] and then you hit rear[-delt]
out." Seth's summary: `[17:30]` "we're saying the same thing, but you're just using different
terminology."

## Form cues and body signals addressed

- **Pull target point (chin vs. nipple line) is the stated lever between upper-back and lower-lat
  emphasis on pull-downs**: `[06:09]`.
- **Rack pulls are explicitly scoped as intermediate/advanced, not a beginner substitute for
  deadlifts**: `[23:35]`.
- **Yanking a heavy rack pull is named as a direct injury risk to the bicep, pec, shoulder labrum,
  or rotator cuff**, especially "if you're dieted down... if you're fatigue[d]": `[26:39]`.
- **Elbow position on one-arm rows is allowed to change mid-set as a fatigue-management strategy**,
  not held rigid throughout: `[15:27]`.
- **A head-on-bench row variant is named as personally ineffective for Seth specifically**,
  framed as an individual-fit issue rather than a flawed technique: `[13:27]`.

## Glossary terms introduced

- **Turtle shell (back development)** — Seth's term for training the entire back as one
  progressive engagement from lower lats through mid-back to traps within a single heavy pull,
  rather than isolating one section `[25:37]`.

## Analogies worth reusing

- **"Turtle shell"** `[25:37]`, `[26:39]` — used repeatedly as the target overall-back shape/
  development pattern on rack pulls.

## Source-stated confidence

Several claims here are explicitly marked as personal preference or individual fit rather than
universal correctness:

- **No single pull-down hand/target technique is claimed superior**: `[07:10]` "is there a right
  way? ... you need one-on-one interaction to [find] the way that works best for your body."
- **The head-on-bench row dislike is stated as personal, not a claim the technique doesn't work
  for others**: `[13:27]`.
- **Rack-pull frequency (every other week) is stated as his own programming choice given an
  already-built strength foundation**, not a rule for less experienced lifters: `[27:42]`.
