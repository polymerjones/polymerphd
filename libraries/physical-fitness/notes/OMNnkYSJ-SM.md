# How To Train Shoulders

## Central claim

**Seth traces his entire shoulder-training philosophy to a specific injury: a broken wrist/radius
that forced him into unilateral, partial-range-of-motion side laterals for six weeks, during which
he discovered his shoulders responded better than years of prior "normal" training had produced.**
`[01:01]` "I couldn't do any pressing, so I sat in the side delt machine and just did sides every
day... From that, I realized I started filling up with blood... I got much better mind-to-muscle
connection with my shoulders doing them unilaterally." `[02:01]` "my shoulders got so much better
in this short period of time than they did for years of training leading up to that. So I was on to
something."

## Delt-specific training frequency (02:01)

Seth states a body-part-specific recovery observation, distinct from his general frequency rules
elsewhere: `[02:01]` "shoulders really never got too, too sore... my front delts would get tender,
but my side delts, it feels like I could do side delts almost every day... you can't train legs
every other day [the same way], but I could with shoulders." He explicitly flags this as
unverified: `[02:01]` "does that go with science? No. This is a bro-science concept, this is
anecdotal experimentation."

## Front raise height: capped at eye level (15:31–17:35)

A precise, tested ceiling is given, distinct from maximizing range of motion: `[15:31]`–`[16:33]`
"right where the top of the dumbbell is just right at eye level — that's where I noticed I'm getting
this great squeeze... never up here... too light of a weight for this long is pointless, 'cause I
want to get the [dumbbell] up this high... that's dumb, in my opinion, 'cause I have all this wasted
[range] here on a lightweight just to get this top."

## Rear delt: short range of motion, heavier weight (20:40–21:41)

Restating and expanding on the dedicated rear-delt How-To, Seth frames this as a direct departure
from what he'd read: `[20:40]`–`[21:41]` "I would only feel my rear delts for a short distance, but I
do the whole thing [range]... I was like, I feel like everybody's crazy, or I'm crazy. Started just
doing my own [thing] and saying, [forget] what everybody else is saying or I'm reading in
magazines... using a heavier weight with a shorter range of motion... I was able to target my rear
delts so much better, because the muscle group is so small [that] a bigger range of motion
incorporated other muscle groups."

## Press selection based on stabilizer demand (11:24–13:27)

A specific rule ties press choice to how many prior side-lateral sets are planned: `[11:24]` "if I'm
doing a barbell press, I'm only going to do a couple sets of sides first, just because barbell gets
a little dangerous, especially if you're starting to push some weight... you're going to use a lot
more other muscle groups... the stabilizer muscles. Whereas if I was doing a Smith machine or some
type of machine press... I'm able to just really get a strong heavy press [and] rip through all
those" additional side sets beforehand.

## Volume and criticism of "that's dumb" (07:05–09:08)

Seth explicitly defends a high total set count on one movement: `[07:05]`–`[09:08]` "there might be
10 sets of sides [in one workout]... a lot of people [say], 'that's dumb'... I don't give a [damn]
what any science person says." He frames the goal as visible striation/separation, not just size:
`[03:02]` "there's guys with huge delts, but you don't see them striated... that separation of the
muscle role is played with the volume side of things."

## Form cues and body signals addressed

- **Barbell/heavy dumbbell presses are paired with fewer prior side-lateral sets** than
  machine/Smith presses, due to stabilizer demand: `[11:24]`.

## Source-stated confidence

Seth is explicit that his shoulder philosophy is anecdotal, not scientifically verified, and
frames it that way from the outset rather than retroactively:

- **The delt training-frequency observation is explicitly labeled "bro science" and "anecdotal
  experimentation"**, not backed by cited research: `[02:01]`.
- **Press-exercise superiority is explicitly declined as a claim**: `[13:27]` "it's hard to say one
  way is better than the other... you got to figure out what's best for you."
