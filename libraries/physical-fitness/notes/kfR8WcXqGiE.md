# Shoulder Training with Missy Truscott | Seth Feroce

## Central claim

**Seth restates his halve-weight-double-reps extended-set technique with a precise worked
example, and notes that John Meadows — a trainer he never got to work with directly — separately
made a video presenting scientific support for the same idea, which Seth received as an
unexpected external validation of something he'd been doing purely by feel.** `[08:25]` "I want
something that forces me to go past failure... I will pick a weight and go to absolute failure with
it, and then I will cut that weight in half and do double the reps... if I do the 70s right now on
side laterals and I get 10, that means I got to do the 35s for 20... if I only get to 18, I have to
put them down, get my breath, and then finish the two." `[09:26]` "John [Meadows] did the video, and
he gave the scientific evidence behind it that it's actually a good idea."

## Bodybuilding-specific spotting philosophy (04:20)

Seth states the same principle documented elsewhere in this library, this time framed explicitly
against powerlifting norms: `[04:20]` "in bodybuilding we're not powerlifting... the goal isn't,
oh, you're a [failure] if you get help on the weight, because it doesn't matter, we're just trying
to look better... I've always kept my hands on my partner's... elbows so I could feel when they lose
— when they're failing... I'm just going to continue to help them so that they benefit from the
spot."

## "Hug a tree" side laterals with drift (05:21–06:22)

Seth restates the bent-arm side-lateral technique from other notes, adding a specific drift pattern
through the set: `[05:21]`–`[06:22]` "I'm hugging a tree, okay, and then I just put them at my sides...
if I move them ever so slightly forward, there's still sides, but you'll see me as I continue to do
them, I'll go from right out to the sides and then you'll start to see me creep forward and in, and
then go back... because that's [me] thinking of a three-dimensional delt."

## Rear delt raise paired with a pose-mimicking face pull (10:32–13:35)

Consistent with this library's other rear-delt notes, with the same pairing structure documented
independently by a different athlete: `[11:33]` "bent-over dumbbell raises... with heavier weight
but a small movement... if I go back too far, now I'm engaging my back and not my rear delts."
Immediately supersetted: `[12:34]` "I would bend over like this and put my head between my arms and
then pull down like I'm doing a rear double bicep... I pull with my pinkies... no lats — do not let
your lats engage whenever you're pulling back."

## Form cues and body signals addressed

- **Extended sets past self-perceived failure are structured around a fixed numeric target**
  (double the reps at half the weight), consistent with `yM7JZA2WkLQ`'s "Seth set": `[08:25]`.
- **A spotter's job in bodybuilding is to feel for true failure and assist through it**, not to
  withhold help to preserve a "clean" unassisted lift: `[04:20]`.
- **Rear delt engagement is capped before the back takes over**, using heavy weight and a
  deliberately short range: `[11:33]`.
- **Lat engagement during a rear-delt-focused pull is explicitly named as an unwanted
  compensation to watch for**: `[13:35]`.

## Source-stated confidence

Seth explicitly credits John Meadows with providing external, evidence-based support for a
technique he arrived at independently through feel, rather than claiming he derived it from
research himself:

- **The extended-set technique is stated as something Seth developed by feel, later validated by
  someone else's cited evidence**: `[08:25]`–`[09:26]` "I don't know [if it's science]... John did the
  video and gave the scientific evidence behind it."
- **The rear-delt/lat-engagement caution is stated as personally observed**, verified in real time
  on his training partner during this session: `[13:35]` "see how they engaged? ... I see them, the
  whole time."
