# Fxck Around & Find Out | Chest & Shoulders w/ The Young Bucks

## Central claim

**Seth defends training past full-range-of-motion failure using progressively shorter partial
reps — explicitly responding to critics who say partial reps "don't count" — while separately
acknowledging, unprompted, that his own training philosophy might only work for his own body.**
`[21:40]`–`[22:42]` "after you go to failure with full range of motion, and then you go to failure to
half range of motion, and then you continue to go to failure to a quarter range of motion... you
just keep getting that more and more pull... a lot of people don't even know what that feels like."
`[10:40]` "what if everything I ever did only worked for me? ... that means all of my great, grand,
wonderful philosophies... are just for me."

## The triple rest-pause protocol (00:00)

A specific structure is stated at the top of the session: `[00:00]` "we're working up to one all-out
set of 15 to 20 with good form, but you're going to fail within 15 to 20. And then for [the] second
set, we're going to do the same weight, and it's going to be a triple rest-pause — you're going to
do as many as you can the first time, you're going to fail, take 15 deep breaths, going to go until
failure again, 15 deep breaths, and one more time of going until failure."

## Progressive-range partial reps, with a named exception (22:42–23:42)

Seth states the technique isn't universally applicable to every exercise: `[22:42]`–`[23:42]` "on
certain exercises you can't do quarter reps like that, because then you're risking too much injury...
but if I can do it comfortably... on side raises... I know for a fact, 'cause I'm feeling it." He
directly rebuts the objection that feel doesn't matter: `[23:42]` "people are like, well, it doesn't
matter about the feel — and I'm like, well, it [does], because I know it's doing something, and I
like what's occurring, because whenever I hit certain shots, I can see all those things that I was
trying to achieve during that set happen."

## A disclosed coaching critique of his own technique (11:42–12:43)

Seth names a specific correction from a coach (Matt) about his own eccentric tempo, offered as
evidence that even his stated philosophy has real flaws being actively worked on: `[12:43]` "that's
one thing that Matt has been harping on me about when I send him my training videos every week — is
that I need to slow my eccentrics down, [because] I go too fast with them."

## Fullness/pump as verification that training and nutrition are combining correctly (19:38–21:40)

Seth uses a training partner's unusually full appearance mid-session as direct evidence a specific
combination of factors is working: `[19:38]` "what you ate yesterday, all the stuff you're doing is
playing a role in how you look right now... look at the fullness in your shoulders... your chest is
dense... you're seeing everything start to happen." He generalizes this into an actionable
principle for a lagging body part: `[19:38]`–`[20:39]` "you figure out what worked for a specific
body part... now imagine your arms getting just as good as this is on arm day."

## Form cues and body signals addressed

- **A triple rest-pause set is defined with a specific breath-count rest interval (15 breaths)
  between failure points**, not an arbitrary pause: `[00:00]`.
- **Progressive partial reps past full-range failure are explicitly scoped to exercises where it
  is safe**, not applied universally: `[22:42]`.
- **A named coaching correction (eccentric tempo) is disclosed as an active, ongoing fix**, not
  presented as already resolved: `[12:43]`.
- **Visible fullness/pump is used as real-time evidence that a specific day's food, hydration, and
  training combination is working**, generalized as a target feeling to replicate on other training
  days: `[19:38]`–`[20:39]`.

## Glossary terms introduced

- **Triple rest-pause** — a set structure: work to failure, rest for 15 deep breaths, work to
  failure again, rest 15 breaths, work to failure a third time, all at the same weight `[00:00]`.

## Source-stated confidence

Seth is unusually explicit in this video that his own training philosophy is unverified beyond his
own results, and treats this as an open question rather than dismissing it:

- **He states directly that his methods may not generalize**: `[10:40]`–`[12:43]` "what happens if
  everything I ever did only worked for me?" — contrasted with Branch Warren's very different but
  also effective style.
- **He states a wish to formally test his own training concepts** rather than asserting they're
  already proven: `[13:44]` "to actually dive into training and... see if the philosophies and
  concepts that I've developed over the years... make sense from [a scientific] standpoint would be
  a beautiful thing. One day."

this note adds a rare moment of Seth questioning whether his own approach generalizes at all — a
