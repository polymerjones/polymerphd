# Bro Split Day 1 - Chest

## Central claim

**Seth explicitly names the mental adjustment required to shift from fast-paced functional
training back to bodybuilding-style training: slowing down and taking real rest between sets
rather than continuously moving, because his body had adapted to the opposite pace.** `[08:16]`
"I've been doing so much functional training and incredibly fast-paced workouts that that's what
my body is used to, so I need to take longer rest periods and exert as much as I can per set...
[rather than] completing tasks." `[09:17]` "I was doing my warm-ups and I'm like, ah... settle
down, Seth. Do the warm-up set, take a break, warm-up set, take a break, warm-up set, take a
break, start your sets."

## The "set/set" notation (05:04–06:05)

Seth explains his own program-notation shorthand directly: `[05:04]` "when you see this in my
programming, like a number and then a slash and then another number, [that's a] set/set — all
we're going to do is do as many as we can right here at [near] failure, the goal is to shoot for
eight, and then we're going to cut the weight in half and double the reps, and we're going to push
to get 16."

## Posing saved for the end of the workout (06:05)

A deliberate energy-management decision, not incidental timing: `[06:05]` "towards the end of the
workout I'm going to begin to pose and push more blood... so that I don't expend more energy at
the beginning of the workout and sit there just... squeezing and pushing blood, and then... I end up
going [flat] by the end of the workout. We don't want that — we want to stay full."

## Gradual calorie reintroduction when starting a bulk (15:32–17:35)

Seth states a specific, tested method for increasing calories rather than jumping straight to a
target intake: `[15:32]` "if you come out of the gate heavy with calories, you're not going to feel
good... you should increase them slowly... add in another cup of rice throughout the day... and
instead of adding those carbohydrates one [day], try adding some extra fats just to see how your
body responds." He names a stable baseline for comparison: `[16:34]` "with rice, I can do chicken
and rice six meals a day to get back to a baseline... now I can begin to experiment with these
other things and put them into my program and recognize that... was a good idea or a bad idea
because our baseline is chicken and rice."

## Dumbbell pullover: hip-sink technique (19:37–20:39)

More granular than the dedicated pullover tutorial, this note adds a specific hip movement tied to
stretch depth: `[20:39]` "whenever I stretch back, you'll watch me sink my hips down — so I'll sink
my hips down, everything is stretched, and as I pull over I will lift my hips." He restates the
purpose as rib-cage expansion, not chest mass: `[19:37]` "I want to tear my chest open, that way
when I'm doing my front double bi[ceps] I get this big wide illusion."

## Form cues and body signals addressed

- **A deliberate pacing reset (longer rest between sets) is required when transitioning from
  fast-paced functional training back to bodybuilding-style lifting**: `[08:16]`–`[09:17]`.
- **Posing/flexing is scheduled for the end of the session specifically to conserve pump for
  later**, not done freely throughout: `[06:05]`.
- **Calorie increases when starting a bulk are added gradually and tested one variable at a time**
  (carbs one day, fats another), with a stable baseline diet (chicken and rice) as the comparison
  point: `[15:32]`–`[16:34]`.
- **Hip position (sunk during stretch, lifted during the pull) is a specific, named part of the
  pullover's technique**, not incidental body movement: `[20:39]`.

## Glossary terms introduced

- **Set/set** — Seth's programming notation for the halve-weight-double-reps intensifier: a number
  before the slash is the target failure rep count at full weight, the number after is the target
  rep count at half that weight `[05:04]`.

## Analogies worth reusing

- **"Choke the [out] of yourself"** `[23:59]` — repeated here for the upper-chest-thickness visual
  goal on incline presses, consistent with its use in `yM7JZA2WkLQ`.

## Source-stated confidence

Seth is explicit that the calorie-reintroduction advice and pacing adjustment are personal,
in-progress observations from this specific transition period, not settled long-term rules:

- **The gradual-calorie-increase method is stated as advice for someone without a coach**,
  explicitly framed as learning-by-experimentation rather than a fixed protocol: `[17:35]` "I want
  you to learn your body... this is your science experiment."
- **The functional-to-bodybuilding pacing adjustment is stated as an active, first-day
  observation** ("that's first thing out of the gate in my head that I'm going to have to work
  towards"), not something already resolved: `[09:17]`.

## Conflicts with other sources

Against `yM7JZA2WkLQ`'s "Seth set" (fixed numeric target beyond failure) and this note's "set/set"
notation, both describe the same halve-weight-double-reps intensifier — confirming it as a
standing part of Seth's programming vocabulary rather than a one-off technique. Against `qi_Ie7oe1cw`'s
and `xlNCg_GWGy0`'s instinctive-training framing (objective self-assessment, document everything),
the gradual-calorie-testing method here extends the same "document and experiment" philosophy from
training technique to nutrition specifically.
