# Beast Cardio

## Central claim

**Sagi Kalev frames this session in direct opposition to steady-state cardio, positioning
short weighted-interval work as the substitute for time on a treadmill, and later names the
combined strength-plus-cardio effect "anaerobic" rather than purely cardiovascular.** `[00:00]`
"Forget about walking on a treadmill for two hours. Cardio with the Beast." The anaerobic framing
is stated directly mid-session during the squat-and-upright-row block: `[20:31]` "As you go down,
you go up. You're working your shoulders. At the same time, you're working your legs. Everything
is working. You're anaerobic, anaerobic. Same time. Power."

## Warm-up (00:00–02:08)

High knees, hip circles ("turn circles... hips up and down... side, side. You got to warm up the
hips real good" `[01:08]`), alternating reverse lunges (chest up, `[01:08]`), side squat (`[01:08]`
–`[02:08]`, going progressively deeper: "squat deeper now... go as low as you can"), sumo squat
(bodyweight, `[02:08]`), then back to high knees at increased pace before weights are picked up.
Effort is stated as self-paced from the start: `[02:08]` "as long as you are comfortable with
whatever you got... whatever you take."

## Weighted squat-to-press block (02:08–06:11)

Light dumbbells introduced: squat down, press overhead on the way up, run continuously for a
20-second work interval followed by a 10-second held-squat hold, for three rounds `[03:09]`–
`[04:09]`. Form cue on load path: `[03:09]` "right when you go down, bring the weights right with
you." The hold segment is framed as strength-building through isometric tension: `[08:12]` (later,
same cue reused on the squat-jump hold) "what happens when you hold, your muscles getting so
tight. That's how you build strength."

Followed by one minute of calf raises / toe taps on a bench, with an explicit no-equipment
substitute offered: `[05:10]` "If you don't have a bench, watch Nick. Whatever it takes."

## Mid-session intensity check-in (06:11–07:12)

After the first block, Kalev has the group rest, hydrate, and self-rate effort on a 1–10 scale,
setting the floor explicitly high: `[06:11]` "I want you to think about, from a scale 1 to 10,
where are you at? I want you to give me a 10. I don't expect a 6 or a 5. For me, it's not good
enough. I want at least you start on a 9 and go up to a 10 on your own pace."

## Squat jump block (07:12–09:12)

Squat down to a half-height jump, escalating to a full-height jump, with an explicit correction on
foot position mid-set: `[07:12]` "I want your feet to be closer. If you screw up, if you don't do
it right, I'm going to take you through." The hold at the bottom of the jump is again tied to a
strength rationale rather than presented as a stretch: `[08:12]` "That's how you build strength.
That's how you build muscle."

## Mountain climber, row, and plank block (09:12–11:14)

Mountain climbers (`[09:12]`, "squeeze your abs. Bring your knees towards your chest"), into a
weighted row (`[09:12]`–`[10:14]`, with a load regression named directly: "you can't do it. Go to a
lighter weight. As long as you give me all you got"), into a held plank with active glute and arm
tension: `[10:14]` "squeeze your glutes. Squeeze your arms... everything is locked. Everything is
tight."

## Sumo squat with pause (11:14–13:19)

Weight held under the chin, sumo squat with a pause at the bottom "just for fun" `[11:14]`. Kalev
states three explicit session rules here rather than leaving them implicit: `[11:14]` "Rule number
one, stay safe. Rule number two, keep your form. And rule number three, you got to have fun with
the beast." Knee tracking is called out directly: `[12:15]` "right there, my knees. I don't let
them buckle out."

## Burpee and plank-push-up block (13:19–18:21)

Burpee into a stand with a weighted press, 20 seconds, followed immediately by a plank push-up held
15 seconds, alternating `[13:19]`–`[15:20]`. A single-leg/single-arm hold variant follows
(`[15:20]`, "leg up, arm up... opposite side"), with an explicit bail-out offered mid-hold: "if you
need to bring your arm down, just like that. Hold it down." The block closes with three rounds of
push-ups held down for 10 seconds, with a named safety cue: `[16:20]` "very soft on the way down.
You want to watch your wrist." A no-jump regression is given for the burpee variant that follows:
`[17:20]` "you don't have to jump up... you can get on your knees."

## In-and-out lateral pulse (18:21–20:31)

One minute of a low, wide squat with lateral in-and-out stepping, named as targeting "the inner
thighs, other thighs, the glutes" and hip strength `[18:21]`. Depth is again pegged to the same
1–10 self-rating language used earlier: `[18:21]` "give me a 10. No. Not a nine. Not an eight.
Unacceptable."

## Squat with upright row (20:31–22:31)

Squat down, stand while performing an upright row, hold at the top for 10 seconds `[20:31]`. Load
regression stated up front rather than only if someone struggles: `[20:31]` "If you have to go low
weight, low weight. If you have to drop the weight, drop it as long as you don't stop."

## Lateral lunge with hold ("Clio lunge") (22:31–24:31)

Lunge and switch sides continuously, then hold on the forward leg for 10 seconds before resuming
`[22:31]`. Encouragement is tied explicitly to urgency rather than pacing: `[23:31]` "There's no
tomorrow. It's only now."

## Plank-to-push-up hold (24:31–26:34)

From a plank, lower to a push-up and back up, then hold the plank with glutes engaged: `[24:31]`
"Going down from a plank. Coming up to a push-up... we're going to hold it on the plank... working
the core." Stated target areas broaden beyond the arms: `[25:34]` "You're working core. You're
working your midsection. You're working your lower back."

## "The beast" twist and sprint, with a bonus round (26:34–28:34)

Named by Kalev as his own favorite move: `[26:34]` "It is my favorite move. The beast." Side-to-side
touch-downs with a twist, into a stationary sprint, run for 20-second intervals with a 10-second
finisher; an unscripted extra round is added on the spot: `[28:34]` "I'm giving you a bonus round."

## Stretch and cooldown (29:35–30:35)

Standing hamstring reach paired with a spoken affirmation rather than a silent hold: `[29:35]`
"reach your toes and hold. All you have to do is hold and say, I'm a winner." The other side pairs
with "I am a beast." Standing quad stretch follows with an equipment substitute offered: `[29:35]`
"if you can grab it, if you can stand, if not, you bring the bench up." The closing kneeling
hip-flexor/groin stretch pairs with a spoken "thank you" rather than a rep count: `[30:35]` "lean
back and say thank you for the gift you just gave me."

## Form cues and body signals addressed

- **Effort is self-paced throughout, stated repeatedly rather than once**: `[02:08]` "as long as
  you are comfortable with whatever you got"; `[17:20]` "you don't have to jump up... you don't
  have to go all the way up."
- **A 1-to-10 self-rated exertion scale is used as the explicit intensity check**, with a stated
  floor rather than a ceiling: `[06:11]`, `[18:21]` "I don't expect a 6 or a 5... I want at least
  you start on a 9."
- **Knee tracking is named directly on both the sumo squat and squat jump**: `[12:15]` "I don't let
  them buckle out"; `[04:09]` "don't buckle your knees."
- **Wrist safety is called out specifically on the push-up hold**: `[16:20]` "very soft on the way
  down. You want to watch your wrist."
- **A mid-hold bail-out is explicitly offered rather than requiring the hold be completed**:
  `[15:20]` "if you need to bring your arm down, just like that. Hold it down."
- **Load is treated as freely adjustable without stopping the set**: `[10:14]`, `[20:31]` "you
  can't do it. Go to a lighter weight"; "if you have to drop the weight, drop it as long as you
  don't stop."

## Glossary terms introduced

- **"The beast" (move)** — a standing side-to-side touch-down-and-twist sequence into a stationary
  sprint, named by Kalev as his favorite move in the session `[26:34]`.
- **"Clio lunge"** — the source's own name (as transcribed) for a continuous alternating lateral
  lunge with a 10-second hold on the forward leg `[22:31]`.
- **In-and-out** — a low, wide squat with lateral stepping in and out, targeted at the inner
  thighs, hips, and glutes `[18:21]`.

## Analogies worth reusing

- **"That's what separates the men from their boy"** `[22:31]` — used at the end of a sumo-squat
  hold to frame the isometric hold as the differentiating part of the move.
- **The 1-to-10 scale itself as a communication tool** `[06:11]`, `[18:21]` — used repeatedly across
  the session as a shorthand for "not hard enough yet," reusable independent of this specific
  workout.
- **"Thank you for the gift you just gave me"** `[30:35]` — reframing a stretch hold as gratitude
  rather than a chore.

## Source-stated confidence

An instructional/motivational follow-along video, not a studied claim; intensity feedback is
entirely self-reported by the on-camera participants (Nick and Eric) rather than measured:

- **The exertion target is stated as self-assessed, with the instructor pushing the floor up
  rather than measuring it independently**: `[06:11]` the 1–10 scale is Kalev asking the
  participant to name a number, not a heart-rate or other external measurement.
- **Regressions are offered as standing options throughout, not exceptions**: `[05:10]`, `[10:14]`,
  `[17:20]`, `[20:31]`, `[29:35]` — no-bench, lighter-weight, no-jump, and no-partner-equipment
  substitutes are each named in the moment they'd be needed, not held back as advanced-only
  content.
- **Fun and safety are named as co-equal rules to form, not subordinate to intensity**: `[11:14]`
  "Rule number one, stay safe. Rule number two, keep your form. And rule number three, you got to
  have fun with the beast" — stated as a fixed rule set partway through a high-intensity segment.

## Conflicts with other sources

No other Body Beast cardio-specific session exists yet to compare against. Against `basics`, which
names the Beachbody Jump Mat specifically for this workout ("You will really appreciate this mat
when doing Beast Cardio" — `basics`[05:06]), this session's own transcript never mentions a mat or
jump-impact surface, only a bench for calf raises/toe taps — worth checking against the video
itself (not just its audio) once other Body Beast sessions are notated, since a floor mat could be
visible on screen without being named aloud. Against RUSHFIT's `balance-and-agility`[37:01], which
draws its safety line at joint/body pain rather than form imperfection, this source's safety framing
is closer to "keep going, adjust load or range, but don't stop" — a difference in where the
stop-signal is set that is worth tracking as more Body Beast sessions are added.
