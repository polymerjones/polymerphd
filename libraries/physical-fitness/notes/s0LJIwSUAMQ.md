# LEG DAY Explained | Seth Feroce

## Central claim

**Seth assigns each leg exercise a specific visual purpose rather than treating leg day as
generic volume — leg press for the outer sweep and hamstring tie-in seen from a side pose, power
squat for overall mass, Smith machine lunges for the teardrop, front squats for quad separation —
and states this has been his approach since the start of his training career.** `[07:20]` "each
exercise that I'm doing — leg press, power squat, stiff-legged deadlifts — each one is chosen for
a specific reason for my legs to look a certain way." `[20:17]` "each exercise has a purpose."

## Leg press: foot width and height as independent levers (07:20–09:21)

Two separate variables are named: `[07:20]`–`[08:21]` "the closer your feet go, the more outside of
the quads you work, the wider your feet go, the more insides they work... the further down my feet
are placed, the more emphasis on the quads, the higher my feet placed, more emphasis on the
hamstrings." Seth states his own standing choice and the pose it's built for: `[08:21]`–`[09:21]`
"I would do leg presses closer together with my feet placed a little higher than midpoint... that
way I'd still get a nice emphasis on the hamstring... whenever I'm hitting a side chest shot or a
side tricep shot... you'd be able to see the hamstring."

## A warm-up set on every new exercise, not just the first (04:15–05:15)

Restating a principle from other body parts, applied specifically to leg day's exercise
transitions: `[05:15]` "when you see me go from the leg press into the hack squat or the power
squat, you'll see me do one warm-up set that's just for my body to become familiar with... the way
the exercise is supposed to go, so that my body knows and can get used to that specific exercise,
and then also I can make sure... how do my hamstrings feel on the way down, instead of just loading
it up."

## Power squat/hack squat: chosen for overall development, not isolation (12:36–13:38)

Seth states a specific preference against the back-supported hack-squat variant: `[12:36]` "I just
don't like the way they feel... I like putting my face into it — I want this exercise... for overall
leg development, from my [glutes]... hips, all the way into my quads, hamstrings, glutes,
everything."

## Smith machine lunges: torso lean as a teardrop-emphasis lever (15:53–16:58)

Foot placement is fixed for consistency, and torso angle is the tested variable: `[15:53]` "I'm able
to place my foot in one specific place and not move... focus on... glute, hamstring, quad right
here... if I move my body forward and have... less of an angle... I'll place more emphasis on my
teardrop."

## Front squats: quad separation, credited to Hany Rambod (20:17–21:18)

A specific historical detail ties the exercise's purpose to contest-prep coaching: `[20:17]`–`[21:18]`
"training for Nationals, training for Dallas, back in '09, in 2010, Honey would always [be] on my
ass about doing front squats and making sure that whenever I'm doing them, I'm going to work all the
way up into my hips, so that the separation begins all the way through my quads."

## Deliberately combining weight and volume (01:04)

Seth names his approach as intentionally atypical: `[01:04]` "usually in the weightlifting world,
everybody's like, you do higher weight with some lower volume, or moderate volume with some higher
weights — me, I like a mix of everything, because I'm a bodybuilder, I don't give a [damn about]
effectiveness... I just want to look cool." He also explicitly rejects using pharmacological
enhancement as an excuse for effort: `[02:04]` "are you going to have an advantage? Absolutely. But
why use that as a crutch... you should push yourself to limits."
