# Volume Back Workout at Pump Town USA | Seth Feroce

## Central claim

**Seth states a direct, unqualified rule for anyone whose back is a weak point: do pull-ups, in
volume, regardless of how basic that sounds.** `[00:04]` "If your back sucks, if it is a weak
part, I cannot stress doing pull-ups enough. If you are not strong enough to do regular pull-ups...
assisted pull-ups... [I'll do] anywhere from five to ten sets of ten reps each set, so 50 to 100
pull-ups. Why? Well, because it's hard."

## Exercise selection and target (00:04–03:07)

Each exercise is named with an explicit target rather than left generic: `[00:04]`–`[01:04]`
corner-loaded ("old school") T-bar rows or a close-grip cable row for "lat thickness"; bent-over
barbell rows for "overall back development, upper back development"; one-arm dumbbell rows,
described as his personal favorite, again for "lat thickness." Wide-grip pull-downs follow as
straight sets, and reverse-grip low cable rows close the pulling work with an explicit tempo cue:
`[02:06]` "whenever I'm pulling, I'm going to get a good stretch and a good squeeze, one to two
second pause." The session ends with unweighted hyperextensions and stretching, named as a
standing priority: `[03:07]` "do some post-workout stretching, at least five to ten minutes, make
it a priority — you won't regret it, especially when you get old."

## Training to genuine failure, not a pre-picked number (01:04–02:06)

On one-arm dumbbell rows: `[01:04]` "I didn't go through the sets and reps, straight sets... one
warm-up set, and it has a failure right here — every single set will be to failure." On wide-grip
pull-downs, he states the rule generally and names the specific mistake it corrects: `[02:06]`
"these reps should be your maximum, you should be increasing the weight, pyramiding up in weight
and pushing yourself each set — don't just quit at eight... understand that you might have cut
yourself short and be able to do more weight to build more mass."

## Lower-lat activation as a named weak point (09:20–10:22)

The reverse-grip low row is chosen specifically to target an area he calls commonly underdeveloped:
`[09:20]` "the low rows, thick in the lower lats — I probably say that's like a huge lacking area
on everybody." The verification method is the same felt-activation rule used throughout this
library's Seth Feroce notes: `[09:20]` "activating your lower lats, feeling — if you don't feel
your lower lats working when you're doing a lower[-lat] exercise, you're not working your lower
lats." Grip and body position are named as the specific levers: `[10:22]` "if you change the grip
and go with a close grip or this angled grip here, or prop yourself a little further up on this
pad, you can start hitting your lower lats."

## On volume-style training as one approach among several (05:10–06:11)

Seth directly addresses pushback on doing five to seven exercises for three to four sets each:
`[05:10]`–`[06:11]` "some people don't agree with doing a volume-style workout, they're like, 'oh,
who would do five to seven exercises, three to four sets each — that's too much.' Well, maybe for
you it is... there's more than one way to skin a cat... the whole goal in bodybuilding is [to]
attain a look, so you have to find out what works for you." He also names a warm-up discipline: not
turning the first working set into an accidental all-out set. `[05:10]` "when you're warming up,
don't get caught in making this an exercise — make it a warm-up, get the blood flowing. You don't
have to do crazy weight."

## Form cues and body signals addressed

- **Pull-up volume is stated as a non-negotiable fix for a weak back**, not an optional add-on:
  `[00:04]`.
- **"To failure" is defined operationally as a per-set target, distinct from a fixed rep count**:
  `[01:04]`, `[02:06]`.
- **Felt muscle activation is the stated verification for whether an exercise is working the
  intended target**: `[09:20]` "if you ain't feeling it, it ain't working."
- **Warm-up sets are explicitly bounded**, kept light and separate from working sets: `[05:10]`.

## Glossary terms introduced

- None beyond terms already established in this library's other Seth Feroce notes (lat thickness,
  pyramid loading) — no new coined term in this video.

## Analogies worth reusing

- **"There's more than one way to skin a cat"** `[06:11]` — used to defend volume-style training
  against the view that it's excessive, by pointing to the range of approaches that produce
  results across bodybuilding, powerlifting, strongman, and CrossFit.

## Source-stated confidence

The training claims here are stated as personal method and preference, explicitly defended as
such rather than presented as the only correct approach:

- **The volume-training approach is explicitly not claimed as universally correct**: `[06:11]`
  "maybe for you it is [too much]... you have to find out what works for you."
- **Session length/intensity preference is stated as personal, not prescriptive**: `[06:11]` "I
  like being in here for an hour and a half... maybe it's what I need... if I'm in here for 45
  minutes some days, that ain't enough — I need more out of it."

Against `L9Py5s8V58E` (this channel's other back-day note), both sessions use the same grip/pull-
