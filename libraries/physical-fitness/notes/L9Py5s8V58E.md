# Rebuilding My Physique: Back Day | Seth Feroce

## Central claim

**Seth frames this entire session as a deliberate return to fundamentals after roughly ten weeks
away from training and an ongoing elbow issue, choosing exercises specifically to rebuild lat
thickness he says he lost, rather than to test his current strength ceiling.** `[02:04]` "This
back workout today is based around that [lost thickness]... whenever I was building a back
starting from the ground up, from the basics — I'm back there in my mind, I need it." `[06:09]`
"I have to view it as me being back here at square one... because when I look in the mirror I'm
not really that happy with how I look."

## Grip cue: taking the arms out of pulling movements (04:08–09:14)

The technique he repeats across pull-ups, pull-downs, and rows is a hand-grip cue meant to reduce
bicep/forearm involvement: `[07:10]` "the tip that I'm using right now... whenever I grab the bar,
pull-down to pull-up, I'm going to hold 'guns' in my hand... very important, because that's going
to help activate my lats. If I pull with my forearms and biceps, I'm going to use less of my lats
and back." Pull direction is a second, independent variable: `[07:10]` "I'm going to pull down to
the center of my face... not... to my chin... when I pull it down in front of me like this, you feel
your lats activate." Pulling to the chin instead shifts the target: `[08:11]` "if I... pull straight
to here, like my chin... I'm going to feel it much more in my upper back. I don't feel it so much
in my lats." He also names a stretch cue for warm-up sets specifically: `[10:14]` "whenever I stick
my chest up, I can feel my lats stretch out like crazy, and me personally I'm a big full-range-of-
motion [guy] whenever I'm doing these, especially during my warm-up."

## Barbell rows and T-bar rows: angle and target (11:14–15:22)

On barbell rows, torso angle is named as an adjustable, not fixed, variable: `[11:14]` "I like to
keep a little bit more bent over than straight up... whenever you have less of an angle, still
beneficial, you still can manipulate that movement so you hit your lats really well." He states his
own current-session preference explicitly rather than a universal rule: `[11:14]` "for me right
now, a little bit more of an angle." T-bar rows are framed as a deliberate shift in target from the
barbell row that preceded it: `[14:20]` "I just got done doing my bent-over barbell rows, focused
lat development, mass building, overall back — focused on lats. Here we're focusing on the upper
back, your rhomboid, teres major, teres minor, your traps."

## One-arm dumbbell rows: "a row, not a lift" (15:22–18:24)

Base and pull direction are both named explicitly: `[16:22]` "I like to have a wider stance... this
is a row — row, row, not up. If you pull up, that's okay too, but now you're using a lot more of
your upper back and not so much your lat... hence one of the reasons I put my foot so far back." He
closes the exercise-selection portion of the video with a direct statement on effort over novelty:
`[17:24]` "I'm not overcomplicating anything in these exercises or in this workout — the basics
will always work, you just have to do it with a purpose: intensity, perfection, feel the weight,
feel the exercise."

## Reverse-grip pull-downs: lower-lat and serratus focus (18:24–20:26)

The last back exercise is chosen for a specific visual target rather than pure loading: `[18:24]`
"reverse-grip pull-downs... great [for] front serratus... whenever you come up for your front
double bi, your front lat spread — nice, big, thick." Execution cue: `[18:24]` "stick your chest
up, and when you pull down, focus on that lower lat insertion... you're gonna pull and squeeze [it]
the whole time, you'll feel it."

## Form cues and body signals addressed

- **An unhealed elbow injury is monitored, not ignored, across the session**: `[04:08]` assisted
  pull-ups are chosen partly "especially right now my elbow still isn't the greatest," and by the
  final set, `[21:28]` "my elbow's getting cooked, I can feel it now... I'm just testing the
  waters."
- **Grip/pull-direction is the checked variable that determines whether lats or upper back take
  the load**, repeated across three different pulling exercises: `[07:10]`, `[08:11]`, `[16:22]`.
- **A discouragement-to-recovery framing is stated directly rather than minimized**: `[05:09]`
  "watching myself get smaller... was a huge kick in [the pants]... you have to take the time to
  recover. That discouragement that I felt, I'm using that as fuel right now."

## Glossary terms introduced

- **"Guns in your hands"** — Seth's cue for a grip/forearm-tension technique on pulling movements
  meant to reduce bicep/forearm takeover and increase lat activation, used across pull-ups,
  pull-downs, and rows in this session `[07:10]`.

## Analogies worth reusing

- **"A row, not a lift"** `[16:22]` — his shorthand for keeping the pull direction on one-arm
  dumbbell rows horizontal (toward the hip) rather than vertical, to keep the lat as the target
  muscle.

## Source-stated confidence

Seth is explicit that this session reflects a personal rebuilding circumstance (post-layoff,
recovering elbow) rather than his peak-condition technique, and that effort/consistency, not
novel programming, is what he credits for results:

- **Muscle memory is named but explicitly qualified**: `[06:09]` "muscle memory plays a role, but I
  have to view it as me being back here at square one."
- **Basic exercise selection is stated as sufficient, with intensity/effort named as the
  differentiator over exotic programming**: `[17:24]`–`[18:24]` "if you can't put in the amount of
  work that it takes to build the muscle, it's not going to work."

## Conflicts with other sources

Against `eY2npBUVU4Y`'s and the How-To curl videos' grip-placement-changes-emphasis pattern
(closer/wider hands shift bicep emphasis, elbow flare shifts tricep emphasis), this note extends
the same principle to back training: grip style ("guns in your hands") and pull direction (to the

consistent with, not contradicting, the emphasis-through-positioning pattern seen across this
