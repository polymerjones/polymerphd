# Tempo Back Bis

## Central claim

**This session's difficulty comes from counted tempo, not primarily from added weight — Kalev
states the goal as teaching controlled negative and positive phases explicitly, using the technical
vocabulary (eccentric, concentric) rather than only "slow" and "fast."** `[00:00]` "We're going to go
back to the roots of counting. We're going to get your back, your lats, your biceps... so big, you
will be in shock because you're going to learn how to count slow, fast, negative, positive,
eccentric, concentric."

## The tempo device, defined (03:00)

Rather than a new named set type, this session applies a counted ratio to the existing single-set
structure: `[03:00]` "This workout is all about tempo. This workout is all about going on the
positive and the negative... 15 reps. Six seconds down. Six seconds up." Each of the five sets below
runs the same shape — three rounds, weight rising as the tempo ratio compresses (6/6 → 6/3 → 3/3),
reps falling accordingly (15 → 12 → 8).

## Warm-up (01:00–03:00)

Arm circles, plank walk-outs, then light-weight rehearsals of the session's main lifts: deadlift
(`[02:00]`, "glutes up. Good. Glutes going out"), bent-over row (`[02:00]`, "elbows up. Bring the
weights as high as you can"), and reverse fly (`[02:00]`–`[03:00]`, "working the back of the
shoulders... all the way to the traps").

## Set 1 — Pull-over, tempo set (03:00–10:07)

A specific stopping point is named to protect the shoulder rather than maximizing range for its own
sake: `[05:03]` "this is where you want to stop. Right when you get to your chin, that's where you
want to go. When you pass your forehead, you want to go right up." Isolation is checked by what
should *not* be felt: `[06:03]` "everything coming from the lats. You should not feel your
triceps." Followed by a 10-rep plank knees-in-and-out finisher before the water break `[10:07]`.

## Set 2 — Assisted pull-up, tempo set (11:09–17:11)

Named directly as Kalev's own favorite move in the session: `[11:09]` "you know what's coming next?
My favorite move, I have to get huge back pull-ups." An assist band is introduced and its use
demonstrated rather than assumed: `[15:11]` "if you need the assistant [assistance], as long as you
finish the reps, that's what it takes." A grip-failure moment is treated as continuation, not
stopping: `[13:10]` "losing grip... there's no excuses in the beast... you have to go up. You have
to go down. You go down. But you know what? Look at him. He won't stop. He won't quit." Followed by
a "rainbow circles" ab finisher — lying leg circles, knees together, 10 reps each direction,
`[17:11]`.

## Set 3 — Bent-over reverse-grip EZ-bar row, tempo set (18:12–24:22)

Safety is restated as the first priority before the move is even shown: `[18:12]` "safety is number
one. Always be safe. Get the safeties right with you." A load-vs-tempo tradeoff is stated directly:
`[20:15]` "you don't have to use heavy weights. You just work on your tempo and you're teaching your
muscles how to control the weight. Nothing else." A specific tension-redirection cue: `[21:15]`
"all the tension of your body going on your quads. Not on your lower back."

## Set 4 — One-arm row with a hold-and-twist (25:22–28:29)

Named as a second favorite move: `[25:22]` "this is my favorite move." A twist is added at the top
of the row, held for the tempo count on each side: `[26:28]` "go down slow, hold, twist... we got 10
reps. Six, six." Kalev names a specific ab-engagement rationale for the twist rather than treating
it as incidental: `[26:28]` "you have to work your abs."

## Set 5 — Seated bicep curl, tempo set, each arm (28:29–38:35)

Run on each arm separately at 15 (6/6), 12 (6/3), and 8 (3/3) reps. A specific safety/effectiveness
rationale is stated directly rather than left as a bare instruction: `[33:34]` "you teach your
muscle memory. It always will know how to keep it right so you don't get hurt. You don't get
injured and that's how you stay safe and results will follow." A mid-set encouragement explicitly
answers the moment someone wants to quit: `[35:35]` "when you start hurting, when you start
burning... when you start saying, I can't do it. It's too hard. I can't finish. You know what? Yes,
you can." Followed by a lying hip-roll leg-raise ab finisher with a held 90-degree top position,
10 reps, `[38:35]`–`[40:38]`.

## Set 6 — EZ-bar curl, three grips (40:38–46:46)

Three grips run in sequence, each with its own rep count and tempo, stated up front as a single
plan: `[40:38]` "we got wide grip. We got close grip. And then we got reverse grip. 15, 12, 8, 6, 6,
6, 3, 3, 3." Wide grip: 15 reps at 6-up/6-down. Close grip: 12 reps at 6-up/3-down. Reverse grip: 8
reps at 3-up/3-down, with a completion-over-form-risk rule restated: `[42:38]` "don't risk your
form if you have to drop the weight. Drop the weight. Keep going." Closes with a 30-second mountain
climber finisher `[46:46]`.

## Stretch and cooldown (47:46–49:46)

Standing side-bend arm stretch, bench-assisted lat stretch (`[48:46]`, "hold your head... stretch
it. And breathe"), and a closing standing shoulder stretch with paced breathing `[48:46]`.

## Form cues and body signals addressed

- **A specific range limit is named to protect the shoulder joint, not just to maximize the
  stretch**: `[05:03]` "when you pass your forehead, you want to go right up" — a rare instance of
  a Body Beast session naming an upper bound on range rather than only a lower one.
- **Isolation is checked by naming what should not be felt**: `[06:03]` "you should not feel your
  triceps. Just your lats stretching."
- **A tension-redirection cue moves load away from the lower back onto a named alternate area**:
  `[21:15]` "all the tension of your body going on your quads. Not on your lower back."
- **A mechanical rationale ties tempo control directly to injury prevention**: `[33:34]` "you teach
  your muscle memory... you don't get hurt. You don't get injured."
- **A completion-over-form-risk rule is restated at the session's hardest, most fatigued point**:
  `[42:38]` "don't risk your form if you have to drop the weight. Drop the weight. Keep going."

## Glossary terms introduced

- **Tempo set (numeric)** — a single-set structure where the positive (concentric) and negative
  (eccentric) phases of each rep are each held for a stated number of seconds, tightening as weight
  rises across rounds (e.g. 6/6 → 6/3 → 3/3), applied as this session's primary difficulty lever in
  place of load alone `[03:00]`. Builds on the negative/positive vocabulary first named in
  `build-chest-and-tris`[31:19] by attaching explicit counts to each phase.
- **"Rainbow circles"** — the source's own name for a lying leg-circle ab exercise, knees together,
  swept in an arc `[17:11]`.

## Analogies worth reusing

- **"Shoot for the stars. Land on the moon."** `[16:11]` — used as an aspirational aside about
  effort, reusable as general motivational phrasing.
- **"That's pain. But that's love. That's what it takes."** `[38:35]` — reframing discomfort as
  evidence of commitment rather than a warning sign, distinct phrasing for a device used repeatedly
  elsewhere in the program (e.g. `bulk-legs`[23:27]).

## Source-stated confidence

An instructional/motivational training-partner video; its evidence is Kalev's technique narration
and two training partners' (Sean and Brian's) visible performance, not citation or measurement:

- **A specific mechanical rationale is given for why controlled tempo matters, tied explicitly to
  injury avoidance rather than only muscle growth**: `[33:34]` "that's how you stay safe and
  results will follow" — cause and effect stated in that order.
- **Smart pacing is explicitly praised over pride when a training partner picks too light a
  weight**: `[22:18]` "that's a girly man... no. More next time. It's learning... that was smart.
  That's what you want to do. You don't need to go so high. You want to go on your own pace... stay
  smart" — the same "know your limit" caution seen in `build-shoulders`[36:36].
- **A completion-optional, form-first rule is repeated across multiple sets**: `[15:11]`, `[42:38]`,
  `[45:45]` "as long as you finish the ribs [reps], that's what he takes"; "if you didn't finish it,
  write it down. Drop the weights. That's what I want from you. As long as you finish the reps."

## Conflicts with other sources

This session names no new set type from `basics`[01:01]'s original eight-item list, instead applying
a numeric tempo dimension on top of the single-set structure already defined in `build-back-and-bis`
— worth treating as a session-specific technique variant rather than a ninth set type. Its explicit
"teach your muscle memory... don't get injured" rationale (`[33:34]`) is the most direct
statement found so far, across the Body Beast sessions notated, connecting controlled execution to
injury prevention as cause and effect, comparable in spirit to `bulk-back`[04:20]'s pull-over depth
correction and `bulk-legs`[30:27]'s lower-back-tension rationale, but more general in scope. The
"smart pacing over pride" moment at `[22:18]` directly echoes `build-shoulders`[36:36]'s "know your
limit" caveat, reinforcing that this is a recurring, if occasional, counterweight to the program's
more common "whatever it takes" framing. Against RUSHFIT's `balance-and-agility`[37:01], whose
safety line sits at pain rather than form, this session's explicit range-limit cue on the pull-over
(`[05:03]`) is a rare case of a Body Beast session bounding a movement's top range for safety
rather than only cautioning against low range or heavy load.
