# 25 Sets of SQUATS! - Leg Day | Seth Feroce

## Central claim

**Seth explicitly agrees with a viewer's criticism that 25 sets of squats at a fixed weight is
"junk volume" with no bodybuilding value, and states directly that this session was never intended
to build muscle — it was a deliberate mental-health and stress-release practice, not a
hypertrophy protocol.** `[01:01]` "You know what? He's right. There's no reason from a
bodybuilding standpoint to do 25 sets of 225 at 10 reps a set. No point, it's stupid — really,
you're fatiguing the muscle, you're really not getting anything beneficial out of it." `[02:01]`
"It is junk volume. You're fatiguing the muscle, you're not even pushing [to] failure, you're just
draining it of everything — [but that's] because I'm not doing it for physical appearance, from a
bodybuilding standpoint. [I'm] doing it for this thing, for [in] here."

## The session's actual stated purpose (02:01–04:05)

Seth frames the workout as emotional processing rather than physical development: `[02:01]`–
`[03:02]` "whenever you have one hell of a stressful day... this turns into a release, you can take
out aggression... just do squats until you can't squat no more, because during that time you're
going to forget about... the day, you're going to realize that what you're doing right now kind of
sucks, but you're getting enjoyment out of it, and you're starting to reconnect and put yourself
back together before you go home to your family." He states a specific alternative he's avoiding:
`[03:02]` "after a really hard day, I don't want to go home and have a drink and then see my family —
no, I'd rather get my [head straight] in here."

## A stated frequency recommendation for this specific practice (10:40)

Distinct from a regular training protocol: `[10:40]` "take at least one leg workout every six [to]
eight weeks, just do squats... it's not all about how you look, it's about what you got."

## Form cues and body signals addressed

- **The session is explicitly not to failure in the trained-hypertrophy sense**, but repeated
  fixed-load sets to physical and emotional exhaustion: `[02:01]`.
- **Legs shaking and emotional frustration during the session are named as the intended signal**
  that the practice is working, not a warning sign: `[04:05]` "make yourself go so far at this that
  the physical part, like your legs shaking, and you start to get upset — yeah, that's good."
- **This is stated as a periodic practice (every 6-8 weeks)**, not a regular training template to
  follow every leg day: `[10:40]`.

## Source-stated confidence

This is the clearest instance in this library of a source explicitly agreeing with an external
training-effectiveness critique rather than defending a technique on hypertrophy or strength
grounds — the hedge is the substance of the video, not incidental:

- **Seth states directly, twice, that the criticism (junk volume, no bodybuilding benefit) is
  correct**: `[01:01]`, `[02:01]` "it is junk volume" — repeated verbatim near the video's end as
  well: `[09:38]` "it's junk volume, kid, you're right, it is."
- **The claimed benefit is explicitly scoped to mental health/emotional regulation, not physical
  development**, and presented as personal experience rather than a general prescription: `[04:05]`
  "at least that's what it's done for me."

## Conflicts with other sources

Against `w41YZ6czsZY`'s fatigue-vs-failure distinction (Hany Rambod's "tapping a pencil vs. a
hammer" framing, where fatigue-only training is explicitly named as non-growth-producing), this
video is a direct, self-aware demonstration of exactly the "fatigue, not failure" training Hany
describes — except Seth agrees it isn't for growth and reframes its value entirely outside the
hypertrophy framework other notes in this library use to evaluate training. This is not a
contradiction; it's the same physiological claim (fatigue alone doesn't build muscle) applied
honestly to a session with a stated different goal.
