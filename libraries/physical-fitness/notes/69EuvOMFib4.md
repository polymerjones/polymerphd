# Shoulders With Chris Bumstead And Hany Rambod | Seth Feroce

## Central claim

**Hany Rambod introduces a "reverse drop set" — starting light and ascending in weight through a
set rather than the conventional descending drop set — as a deliberate variation for finishing
shoulder work.** `[14:24]` "Let's do a reverse drop... start with 15, 20... [then] the next one
around 10 or 12 reps, heavier weight — so it's a drop set, but in reverse... ascension."

## Correcting trap takeover mid-set (10:18)

Hany gives a direct, real-time form correction distinguishing which muscle should be doing the
work: `[10:18]` "watch your traps, watch your traps... control, control, stop, control top, that's
it, good — let your shoulders do the work, not your traps... slow it down a little bit more."

## Changing machine angle to finish (20:20)

Rather than repeating the same press setup, the session deliberately changes the machine's angle
for a later set: `[20:20]` "we're going back to shoulder press, but this time... we're doing [it on
a] machine, changing up the angle a little bit, just to be able to really finish off those
[delts]."

## Form cues and body signals addressed

- **Trap takeover during a shoulder press is named directly and corrected in real time**, with
  "let your shoulders do the work, not your traps" as the explicit cue: `[10:18]`.
- **Machine press angle is deliberately varied within the same workout** to change how the delts
  are finished, rather than repeating one setup throughout: `[20:20]`.
- **Muscle shaking during a drop set is named as a normal, expected response** to the sudden weight
  decrease: `[19:17]` "you see how the muscles [are] shaking, because we're so used to dropping your
  weight."

## Glossary terms introduced

- **Reverse drop set** — a set that starts at a lighter weight and ascends in weight (with
  correspondingly fewer reps) rather than the conventional descending drop set `[14:24]`.

## Analogies worth reusing

- None stated as such in this source.

## Source-stated confidence

The reverse drop set and angle-change technique are both presented as Hany's live coaching
judgment calls for this specific session, not asserted as universally superior to standard drop
sets or a fixed press angle:

- **The reverse drop set is introduced casually as a variation to try**, not framed as research-
  backed: `[14:24]`.
- **The angle change is stated as serving a specific in-session goal (finishing the delts)**, not a
  general rule for all shoulder pressing: `[20:20]`.
