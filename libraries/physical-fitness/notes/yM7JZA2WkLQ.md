# Upper Chest Whiteboard Workout

## Central claim

**Seth structures the entire session around one specific lever — how low the dumbbells descend on
incline presses and flies — as the difference between building upper chest and inadvertently
training the front delts, and pairs it with a named intensifier ("the Seth set") that requires
hitting a fixed numeric target rather than an open-ended "go to failure."** `[04:07]` "If I'm
bringing them down up high, my first engagement is going to be my front delts, not my upper pec...
I'll have them a little bit lower — bringing the dumbbells down right above my nipple line — and
I'll be able to push, and [that] will bring that upper chest first rather than engage my delts."

## The "Seth set": a numeric target beyond perceived failure (05:07–07:10)

The intensifier is defined precisely: `[05:07]`–`[06:09]` "pick a weight you go to failure on... drop
that weight, wait 10 seconds, cut that weight in half... double the reps. If I did the 120s for 10,
I'm going to do the 60s for 20." He explains the reasoning directly — an open-ended "go to failure"
lets the lifter stop early without realizing it: `[07:10]` "if you did 10 and then you did 12 and
that's all... was your failure? No — we're supposed to get 20... [if you] only got 12, set the
[weight] back down, wait 10 seconds... pick it back up and get the remaining eight reps to get to
20. The concept was pushing yourself further than failure [by giving] it a number... you have to
reach it, you have to achieve it."

## Dumbbell descent height, restated on flies (09:11–10:13)

The same mechanism from the press section is applied to incline flies: `[09:11]` "again with this,
how you hold the dumbbells and where everything is placed is going to play a role... in my head I'm
thinking [of] this area, that upper pec — I'm squeezing... on the inside when I come in."

## A real-time decision to skip a planned exercise (31:30–32:31)

Seth abandons dips mid-session rather than forcing them through: `[31:30]`–`[32:31]` "I'm good on
dips, I'm going to hurt myself, something's going to rip... I don't think I'm there yet, [a] few more
weeks and I'll be stable enough to do so... just because your program says like dips 3x12 today, if
you get to them and something's tweaked, don't hurt yourself." He states the underlying rule
directly: `[32:31]` "I pushed myself on all three of these exercises... they didn't go too well right
now, so I just got to keep that in my head: no dips, Seth, don't do dips."

## Elbow position: a stated shoulder-safety trade-off, knowingly accepted (22:15–23:15)

On a band-resisted press movement, Seth names the biomechanically safer option and explains why he
doesn't always take it: `[22:15]`–`[23:15]` "with your elbow tucked a little bit... not all the way
down but not all the way out, like a 45, halfway between them — it's going to feel way more
comfortable on your shoulder from a biomechanical standpoint... but if I'm looking for overall chest
development, I'm going to put my shoulder at a little bit more risk. However, I'm going to stay in
control of the weight rather than just be all over the place."

## "Bro science" vs. biomechanical optimization, stated explicitly (28:25–30:26)

Seth names the actual philosophical disconnect directly rather than dismissing the criticism:
`[28:25]` "that's one of the reasons... science people don't like the bro-science side of things —
because I'm not really trying to do anything perfect from a biomechanical or science standpoint. I'm
trying to sculpt the physique... that's where the disconnect [is] between bro science and... the
real science." He frames every exercise as tied to a specific competition pose rather than a
biomechanics textbook: `[29:26]` "whenever I'm doing anything... I'm thinking of a specific pose in
bodybuilding that I was hitting."

## Dumbbell pullover as rib-cage work, not a mass exercise (10:13, 32:31–34:34)

Consistent with the dedicated How-To video on this movement, Seth restates its purpose: `[32:31]`–
`[33:33]` "as I'm doing these, I'm thinking rib cage expansion... I dip my hips down and I'm
stretching all this out... I feel it in my serratus, I feel it in my pecs." He ties the stated goal
directly to a posing outcome: `[33:33]`–`[34:34]` "you want thick pec[s] so that whenever you hit
your front double bicep, your chest doesn't disappear."

## Form cues and body signals addressed

- **Dumbbell descent height on incline presses/flies is the stated lever between upper-pec and
  front-delt engagement**: `[04:07]`, `[09:11]`.
- **A planned exercise (dips) is abandoned mid-session based on real-time joint feel**, rather
  than completed because the program called for it: `[31:30]`–`[32:31]`.
- **A biomechanically safer elbow position is named and knowingly traded for more chest
  development, with the explicit condition of staying in control of the weight**: `[22:15]`.
- **A fixed numeric target (not just "failure") is the stated mechanism for pushing past a
  lifter's self-perceived limit**: `[07:10]`.

## Glossary terms introduced

- **Seth set** — an intensifier: take a set to failure, then after a 10-second rest, halve the
  weight and hit exactly double the original rep count, resting mid-set if needed to reach that
  fixed number rather than stopping at self-perceived failure `[05:07]`.

## Analogies worth reusing

- **"Hug a tree" (fly-press arm bend)** `[27:25]` — restated here consistently with `BRYj0qirZH8`,
  confirming it as a standing part of his fly technique rather than a one-off phrase.
- **"Choking the [out] of you"** `[27:25]` — his description of the upper-chest pump sensation at
  the top of an incline press/fly.

## Source-stated confidence

Seth is unusually explicit in this session about the difference between what he's doing and what a
biomechanics-first approach would recommend:

- **The elbow-position/shoulder-risk trade-off is named directly as a deliberate choice**, not
  presented as risk-free: `[22:15]`–`[23:15]`.
- **The whole "bro science" framing is stated as an acknowledged gap from "real science,"** not
  disputed as equally valid on scientific grounds: `[28:25]` "is it right, is it wrong? I don't
  know. That's the beauty of bodybuilding."
- **The mid-session decision to skip dips is framed as provisional** ("a few more weeks"), not a
  permanent exclusion of the exercise: `[31:30]`.

## Conflicts with other sources

Against `BRYj0qirZH8`'s "hug a tree" fly cue and low-incline fly preference, this note is fully
consistent — same technique, same reasoning. Against the back-day notes' "train every set to
failure" framing, the Seth set adds a more specific mechanism (a fixed numeric target beyond
self-perceived failure) for the same underlying goal of pushing past a comfortable stopping point.
No other physical-fitness note yet documents a mid-session decision to abandon a planned exercise
based on real-time joint feel — worth flagging as a concrete example of the "don't force it"
principle stated more abstractly elsewhere in this library.
