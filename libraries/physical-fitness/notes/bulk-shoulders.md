# Bulk Shoulders

## Central claim

**Sagi Kalev states the mechanism for "3D" shoulders directly — training all three deltoid heads
(front, top, back) rather than just the visible front — before any lift begins.** `[00:00]` "I love
shoulders. You know why? Because when you work the front, the top and the back, that's so you get
that 3D dimension all around. This is dynamic set training. We're going up with the weight, down
with the reps, and that's what it's all about. Getting round shoulders."

## Warm-up (01:01–03:02)

Arm circles both directions, then light-weight rehearsals of the session's exercise families: front
raise to a V shape (`[02:02]`, "we are warming up the shoulders from all corners. All sides"), side
raise, shoulder press (`[02:02]`–`[03:02]`, "make sure you don't lock your elbows"), upright row, and
reverse fly (`[03:02]`, "nothing else moving. Side of your arms going up and down").

## Set 1 — Side lateral raise / Arnold press superset (04:02–10:10)

Two exercises with no rest between, escalating together across three rounds (15+15 → 12+12 → an
8+8 drop set). Arnold press form — named directly, seated, weight rotated from a curled start to a
pressed finish: `[04:02]` "start from the knees. Kick, kick. Right in front of you, turn, twist,
extend. Lock on your shoulders." A named target framing recurs from `build-shoulders`[00:00]: `[05:05]`
"we got to get them huge. We got to get them ripped. We got to get them shredded like two balloons
sticking out."

## Set 2 — EZ-bar upright row, progressive set, forward then reversed (11:10–17:14)

Three weights run light-to-heavy with no rest (15 → 12 → 8), immediately reversed heavy-to-light
(8 → 12 → 15) — matching the forward/reverse progressive-set structure confirmed across
`bulk-back`[14:38], `bulk-chest`[13:20], and `bulk-legs`[10:14]. A knee-sway correction is given
live: `[21:15]` "make sure your feet and your knees not dancing when you lift it up... focus on
your core right here."

## Set 3 — Plate front raise with a twist (17:14–24:19)

Named as Kalev's own favorite move in the session: `[17:14]` "this is my favorite move coming
next." A weight plate is raised straight up, then rotated ("looked through") and twisted before
lowering: `[18:14]` "you're going to bring this up. And you're going to lift it up. You're going to
look right through the hole. And you're going to twist, twist, bring it down." A specific visual
target is named for what correct execution should produce: `[19:14]` "that's what separates the
chest from the shoulders. And when you stand, it just sticks out."

## Set 4 — Seated reverse fly, progressive set, forward then reversed (24:19–30:25)

Chest braced against an inclined bench, matching the same forward/reverse progressive-set pattern
used earlier in this session. Bench-hugging form cue: `[26:22]` "hug the bench with your knees and
your quads. Chest down." A named muscle-group caution distinguishes this exercise from the session's
front-loaded moves: `[26:22]` "we work in the front, the side, and not the back [normally] ... you
work in the back of your shoulders" here specifically. A no-weight continuation is offered at
failure rather than stopping: `[29:24]` "too heavy. Too heavy. Drop it. Keep going with no weights...
squeeze it. That's all you need."

## Set 5 — Superman / plank twist superset, bodyweight (30:25–34:27)

The session's only unweighted set, run as a finisher: prone alternating opposite-arm-opposite-leg
raises ("Superman"), superset with a 30-second plank twist. `[30:25]` "extend your arm and your
leg. We are alternating... keep your hands and your back of your heel in the same line." Named
target breadth: `[31:26]` "you want to work the shoulders. You want to stretch the glutes. You want
to squeeze the glutes. You want to work your lower back, both sides. We work in the core, right in
the center."

## Stretch and cooldown (34:27–36:28)

Overhead behind-head stretch, hands clasped and pulled back, explicitly capped by force: `[34:27]`
"raise him up. As high as you can. Don't force it." Standing arm-across-body stretch, breath-paced,
each side `[34:27]`. Closes with a standing shoulder-roll stretch, chin dropped `[35:28]`.

## Content note: appended promotional material (36:28–end)

The workout itself ends at `[36:28]` ("that was bulk shoulders... see you next time"). What follows
to the end of the file is a Shakeology advertisement — not Body Beast programming and not reflected
in this note's exercises or training-focus facets.

## Form cues and body signals addressed

- **A joint-lock caution is stated on the shoulder press specifically**: `[02:02]` "make sure you
  don't lock your elbows."
- **A knee/foot instability is named and corrected with a specific redirect**: `[21:15]` "make sure
  your feet and your knees not dancing when you lift it up... focus on your core."
- **A no-weight continuation is offered at failure rather than ending the set**: `[29:24]` "too
  heavy. Drop it. Keep going with no weights... squeeze it. That's all you need" — the same pattern
  as `build-shoulders`[33:34]'s Giz-move failure response.
- **Full-body contact with support equipment is checked explicitly, not assumed**: `[27:22]`,
  `[28:22]` "you're too low"; "I want your full body to touch the bench. I want your knees to hug
  the bench."
- **A stated visual landmark, not just a muscle name, defines correct execution**: `[19:14]` "that's
  what separates the chest from the shoulders... it just sticks out."

## Glossary terms introduced

- **Arnold press** — named directly in-source: a seated press starting curled at the shoulders,
  rotating outward to a locked overhead finish `[04:02]`.
- **Plate front raise with a twist** — a straight-arm raise using a weight plate, rotated to look
  through its center hole, then twisted before lowering; named as Kalev's own favorite move this
  session `[17:14]`.
- **"Superman"** — the source's own name for a prone, alternating opposite-arm-opposite-leg raise,
  used here as a bodyweight finisher rather than a loaded lift `[30:25]`.

## Analogies worth reusing

- **"That's what separates the chest from the shoulders. And when you stand, it just sticks out."**
  `[19:14]` — a concrete visual landmark for judging shoulder development, reusable wherever a
  program needs a non-numeric success criterion.
- **"There's always someone that train harder. There's always someone that want it more."**
  `[30:25]`–`[31:26]` — a comparison-to-others motivational device, distinct from this program's more
  common self-referential "beast" framing.

## Source-stated confidence

An instructional/motivational training-partner video; its evidence is Kalev's technique narration
and two training partners' (Scotty and Brian's) visible performance, not citation or measurement:

- **Form is named as the overriding rule when a set stalls, not intensity**: `[30:25]` "when you get
  stuck and it doesn't go, form is rule number one."
- **A no-weight continuation is offered explicitly rather than treating failure as a stopping
  point**: `[29:24]`, `[31:26]` "keep going with no weights"; "drop the weights. Keep going with no
  weights."
- **Individual load and rep variation is shown directly**: `[06:06]` Kalev and Brian state different
  personal weights on the same lift (15/40 vs. 25/25) rather than one prescribed number.

## Conflicts with other sources

The forward/reverse progressive-set structure appears twice in this single session (Sets 2 and 4),
reinforcing that it is a fixed, reusable program pattern rather than a one-off — consistent with
`bulk-back`[14:38], `bulk-chest`[13:20], and `bulk-legs`[10:14]. This session's no-weight-continuation
response to failure (`[29:24]`) matches `build-shoulders`[33:34]'s Giz-move handling of the same
situation, suggesting it is the program's standard response to hitting failure on an isolation move
specifically, distinct from the "drop weight and keep the rep count" response used on compound
lifts elsewhere (e.g. `bulk-legs`[06:09]). This is the only session notated so far with a fully
unweighted bodyweight finisher set (Superman/plank twist) — worth checking whether other
not-yet-notated sessions include a similar core-stability closer. Against RUSHFIT's
`balance-and-agility`[37:01], whose safety line sits at pain rather than form, this session's "form
is rule number one" statement at a stall point is a direct match in spirit though not in wording.
