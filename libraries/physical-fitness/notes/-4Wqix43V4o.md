# Intense Shoulder Training | Jammed, Jacked, & Juicy

## Central claim

**Seth structures the session to fill the entire shoulder with blood — sides, then a touch of
rear, then front — before ever moving to presses, and separately identifies a specific felt
phenomenon behind his "set set" intensifier: after moving genuinely heavy weight, a much lighter
weight suddenly feels unusually light and easy to connect with.** `[04:01]` "fill the whole
shoulder up... so as we work up in here, fill the whole shoulder up, make sure whenever you're
doing your sides and then bend over, get a little bit of rear in there, even come to the fronts a
little bit... and then we rip into presses." `[09:12]` "the 50s feel light after doing the 70s...
that's where the set-set thing came from — like after I would do heavy weight... then I would go to
the weight that I'd feel really comfortable at... you're able to get that mind-muscle connection
with weight that you normally wouldn't be able to."

## Whether the "lightness" effect is bro science or real science (09:12–10:15)

Seth is asked directly and gives an honest, uncertain answer rather than claiming authority:
`[09:12]`–`[10:15]` "is that bro science or real science? ... there probably is some type of real
science behind that concept, but I just found out that if I move a heavier weight for like only a
few reps... you go to touch the lighter weight, you're like, oh, look, it's lighter."

## Rear delt raise: grip position tested live (14:25–16:25)

Building on the short-range/heavier-weight technique documented elsewhere, this session tests grip
position directly on a training partner: `[15:25]` "you are kicking the bottoms out — put your
hands in more of a neutral position or a pronated position... see the difference? Still doing the
same exercise, but just different hand placement." The exercise is paired deliberately with face
pulls: `[16:25]` "superset-ing with face pulls, three sets here, three sets of face pulls... find
[different] manipulation tactics to just help build that small muscle."

## Form cues and body signals addressed

- **Filling the entire shoulder (sides, rear, front) before pressing is a deliberate sequencing
  choice**, not incidental: `[04:01]`.
- **A felt "lightness" effect after heavy work is used deliberately to improve mind-muscle
  connection at lower loads**, with Seth explicitly uncertain whether this is a real physiological
  effect or a subjective one: `[09:12]`–`[10:15]`.
- **Grip position (neutral vs. pronated) on the same rear delt exercise is tested live and shown to
  change the feel**, consistent with grip-position claims elsewhere in this library: `[15:25]`.
- **Face pulls are explicitly named as insufficient alone for rear delt growth**, requiring the
  heavier bent-over variant as well: `[13:22]` "I like face pulls only because they help connect and
  push blood, however you still need to grow your rear delts [the other way too]."

## Source-stated confidence

Seth is unusually candid in this video about not knowing whether one of his own techniques has a
real physiological basis:

- **The post-heavy-set "lightness" phenomenon is explicitly left as an open question**: `[09:12]`
  "is that bro science or real science? ... there probably is some type of real science behind that
  concept, but..."
- **Rear delt/genetics discussion is framed as observational**, not a settled claim: `[19:38]` "some
  people have really great rear delts and do it every different variation... whatever you do to
  make that connection is what you should stick with."
