# 07 — Plain English Glossary

Every program-specific or technical term the notes introduce, defined in plain language.
Alphabetical, no citations — see each note's own "Glossary terms introduced" section for the
sourced version with anchors.

**Addressing the dumbbell** — the technique for approaching and picking up a dumbbell set on an
elevated surface (chair, table, rack height) with a flat, neutral spine, as distinct from the
lift itself.

**Airplane** — a balance exercise: standing on one leg, arms out wide, rotating the torso side to
side.

**Alligator crawl (bear crawl)** — an elbow-to-knee crawl kept as low to the floor as possible;
the lower the hips, the harder it is.

**"Around the world"** — a single-dumbbell exercise that swings the weight in a circle from one
hand to the other, with the heels lifting off the ground.

**Basal Metabolic Rate (BMR)** — the number of calories a body needs in a day, calculated from
height, weight, age, gender, and activity level.

**The calorie equation** — calories burned minus calories eaten equals the calorie deficit that
drives fat loss; a 3,500-calorie deficit equals about one pound of fat.

**Chair iso-squat** — holding a squat at the bottom position without moving, rather than doing
repetitions.

**The "championship round"** — the program's own name for a workout's final, hardest round,
meant to feel like the decisive round of a fight.

**Checking a kick** — a defensive move: raising the leg to block an incoming kick while covering
the face with the other arm.

**Clench-and-relax** — a whole-body relaxation sequence at the end of a workout: tense every
muscle on an inhale, then let go completely on the exhale.

**Closed guard position** — a ground-fighting position (on your back, legs around an opponent)
that some floor exercises are built to simulate.

**Fencer's lunge** — an explosive jumping lunge that alternates which direction you face.

**Floor pull-up with pop-up** — lying face down, pulling yourself forward along the ground with
your elbows, then popping up to standing.

**Ginga** — a Capoeira dance/martial-arts step, moving side to side like a backward lunge with a
raised guard hand.

**Gorilla squat** — hands on the floor like a gorilla, hopping the feet up into a full squat and
standing, then repeating.

**Hip-heist** — standing back up from underneath an opponent using a hip-driven motion; the same
term this program's automatic transcription rendered several different ways ("hip eyes," "hip
ice," "hip-hist") across different videos.

**The low shuffle** — a lateral shuffling drill done in a low stance, said to improve kicking
power.

**"Mawa shigiri"** — almost certainly "mawashi geri," the standard term for a roundhouse kick;
hip-driven, with the heel pointed at the target on impact.

**Mule kick (bullfrog drill)** — hands braced on the floor, kicking both legs up together, then
standing.

**Navasana** — the yoga "boat pose": sitting with legs and chest lifted, forming a V shape.

**The opposites drill** — a stability exercise on all fours or kneeling, lifting the opposite arm
and leg together and holding.

**"The piston"** — this program's name for a dumbbell squat, because the up-and-down motion looks
like a piston.

**Quality over quantity** — the program's repeated rule for adding weight: use only what you can
lift with good form, not as much as possible.

**RUSHFIT Assessment** — a four-exercise fitness test (squat, push-up, sit-up, burpee, one minute
each) used to measure progress before and during the program.

**The scramble** — a fast-paced drill moving between full and half body rotations, supported only
on the hands and feet.

**Sit-through** — a transitional floor movement passing through a push-up-like position on the
way from lying down to standing.

**Staggered butterfly guard / staggered hooks guard** — ground-fighting positions that the
dumbbell get-up's starting posture is said to functionally resemble.

**Straddle** — a wide seated or standing leg position used as the base for several stretches.

**The stretch-shortening cycle** — the mechanism behind explosive (plyometric) training: a muscle
stretched quickly, like a rubber band, snaps back with more force than a muscle moved slowly.

**The training pyramid (Conditioning / Technical / Tactical)** — the program's own three-level
model of athletic development: a base of general fitness (Conditioning), then sport-specific
skills (Technical), then match-specific strategy (Tactical).

**Triple extension** — the simultaneous straightening of the ankle, knee, and hip joints that
powers an Olympic-lift-style jumping motion.

**Uchi-mata** — a judo hip throw, adapted in this program as a defensive response to being grabbed
or clinched.

**The "yin to the yang"** — this program's own phrase for how flexibility work balances out its
harder conditioning work — necessary, not optional.
