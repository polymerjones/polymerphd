# 01 — Core Principles

This library is new, with three notes so far from two creators. One pattern already recurs across
all three, independently arrived at from three different angles — worth naming even this early,
since it's exactly the kind of cross-source convergence this library exists to surface.

## Subtraction beats addition when someone feels stuck or exhausted

All three sources converge on protecting a small number of things deliberately rather than adding
more, each from a different starting mechanism:

- One video argues from physiology — sitting, ultra-processed food, chronic cortisol, sleep loss,
  and isolation each degrade a specific body system, and the fix is a handful of concrete daily
  practices, not one more habit stacked on an already-overloaded life.
- A second argues from a time-budget and societal-incentive angle — with only 6-8 hours a day left
  after sleep and work, the fix is protecting that remaining time deliberately, on the calendar,
  rather than trying to fit more in.
- A third argues from cognitive load — every open commitment runs as a background mental process
  draining energy whether or not it's active, and the fix is a two-stage filter down to the two or
  three things that actually produce results.

No source names the other two — this convergence is this library's own observation, not a claim
any single video makes. See each note's own "Conflicts with other sources" section for the full
comparison with citations. As more sources are added, watch for whether this pattern holds or
breaks.
