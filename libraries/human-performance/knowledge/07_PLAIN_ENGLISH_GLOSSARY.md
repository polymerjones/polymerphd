# 07 — Plain English Glossary

Every technical term the notes introduce, defined in plain language. Alphabetical, no citations —
see the individual note's "Glossary terms introduced" section for the source and timestamp behind
each term.

**Adenosine** — a molecule that builds up in the brain through waking hours and binds receptors
that slow neural activity, producing sleepiness; caffeine works by blocking these same receptors
without activating them.

**Allostatic load** — the cumulative biological wear and tear a body accumulates from chronic
stress over time.

**Alpha-2 adrenergic receptors** — receptors found at higher density on lower-abdominal fat cells
that resist the hormonal signal to release stored fat, part of why that region is the last to lean
out.

**AMPK** — a cellular signaling pathway triggered by energy stress inside a cell; central to
building new mitochondria, and activated much more strongly by higher-intensity exercise than by
easy, steady cardio.

**Amyloid beta** — a protein cleared from the brain mainly during deep sleep by the glymphatic
system; also implicated in Alzheimer's disease when it accumulates.

**Anterior cingulate cortex** — a brain region that computes whether an effort is "worth it" ahead
of conscious choice, continually recalibrated by how much reward recent experience has delivered
for how little effort.

**Aromatization** — the conversion of testosterone into estrogen by fat tissue, more pronounced
with more visceral (organ-surrounding) fat, which compounds the hormonal barrier to losing that
fat.

**Attention fragmentation** — a trained expectation that new input will arrive every few seconds,
which produces genuine discomfort when input stops.

**Background processes** — commitments, goals, or decisions you haven't resolved yet, which
continue consuming mental energy in the background even while you aren't actively thinking about
them, the way an open app keeps running even when it's not the one on screen.

**BDNF (brain-derived neurotrophic factor)** — a compound released during exercise that supports
neuron survival and growth and is tied to memory, focus, and the ability to learn.

**Bliss point** — the specific combination of sugar, fat, and salt that food companies engineer
into a product to make it as hard as possible to stop eating.

**C-reactive protein** — a blood marker doctors use to track systemic inflammation.

**Cholesterol sulfate** — a compound the skin produces from UVB sun exposure, separate from vitamin
D, used in cell membrane function throughout the body.

**Cognitive tax** — the ongoing mental cost of keeping a commitment open (a goal, a habit, a
to-do item) whether or not it's actually producing anything for you.

**CreaT** — the specific transporter that actively pulls creatine out of the blood and into muscle
and brain cells.

**Creatinine** — the breakdown product of creatine, used by physicians as a kidney-function marker;
a slightly elevated reading from creatine supplementation is the source of the "creatine harms
your kidneys" myth.

**CYP1A2** — the gene that controls how quickly a person breaks down caffeine, explaining why the
same dose hits different people very differently.

**Default mode network** — the brain network that activates when attention isn't fixed on an
external task; credited with memory consolidation, processing experience, and generating creative
ideas.

**Effort discounting** — the brain treating effort as a genuine, subtracted cost against a reward's
value, the way a price is subtracted from a purchase decision.

**Endothelial function** — how well the lining of blood vessels dilates and responds, tied to blood
pressure regulation and cardiovascular risk.

**Familial hypercholesterolemia** — an inherited condition, present from birth, affecting roughly 1
in 250-300 people, that causes elevated cholesterol independent of diet or exercise and generally
requires medication.

**"Food noise"** — the constant background mental preoccupation with the next meal; many long-term
sufferers only recognize it as abnormal once it's gone (commonly reported by people on GLP-1
medications).

**Frontoparietal control network** — the brain system(s) responsible for sustained attention,
trainable and losable the same way physical fitness is.

**GABA** — a calming neurotransmitter; part of the mechanism through which magnesium supports
sleep.

**Ghrelin** — the hunger hormone; runs higher in people carrying more body fat and after poor
sleep, making hunger genuinely stronger, not just a matter of willpower.

**GLP-1 (glucagon-like peptide-1)** — a hormone the gut naturally produces after eating that
signals insulin release, slows digestion, and produces fullness; GLP-1 weight-loss medications are
a synthetic version delivered at a dose the body would never make on its own.

**Glymphatic system** — the brain's waste-clearance system, active mainly during deep sleep,
which flushes out metabolic waste including amyloid beta.

**Hedonic treadmill** — the tendency to return to a stable baseline level of happiness regardless
of what happens, positive or negative, which is part of why achievement alone rarely produces
lasting satisfaction.

**Heat shock proteins** — molecules triggered by heat stress (such as sauna use) that repair
damaged proteins inside cells.

**HPA axis** — short for hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal axis, the system that regulates stress
hormones; described as becoming dysregulated under chronic, ongoing stress.

**Journal speak** — a specific free-writing technique used before bed: write out whatever is in
your head for about 15 minutes and never reread it, purely to clear it rather than to solve
anything.

**Leptin** — the hormone that signals fullness/satiety to the brain; runs lower in people carrying
more body fat, making it genuinely harder to feel full on the same amount of food.

**Moai** — an Okinawan concept of small friend groups that commit to mutual support, sometimes for
life, cited as precedent for cohort-based accountability structures.

**Neuroplasticity** — the brain's tendency to reshape itself around whatever it repeatedly does,
for shallow, fragmented attention just as readily as for depth and focus.

**Nitric oxide** — a short-lived signaling molecule that relaxes blood vessels and lowers blood
pressure; released both by sun exposure on skin and by the friction of blood flow against vessel
walls.

**Phosphocreatine** — creatine with a phosphate group attached; the form that directly regenerates
a muscle's immediate energy supply (ATP) during short maximal effort.

**Polarized training model** — a training structure (roughly 80% low-intensity, 20% maximal
effort) originally studied in elite endurance athletes training many hours a week, later
over-generalized to casual exercisers for whom it doesn't clearly apply.

**Premeditatio malorum** — a Stoic practice of deliberately imagining the loss of what you have, as
a route to gratitude and sharper presence.

**Progressive overload** — the principle that continued strength or fitness gains require
gradually increasing the demand placed on the body (usually added weight), since adaptation slows
once the stress stops increasing.

**Rhabdomyolysis** — the breakdown of muscle tissue from prolonged compression under body weight
(such as after a fall), which can be severe enough to damage the kidneys.

**Sarcopenia** — age-related loss of muscle mass beginning in the 30s and accelerating with age,
directly tied to fall risk, frailty, and loss of independence later in life.

**"Second brain"** — a searchable, cross-linked personal knowledge base built from saved articles,
notes, and other consumed content.

**Senolytics** — compounds that selectively trigger aged, dysfunctional ("senescent") cells to die
off, an active area of longevity research.

**Social isolation vs. loneliness** — social isolation is the objective measure (few social
contacts, living alone); loneliness is the subjective felt experience of disconnection. The two
are related but distinct, and both are independently linked to worse health outcomes.

**Telomeres** — protective caps on the ends of chromosomes that shorten with each cell division;
their length is used as a marker of cellular aging.

**The shadow** — a Jungian concept for the parts of oneself a person avoids examining; achievement-
chasing is described as one common way of avoiding that confrontation.

**Urolithin A** — a compound produced by gut bacteria from foods like pomegranates and walnuts that
activates mitophagy (the recycling of damaged mitochondria); also sold as a standalone supplement.

**Vagus nerve** — one of the body's longest nerves, running from the brainstem through the gut;
central to switching the nervous system into its "rest and repair" (parasympathetic) state.

**VO2 max** — a measure of how much oxygen the body can use during hard exercise; considered the
gold-standard marker of aerobic fitness and one of the most consistent predictors of longevity in
population studies.
