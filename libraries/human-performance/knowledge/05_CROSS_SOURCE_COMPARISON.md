# 05 — Cross-Source Comparison

This library intentionally spans two creators (Seth Capehart MD and Dr. Mike/Matt Jones) plus,
where genuinely relevant, this project's other libraries. Each individual note's own "Conflicts
with other sources" section is the primary, timestamped record of these comparisons; this file
summarizes the patterns that showed up repeatedly once enough notes existed to see them.

## Strong convergence between the two creators, arrived at independently

Neither creator references the other by name anywhere in this collection. That makes it notable
when both land on the same conclusion from different starting points:

- **Subtraction over addition** as the fix for feeling overloaded or exhausted — one creator
  reasons from cognitive load and dopamine recalibration, the other from physiology and a
  societal time-budget argument. Same practical answer, different mechanism.
- **Chronic stress as more damaging than acute stress**, applied by one creator to cardiovascular
  and metabolic markers (blood pressure, cholesterol, cellular aging) and by the other to
  motivation, drive, and cognitive function. Neither treats stress itself as avoidable; both treat
  daily parasympathetic recovery as the actual lever.
- **Fitness and cardiorespiratory capacity mattering independently of body weight or appearance** —
  cited via population mortality data by one creator and via a specific "fat but fit" research
  finding by the other.
- **Physical friction beating willpower** for digital habits — both creators independently arrived
  at, and specifically recommend, the same category of lockable physical device rather than
  software-only limits, for the identical stated reason (software limits get overridden in the
  moment).

## Genuine tensions worth holding onto rather than smoothing over

- **Coffee/caffeine:** one video in this library's companion nutrition library argues against
  coffee specifically for methylxanthine-driven mineral depletion, while this library's dedicated
  caffeine video cites a large meta-analysis finding a mortality benefit around 3-3.5 cups/day.
  These aren't strictly incompatible (mineral loss could coexist with a net benefit at the
  population level), but neither source engages with the other's specific claim, and a reader
  should treat this as an open tension, not a resolved one.
- **Creatine, daily use versus food-first framing:** one creatine-focused video argues food should
  be the default source of nutrients like this where possible, while explicitly treating creatine
  as an exception worth supplementing directly since it's "just applied battery physics," not a
  whole-food nutrient profile — a distinction worth noting rather than treating all supplement
  recommendations as following one consistent rule.
- **Explore/fail small versus protect a few things intensely:** one video argues for deliberately
  trying many small, cheap, fast experiments; another argues for cutting distractions down to a
  small number of protected priorities. Read together rather than in isolation, these describe two
  different phases (exploring what's worth committing to, versus protecting something once it's
  working) rather than a real contradiction — but a reader skimming only one of the two could walk
  away with the opposite advice.

## A demonstrated self-correction, worth treating as a model

One video in this collection is an explicit, named reversal of a claim (zone 2 cardio's uniqueness
for mitochondrial health) the same creator had previously promoted on the same channel, citing a
specific 2025 research review rather than quietly dropping the earlier position. It's flagged here
because it's the clearest instance in this library of a source updating on evidence in public, and
it's a reasonable model for how to hold every other confidently stated claim in this collection —
provisionally, not as settled fact.

## Where this library connects to this project's other libraries

- The restorative-physiology library's treatment of general adaptation syndrome, progressive
  overload, and detraining timelines is a more physiologically detailed companion to this library's
  training-related notes, which tend to stay at a practical, prescription level.
- The restorative-physiology library's coverage of isometric wall sits (a specific, well-studied
  blood-pressure intervention) and this library's coverage of sauna use converge on a shared
  underlying vascular mechanism (nitric oxide, endothelial signaling) reached through two very
  different practices.
- The nutrition library's Paul Saladino content and this library's Seth Capehart content converge
  heavily on general dietary philosophy (whole/unprocessed food, seed oil and processed sugar as
  primary dietary concerns) despite being different creators and different libraries — worth
  reading as independent corroboration where the underlying claims overlap.
