# The REAL Cause of Brain Fog

## Central claim

**"Brain fog" isn't a diagnosis and isn't a normal thing to just accept — it's a symptom cluster
with three main identifiable, mostly fixable causes: poor sleep, unstable blood sugar, and chronic
stress.** `[01:01]` "It's not normal. It's not just stress. It's not just aging. It's definitely
not something you should be accepting as part of your regular life." Framed physiologically: "Your
brain is the most metabolically expensive organ in your body. It runs on a highly regulated system
of blood flow and glucose delivery and neurotransmitters and inflammation control."

## Cause one: sleep, via the glymphatic system

`[02:03]`-`[03:03]` — names the mechanism directly: the **glymphatic system**, active almost
exclusively during deep sleep, flushes metabolic waste from the brain, including **amyloid beta**
(the protein that accumulates in Alzheimer's disease). Cut sleep short and "the next day your brain
is literally operating with yesterday's garbage still inside of it. And that's not a metaphor,
that's actual physiology." States that even a single night of poor sleep "measurably impairs
memory, reaction time, decision making, and executive functions." **Fix:** 7-9 hours, consistent
timing, dark/cold/quiet room, no screens an hour before bed — and a sleep study if exhaustion
persists despite adequate hours, since he flags **sleep apnea** as commonly underdiagnosed.

## Cause two: blood sugar instability

`[03:03]`-`[05:06]` — the brain is "almost entirely dependent on glucose for fuel" and, unlike
muscle, can't readily switch to burning fat, so unstable blood sugar means an unreliable fuel
supply. Describes the standard cycle: processed-carb breakfast → glucose spike → insulin surge →
crash → craving → repeat, several times a day. **Names the familiar 2pm wall directly as this
mechanism, not laziness**, and notes a carb-heavy late meal can produce next-morning fog via an
overnight crash. **His own reported result** on switching to whole-food meals built around
protein/fat: "the afternoon crashes stopped suddenly, literally within days, not gradually."

## Cause three: chronic stress and cortisol

`[05:06]`-`[07:09]` — distinguishes acute cortisol (useful, short bursts) from chronic, never-fully-
resolving stress. **Cited finding:** chronically elevated cortisol "impairs the hippocampus, the
part of the brain responsible for memory and learning... research has shown it actually shrinks the
hippocampus over time," in addition to disrupting neurotransmitter production and degrading sleep
(looping back to cause one). **Interventions he names as having evidence specifically for cognitive
function, not just subjective wellbeing:** aerobic exercise, sleep, social connection, and
deliberate parasympathetic activation (breathwork, walking without headphones, time in nature). His
own practice: 10 minutes outside daily "in perfect silence," which he also runs inside his Primal 60
program.

## What doesn't work, and a qualified word on supplements

`[07:09]`-`[08:10]` — names numbing behaviors (alcohol, doom-scrolling, late-night bingeing) as
actively suppressing the recovery mechanisms needed, despite feeling like stress relief. On
supplements: not dismissive — names **omega-3s, magnesium, and vitamin D deficiency** as genuinely
linked to cognitive impairment when actually deficient — but states plainly: **"If you're sleeping 6
hours, eating processed food three times a day, and running on chronic stress, no supplement is
going to fix your brain fog... fix the foundation first."**

## Symptoms and body signals addressed

- Trouble concentrating, feeling mentally slow, forgetting why you walked into a room, losing your
  train of thought mid-sentence, feeling "half asleep" while technically awake `[01:01]`.
- A reliable 2pm energy/cognition wall `[04:04]`.
- Next-morning fog specifically following a carb-heavy late meal `[04:04]`.

## Glossary terms introduced

- **Glymphatic system** — the brain's waste-clearance system, active mainly during deep sleep,
  clearing metabolic waste including amyloid beta `[02:03]`.
- **Amyloid beta** — the protein cleared by the glymphatic system, also implicated in Alzheimer's
  disease `[02:03]`.

## Source-stated confidence

The glymphatic system and amyloid beta clearance during sleep is real, actively researched
neuroscience, stated without a specific citation here. The hippocampal-shrinkage-from-chronic-
cortisol claim is described as "research has shown," also without a named study. The blood-sugar
mechanism is standard physiology; his own "crashes stopped within days" report is explicitly
personal/anecdotal. The supplement section is notably calibrated — neither dismissing all
supplements nor endorsing "20 supplement stacks" — a more measured stance than a pure product pitch.
Closes with the standard Primal 60 Challenge / one-on-one coaching pitch.
