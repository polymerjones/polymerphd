# I Ate Primal for 60 Days and This Happened

## Central claim

**A 60-day elimination of all processed food and added sugar — eating only foods available before
the agricultural revolution — produced, in the speaker's own case and in "over a thousand" people
he's since put through the same protocol, a fairly consistent and specific sequence of physiological
changes, week by week, that he attributes to removing food engineered to override normal appetite
and metabolic regulation.** `[00:00]` "Primal eating is pretty simple. It's basically meat, fish,
eggs, vegetables, fruits, tubers, nuts, seeds, natural fats... If your great-great-grandmother would
have recognized this food, it's probably in."

## The reported timeline

`[01:00]`-`[08:07]`:

| Period | Reported changes |
|---|---|
| **Days 1-3** | Headaches, fogginess, cravings — attributed to the shift from burning glucose to burning fat; one community member (Nicholas) reported 7 hours of unbroken sleep by day 3, attributed to blood sugar stabilizing overnight |
| **Week 1** | Cravings quieting, steadier (not spike-crash) energy; his own **acid reflux fully resolving** after years of needing an inclined bed; his wife's **eczema (atopic dermatitis)** beginning to clear |
| **Week 2** | Afternoon energy crashes (his own recurring 2-3pm wall during ER shifts) disappearing; reported gains in focus and productivity, attributed to gut microbiome shift; a community member's seasonal allergies dropping to "a 0.5" on a 0-10 scale by May |
| **Weeks 3-4+** | Visible weight/composition changes reported by several named community members (8-12 lb loss, reduced waist measurement, "best sleep in years"); one member reaching a normal BMI for the first time in 20 years |

## The acid reflux claim, cited specifically

`[03:03]` — **"Multiple randomized controlled studies show that removing processed food and refined
sugar significantly reduces esophageal acid exposure. In one study, it was 45% of patients were able
to completely stop their acid reflux medications from diet alone."** No specific study is named by
author or journal in this transcript.

## The morning-wood claim

`[04:03]` — in response to a community member's post, he states directly: **"Morning wood... is
actually a clinically recognized indicator of healthy testosterone and cardiovascular function"** —
attributing improved testosterone production, reduced inflammation, and improved blood flow to
removing ultra-processed food, without citing a specific study for the testosterone-production
claim itself.

## The stated mechanism, summarized

`[08:07]` — **"Ultra-processed food is engineered to override your biology. It spikes your blood
sugar, it drives insulin, it promotes fat storage, it creates inflammation throughout your body, it
disrupts your gut, it messes with your hormones, it keeps you hungry so that you eat more of it. And
that is by design."**

## Symptoms and body signals addressed

- Acid reflux severe enough to require an inclined bed, resolved within about a week `[02:02]`.
- Atopic eczema, resolving within the first week `[03:03]`.
- Recurring 2-3pm energy crashes, disappearing by week two `[05:04]`.
- Seasonal allergy symptoms (sneezing, itchy eyes, sinus headaches), dropping sharply `[06:05]`.
- Loose-fitting clothes, waist reduction, and improved sleep reported at 3-4+ weeks `[07:06]`.

## Source-stated confidence

Mixed: the acid-reflux/RCT claim is stated with real specificity (a percentage, "randomized
controlled studies") but no named source; most other claims (eczema, allergies, afternoon energy,
morning wood) are supported by individual community-member testimonials rather than controlled
data, and are explicitly anecdotal even though framed with physiological explanations layered on
top. The video includes a paid sponsor segment (a body-composition scale/app, with a discount code)
disclosed as such, and closes with a pitch for his own recurring paid program, the Primal 60
Challenge, which he also runs himself and profits from — a commercial interest worth flagging
alongside the testimonials, since the people quoted are current or former paying members of that
same program.
