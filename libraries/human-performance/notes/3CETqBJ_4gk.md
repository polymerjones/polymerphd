# Why You Need To Fail

## Central claim

**Most people don't fail because they're bad at what they do — they fail to succeed because they
don't fail often enough, in small enough doses, to build the skills success requires.** `[00:00]`
"Most people don't fail because they're bad at what they're doing. They fail because they don't
fail enough... That's the reason that my success now looks fast even though it took years to
build." He's explicit this isn't about big, life-altering failure: "I mean small, contained, low
stakes failures."

## The personal case for it

`[02:02]`-`[03:03]` — In college, he ran a GoPro rental business that lost him roughly $2,000
(most of his money at the time) spread over a year — "I didn't win at this business... but I
gained skills that never left me." He describes repeating the pattern deliberately: "apps that
failed... tools that never launched," and an early, embarrassing livestreaming attempt in medical
school that took four to five years to reach 115,000 followers while still unprofitable. **The
throughline he draws:** `[04:05]` "Success compounds on skills... you may be going like this for a
very long period of time with ever so slight improvements and then your success just skyrockets.
What that often comes down to is you have finally developed the skills to break through the
threshold."

## What's actually blocking people, by his account

`[05:05]` — Not fear of losing money specifically: **"They're afraid of looking stupid, wasting
effort, starting over, not being instantly good. So instead of trying, they plan and plan some
more and plan forever."** He offers a counter-example from the week of filming: he fully scoped an
app idea (competitors, backend, API costs) down to a concrete $200 launch cost, then chose not to
launch it — **"Am I going to launch it? No, not right now. But I understand a heck of a lot more
about the market. Learning is the point here."**

## The four-step framework

`[07:08]`-`[09:09]` — presented as the mechanism, not just anecdote:

1. **Ask what skill would help regardless of outcome** — not "I want to build an app," but "what
   skill would make my future easier no matter what" (marketing, writing, sales, editing, audience
   building).
2. **Constrain the damage** — a hard time cap, money cap, and emotional cap set in advance ("I'll
   give this one weekend. I'll spend no more than $300... If it doesn't work, I will shut it down
   without doubling down"). **States the mechanism directly:** "People don't procrastinate because
   they're lazy. They procrastinate because the risk feels infinite. You need to make it finite."
3. **Build ugly, fast, and real** — "your first version will probably be slightly embarrassing. If
   it's not, you waited way too long."
4. **Make a decision** — kill it, iterate, or double down, with no dragging it out: "Most people...
   suffer not because they fail, because they linger."

**The closing reframe:** `[06:06]` "I didn't succeed just because my ideas worked. I succeeded
because I stopped needing them to."

## Symptoms and body signals addressed

- Feeling "stuck," capable of more but hesitating, overthinking, or waiting for a better idea
  `[00:00]`.
- Paralysis specifically identified as fear of looking stupid or wasting effort, not fear of the
  stated financial risk `[05:05]`.

## Glossary terms introduced

- **"Buying skills at a discount"** — the speaker's own reframe for a failed attempt: not a loss,
  a purchase of experience at low cost `[04:05]`.
- **"Low stakes experiment plan"** — his name for the four-step framework above `[07:08]`.

## Analogies worth reusing

- **"Buying skills at a discount"** rather than "failing" `[04:05]`.
- **The exponential curve** — long flat accumulation, then a sudden visible payoff, attributed to
  finally crossing a skill threshold rather than luck `[05:05]`.
