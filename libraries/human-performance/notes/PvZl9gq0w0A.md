# What Your Feed Is Actually Doing to Your Brain

## Central claim

**Feeds are not optimized to show you what you like — they're optimized to keep you on the
platform, and the fastest way to do that is to make you feel something strongly (outrage, anxiety,
comparison), which over time shifts your baseline emotional state upward rather than just wasting
time.** `[02:03]` "The algorithm is not trying to show you things that you like. It's trying to
keep you on the platform. Those are two very different things... Outrage keeps most people
scrolling longer than happiness does."

## The clinical pattern he reports seeing

`[03:03]`-`[05:05]` — patients presenting with chronic low-level anxiety and irritability who
attribute it to everything except their media diet: **"I'm just not myself anymore. It must be
this"** — and when he investigates daily inputs specifically, "a lot of times, it really starts to
make sense." He's explicit this is a real, actionable clinical lever: **"That is one less
prescription I have to write someone... that is a dang successful day in my practice."** He also
adds an unusually direct disclaimer distinguishing personal interpretation from data — see
Source-stated confidence.

## Baseline drift, and why it's hard to notice

`[05:05]`-`[08:09]` — the mechanism as he frames it: constant exposure to emotional swings
recalibrates what feels normal, so genuine calm starts to feel like something's missing rather than
like relief. **His own reported experience:** "I couldn't relax at the end of the day without
picking up my phone... when I would put it down there was this like residual noise. And my nervous
system was like waiting for something to react to. It's like it was so ready. It was primed. That
isn't a biological flaw. That's trained." **Stated costs**, some flagged explicitly as his own
interpretation rather than cited data: elevated perceived stress, harder time returning to calm
after a stressor, negativity bias, deep work and reading becoming harder, and ordinary conversation
starting to feel like it's "missing something."

## The intervention: friction over willpower

`[12:13]`-`[15:17]` — states directly that this shouldn't be treated as a willpower problem, since
"these systems are engineered specifically to override your intentions." His specific
recommendation: a physical NFC-tag app blocker (named product: Brick) that cannot be overridden
from the phone itself, unlike built-in screen-time limits — **"I went like 38 days with my social
media's blocked, and I do this for a living."** The second half of the intervention: give the
nervous system something to return to — 20 minutes daily of genuinely unstimulated time (a walk
without audio, sitting without a screen, journaling), explicitly framed as making space for
whatever unprocessed material surfaces once the noise stops, rather than avoiding that discomfort.

## Symptoms and body signals addressed

- Feeling worse (anxious, distant, irritable, less present) immediately after finishing a scrolling
  session `[00:00]`.
- Chronic low-grade anxiety and irritability with no identified cause, in a clinical setting
  `[03:03]`.
- A reflexive urge to pick up the phone the moment stimulation stops, including during trivial
  moments (his own bathroom example) `[06:07]`.
- Ordinary conversation or reading starting to feel like it's missing intensity `[10:10]`.

- **"The algorithm is not trying to show you things that you like. It's trying to keep you on the
  platform."** `[02:03]`
- **A trained, primed nervous system "waiting for something to react to"** even in silence
  `[06:07]`.
- **Physical friction versus willpower** — a lock that requires a physical action to open versus a
  software timer that can be overridden in the moment `[12:13]`.

## Source-stated confidence

Unusually explicit about separating interpretation from evidence for a video in this creator's
catalog: `[07:08]` **"This whole thing I just did, that is not something that I have like sheer
scientific data on. That's more my interpretation of the data... it to me makes common sense, but I
haven't found like a clear representation of that concept."** He states plainly that this
particular baseline-drift mechanism is his own synthesis, not a cited finding — a hedge worth
noting since it isn't always present in this creator's videos. The claim that platforms are
engineered around engagement-maximizing emotional activation is broadly consistent with public
reporting and internal-document disclosures about recommendation-algorithm design, though no
specific source is cited in this transcript. The Brick recommendation carries a disclosed affiliate
link alongside a non-affiliate alternative.
