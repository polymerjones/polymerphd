# Loneliness is a biological advantage for cancer | David Linden

## Central claim

**Speaking as a neuroscientist with a terminal cancer diagnosis, Linden argues that psychosocial
support, love, and exercise affect cancer outcomes through specific, increasingly-understood
biological pathways — not merely as comforting behavioral advice — because tumors and nerves
actively signal to each other, and disrupting supportive signaling (isolation, chronic stress)
measurably worsens outcomes while supportive signaling measurably improves them.** `[09:11]` "It
would be obviously comforting to think that social support, psychotherapy, the love of one's
family and friends, and social connectedness can help you have a better cancer outcome... is this
just something we like to think, or is it real?... there is enough epidemiological evidence
suggesting that psychosocial support... does seem to have some beneficial effect on cancer
progression."

**Note on this note's title:** the word "loneliness" does not actually appear anywhere in this
transcript. The video's entire argument runs in the positive direction — presence of social
support/love suppressing cancer progression via specific biological pathways — and the title
appears to be the platform's own inversion of that argument (the absence of that support would
logically favor the tumor), not a claim Linden states directly. This note follows what is actually
said.

## How a tumor recruits and co-opts nerves

`[04:05]`–`[06:09]` Linden describes cancer as actively co-opting other body systems, not merely
uncontrolled cell division: tumors secrete **neurotrophins** (nerve growth factor, BDNF) that draw
nerve fibers to grow into the tumor. In turn, pain-sensing nerves secrete a molecule called
**CGRP**, which suppresses the CD8-positive T lymphocytes patrolling the tumor's edge — normally,
these cells "nibble" at and kill edge cells, restraining growth. **"Tumors, when they are
innervated, your prognosis becomes worse. And if we can prevent tumors from becoming innervated,
this is a whole new promising realm in cancer therapy."**

## From mouse experiments to a proposed mechanism in humans

`[07:10]`–`[12:14]` Citing lab work from Asya Rolls's laboratory in Israel: mice given artificial
heart attacks recovered heart function faster when their brain's dopaminergic reward/anticipation
circuitry (the ventral tegmental area) was artificially activated — mirroring the known clinical
finding that humans with social support recover better from heart attacks. Linden extends this to
his own case, proposing (explicitly as **speculation, "we don't entirely know"**) that his wife's
love activates this same reward circuitry, which may suppress his tumor's growth via direct nerve
signaling and/or boost his immune system's tumor-fighting capacity via cytokine signaling.
Separately, **beta-blockers** (heart-rate/anxiety medications) are named as slowing progression in
cancers bearing beta-adrenergic receptors, since noradrenaline signaling through those receptors is
said to promote tumor growth — offered as an example of how brain-to-body signaling research can
inform new drug therapies, not just behavioral advice.

## Exercise: randomized evidence, not just correlation

`[12:14]`–`[14:16]` Linden explicitly flags the standard confound in correlational exercise-cancer
studies (healthier or less-sick people may simply be more likely to exercise) before citing a
specific 2025 Australian randomized controlled trial in colon cancer patients: **the exercise
group showed a 30% improvement in mortality over 8 years** — a result he says would be treated as a
blockbuster drug effect if it came from a pill. His stated conclusion: **"If you're battling cancer,
and you can bring yourself to do it, exercise is one of the very very best things that you can
do."**

## The brain as a prediction machine, and why we can't truly imagine our own death

`[22:29]`–`[24:34]` Reflecting on his own diagnosis, Linden describes being unable to cognitively
"engage with the idea of my own death" despite handling its practical logistics — and reframes this
as a structural feature of a healthy human mind, not a personal failing. He cites a shift in
neuroscience away from viewing the resting brain as passive: **"the brain is a prediction
machine... trying to predict the near future,"** which presupposes a future will exist. He
speculates (again explicitly as his own extrapolation) that this same future-predicting circuitry
may be the evolutionary root of the near-universal presence of afterlife beliefs across the world's
religions, despite stating his own lack of religious faith.

## Symptoms and body signals addressed

None presented as symptoms to self-diagnose from — this is a first-person illness narrative and
mechanistic explanation rather than a checklist of signs.

## Glossary terms introduced

- **Neurotrophins (including NGF and BDNF)** — signaling molecules a tumor secretes to attract
  nerve growth into itself. `[05:06]`
- **CGRP (calcitonin gene-related peptide)** — secreted by pain-sensing nerves, suppresses the
  immune cells (CD8-positive T lymphocytes) that would otherwise attack a tumor's edge. `[06:09]`
- **Beta-adrenergic receptor** — a receptor for noradrenaline/norepinephrine present on some tumor
  cells, targeted (via beta-blocker drugs) as a way to slow cancer progression in a subset of
  cancers. `[11:13]`

## Analogies worth reusing

Being simultaneously "exceedingly angry and exceedingly grateful" at diagnosis — used to challenge
the assumption that mental states are unitary (fight-or-flight versus rest), illustrating that
divergent emotional states can coexist. `[19:26]`

## Source-stated confidence

Explicitly and repeatedly hedged: Linden flags the proposed link between his wife's love and his
own tumor suppression as speculative ("we don't entirely know," "it is also very likely," offered
as one of two possible pathways), distinct from the more established, cited evidence for exercise
(a named 2025 randomized controlled trial) and psychosocial support generally (described as
"epidemiological evidence," not proof, and explicit that it "can't replace chemotherapy or surgery
or radiation"). The afterlife-belief speculation is explicitly flagged as his own extrapolation
("I would speculate").
