# Insect Bites and Stings | Adult First Aid | EMT First Aid Training

## Central claim

**Bee and wasp stings require different first-aid technique because only a bee leaves its stinger
embedded — a bee sting must be scraped out sideways with a flat edge, never squeezed or pulled with
tweezers, both of which push more venom into the wound — while general insect bites are treated
with wash, cold compress, and elevation, and every sting or bite should be watched for signs of
anaphylaxis.** `[02:01]` "With a bee sting, we can remove that quite easily by using a credit card
or a business card and we scrape it away. We don't use tweezers, because if we use tweezers... we
can inject any poison that's still left in that sting into the casualty."

## Bee vs. wasp: the mechanical difference driving different treatment

`[01:00]`–`[02:01]` The source frames this as a common point of confusion: "the reason I know it's
a bee that stung her and not a wasp is because there's a sting still left inside... wasps, although
they sting you, do not leave the sting behind, whereas bees do." This single anatomical fact is
what determines whether scraping-removal technique is needed at all.

## Escalation: infection vs. allergic reaction, and anaphylaxis specifically

`[04:03]`–`[05:04]` The source draws a diagnostic distinction: **"redness and swelling that starts
and spreads quickly is more likely due to an allergy than to an infection,"** whereas infection
develops more gradually with pus, worsening pain, or systemic signs like fever and swollen glands.
Anaphylaxis-specific signs needing an emergency call are listed directly: wheezing, breathing
difficulty, facial swelling, nausea/vomiting, increased heart rate, dizziness, or faintness.

## Symptoms and body signals addressed

Bee/wasp sting: local pain, swelling, and — if allergic — a life-threatening reaction. General
insect bite: itchy red lumps or swelling appearing immediately and lasting up to two hours, often
followed by a firmer itchy lump developing up to 24 hours later and lasting several days. Infection
signs: redness, tenderness, pus, spreading over several days. Anaphylaxis signs as listed above.
