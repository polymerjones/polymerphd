# What is a SPINAL injury, How do you get a SPINAL injury and HOW to treat a SPINAL injury

## Central claim

**The core rule for a suspected spinal injury is not to move the person and to support their head
— "there's always going to be a possibility they might have a spine injury, so we always want to
keep them as still as possible" — with moving them justified only by immediate danger, a
compromised airway, or the need to perform CPR.** `[01:00]` "The simple rule to remember if a
spinal injury... is if you suspect someone's hurt their back, tell them to keep still, do not move
them."

## Why: what spinal cord damage actually does

`[00:00]` The stated reasoning ties directly to anatomy: "our spinal cord is where the brain sends
signals to the rest of the body, and if that spinal cord becomes damaged it just can't be
repaired." The location of damage determines its severity — lower-spine damage is described as
risking loss of leg function, while higher damage "can result in a loss of control to my vital
life support systems such as my breathing system." Treating every suspected case as potentially
serious, rather than waiting to find out, follows directly from this "can't be repaired" framing.

## The log roll: the one sanctioned way to move someone

`[02:01]`–`[03:01]` When movement truly can't be avoided — turning someone face-down to perform
CPR, or because they're vomiting — the source specifies a **log roll**: the patient is turned "in a
single motion," requiring several people, with one person dedicated to supporting the head and
keeping it aligned with the body throughout. The person at the head is described as directing the
roll, which the source frames as requiring "good communication skills" among the helpers.
