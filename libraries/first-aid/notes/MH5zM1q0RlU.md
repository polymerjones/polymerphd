# Reach, Throw, Don't Go — what to do if someone's struggling in water

## Central claim

**A bystander's instinct to go into the water after a struggling swimmer is explicitly discouraged
— "people drown trying to rescue people. You become the second casualty" — with the recommended
response instead being to call for rescue services, tell the casualty to float, and reach or throw
something buoyant rather than entering the water.** `[00:00]` "If someone's struggling in the
water, your instinct is to go in after them. Please don't... Reach, throw, do not go."

## The float instruction, and self-rescue

`[00:00]` The instruction to shout at the casualty is specific: "float. Head back, ears in the
water, relax. It costs you nothing and it buys them minutes." The same instruction is stated to
apply to the rescuer if roles reverse: "if you're the one who falls in, float. Cold water shock
takes your breathing away and fighting it is what drowns people."

## Symptoms and body signals addressed

None in the sense of physical symptoms — the video addresses a rescue-decision scenario (a
struggling swimmer) rather than post-rescue signs to check for.

## Glossary terms introduced

- **Cold water shock** — described as taking away a person's breathing, with fighting the
  sensation (rather than floating through it) named as the actual mechanism of drowning. `[00:00]`
