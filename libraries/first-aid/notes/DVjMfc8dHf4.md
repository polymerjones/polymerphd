# How to help a baby choking

## Central claim

**An infant's choking response keeps the airway in a neutral position throughout and uses chest
thrusts in line with the nipples rather than the abdominal thrusts used on an adult or child,
alternating up to five back slaps with up to five chest thrusts until the blockage clears or the
infant becomes unresponsive.** `[01:02]` "The difference with a thrust is actually in line with
their nipples. It's not down here in the abdominals like it is on a child and adults."

## Why infant airway neutrality matters here too

`[00:00]` As in this channel's recovery-position videos, the source repeats that tilting an
infant's head back — the technique used to open an adult's airway — actually closes an infant's:
"if we took the head back, which we would do on ourself to open an airway, on a baby that actually
closes the airway. Same if it goes forward, this will close the airway." The choking response is
performed with a hand kept on the jawline specifically to preserve this neutral position throughout.

## Distinguishing a thrust from a compression

`[02:02]` The source is explicit that the chest thrust used here is not the same motion as a CPR
compression, despite the similar hand position: "it looks like a compression, but it's not. It's a
thrust... harder but slower in between. Whereas a compression is quite fast."

## Symptoms and body signals addressed

A choking infant: going "very starry-eyed," gasping, making no noise. Recovery of the airway is
signaled by the infant crying or gasping for air.
