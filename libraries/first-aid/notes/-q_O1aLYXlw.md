# How to Stop a Nosebleed (The Right Way)

## Central claim

**Contrary to the common instinct to tilt the head back, the correct technique for a nosebleed is
to lean forward and pinch the soft part of the nose (not the bony bridge) with firm, uninterrupted
pressure for 10 minutes.** `[00:00]` "Stop tilting your head back during a nose bleed... you need
to lean forward, not back... pinch the soft part of your nose just below the bony bridge... hold
for firm pressure for 10 minutes straight. And we're not going to let go."

## Why tilting back is wrong, and when to escalate

`[00:00]` Stated reason to avoid tilting back: it "sends blood down your throat, which can make
you feel sick or even vomit." Escalation thresholds given: seek medical help "if the bleeding won't
stop after 30 minutes, or if it's heavy, or if it's followed because you've actually hit your head"
— i.e., a nosebleed following a head injury. `[01:02]` The clip closes mid-sentence on a follow
prompt rather than additional first-aid content.

## Symptoms and body signals addressed

A nosebleed, including the escalation signs of heavy bleeding, bleeding lasting over 30 minutes, or
occurring after a head injury.
