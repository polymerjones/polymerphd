# How to treat a bleed | Adult First Aid | EMT First Aid Training

## Central claim

**Any bleeding wound follows the same core sequence — clean it if there's time, put pressure on
it, and dress it — but the exact technique is adapted by wound location: a fist for a
palm wound running across the hand, and wrapping at the base of the skull (not higher) for a head
wound.** `[00:00]` "Clean the wound, put pressure on the wound, and put a dressing on there... you
just need to be able to adapt the skills I'm going to show you into any different bleed that
someone's going to have."

## Why speed can override cleaning, and why one dressing at a time

`[00:00]` Cleaning is explicitly deprioritized when bleeding is severe: "depending on the type of
bleed, you might not have time to be able to clean it because actually you need to stop the
bleeding. If you don't, they're going to go into hypovolemic shock." `[02:02]` If blood soaks
through a dressing, the source's instruction is to remove it and apply direct pressure with a
gloved hand rather than adding another layer on top, then dress again once bleeding has stopped —
"one bandage at a time."

## The palm-wound fist technique

`[02:02]`–`[03:03]` A specific and easy-to-get-backwards detail: "if the wound is up and down,
however, if you make a fist, it will actually make the wound open, which is worse. So, if you've
got a wound going across the way, get them to put the gauze in the hand, make a fist" — the fist
trick only applies to a wound crossing the palm, not one running up and down it.

## Symptoms and body signals addressed

Signs implied rather than named directly: continued bleeding through a dressing (needs replacing),
and the broader risk of hypovolemic shock from uncontrolled blood loss.

## Glossary terms introduced

- **Hypovolemic shock** — explained in the source's own words as "hypo, low, volemic, volume. So
  low volume, meaning they've lost fluids, therefore their blood pressure is dropping." `[00:00]`

## Analogies worth reusing

The bandaged fist described as looking "like a boxing glove," with the thumb left free to tie the
bandage — a concrete visual for the palm-wound dressing technique. `[03:03]`

## Source-stated confidence

Presented as direct, professionally-delivered procedural instruction with no cited guideline
document — the channel's own stated standard technique.
