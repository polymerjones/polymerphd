# Diabetes | Adult First Aid | EMT First Aid Training

## Central claim

**When a diabetic emergency's exact type is uncertain, the source's stated rule is to give sugar
regardless and call emergency services — reasoning that sugar will not harm someone who turns out
to be hyperglycemic, but could be lifesaving for someone who is hypoglycemic and deteriorating
quickly.** `[04:03]` "You will not hurt somebody who's already in a hyperglycemic attack... but you
could save someone's life in a hypo... if you can't take a reading, give them some sugar."

## Distinguishing hyper from hypo, and why testing beats guessing

`[01:01]`–`[03:03]` The source gives a rough behavioral distinction — hyperglycemia has a slower
onset with thirst, frequent urination, and fruity-smelling breath, while hypoglycemia comes on fast
with dizziness, aggression, irritability, and sweating — but is explicit that **"the only way
you're really going to know for sure is by getting a blood sugar measurement,"** stating normal
range as 4–7, with readings above 7 hyperglycemic and below 4 hypoglycemic.

## Two testing methods, and where a first aider's role stops

`[02:02]` The source describes a traditional finger pin-prick test and a newer arm-worn sensor read
via a Bluetooth device (lasting about a week), noting the sensor is less invasive since "you're just
holding a device up to it." But the stated first-aid boundary is firm: **"as a first aider, we
should not be doing pin-prick tests on anybody... unless of course our job has provided the training
for us to be able to do that"** (naming childminders and nursery workers with a care plan as an
exception) — encouraging the person to test themselves rather than testing them.

## Symptoms and body signals addressed

Hyperglycemic: thirst, frequent urination, fruity-smelling breath, slower onset. Hypoglycemic:
dizziness, aggression, irritability, sweating, fast onset.

## Glossary terms introduced

None beyond hyperglycemic/hypoglycemic, which the video treats as terms to distinguish rather than
newly introduce.
