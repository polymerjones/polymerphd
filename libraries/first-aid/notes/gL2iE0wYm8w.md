# How to help with a fracture | Adult First Aid | EMT First Aid Training

## Central claim

**Regardless of fracture type — closed, open, complicated, or greenstick — the first-aid treatment
is the same: immobilize the injury and keep the person still, rather than attempting to move, jump
up, or self-splint, calling an ambulance for anything serious enough that safe transport isn't
possible.** `[02:01]` "Just remember the treatment for a fracture is to immobilize it."

## The four fracture types named

`[00:00]` **Closed**: bone snapped but still inside the body. **Open**: bone broken and piercing
through the skin. **Complicated**: a break causing a secondary complication such as "piercing a
lung, trapping a nerve." **Greenstick/hairline**: common in children, "because a bones are still
quite bendy." Signs across all types: pain, swelling, lack of movement, and possible visible
deformity, with an X-ray needed for confirmation in most cases.

## Two sling techniques, demonstrated step by step

`[01:01]`–`[05:03]` **Support sling** (for a sprained wrist, broken humerus, or forearm bones): the
triangular bandage's long side runs vertically, slid between the arm and body with the point toward
the injured elbow, both ends tied at the side of the neck — "not behind the neck because it can
pull the head forward." **Elevation sling** (for a suspected broken clavicle or dislocated
shoulder): the bandage lays over the top of the arm rather than underneath, tucked from the
fingers down to the elbow with the palm facing up, gathered and tied behind the back, avoiding
direct pressure over the injured collarbone or shoulder. A specific update is noted in passing:
"you would have noticed in the previous videos we don't actually elevate bleeds anymore" — this
sling's name refers to arm position, not the now-discontinued bleeding-elevation practice.

## When not to move the casualty at all

`[06:05]` For a pelvic fracture, femur fracture, or skull fracture: "make sure you call the
ambulance, you cannot move these casualties, you'll only make it worse if you move them" — a firmer
instruction than the general "immobilize" guidance for less severe fractures.

## Symptoms and body signals addressed

Pain, swelling, lack of movement, visible deformity at the injury site; for an open fracture,
visible bone through broken skin.
