# Hypothermia | Adult First Aid | EMT First Aid Training

## Central claim

**Hypothermia must be rewarmed slowly, not quickly — rapid rewarming is stated to risk causing
cardiac arrest — with wet clothing removed immediately since water draws heat from the body far
faster than air.** `[00:00]` "It's really important actually not to reheat them too quickly, as
this can complicate the matters and it can actually cause a cardiac arrest."

## Why wet clothing removal is urgent

`[00:00]` The stated mechanism: "water or wet clothing can draw heat out of the body 20 to 25 times
faster than actual air itself" — so removing wet clothing and gently drying the person is
prioritized even before formal rewarming begins.

## Frost nip vs. frostbite: different severity, different treatment

`[02:04]` **Frost nip**: skin freezes, turns red and white, is painful, but can be self-treated by
warming the area (e.g. tucking fingertips under the armpits) — with a caution to watch that it
doesn't progress further. **Frostbite**: described as more serious, where "the body's tissues,
muscles, and blood vessels freeze." Its rewarming rule is stricter and the opposite instinct from
what might seem intuitive: **"never rub or massage frostbitten areas because that's going to make
it worse"**, and rewarming should use water no warmer than 40°C, done slowly, with emergency medical
guidance since the process "is usually quite painful."

## Prevention, and a myth about foil blankets

`[01:02]` The source stresses hypothermia isn't only an outdoor risk — it can happen at home,
"especially in the winter when people are trying to save on their energy costs." A specific
correction: **"a foil blanket from a first aid kit is not sufficient in warming someone up — all it
does is keep them warm"** — framing it as a heat-retention tool, not an active rewarming one.

## Symptoms and body signals addressed

Uncontrollable shivering, disorientation, confusion, and in severe cases unresponsiveness with a
pulse "so slow and so weak it's very difficult to detect."

## Glossary terms introduced

- **Frost nip** — skin freezing that turns red and white and is painful, but is milder than and
  can precede frostbite. `[02:04]`
- **Frostbite** — freezing of the body's tissues, muscles, and blood vessels; more serious than
  frost nip, requiring slow rewarming and professional guidance. `[02:04]`

None distinct.
