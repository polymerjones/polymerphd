# Panic Attacks | Adult First Aid | EMT First Aid Training

## Central claim

**A panic attack is frightening but not physically dangerous, and the first-aid response is
reassurance and staying with the person rather than treating it as a medical emergency — reserving
that escalation for genuine uncertainty about whether something more serious is happening.**
`[02:02]` "Although panic attacks are frightening, they're not dangerous. An attack will not cause
you any physical harm and it's unlikely you're going to be admitted to hospital if you're having
one."

## The "cycle of living in fear of fear"

`[00:00]` The source describes how panic disorder can become self-sustaining: someone may "start to
avoid certain situations because you're fearful... they're going to trigger another attack. This
can create a cycle of living in fear of fear and it can add to the sense of panic and may cause you
to have more attacks" — framing avoidance itself as a driver of further attacks, not just a
response to them.

## Symptom overlap as a genuine safety concern

`[02:02]` The source is explicit that panic-attack symptoms can mimic other conditions: **"most of
these symptoms can also be symptoms of other conditions or problems, so you may not always be
having a panic attack, and that's why it is important to call the emergency medical services if
you're in doubt."**

## Longer-term treatment: CBT and medication

`[04:03]` Two main treatment routes are named: cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT), described as
teaching "ways of changing your behavior to help you keep calm during an attack," and
antidepressant medication, with an explicit timeline caveat — "antidepressants can take two to four
weeks before they start to work, and up to eight weeks to work fully" — alongside instruction to
keep taking medication even if it doesn't seem to be working yet, unless a doctor advises
otherwise.

## Symptoms and body signals addressed

Racing heartbeat, feeling faint or dizzy, sweating, clamminess, nausea, chest pain, shortness of
breath, trembling, hot flushes, chills, shaky limbs, a choking sensation, numbness or pins-and-
needles (especially in extremities), dry mouth, an urge to go to the toilet, ringing in the ears,
fear of dying, and a feeling of disconnection from one's own body. Attacks are stated to typically
last 5–20 minutes, occasionally up to an hour.
