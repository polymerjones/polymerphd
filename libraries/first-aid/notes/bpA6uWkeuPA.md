# Recovery Position for Children and Infants

## Central claim

**The channel's primary-survey/secondary-survey/recovery-position sequence is stated to apply
across ages, but an infant (0-1) requires two specific adaptations: the airway must be kept
completely neutral rather than tilted, and the recovery position is a cradle hold rather than a
floor roll.** `[07:04]` "We still do our primary survey, we still do our secondary survey, we still
do the recovery position, but we just adapt it."

## Why infant airway handling is different

`[08:05]` The stated reason: "if you put a baby's head all the way back... it actually closes the
airway. And the same if we have the baby's head all the way forward, it will close the airway. So,
with baby's airway, we need to have a completely neutral airway." This directly inverts the
adult/child head-tilt chin-lift technique used earlier in the same video.

## The infant recovery position

`[09:05]` Rather than rolling the infant onto their side on the floor, the stated technique is to
cradle them: "keeping the hand on the head to make sure the baby's airway is neutral... keeping the
head slightly lower than bottom, so if the baby is sick, they're not going to choke on any vomit...
just cradling the baby," facing either toward or away from the rescuer.

## Symptoms and body signals addressed

Age-specific normal-breathing benchmarks used during the primary survey: roughly 2-4 breaths per 10
seconds for an adult (stated slightly differently here than in `ccDPTLFu9Po`'s "2 to 3"), around 10
for a baby in this video's figure, and 8-10 given later in the same video for the infant secondary
check — the source is not fully internally consistent on the exact infant number, which is worth
noting rather than smoothing over.
