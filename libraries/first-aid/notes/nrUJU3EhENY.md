# How to treat Burns | Adult First Aid | EMT First Aid Training

## Central claim

**Regardless of cause, the treatment for any burn is to cool it under running water for at least
20 minutes, and — if skin has been removed — protect it afterward with cling film loosely layered
over the area, while severity is judged using the SCALD framework (size, cause, age, location,
depth).** `[02:07]` "The treatment is to cool the burn down for at least 20 minutes... always cool
all burns down for 20 minutes." `[04:09]` "So we cool the burn down for 20 minutes and if we've
taken a layer of skin off we must cover it with a layer of cling film to protect it."

## Hot sand as an under-recognized burn source

`[01:06]`–`[02:07]` The video opens with a real example: a friend walked roughly 15 seconds on
beach sand without shoes and sustained second-degree burns, because "the sand can get up to 80
degrees celsius" even when air temperature is a mild 26–30°C. This is used to make the point that
burns aren't limited to fire or cookers — the stated moral is "please always wear footwear on the
beach."

## Judging severity: SCALD and the rule of nines

`[05:09]`–`[06:09]` **Size** is estimated with the rule of nines (a hand ≈ 1% of body surface, the
head 9%, front and back of the torso 18% each, each leg 18%, each arm 9%). **Cause**: electrical
burns are called out as more serious because there are two burn sites (entry and exit). **Age**:
older and younger skin is described as thinner and more burn-prone. **Location**: burns near the
airway are flagged as especially dangerous for breathing. **Depth**: superficial (discoloration
only), partial-thickness (blistering, may burst), and full-thickness (through all skin layers) —
with the counterintuitive note that full-thickness burns "could sometimes be really painful but
other times not because all the nerve endings have actually been removed."

## When to escalate to medical help

`[07:10]`–`[08:11]` Escalation triggers named: a burn on a child or baby, a burn larger than about
1% of body surface (the size of the person's own palm), a full-thickness burn, a burn that "goes
around a whole body part," or a burn on the head, hands, feet, or genitals — plus a direct
prohibition on creams, ointments, touching the burn, bursting a blister, or removing stuck-on
clothing.

## Symptoms and body signals addressed

Burn depth signs: discoloration (superficial), blistering that may burst (partial-thickness), and
skin damage through all layers, sometimes with reduced pain due to nerve destruction
(full-thickness). Infection is also flagged as a delayed complication once skin's protective
barrier is gone.
