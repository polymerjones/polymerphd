# What to put inside your first aid kit | HSE BS 8599 | EMT First Aid Training

## Central claim

**A workplace first aid kit's contents should be driven by a first-aid-needs assessment specific
to that workplace, not a fixed checklist — with the BS 8599 standard offered as a reference point,
not a legal requirement — and should exclude "potions, lotions, and sprays" like painkillers and
medicated creams that a first aider isn't qualified to give.** `[00:00]` "The health and safety
exec... simply state the content of your workplace first aid kit should be based on your first aid
needs assessment... by law your kit doesn't have to meet this standard, but you should check it
actually contains what you've identified in your first aid needs assessment."

## What's demonstrated in this kit, item by item

`[02:00]`–`[05:03]` Repacking a real leisure-center kit, the presenter walks through: disposable
ice packs (for sprains/head injuries, useful with no freezer nearby), sterile dressings in three
sizes (medium for arms/lower legs, large for bigger wounds, small in place of a plaster for a
finger or eye), scissors (to cut clothing away from a wound or during CPR), micropore tape,
triangular bandages (for suspected fractures or a sprained wrist), hypoallergenic plasters (with a
warning to check for plaster allergies first), cleaning wipes, six pairs of latex-free gloves and
two face shields (two, specifically so two helpers doing CPR in turns don't have to share one), and
foil blankets — with an explicit caveat that foil blankets are for keeping an already-warm casualty
warm, **not** for warming up someone who is already cold.

## What NOT to put in a kit

`[06:04]` "You should not have anything inside your first aid kit that is dangerous... I normally
class dangerous items as things I call potions, lotions, and sprays" — naming paracetamol,
ibuprofen, and similar tablets as "potions"; antiseptic/antihistamine creams as "lotions"; and
plaster spray, deep heat spray, or ice spray as examples to exclude, on the stated grounds that
these can cause an allergic reaction and fall outside a first aider's role: "we are the first
aider... we can pass them over to somebody else who might advise these types of treatment."
