# Adult CPR & AED Pad Placement – 2025 Update (What's Changed?)

## Central claim

**With helpers available, adult CPR (30 compressions to 2 breaths) continues without interruption
while a helper retrieves a defibrillator, which is then applied immediately without delay and used
according to its voice prompts — including a reassurance that using an AED cannot itself cause
harm.** `[01:01]` "We're going to put it on straight away without a delay... in an ideal world we'd
want the helper to carry on with CPR while I would then put the defibrillator on. But we need to
get it on without a delay."

## Solo rescuer vs. rescuer with helpers

`[00:00]` The source draws a specific distinction: alone with no phone or helpers, the rescuer must
leave to call emergency services after confirming the person isn't breathing, only detouring for a
defibrillator "if we can get there and back within a minute." With helpers present, the rescuer
stays with the patient doing continuous CPR while a helper is sent for both the phone call and the
AED.

## Adult pad placement, precisely

`[02:01]` "The first pad down... must be underneath their right clavicle. The second pad must be
underneath their left armpit," spaced a hand's width apart, applied to dry, bare skin — shaving
hair at the site if it would prevent the pad sticking. This placement differs from the pediatric
AED placement covered in `AmXN6YjxtII` (left of the chest midline, and directly opposite on the
back).

## Symptoms and body signals addressed

An unresponsive adult not breathing normally, confirmed via the standard look/listen/feel check for
10 seconds.

## Glossary terms introduced

None new — AED and CPR are used consistently with this collection's other notes.

None distinct.

Presented as direct, dated ("2025 update") procedural instruction with no cited resuscitation
guideline document by name — treated as the channel's own stated current standard.

## Conflicts with other sources

Extends `AmXN6YjxtII` ("Child Collapses & Not Breathing"), which teaches the pediatric CPR ratio
(15:2, one-handed, one-third depth) and pediatric pad placement — this note supplies the adult
equivalents (30:2, two-handed implied, right-clavicle/left-armpit pad placement), making the two
notes complementary age-specific halves of the same protocol. Converges with `dLz7tn-bR9w` ("CPR in
a Bed") on immediate, uninterrupted compressions as the governing priority.
