# Treatment of Small Cuts and Splinters | Adult First Aid | EMT First Aid Training

## Central claim

**Minor injuries — small cuts, scratches, blisters, and splinters — are treated with simple,
distinct rules rather than the full wound-dressing protocol used for larger bleeds: clean and
cover a cut, leave a blister intact, and remove a splinter only if it's visible and accessible,
pulling it out in the direction it entered.** `[00:00]` "First aid is not just about dealing with
serious accidents and injuries that can occur, we also need to be able to deal with some of the
minor things."

## Why blisters shouldn't be burst

`[01:01]` The reasoning given is direct: **"please don't burst it because if you do burst it then
there's going to be a lot more chance of it getting infected. Just leave the blister as a blister
because what the blister is doing is actually protecting the skin underneath"** — bursting it
exposes that skin to infection risk instead.

## Splinter removal technique

`[02:03]`–`[03:03]` Only attempt removal if the splinter's end is visible and not buried under the
skin. Clean first, grip the visible end with tweezers, and pull "in the direction that it entered
the actual body to avoid breaking it in half." Afterward, squeezing either side of the wound is
recommended to encourage brief bleeding that can flush out any remaining dirt, before covering with
a plaster.

## Analogies worth reusing

A dirt-contaminated wipe used on broken skin described as being "like sandpaper on somebody" — the
stated reason to rinse under water instead of wiping when a wound has visible dirt in it. `[00:00]`
