# How to help Concussion in Sport | Adult First Aid | EMT First Aid Training

## Central claim

**Concussion does not require unconsciousness or even a direct head injury, cannot be confirmed by
imaging, and once suspected the response is immediate and complete rest — "if in doubt, sit them
out" — followed by a graduated, time-gated return to sport (no training for 14 days, no
competition for 21) rather than a symptom-driven "feel better, go back" approach.** `[03:04]`
"I want you to always remember though, if you're ever in doubt, please always sit them out."

## Three myths the source explicitly corrects

`[02:02]`–`[03:04]` **Myth 1**: concussion requires being knocked unconscious — the source states
this occurs in "less than 10% of people with concussion." **Myth 2**: a head injury must occur —
concussion can follow "a blow to another part of the body which causes a transmitted force to the
brain," including whiplash-type mechanisms. **Myth 3**: concussion would be visible on imaging —
"a concussion cannot be seen on an X-ray, a CT scan, or an MRI of the head," which is the stated
reason recognition relies entirely on signs and symptoms rather than a test.

## Recognizing concussion: signs versus symptoms

`[03:04]`–`[04:05]` **Signs** (visible): a dazed or blank look, lying motionless and slow to get
up, balance and coordination problems, loss of consciousness or responsiveness, confusion,
grabbing at the head, head cuts, seizures, or an unusual change in behavior. **Symptoms**
(reported): headache, dizziness, double vision, "brain fog," severe neck pain, weakness or tingling
in the limbs, and sensitivity to light or noise. The source also notes symptoms "typically occur
immediately, but they could also be delayed... up to 72 hours after the initial injury," which is
given as the reason for repeated assessment rather than a single check.

## The return-to-sport timeline, as a fixed sequence

`[07:07]`–`[09:10]` Rest (physical and cognitive) is described as "the initial cornerstone,"
lasting 24–48 hours before even returning to work or study. A graduated return-to-play protocol
then governs sport specifically: no sport-specific training until at least 14 days after injury,
no competition until at least 21 days — and progression through the protocol's stages is
explicitly conditioned on an activity not "more than mildly" worsening existing symptoms.

## Symptoms and body signals addressed

Covered in full under "Recognizing concussion" above — visible signs (balance problems, dazedness,
motionlessness) and reported symptoms (headache, dizziness, neck pain, light/noise sensitivity),
organized by the source into four domains: physical, cognitive, mood, and sleep.
