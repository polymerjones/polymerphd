# Unconscious Adult 2025 Update – Primary & Secondary Survey + Recovery Position

## Central claim

**For an unconscious but normally-breathing adult with no catastrophic bleed, the source's full
sequence is: primary survey (danger check, response check, call for help, open airway, check
breathing for 10 seconds), then secondary survey (head-to-toe check for injury), then the recovery
position (link far hand under cheek, bend near knee, roll onto side, re-open airway).** `[00:01]`
"We're going to put the primary survey together with the secondary survey together with the
recovery position."

## Why the recovery position, and the fallback for a broken limb

`[05:05]` The stated purpose of the recovery position is airway maintenance: "we put them in the
recovery position because... it allows them to maintain an open airway," letting vomit or blood
drain away rather than pool. `[06:06]` If a fracture rules out the recovery position, the fallback
is to keep the person on their back and hold the airway open by hand — with the stated downside
that "my hands are now attached to that casualty," which becomes a problem for a lone rescuer who
eventually needs to let ambulance crews in.

## Symptoms and body signals addressed

Normal breathing rate benchmarks used to judge whether someone is breathing adequately (2-3 breaths
per 10 seconds for an adult), agonal gasps as a false-reassurance sign, and secondary-survey
injury signs: lumps/bumps on the head, fluid from the ears (possible spinal injury), lacerations,
a hard or rigid abdomen (possible internal bleeding), and pelvic instability.

## Glossary terms introduced

- **Agonal gasps** — described as possibly "the last breath that someone breathes," distinct from
  normal breathing and a sign CPR may be needed rather than the recovery position. `[02:03]`

## Analogies worth reusing

None distinct beyond the step-by-step procedural sequence itself.

Presented as direct, professionally-delivered procedural instruction with no cited guideline
document or study — treated as the channel's own stated standard practice.
