# Hyperventilation | Adult First Aid | EMT First Aid Training

## Central claim

**Hyperventilation is over-breathing that depletes the body's carbon dioxide, producing symptoms
that drive further over-breathing in a self-reinforcing cycle — broken by either a slow
paced-breathing technique (in for 4, hold for 4, out for 4) or a cupped-hands re-breathing
technique, not by the older brown-paper-bag method, which the source says isn't taught anymore.**
`[01:00]` "These reactions are literally caused by having too little carbon dioxide, and they're
very frightening in themselves and they can lead to more over-breathing, and this is known as the
vicious circle of hyperventilation."

## Two techniques, and why the paper bag isn't one of them

`[02:01]`–`[04:04]` The **breath-control technique**: sit somewhere quiet, release tension, breathe
in for a count of four, hold for four, out for four, repeating until calm. The **re-breathing
technique**: cup both hands over the nose and mouth and breathe in through the nose, out through
the mouth, into the cupped hands — no more than four repetitions — to re-inhale some of the just-
exhaled, CO2-rich air. The source addresses the brown paper bag directly: it works on the same
principle, but **"medical research on using it to help hyperventilation is divided on why that
really actually works... in first aid we don't actually teach that method anymore"** — partly
because holding a bag over someone's face can itself be frightening, and it must never be a plastic
bag.

## Distinguishing from a more serious condition

`[05:06]` A specific safety caveat: hyperventilation and a heart attack can both present with chest
pain, so **"if you've ever seen about someone... hyperventilating and you're not quite sure if it
is there, or maybe it is a heart attack that's causing it... always seek emergency medical advice,
never just leave this person."**

## Symptoms and body signals addressed

Difficulty catching breath, chest tightness, rapid pulse, sweating/clamminess, tingling or numbness
(especially in the extremities), vision problems, rigid muscles and cramps, sudden emotional
outbursts (crying), and feeling too hot or too cold.

## Glossary terms introduced

None beyond "hyperventilation" and the "vicious circle" framing, captured above as concepts.

## Analogies worth reusing

Counting "elephants" (one elephant, two elephants...) as a simple, memorable way to pace the
four-count breathing technique. `[02:01]`
