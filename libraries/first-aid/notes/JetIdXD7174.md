# Shock - Hypovolemic Shock | Adult First Aid | EMT First Aid Training

## Central claim

**Hypovolemic shock — insufficient circulating blood or fluid, usually from hemorrhage or severe
vomiting/diarrhea — is treated as a medical emergency: call for help, stop the underlying blood or
fluid loss, lay the patient down with legs raised, keep them warm, and give nothing to eat or
drink.** `[04:06]` "Shock, as we said before, is a medical emergency. We must make sure we call the
emergency medical services straight away... lay them down and lift their legs up in order to help
the blood circulate around our vital organs."

## Four types of shock, named for context

`[00:00]`–`[02:02]` Before narrowing to hypovolemic shock, the source names all four medical
types: **hypovolemic** (low blood/fluid volume), **cardiogenic** (heart failing to pump, from
muscle damage, a heart attack, or a valve problem), **obstructive** (a blockage restricting blood
flow, such as reduced venous return or an aortic blockage), and **distributive** (abnormal blood
distribution to tissue — covering septic shock from overwhelming infection, anaphylactic shock, and
neurogenic shock from central nervous system damage). Only hypovolemic shock's treatment is covered
in this video.

## Why nil by mouth — two stated reasons

`[05:07]` The instruction to withhold food and drink even from a thirsty patient is given two
explicit justifications: **"they might need an operation and you don't want them to vomit under
general anesthesia"**, and — described as the more important reason — that digestion diverts blood
to the digestive system, away from the heart, lungs, and brain that need it most during shock.

## Symptoms and body signals addressed

Skin turning pale, cold, and clammy; anxious or agitated behavior progressing to unresponsiveness;
weak, fatigued limbs; dizziness, blurred vision, enlarged pupils; nausea or vomiting; rapid,
shallow breathing with "air hunger."

## Glossary terms introduced

- **Hypovolemic shock** — defined here in more depth than in `cdNHeYQ2Knc` ("How to treat a
  bleed"): "hypo means low. Volemic means volume... insufficient blood circulating around the
  body," with a stated onset around 20% blood/fluid loss and a stated lethal threshold around 40%.
  `[00:00]`
- **Air hunger** — the source's description of shock-related breathing distress: "feels like when
  someone's put a plastic bag over your head and you kind of just gasping at whatever breath you
  can have." `[03:03]`
- **Nil by mouth** — the term used for withholding food and drink from a patient in shock.
  `[05:07]`

## Analogies worth reusing

Air hunger compared to breathing with a bag over one's head — a vivid, reusable description of
shock-related respiratory distress. `[03:03]`
