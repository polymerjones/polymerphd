- **Emergencies:** an adult with a partial airway obstruction, still coughing and able to make noise, an adult with a complete airway obstruction, unable to make noise or breathe, a life-threatening condition
- **Procedures:** for a mild/partial obstruction — encourage coughing and stay calm; noise-making is treated as a sign the airway is not fully blocked, for a severe/complete obstruction — ask permission to help, call emergency medical services, then alternate up to five back slaps (heel of the hand, between the shoulder blades, with the rescuer's other arm supporting the casualty's chest) with up to five abdominal thrusts (thumb knuckle placed under the ribcage/above the abdominals, thrusting to expel air upward), repeating until the blockage clears or the casualty becomes unconscious, if the casualty becomes unconscious, check the airway, ensure the ambulance has been called, and begin CPR, after a successful clear, keep the casualty calm, seated, with fresh air and loosened clothing, and always have them seek medical attention afterward
- **Important concepts:** adult and child choking treatment stated to be identical in technique, adapted only in positioning, noise-making treated as the practical marker distinguishing a partial from a complete obstruction, since "all the time they're making a noise they are breathing"

- **Video ID:** `Mzf-sslc2Zo`
- **URL:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Mzf-sslc2Zo
- **Upload date:** 2022-07-14
- **Duration:** 04:49
- **Source file:** `sources/clean/Mzf-sslc2Zo.txt`
- **Main subjects:** the body's temperature-regulation "thermostat," and what happens when it fails at either temperature extreme, with a focus on heat exhaustion and heat stroke
- **Emergencies:** heat exhaustion (body temperature at or above 38°C, hot, sweaty, distressed), heat stroke (body temperature around 40°C, thermostat failure, a medical emergency), febrile convulsion in a child, caused by a rapid body-temperature rise
- **Procedures:** for heat exhaustion: move to a cooler/shaded environment, give sips of water, keep calm, for heat stroke: remove from the heat source immediately and call emergency medical services, then actively cool the patient while waiting — in order of preference: whole-body immersion from the neck down in water between 1 and 26°C, then a cold shower or garden hose, then ice in bags wrapped in a towel placed on the neck, armpits, and groin, or wet towels (removing and replacing a wet towel with a dry one once cooled, to avoid overcooling), for a febrile convulsion: treat the same as any seizure (keep safe, never hold down), while also trying to bring the temperature down; call emergency medical services if in doubt
- **Important concepts:** 37°C as the source's stated normal body temperature, with below 35°C as hypothermia, 38°C and above as heat exhaustion, and 40°C as heat stroke, the loss of sweating (dry skin) as the key distinguishing sign that heat exhaustion has progressed to the more dangerous heat stroke, dehydration named as the underlying risk factor across all heat-related problems
