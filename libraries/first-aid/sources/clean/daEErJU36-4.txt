[00:00] Heart attack versus cardiac arrest. Do you know the difference? Because these two get mixed up all the time. A heart attack is a circulation problem. It's a blocked artery that stops blood reaching the heart muscle. The person is usually conscious and breathing and talking. But common signs tend to be chest pain or pain that spreads through the arm or the jaw. They may be sweaty or clammy and feeling sick. You need to sit them down, keep them calm, call the emergency medical services, and also give them any medication they've been told to do so by their doctor. Whereas a cardiac arrest is an electrical problem. The heart stops pumping all together. The person is unresponsive. They're not breathing normally. This is a medical emergency and every second counts. Call the emergency medical services now. Start CPR now. Get a defibrillator if there is one nearby. So conscious and talking, heart attack. Unresponsive and not breathing, cardiac arrest. Start CPR now. Want to learn CPR? There's a link in our bio for all our courses.
