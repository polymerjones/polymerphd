[00:00] Did you know that going by smoke point is a horrible way to choose your cooking oil? This is going to blow your mind. When it comes to oils that you're going to heat in a pan, most people just choose based on smoke point, which is the temperature at which this oil will start to smoke. But this is really misleading and tells you nothing about the oxidation of the oil, how easily the oil is damaged when you heat it. That is the peroxidation index. It's a technical term, but when choosing a cooking oil, you should go by the peroxidation index, not the smoke point. All of these have about the same smoke point. 420 degrees, 400 degrees Fahrenheit, 400 to 450 degrees Fahrenheit. But because this canola oil is a seed oil and it's polyunsaturated, it's going to oxidize much sooner than olive oil, which is going to oxidize much sooner than tallow when you cook it in the pan. This is why I'm such a huge fan of cooking with animal fats like tallow or butter in the pan because they have a lower peroxidation index, though the smoke point is about the same. Choose fats with a lower peroxidation index like tallow or butter or ghee for cooking fats and you'll do way better.
