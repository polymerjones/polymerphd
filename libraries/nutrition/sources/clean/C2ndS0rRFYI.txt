[00:00] There is no good evidence that red meat causes cancer, and in fact, it may protect you against it. There are multiple nutrients found exclusively or predominantly in red meat that have been found in research studies to be protective against cancers. And there are simply zero randomized controlled trials in humans showing that more red meat is either inflammatory or increases your risk of cancer. In a recent study published in the journal Nature, researchers looked at a compound TVA or transvacinic acid found in animal and dairy fats and discovered that it has multiple anti-cancer properties, both in vitro and in vivo. Similarly, other compounds found predominantly in meat and red meat have also been found to have anti-cancer properties, including taurine, carnitine, creatine, anserine, and 4-hydroxyproline. Of these, creatine has been particularly well studied both in vitro and in vivo animal models and found to possess potent anti-cancer properties. Carnicine has similarly been found to possess anti-cancer properties against multiple human cancer cell lines. Do not fear red meat. It is a health food.
