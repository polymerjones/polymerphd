[00:00] This is how I shop for eggs. There are tons of different varieties of eggs here. How do you even sort through all this? So start with organic. You don't want what the chickens are fed to have pesticides on it because those pesticides are going to accumulate in the eggs. Second thing is that free range versus pasture raised. When an egg says free range, it has a small amount of outdoor space to roam. What's better than free range is pasture raised. In order for an egg to be labeled pasture raised, it must have a much larger amount of space to roam. An average of 106 square feet of space to roam per chicken. Much more than a free range egg. If I can find it, I look for an egg that is corn and soy free. When chickens eat corn and soy, they accumulate excess amounts of linoleic acid, that polyunsaturated acid you find in seed oils in their eggs. And I think that that makes their eggs less healthy. So a corn and soy free organic pasture raised egg is about as good as you're going to be able to do.
