[00:00] What causes a hangover? Drinking alcohol naturally leads to dehydration. That's the first thing. Second thing, alcohol is broken down into acetaldehyde, which is a toxin that has to be metabolized by your body, creates inflammation. The third thing is electrolyte imbalance caused by the diuretic effect from alcohol. These are the three biggest things. But there's also a fourth thing, the congeners, which means when the alcohol gets made, you have the byproducts of alcohol metabolism, compounds that end up in your alcohol. They don't cause the hangover, but they can worsen it. So it's those four things. Clear alcohols have the lowest amounts of congeners. So it's actually true that drinking a clear alcohol, like a vodka, a gin, or a tequila, can lead to lower hangovers, but you're still getting alcohol, you're still getting acetaldehyde, you're still getting electrolyte imbalances. But bourbon, beer, and wine will probably lead to worse hangovers. So if you've got to drink and you want less of a hangover, go for the clear alcohols.
