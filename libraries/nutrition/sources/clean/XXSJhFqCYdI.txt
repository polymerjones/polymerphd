[00:00] Did you know that if you have lactose intolerance, you can probably eat all of these foods without ending up in the bathroom in an emergency? Milk has a good amount of lactose. That causes GI issues for some people. Regular milk from a cow, about 12 grams of lactose per eight ounces. Goat's milk, 10% less than that. But kefir, which is fermented milk, has significantly less lactose because of the fermentation process. About two to five grams of lactose. Significantly less. A lot of people who have lactose intolerance can drink kefir with no GI symptoms. And my favorite way to drink kefir is to add a little bit of raw organic glyphosate-free honey. Yogurt has about four to six grams of lactose per eight ounces. And a Greek yogurt might have a little less than that. And if we get into cheeses, the story is even better. Parmigiano-Reggiano cheese, which is always raw. Pecorino-Romano cheese, or aged Swiss. 0.1 grams of lactose per ounce. You're not stuck drinking plant-based milks like oat milk or almond milk. Just look for things that are fermented, like kefir or yogurt or aged cheeses, preferably the ones that are raw.
