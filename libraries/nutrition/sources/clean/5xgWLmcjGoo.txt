Multiple studies show in people that are metabolically healthy that the relationship between cholesterol and cardiovascular disease is massively attenuated or not even present. There's actually a recent study done with coronary artery calcium scores. And in those people that had a coronary artery calcium score of zero, there was no connection, no relationship between levels of LDL cholesterol and cardiovascular disease. So often when you go to a doctor, they just look at your cholesterol, but they don't ask you what your fasting insulin is. They don't ask you how metabolically healthy you are. They don't even look at your fasting blood glucose. They just wanna get your cholesterol down no matter what. And I think this is just an incomplete story. In metabolically healthy individuals, a slight rise in your cholesterol because you're eating more saturated fat and less seed oils, I don't think there's a shred of evidence in the medical literature to suggest that's going to increase your rate of cardiovascular disease.
