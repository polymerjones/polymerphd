[00:00] How do I look for eggs at the farmer's market? There are two different types of eggs here. We've got free-range eggs, which are outside some of the time, and we've got, these are called caseros. So the caseros here in Costa Rica are a breed of chicken that gets outside much more because they can tolerate the heat. So the longer the chicken is outside, the more it's going to eat bugs and worms, and the less it's going to eat things like grains. This is kind of equivalent to something like cage-free or pasture-raised in the States. Chickens don't really want to eat grains all the time, especially if those grains are sprayed with pesticides like glyphosate. They want to eat bugs and worms. But in order to eat bugs and worms, they have to go into grass and root and peck. So you can tell with the quality of the egg when you crack it, the shell, the yolk, sometimes depending on what the chicken has been eating. But when I'm looking for eggs, I'm looking for the chickens that have been outside the most, that are eating the least amount of grains, and the most amount of bugs and worms. And listen, I know eggs have gotten astronomically expensive recently, so don't let perfect be the enemy of good. If you can't afford pasture-raised eggs, any eggs are nutritious for humans, but do the best you can. I think that it's worth the investment for a better quality egg.
