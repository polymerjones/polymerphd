# Different color steaks

## Central claim

**Comparing two New York strip steaks side by side, the host argues the visibly darker meat and
more orange fat of grass-finished beef reflects a genuinely more nutrient-dense product — more
fat-soluble vitamins from a lifetime of eating grass — while grain-finished beef both lacks those
nutrients and carries residues (glyphosate, pesticides, mold, toxins) from the grain itself.**
`[00:00]` "You can absolutely tell by the difference in color... that this cow is getting way more
nutrients all the way through its life by eating grass, what it's supposed to eat."

## What the color difference is claimed to reflect

`[00:00]` — Grass-finished beef is described as darker in the meat and more orange in the fat,
attributed to more **vitamin A, vitamin D, vitamin E, vitamin K2, and beta-carotene** in the fat.
Grain-finishing, by contrast, is framed as a liability on both ends: fewer of those nutrients, and
more of whatever "is on those grains" — named specifically as **glyphosate, pesticides, mold, and
toxins** — ending up in the meat.
