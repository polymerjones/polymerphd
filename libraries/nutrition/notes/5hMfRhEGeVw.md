# The problem with vegetables

## Central claim

**The host argues vegetables don't work well for everyone, and that physicians rarely consider
vegetable intake — spinach's high oxalate content specifically — as a cause when patients present
with unexplained autoimmune, gut, skin (psoriasis, eczema), mood, or sleep issues, citing oxalate
accumulation in joints, thyroid, and breast tissue, plus kidney failure case studies tied to
oxalate-heavy smoothie cleanses.** `[00:00]` "Very few physicians are going to say to a patient,
the spinach you're eating might be causing your problem."

## The case against spinach specifically

`[00:00]` — Spinach is named as "super high" in **oxalates**, compounds the host says accumulate in
the human body — specifically in joints, thyroid, and breast tissue — and form **calcium oxalate
kidney stones**, described as the most common kidney stone type. He cites "clear case studies" of
kidney failure in people doing smoothie cleanses with high oxalate absorption, and mentions a friend
with recent kidney stones whose diet is "traditionally healthy" — used to illustrate that
conventionally "healthy" eating patterns can still carry this risk.

## Symptoms and body signals addressed

- **Autoimmune issues, gut issues, psoriasis, eczema, mood issues, and insomnia** — listed as
  conditions the host believes can go undiagnosed as oxalate-related because physicians don't
  typically investigate vegetable intake `[00:00]`.
- **Calcium oxalate kidney stones** — attributed specifically to oxalate accumulation from foods
  like spinach `[00:00]`.
- **Kidney failure** — cited via case studies involving high-oxalate smoothie cleanses `[00:00]`.

## Glossary terms introduced

- **Oxalates** — compounds found in high concentration in spinach and other vegetables, said to
  accumulate in body tissue and form kidney stones `[00:00]`.

## Source-stated confidence

Low to moderate. The kidney-failure-from-smoothie-cleanse claim references "clear case studies"
without naming them. The broader claim connecting oxalates to autoimmune, mood, and sleep issues is
stated as an underrecognized pattern rather than a proven causal chain.
