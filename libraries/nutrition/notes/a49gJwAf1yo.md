# Are saturated fats causing heart disease?

## Central claim

**The host argues that around 1875-1900, when Americans ate primarily animal fats, cardiovascular
disease rates were a fraction of today's — calling this a natural experiment that undermines the
saturated-fat-causes-heart-disease hypothesis — and traces the hypothesis's rise to Ancel Keys' 1950s
Seven Countries Study, Eisenhower's 1955 heart attack (treated by cardiologist Paul Dudley White),
and a $1.7 million donation (~$20 million today) from Procter & Gamble, maker of Crisco, to the
American Heart Association.** `[00:00]` "Anyone who wants to argue that animal fats are the cause of
cardiovascular disease, you got a really hard argument to make."

## The historical narrative

`[00:00]` — **Baseline claim**: ~120-130 years ago (1875-1900), Americans ate almost entirely animal
fats, with cardiovascular disease rates far lower than today's — framed as a natural experiment
across "many millions of Americans." **The pivot point**: named as occurring around 1950, involving
Ancel Keys and the Seven Countries Study, Eisenhower's heart attack (with cardiologist Paul Dudley
White named), and a $1.7 million Procter & Gamble donation (Crisco's manufacturer) to the American
Heart Association. **The messaging shift**: the American Heart Association is described as
subsequently promoting saturated fat as bad and polyunsaturated fat as good, with 1960s
advertisements (Mazola corn oil named) urging Americans to "polyunsaturate your family."
