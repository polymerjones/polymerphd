# Beware of greens powders…

## Central claim

**Citing ConsumerLab testing, the host reports a leading greens powder contains 2.1 micrograms of
lead per serving — more than four times California's Prop 65 limit of 0.5 micrograms — and that
the manufacturer's own internal testing allows up to 4.53 micrograms before flagging a batch,
arguing steak delivers the same vitamins and minerals more comprehensively and bioavailably
without this risk.** `[00:00]` "Steak is probably just the new greens powder."

## The cited test and figures

`[00:00]` — **ConsumerLab** ("consumer labs" in the transcript) tested a leading greens powder and
found **2.1 micrograms of lead per serving**, described as exceeding what ConsumerLab would
recommend for children or adults. **California's Prop 65 limit**: 0.5 micrograms/serving — the
tested product is **more than 4x** that limit. **The manufacturer's own threshold**: internal
testing reportedly allows up to **4.53 micrograms/serving** before flagging a batch — more than
double the amount already found. Lead specifically is flagged as concerning because it
"accumulate[s] in the bones" and is "released during pregnancy and aging." The recommended
alternative: steak, for more plentiful, comprehensive, and bioavailable vitamins and minerals.
