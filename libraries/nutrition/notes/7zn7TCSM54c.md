# You'll never touch another receipt…

## Central claim

**Responding to being called "extra" (by Andrew Tate) for avoiding receipts, the host cites four
studies finding measurable BPA transfer into blood and urine from handling thermal paper receipts —
a 2014 study finding 100% of participants had increased BPA after two hours of handling, a cashier
study finding 2-3x increases from all-day exposure, and two further studies from 2017 and 2018 —
concluding minimizing receipt contact is "just being healthy," not excessive.** `[00:00]` "There are
multiple studies that actually show touching thermal paper receipts... can lead to measurable
increases in BPA."

## The four cited studies

`[00:00]` — **2014 study**: participants handled receipts for two hours; **100% showed measurable
BPA increases** in blood and urine. **Cashier study** (unnamed year): cashiers handling receipts
all day showed BPA increases of **2-3x**. **2017 and 2018 studies**: both corroborated the same
skin-transfer mechanism. None of the four studies is named by specific author or journal in this
transcript, though each is given a distinguishing detail (year, population, or effect size) that
makes them more specific than a bare "studies show" claim.
