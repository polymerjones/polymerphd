# The history and DANGERS of seed oils

## Central claim

**Seed oils have no place in the human diet before the last 100–110 years — they began as
industrial machine lubricant, were marketed into the food supply as a cheap, shelf-stable
substitute for animal fat, and their rise tracks a steady increase in chronic illness that the
speaker attributes to a mistaken indictment of saturated fat and LDL cholesterol rather than of
the oils themselves.** `[00:00]` "There was no such thing as a seed oil before 1870... it wasn't
really popularized for human consumption until 1910, 1911 with Crisco" `[00:00]`.

## From machine lubricant to Crisco

`[00:00]`–`[01:00]` — Seeds were first ground for oil to use "as a machine lubricant," not catching
on for human consumption until Crisco in 1910–1911, and not becoming widespread until "the 1950s."
The speaker contrasts this against health records from the same era: **"heart attacks were very
rare, diabetes very rare, obesity very rare"** in the early 1900s, illustrated by old beach photos
showing leaner populations than today.

## A correlation, explicitly flagged as such

`[02:01]` — With seed oils' introduction from 1910, the speaker describes "a steady rise in
chronic illness" — obesity, cancer, heart disease, diabetes — tracking together, most sharply over
the last 50 years but extending back to the early 1900s. He is explicit about the limits of this:
**"this is all correlation... we can only draw a hypothesis from this which we must test."**

## The LBJ heart attack and the start of cholesterol vilification

`[02:01]`–`[03:01]` — The speaker (hedging his own history as "a little fuzzy") ties the mid-1950s
heart attack of a U.S. president to a cultural turning point, after which "the American Heart
Association and the American Medical Association" began "vilify[ing] cholesterol" and, by
extension, animal fat. He references "the stories of Ansel Keys and the seven countries study"
without detailing them, flagging it as a thread to revisit. Before that point, **"people were
eating exclusively tallow... or lard,"** with pigs in 1900 fed diets he says were "probably better"
than today's corn-and-soy-fed pigs, alongside low rates of the same chronic diseases.

## How seed oils were sold: "it doesn't spoil... it looks clean"

`[04:02]` — The marketing pitch is described as cheaper, lighter, non-spoiling. **Crisco and
margarine's stark whiteness is contrasted with butter's natural color, which comes from
flavonoids in grass** — margarine, he says, needed synthetic color pills added to mimic that look.

## Why LDL got blamed, and why the speaker disputes it

`[04:02]`–`[08:06]` — The chain given: saturated animal fat raises LDL in many people; canola
and other seed oils, being high in polyunsaturated fat, generally lower LDL; if LDL is treated as
"a pure cause" of atherosclerosis, this makes animal fat look dangerous and seed oil protective.
The speaker calls this framework **"totally wrong"** and says "we've been wildly misled," extending
the point to ApoB-containing lipoproteins broadly (LDL, VLDL, chylomicrons): "I do not believe that
[this] particle... is causative of atherosclerosis. They may get involved in the process." This is
stated as his own position ("I don't believe"), not as settled consensus, and he says the same
argument was made in an earlier, unaired part of this conversation.

## Symptoms and body signals addressed

None directly by name — obesity, diabetes, cancer, and heart disease are referenced as
population-level rates rather than individual symptom pictures.

## Glossary terms introduced

- **The cholesterol hypothesis** — the speaker's shorthand for the mid-20th-century medical
  consensus that blamed LDL cholesterol, and by extension saturated animal fat, for heart disease
  `[04:02]`.
- **ApoB-containing lipoproteins** — named as the broader class (including LDL, VLDL, and
  chylomicrons) that the speaker argues is not, by itself, causative of atherosclerosis `[07:04]`.

## Source-stated confidence

Explicitly hedged in two places: the seed-oil/chronic-illness link is called **"all correlation...
we can only draw a hypothesis from this which we must test"** `[02:01]`, and the LBJ/AHA history is
prefaced with "my history is a little fuzzy there" `[02:01]`. The LDL-causation rebuttal is stated
as personal conviction ("I don't believe," "I think it's totally wrong") rather than as an
established finding, and no study is named for the seven-countries reference, the LDL-lowering
claim, or the ApoB argument.

## Conflicts with other sources

Shares its core historical claim — seed oil as a repurposed industrial lubricant, unpopular in
food form until the early-to-mid 1900s — with `c1kyubykrvA` (rapeseed/canola specifically, WWII
ship lubricant) and `Tg6qO3aEw9E` (the "last 110 years," WWII naval-lubricant detail); all three
independently land on the same "seed oil was never food" framing without citing each other. The
LDL/oxidation material connects more substantively to `wrL-i4zy9xU`, which spends significant time
on the same cholesterol-hypothesis history and macrophage/foam-cell mechanism, but with a more
qualified position: `wrL-i4zy9xU` argues specifically that *oxidized* LDL (not native LDL) is the
required step in atherogenesis, whereas this video's speaker rejects LDL/ApoB particles as
causative more broadly. The two are not a direct contradiction — both reject "LDL alone causes
heart disease" — but they draw the line in different places, and neither video cites the other.
