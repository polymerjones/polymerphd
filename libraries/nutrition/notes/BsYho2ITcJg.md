# The best diet for humans?

## Central claim

**Reacting to a content creator's account of public backlash over her carnivore meals — despite six
years of vegan eating that coincided with worsening skin and health drawing no public concern — the
host argues society should normalize animal-food-centered eating over ultra-processed junk food,
citing the Rotterdam study's finding that vitamin K2 (found predominantly in red meat) is
consistently associated with lower heart disease rates, and the Maasai as an indigenous population
eating large amounts of animal meat, fat, and full-fat raw milk with very low cardiovascular disease
rates.** `[00:00]` "We need to normalize humans eating food for humans, which is in large part, the
most nutrient rich foods on the planet, animal foods."

## The argument and its evidence

`[00:00]` — **Personal account referenced**: six years vegan, worsening skin and health without
public concern; switching to carnivore (bone broth, tomahawk steaks, yogurt cake, salmon, raw cheese)
drew public alarm instead. **Contrast**: no equivalent public alarm over Slurpees, Big Macs, or
French fries fried in multiple seed oils with 19 ingredients. `[01:03]` **Cited evidence**: the
**Rotterdam study**, associating vitamin K2 (found predominantly or exclusively in red meat) with
consistently lower heart disease rates; the **Maasai** in Africa, an indigenous population eating
large amounts of animal meat, animal fat, and full-fat raw milk, with "vanishingly low rates of
cardiovascular disease."

## Symptoms and body signals addressed

- **Skin and general health decline** — reported by the referenced creator during her vegan period
  `[00:00]`.
- **Cardiovascular disease** — cited as lower with higher vitamin K2 intake (Rotterdam study) and
  low among the Maasai despite high animal-fat consumption `[01:03]`.
