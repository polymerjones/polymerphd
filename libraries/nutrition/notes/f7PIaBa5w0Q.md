# Replace olive oil and avocado oil with tallow / butter

## Central claim

**Butter and tallow are argued to be preferable to olive oil and avocado oil because they are much
lower in linoleic acid — a polyunsaturated fatty acid linked to chronic illness, obesity, diabetes,
and cardiovascular disease — and higher in stearic acid and odd chain fatty acids, which the host
says activate fat burning and mitochondrial function, with salads themselves also dismissed as
"the most highly defended parts of plants."** `[00:00]` "These animal fats are much lower in
linoleic acid... that has been connected with human chronic illness, obesity, diabetes,
cardiovascular disease... These fats, butter and tallow are also higher in beneficial fats like
stearic acid and odd chain fatty acid which turn on fat burning and mitochondria."

## The comparison

`[00:00]` — **Higher linoleic acid** (argued harmful): seed oils, olive oil, avocado oil.
**Lower linoleic acid, higher stearic acid/odd chain fatty acids** (argued beneficial): butter,
tallow. **Additional practical concern raised:** olive and avocado oil are argued to be more prone
to rancidity from shelf storage.

## Source-stated confidence

Low-to-moderate — linoleic acid's association with chronic disease markers is a real, actively
studied and debated area of nutrition science, though contested (linoleic acid is also
conventionally associated with cardiovascular benefit in some research), and no specific study is
cited here; the stearic acid/odd chain fatty acid "activates fat burning and mitochondria" claim is
stated without a named mechanism or source.
