# Plants Vs Meat

## Central claim

**Framed as a direct comparison, the host states that no nutrients essential for optimal human
health are found only in plant foods, while a specific list is found only in animal meat and
organs — creatine, carnitine, choline, anserine, carnosine, vitamin K2, biotin, riboflavin, and
vitamin B12 — linking these to mood, body composition, libido, sleep, memory, and mental
performance.** `[00:00]` "Nutrients found only in plant foods that are essential for optimal human
health. None. Nutrients found only in animal meat and organs that are essential for optimal human
health. Creatine, carnitine, choline, anserine, carnosine, vitamin K2, biotin, riboflavin, vitamin
B12."

## Symptoms and body signals addressed

- **Mood, body composition, libido, sleep, memory, and mental performance** — named collectively as
  dependent on the listed animal-exclusive nutrients `[00:00]`.

## Glossary terms introduced

None — the nine nutrients are named but not individually defined in this clip.

## Analogies worth reusing

None.

## Source-stated confidence

Low — the "none" claim for plant-exclusive essential nutrients and the animal-exclusive nutrient
list are both stated as established fact with no named source; several of the nutrients listed
(e.g. B12, K2) are broadly accepted as scarce or absent in plant foods, while others (e.g. choline,
riboflavin) are present in some plant foods, just often in lower amounts — this clip does not
distinguish "absent" from "present but lower."

## Conflicts with other sources

`e-KTjQyZNvQ` ("An animal-based diet is optimal for humans"), from about a month earlier on the
same channel, lists nearly the identical nine nutrients (creatine, carnitine, choline, carnosine,
anserine, taurine, B12, K2, riboflavin) as scarce in plant foods — this clip drops taurine and adds
biotin, otherwise repeating the same list almost verbatim, suggesting a recurring stock list this
creator returns to rather than two independently derived claims.
