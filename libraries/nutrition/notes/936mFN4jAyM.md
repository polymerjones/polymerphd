# Is your sunscreen aging your skin?

## Central claim

**The host argues some sunscreens marketed as healthy actually contain seed oils like sunflower
seed oil, and that linoleic acid — whether applied topically or accumulated through diet —
contributes to skin aging, recommending both avoiding seed oils on skin and eating a low-linoleic-
acid diet to keep skin resilient, while still encouraging sun exposure itself.** `[00:00]` "Just
like you don't want to eat a seed oil, you don't want to put a seed oil on your skin."

## The claim

`[00:00]` — Seed oils (sunflower seed oil named specifically) are said to appear in "a ton of"
sunscreen products marketed as healthy. Linoleic acid is flagged as accumulating in skin tissue,
whether applied directly (topical seed oil) or absorbed via diet — the host states dietary seed oil
intake specifically makes skin "more susceptible to aging." The recommended fix: avoid seed oils
topically, and eat a low-linoleic-acid diet generally, while still getting regular sun exposure on
the face, eyes, and skin without fear.

## Symptoms and body signals addressed

- **Skin aging** — attributed to linoleic acid accumulation in skin tissue, from both topical
  application and dietary intake `[00:00]`.
