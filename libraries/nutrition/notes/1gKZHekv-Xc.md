# Steak 101 with the butcher

## Central claim

**A butcher (Dustin, of Underbelly in Arizona) walks through where common beef cuts come from on
the animal — rib, short loin, sirloin, round, flank, short plate, shank, and brisket — and what
ground beef is typically made from, as an educational rather than argumentative piece.** `[00:00]`
"This is everything you need to know about where your steak is coming from."

## The cut-by-cut breakdown

`[00:00]`–`[01:02]` — **Rib**: ribeye. **Short loin**: New York strip. **Sirloin**: top sirloin,
picanha (rump cap), sirloin filet. **Round**: top round, bottom round, eye round, sirloin tip.
**Flank**: flank steak and bavette (sirloin flap) — the latter named a personal favorite. **Short
plate**: fatty short ribs and navel (beef belly). **Shank**: high in tough connective tissue,
flagged as a strong collagen source. **Value-added support-muscle cuts**: flat iron, petite tender,
Vegas strip. **Brisket**: the large pectoral muscle, fatty, long-cooking, high in collagen. **Ground
beef**: typically made from tougher round cuts (ground to reduce toughness) combined with fatty
trim mainly from the short plate area, for flavor and fat content.
