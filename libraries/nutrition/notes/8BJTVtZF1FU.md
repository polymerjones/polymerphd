# You should never eat these

## Central claim

**The host argues seed oils are harmful largely because of their industrial refining process
itself — acid washing, neutralization, bleaching, and a 500°F deodorization step that effectively
"deep fries" the oil before it ever reaches a consumer's kitchen — compounded by months of storage
in plastic packaging that leaches antimony into the oil, and recommends eliminating seed oils
entirely in favor of tallow, butter, and ghee.** `[00:00]` "Even before a seed oil goes into a deep
fryer, it's essentially been deep fried in the factory."

## The refining process and the packaging concern

`[00:00]` — Processing steps named in sequence: **acid wash, neutralization, bleaching**, and a
**deodorization step at 500°F**. The host cites "clear evidence from the literature" that deep-
frying seed oils (a process the refining itself already resembles) creates "massive amounts of
harmful chemicals." Separately, once packaged, seed oil sits in **plastic containers for 6 to 12
months** on store shelves, during which the plastic is "known to bleach antimony into the oil."

## Symptoms and body signals addressed

None named individually — the concern is framed at the chemical-exposure/processing level rather
than a specific reported symptom.

## Glossary terms introduced

- **Deodorization** — the final high-heat (500°F) step in seed oil refining, named as a major
  contributor to the oil's chemical degradation before it's ever used for cooking `[00:00]`.

## Analogies worth reusing

- **"Essentially been deep fried in the factory"** — the host's framing for how seed oil refining
  itself replicates deep-frying conditions before any home cooking `[00:00]`.

## Source-stated confidence

Moderate. The refining-process description (acid wash, neutralization, bleaching, 500°F
deodorization) is specific and consistent with how seed oils are actually industrially processed.
The antimony-leaching claim is stated as established ("known to") without a specific study cited in
this clip, though it's independently corroborated elsewhere in this library (see Conflicts).
