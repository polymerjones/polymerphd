# I Went From 500 to 857 Testosterone at 48 (5 Simple Tips)

## Central claim

**Saladino states his testosterone is 857 ng/dL at 48 with no exogenous hormones, and frames the
whole video as the lifestyle stack he credits for it: training without overtraining, an animal-
based diet, a sauna habit that protects fertility, a circadian-protective sleep setup, and limiting
plastic/EMF exposure.** `[00:00]` "I'm 48 years old and my testosterone is 857. I take no drugs, no
hormones. I never have... I'm going to show you everything I do to keep my testosterone above 800."

## Training: don't overtrain, train outdoors, don't overdo cardio

`[00:00]`–`[03:03]` — **"Overtraining will crush your testosterone just like undersleeping will."**
Needing pre-workout is offered as a tell: **"If you need preworkout to go to the gym, you are
underslept and undernourished. Eat a steak and take a nap and go to the gym tomorrow"** `[00:00]`.
For detecting overtraining, Saladino names the **DHEAS-to-cortisol ratio** as "the lab that's most
useful" — cortisol rising while DHEA falls signals overtraining — and says he'll put "the optimal
ratio" on screen, without stating the number verbally `[00:00]`–`[01:00]`. Softer signals: libido
dropping, energy dropping, "not fresh in the gym" `[01:00]`. He trains casually 30–45 minutes daily
(sled pushes, sled pulls for knee support, pull-ups, core work, mobility, occasional box jumps, the
punching bag) rather than "going crazy," and frames strength training as what "probably improves
muscle mass and that supports testosterone," while **"don't overdo cardio. That's going to crush
your testosterone"** `[01:00]`–`[02:01]`. Training outdoors is called "a huge hack": UV light is
tied to "more vitamin D and more testosterone," infrared light to mitochondrial function — **"melatonin
in all of your mitochondria come from infrared light"** — and outdoor light is framed as full-
spectrum and non-flickering versus enriched-blue, flickering indoor light `[01:00]`–`[02:01]`.
Inadequate calorie intake is separately flagged as another testosterone-crushing input, also said to
harm the thyroid, "which is connected to your testosterone" `[03:03]`.

## The sauna hack: a hat, and ice on the testicles

`[03:03]`–`[04:03]` — Citing an unnamed cohort study, Saladino says a sauna over 200°F, "mostly
over 210, 215," is associated with increased dementia risk, so he wears a hat "to protect my
brain." Separately, he states sauna heat harms fertility — **"decrease sperm count, decrease sperm
motility"** — and demonstrates icing his testicles in the sauna (with frozen beef, "for the video,"
rather than an ice pack) to protect them. The reasoning bridges sperm production to hormone status
generally: **"I don't think you can separate sperm production and hormonal health... if you really
want to protect your hormonal health, especially if you're over 40, ice your balls"** `[04:03]`.

## Diet: an animal-based plate built for testosterone

`[04:03]`–`[08:08]` — Walking through a table of what he actually eats, Saladino leads with what's
**absent**: no seed oils, which he says "shrinks your testicles" (no study named on screen or in
speech). He cooks with beef tallow and butter from Radius butcher shop, and cites an unnamed meta-
analysis finding higher saturated-fat diets associated with more testosterone — **"your diet is
probably one of the biggest absolute levers in testosterone production"** `[04:03]`–`[05:05]`.
Carbohydrates come from squash, blueberries, oranges, apples, avocado, and Lineage raw honey
("organic, raw, glyphosate free"); he says he doesn't "fear fat," "fear sugar in fruit," or "fear
calories." Eggs are named for selenium, B vitamins, vitamin A, and choline. He eats liver, and when
he can't get it fresh, substitutes six capsules a day of a Heart & Soil supplement that contains
desiccated testicle — the one product in that line that **fails** the brand's own NSF/Informed
Sport testing, specifically because testicle "contains testosterone and androgens." Saladino
hedges his own claim here: **"whether or not that's part of the effect of eating testicles on
humans is questionable. I think it's more likely there are microRNA and peptides in the testicle
that are helpful for us"** `[05:05]`–`[06:06]`. Bone marrow, beef broth, and beef stock are eaten
for collagen; ribeye steak is grass-fed and grass-finished. His protein target is roughly **1 gram
per pound of bodyweight from animal foods** (he states his weight as 165 lb, so ~165g/day), and
carbohydrate intake is **200–300g/day**, scaled to activity level, with an explicit bio-
individuality caveat: **"be honest with yourself. Check your electrolytes. Check your thyroid
function"** to gauge whether a given carbohydrate level is working `[06:06]`–`[07:07]`. He states
his own testosterone was around 500 ng/dL "when I was strict carnivore," versus 857 recently
`[07:07]`.

## Labs, and denying performance-enhancing drug use

`[07:07]`–`[09:10]` — Recommended panel: **free and total testosterone, SHBG, a full thyroid panel
(T3, T4, free T3, free T4, TSH, reverse T3), fasting insulin, blood lipids, hsCRP, and homocysteine**
`[07:07]`. Saladino repeats a denial he says he makes in every testosterone video — **"I'm not
taking hormones. I don't take steroids. I never have"** — and states his LH and FSH are normal,
directly rebutting a public accusation from "Greg Ducet" that he used clomiphene: **"I bet him
multiple ribeyes... Greg owes me multiple ribeyes"** `[08:08]`. He also takes roughly 5g of
creatine daily `[08:08]`.

## Sleep and the circadian environment

`[09:10]`–`[15:16]` — Saladino keeps a fixed sleep time (~8pm in Costa Rica, ~9pm in the US), uses
only portable, zero-flicker red LEDs after dark, and starts each morning outdoors getting sunlight
"into my eyes" plus grounding (the ocean in Costa Rica, or dewy grass elsewhere) `[10:12]`. The
stated mechanism: outdoor light is far brighter than indoor light even with every light on, which
**"blunt[s] the morning cortisol spike, that cortisol awakening response"** and sets the clock for
sleep 12–16 hours later `[11:14]`. A 10,000+ lux light box is offered as a substitute on dark or
overcast days; he still recommends at least 10 minutes outdoors regardless of weather `[12:14]`. At
night: phone off and out of the room, room temperature around **67°F**, and he travels with a CO2
meter, because **"if the carbon dioxide in your room is over 900 parts per million at night while
you're sleeping, this can affect sleep quality"** `[13:14]`. He states the mechanistic chain from
poor sleep to low testosterone explicitly: **"if you're sleeping like garbage, the pituitary, the
brain is going to tell your testicles, 'Don't make as much testosterone.' Your LH and FSH are going
to go down"** — and attributes his own historically lower testosterone in residency and medical
school to poor sleep during that period `[13:14]`. His CO2 hack is running the bathroom exhaust fan
overnight, bringing bedroom CO2 down to roughly **650 ppm** versus 900–1,000+ ppm without it
(outdoor baseline is stated as ~410 ppm) `[14:15]`. He also blue-tapes window edges and a TV's
standby LED, uses Breathe Right strips and silicone earplugs, prefers cotton bedding to polyester,
and tries to be in bed an hour before his target sleep time to wind down `[14:15]`–`[15:16]`.

## Environment: plastics and RF EMF

`[15:16]`–`[20:21]` — Saladino avoids drinking from plastic, sourcing glass where possible even
while traveling, and says his nonprofit (ABNRF, the Animal Based Nutrition Research Foundation)
tested bottled water for this; he also claims meat from Radius butcher shop tested lower in
plastics than meat from Whole Foods `[16:16]`. He wears wool, including wool underwear, citing
"experiments in dogs and in humans" that polyester next to the testicles impairs sperm formation,
and connects this to hormone formation generally, while acknowledging he can't control every
exposure (some meat and his raw milk still arrive in plastic) `[16:16]`–`[17:17]`. On EMF: he cites
evidence that a phone left on near the bed at night impairs sleep quality, and separately that a
phone carried in pants may affect sperm (or, in women, ovaries) — so his phone goes on full
airplane mode (Wi-Fi and Bluetooth both greyed out, not just cellular) whenever it's in his pocket
`[17:17]`–`[18:18]`. He demonstrates a meter reading jumping from roughly 2,500 to over 2 million
microwatts per square meter when the phone is switched on, and states the phone is "your biggest
exposure" to RF EMF — larger than living near a 5G antenna `[18:18]`–`[19:19]`. He turns his Wi-Fi
router off at night and covers it with a Faraday bag during the day, citing readings of roughly
21,000 µW/m² with the bag on versus 40,000–56,000 µW/m² without it `[19:19]`. He acknowledges the
standard rebuttal — that RF EMF is non-ionizing and low-power — but maintains **"the evidence
continues to mount [that] RF EMF is maybe not benign for humans"** `[20:21]`.

## Symptoms and body signals addressed

- **Needing pre-workout to train** — offered as a sign of being underslept and undernourished
  rather than a training-intensity gap `[00:00]`.
- **Falling libido, falling energy, "not fresh in the gym"** — named as overtraining signals,
  alongside the DHEAS-to-cortisol ratio as the lab-based check `[00:00]`–`[01:00]`.
- **Decreased sperm count and motility from sauna heat** — the stated reason for icing the
  testicles during sauna use `[03:03]`–`[04:03]`.
- **Low LH and FSH** — described as the pituitary's response to poor sleep, and the mechanistic
  link Saladino draws from bad sleep to low testosterone `[13:14]`.
- **Bedroom CO2 above ~900 ppm** — flagged as a sleep-quality disruptor `[13:14]`.

## Glossary terms introduced

- **Cortisol awakening response** — the morning cortisol spike that outdoor light exposure is said
  to blunt, setting the circadian clock `[11:14]`.
- **ABNRF (Animal Based Nutrition Research Foundation)** — Saladino's own nonprofit, cited as the

## Source-stated confidence

Mixed, and largely self-reported. The 857 ng/dL figure and the prior ~500 ng/dL carnivore-era figure
are both personal testimony with no lab report shown in the video itself. Several claims cite
unnamed studies — the seed-oil/testicle claim, the saturated-fat meta-analysis, the sauna/dementia
cohort — with Saladino saying only "I'll put a study/link here for you guys," pointing to an
on-screen graphic that isn't captured in the transcript and so can't be verified against this
source. Saladino explicitly hedges his own testicle-supplement claim, favoring a microRNA/peptide
mechanism over a direct-androgen one **"whether or not that's part of the effect... is
questionable"** `[05:05]`–`[06:06]`. The polyester/sperm and phone/EMF claims cite "studies" in
humans and dogs, or in humans generally, without naming them. The denial of PED/TRT use is stated
directly and repeatedly but, like the lab numbers, is not independently documented here.

address different levers (thyroid there, training/diet/sleep/environment here).
