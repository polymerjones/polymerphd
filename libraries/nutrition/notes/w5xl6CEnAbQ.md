# Plants Have Their Own Agenda (Power Project Podcast Excerpt)

## Central claim

**Speaking on the Power Project podcast, the host argues plants evolved chemical defenses —
pesticides and toxins — specifically to deter being eaten, and that most dietary pesticide exposure
comes from these plant-produced compounds rather than synthetic agriculture, concluding the
tradeoff is a net detriment rather than a net benefit.** `[00:00]` "Plants do not exist to feed
humans, plants have their own agenda... kale doesn't give a shit... Plants can't move, they can't
defend themselves. So what they have done is evolved myriad pesticides and toxins to defend against
rodents, insects, herbivores, and other people."

## The argument's structure

`[00:00]` — Explicitly flagged by the speaker as anthropomorphization ("indulge me"): plants exist
to move their own DNA forward, not to feed animals. Because plants can't flee or physically defend
themselves, natural selection favored chemical deterrents instead. **The specific empirical claim:**
"the majority of what we take in in terms of pesticides are from plants" — framed as outweighing
synthetic pesticide exposure. The speaker allows for **genetic variability** in human tolerance
("if we can tolerate them... perhaps we can tolerate a small amount") before concluding "there's
probably a net detriment."
