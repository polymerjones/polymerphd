# Best foods for magnesium deficiency and sleep

## Central claim

**The host argues magnesium deficiency (affecting an estimated 56%+ of the world's population) can
contribute to poor sleep, and that animal-based sources — milk, meat, heart, coconut water — provide
more bioavailable magnesium than plant sources like nuts and leafy greens, where magnesium is bound
by phytic acid or oxalates.** `[00:00]` "The time in my life when I had the most profound magnesium
deficiency was when I was a raw vegan... despite eating tons of leafy greens and tons of almonds."

## The bioavailability claim and personal anecdote

`[00:00]` — Cited prevalence: over 56% of the world's population deficient in magnesium. Named
animal-based sources: milk, orange juice (unclear framing — possibly a citrus-magnesium comparison),
meat, heart, and coconut water. **Bioavailability mechanism**: magnesium in nuts and leafy greens is
"often bound up in things like phytic acid or can be bound by oxalates," making it "very poorly
bioavailable" despite those foods' reputation as magnesium sources. Personal anecdote: the host's
own most severe magnesium deficiency occurred during a raw vegan period despite heavy leafy-green
and almond intake. Magnesium is described as needed for "more than 300 biochemical reactions" in the
body.

## Symptoms and body signals addressed

- **Poor sleep** — attributed to magnesium deficiency `[00:00]`.

## Glossary terms introduced

- **Phytic acid** — a compound in plant foods (nuts, leafy greens) that binds magnesium, reducing
  its bioavailability `[00:00]`.
- **Oxalates** — compounds in some plant foods that similarly bind magnesium, reducing its
  bioavailability `[00:00]`.

## Source-stated confidence

Moderate. Magnesium's role in 300+ biochemical reactions and the global deficiency estimate are
consistent with mainstream nutrition science; the phytic acid/oxalate bioavailability-reduction
mechanism is real and established, though the personal raw-vegan anecdote is not a controlled
comparison.
