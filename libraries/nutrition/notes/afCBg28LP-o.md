# Does red meat cause gout?

## Central claim

**The host argues red meat, organs, and fruit do not cause gout in insulin-sensitive individuals,
citing his own low uric acid bloodwork from 2020 and August 2022 despite eating a lot of all three
foods, and framing insulin sensitivity — achieved by cutting processed sugars and seed oils — as the
key variable determining whether these foods raise uric acid.** `[00:00]` "Don't fear uric acid or
gout eating these foods if you are insulin sensitive."

## The mechanism and the personal evidence

`[00:00]` — **Gout defined**: "the deposition and crystallization of uric acid in the joints,"
resulting from elevated blood uric acid. **Personal bloodwork**: uric acid levels from August 2022
and 2020, both described as "very low," during periods of high meat, organ, and fruit intake.
**Key variable named**: insulin sensitivity — "in insulin sensitive individuals, uric acid is not
elevated by these highly nutritious, highly nourishing foods." **Practical recommendation**: become
insulin sensitive by cutting processed sugars and seed oils.

## Symptoms and body signals addressed

- **Gout** — reframed as primarily an insulin-resistance-driven condition rather than a direct
  consequence of eating red meat, organs, or fruit `[00:00]`.

## Glossary terms introduced

- **Gout** — the deposition and crystallization of uric acid in the body's joints, typically
  resulting from elevated blood uric acid `[00:00]`.
