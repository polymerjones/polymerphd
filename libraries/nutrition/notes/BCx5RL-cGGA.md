# How to shop for steaks

## Central claim

**Comparing two New York steaks side by side, the host argues grass-finished beef is identifiable
by darker red meat and more orange-colored fat (versus grain-finished beef's paler fat), and prefers
grass-finished for its nutrient content, taste, and environmental/animal welfare benefits, while
naming hanger steak and sirloin flap as his favorite cuts overall, followed by skirt steak, ribeye,
and New York steak.** `[00:00]` "This is better for the environment, better for the cows, better for
humans. Grain-finished steaks can be great, but I always will select a grass-finished steak over a
grain-finished steak."

## The visual comparison and cut ranking

`[00:00]` — **Fat color**: grass-finished steak's fat is more orange; grain-finished steak's fat is
paler. **Meat color**: grass-finished steak is darker red. **Marbling**: for tenderness, the host
recommends some fat throughout the meat rather than a very lean cut, which can be tougher (with the
caveat that this depends on the specific cut). **Favorite cuts, ranked**: hanger steak and sirloin
flap (top choices), followed by skirt steak, then ribeye and New York steak.

## Source-stated confidence

Low to moderate. Fat color as a grass-fed indicator (from beta-carotene in grass) is a commonly cited
and broadly accurate heuristic; the marbling-and-tenderness relationship is a widely accepted
culinary principle rather than a specific cited study in this clip.
