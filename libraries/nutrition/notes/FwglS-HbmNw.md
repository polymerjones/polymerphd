# Why I Don't Eat Most Chicken or Pork..

## Central claim

**Chicken and pork are avoided because both are monogastric animals that accumulate omega-6
linoleic acid from the corn, soy, and seed oils they're typically fed — with the host arguing it's
nearly impossible to find chicken or pork raised on a species-appropriate diet (bugs/grubs/worms for
chickens, foraging for pigs), whereas grass-fed, grass-finished beef is comparatively easy to
source.** `[00:00]` "Both pigs... and chickens are monogastric animals. That means they accumulate
seed oils. They accumulate omega-6 linoleic acid in the feed that they eat... This is why I prefer
beef. It's easier to get grass-fed, grass-finished beef."

## The species-appropriate-feed argument

`[00:00]` — Chickens' natural diet: bugs, grubs, worms. Pigs' natural diet: rooting/foraging like a
wild pig. Neither is commercially available at scale, unlike grass-fed beef, which the host argues
is comparatively easy to find because cows are ruminants naturally suited to grass.

## Symptoms and body signals addressed

None named directly — this addresses fat composition and sourcing rather than a specific symptom.

## Glossary terms introduced

- **Monogastric** — used without full definition; animals (like pigs and chickens) with a single-
  chambered stomach, contrasted implicitly with ruminants like cows `[00:00]`.

## Analogies worth reusing

None distinct.

## Source-stated confidence

Moderate — the biological claim that monogastric animals' fat composition more directly reflects
dietary fat intake (including linoleic acid) than ruminants' does is a real, documented pattern in
animal nutrition science; the claim that it's "nearly impossible" to find species-appropriate-fed
chicken or pork is a sourcing observation rather than a nutritional one, and doesn't account for
genuinely pasture-raised/foraged poultry and pork producers that do exist, just less commonly.
