# Does your sea salt contain heavy metals?

## Central claim

**Citing an independent heavy metal analysis, the host reports Celtic sea salt (his former choice)
delivers almost 5 micrograms of lead from a typical 9-gram daily intake — nearly 10 times the
California Prop 65 limit of 0.5 micrograms/day, though still under a cited FDA tolerable limit of
12 micrograms/day — and separately flags Redmond Real Salt for significant lead and other heavy
metals.** `[00:00]` "Three of these sea salts had levels of heavy metals like lead that were pretty
darn high."

## The specific figures

`[00:00]` — **Celtic sea salt**: ~5 micrograms of lead from 9 grams (an average daily intake),
against **California Prop 65's 0.5 microgram/day limit** (roughly 10x over) and a cited **FDA
tolerable limit of 12 micrograms/day** (still under that threshold). **Redmond Real Salt**: also
found with "a significant amount of lead and other heavy metals," though a specific figure isn't
given in this transcript. The host frames the Prop 65 figure as the more protective standard worth
paying attention to, even though the FDA limit is more permissive.

## Source-stated confidence

Moderate. The Prop 65 limit and the specific 9-gram/5-microgram calculation are precise and
checkable. The cited "FDA tolerable limit of 12 micrograms/day" is worth noting alongside a
different FDA figure cited elsewhere in this library (see Conflicts) — the two aren't necessarily
contradictory since one may apply to children specifically, but the discrepancy is worth flagging
before citing either figure as "the" FDA limit.

## Conflicts with other sources

`9cmx-lt3n1w` ("I Tested the Top 7 Salts for Toxins") independently corroborates Redmond Real Salt
containing lead over the Prop 65 limit, giving a specific figure — **0.6 mcg lead/tsp** — that this
short doesn't include. That note cites a different FDA lead figure, though — **"FDA child limit 2.2
micrograms/day"** — versus this short's **"FDA tolerable limit of lead in a day is 12
micrograms."** These may refer to different populations (children versus general/adult), but the
two sources should not be treated as citing the identical FDA standard without checking which
population each applies to.
