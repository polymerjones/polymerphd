# Celery = sunburn??

## Central claim

**The host argues celery, despite its popular "superfood" reputation (particularly as celery juice),
is a defended plant containing furanocoumarins — compounds that accumulate in skin and cause
phototoxic injury upon sun exposure — citing a documented case study of a woman who developed
phototoxic skin injury after drinking celery juice and going in the sun.** `[00:00]` "Celery is
defended just like every other vegetable... it's full of furanocoumarins, which accumulate in your
skin and can cause phototoxic injury when you're exposed to the sun."

## The mechanism and the case study

`[00:00]` — Mechanism: **furanocoumarins**, present in celery (a stem with leaves), accumulate in
skin and cause **phototoxic injury** when the skin is subsequently exposed to sunlight. Cited
evidence: a specific case study of a woman who drank a large quantity of celery juice, believing it
healthy, and was subsequently burned on her skin after sun exposure.

## Symptoms and body signals addressed

- **Phototoxic skin injury (burning)** — attributed to furanocoumarin accumulation from celery
  consumption followed by sun exposure `[00:00]`.

## Glossary terms introduced

- **Furanocoumarins** — compounds in celery that accumulate in skin and cause phototoxic injury upon
  sun exposure `[00:00]`.
- **Phototoxic injury** — skin damage caused by a photosensitizing compound (here, furanocoumarins)
  reacting with sunlight exposure `[00:00]`.

Moderate to high. Furanocoumarin-induced phototoxicity from celery (and related Apiaceae plants like
parsnip and parsley) is a well-documented dermatological phenomenon, and the host explicitly cites a
