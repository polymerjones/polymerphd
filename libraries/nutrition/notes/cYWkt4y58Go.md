# What to eat for a healthy libido

## Central claim

**Citing corn flakes' 1894 origin as a bland, plant-based food deliberately prescribed by Harvey
Kellogg at the Battle Creek Sanitarium to suppress patients' libido, the host argues a vegan diet
is "the ultimate anti-libido diet," and that animal foods (organs, meat, fruit, honey, raw dairy)
are what optimize libido and sexual performance instead.** `[00:00]` "Cornflakes were developed in
1894 by Harvey Kellogg for patients at the Battle Creek Sanitarium to get rid of their libido. The
prescription? A bland, plant-based diet high in cornflakes that would be deficient in the nutrients
that we know are found in things like butter and eggs and meat."

## The etymology argument

`[00:00]` — Carnitine and carnosine are noted to share a root with "carnis," the Latin word for
meat, used rhetorically ("carnal desires can be healthy") to link animal-food nutrients to libido,
though this is presented as wordplay rather than a mechanistic claim.

## Source-stated confidence

Low-to-moderate for the historical claim — Kellogg's development of corn flakes as part of a
bland, anti-masturbatory dietary regimen at Battle Creek Sanitarium is a documented historical
episode, though no specific historical source is cited in this clip. **Low for the nutrient-libido
link** — carnitine and carnosine are named as present in meat, but no mechanism or study connects
them to libido specifically in this clip; the "carnis" etymology point is explicitly a rhetorical
device, not a biological claim.
