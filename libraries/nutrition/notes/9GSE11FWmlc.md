# Darker eggs yolks = more nutrition

## Central claim

**Comparing a pasture-raised egg and a cage-free egg side by side, the host argues the darker
yolk and white color of the pasture-raised egg reflects higher beta-carotene from a diet of bugs,
worms, and other nutrient-rich foods (versus corn and soy for cage-free chickens confined to a
barn), and recommends pasture-raised, organic, corn- and soy-free eggs as higher quality.**
`[00:00]` "You can literally see the difference in quality between these two eggs based on what
these chickens are eating."

## The comparison

`[00:00]` — **Pasture-raised**: 108 square feet of roaming space per chicken, eating bugs, worms,
and other nutrient-rich foods, credited with producing more beta-carotene and a darker yolk and
white. **Cage-free**: not caged, but confined to a barn with no outdoor access, fed only corn and
soy, producing a "pale, weak egg yolk and a much runnier, weak egg white."
