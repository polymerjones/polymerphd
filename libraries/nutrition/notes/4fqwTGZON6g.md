# Paper cups are not paper…

## Central claim

**The host demonstrates that both paper cups and aluminum cans are internally lined with a thin
plastic film — peeling it back from a paper cup, and dissolving an aluminum can's metal with sodium
hydroxide to reveal the plastic liner underneath — arguing hot liquids (coffee) and acidic drinks
(Coke) drive substantial microplastic release from this lining, and that reducing plastic exposure
generally can improve testosterone and erectile function.** `[00:00]` "It is just exploding plastic
particles into your coffee."

## The demonstration

`[00:00]` — Two visual demonstrations: peeling the paper layer off a cup to reveal a plastic lining
underneath (described as looking "like a condom"), and using sodium hydroxide to dissolve an
aluminum can's metal, revealing the same type of plastic liner inside. The host attributes Coke's
particularly high microplastic content to its acidity interacting with the can's plastic lining, and
separately states boiling coffee poured into a plastic-lined paper cup releases a large amount of
microplastic. He closes by linking overall plastic exposure reduction (cans, bottles, paper cups,
plastic food storage/heating) to improved testosterone and potentially improved erectile
dysfunction, "without these medications."
