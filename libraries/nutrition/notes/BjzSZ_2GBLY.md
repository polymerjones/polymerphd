# Dermatologists lied to you about the sun

## Central claim

**Responding to a dermatology conference where attendees wore plastic clothing and chemical
sunscreens even on cloudy days, the host argues this level of sun avoidance is excessive — while
explicitly acknowledging real UV risks (photoaging, increased cancer risk) — and offers specific
mitigation strategies instead: timed low-UV sun exposure to build a "solar callus," mineral (zinc)
sunscreen over chemical sunscreen, and natural-fiber clothing over plastic.** `[00:00]` "The sun is
an essential part of being healthy. Use it responsibly and you'll thrive."

## The acknowledged risk and the recommended mitigations

`[00:00]` — **Acknowledged risk**: too much UV light can cause photoaging and increase cancer risk,
depending on skin tone — the host does not deny this. **Mitigation 1 (fair skin)**: sun exposure in
morning and evening low-UV windows to build a **solar callus** (tan), described as "somewhat
protective," before attempting midday exposure. **Mitigation 2 (sunscreen)**: non-nano zinc plus
tallow-type mineral sunscreen preferred over chemical sunscreens containing **avobenzone,
octocrylene, and oxybenzone** — named as endocrine disrupting, potentially cancer-associated, and
absorbed through skin (with excretion in urine and feces cited as evidence of systemic absorption).
`[01:00]` **Mitigation 3 (clothing)**: natural fibers preferred over polyester/plastic clothing, which
the host says also contains endocrine-disrupting chemicals absorbable through skin.

## Symptoms and body signals addressed

- **Photoaging and cancer risk** — acknowledged as real risks of excessive UV exposure `[00:00]`.
- **Hormone disruption** — attributed to chemical sunscreen ingredients and plastic clothing fibers
  `[00:00]`–`[01:00]`.

## Glossary terms introduced

- **Solar callus** — a tan built through gradual, low-UV sun exposure, described as somewhat
  protective against subsequent sun damage `[00:00]`.
