# Can't afford organic? Try this!

## Central claim

**Demonstrating with pesticide detection strips, the host shows conventional strawberries washed
in a 1% baking soda solution (one teaspoon per liter of water) for 15 minutes test significantly
lower for pesticide residue than unwashed conventional strawberries, offering this as a
budget-friendly option when organic produce isn't affordable or available.** `[00:00]` "I think
organic is clearly best, but if you can't find or you can't afford organic, just washing and simple
baking soda... that clearly made a difference and reduced the pesticides significantly."

## The demonstration

`[00:00]` — Method: conventional strawberries tested with pesticide detection strips (blue
indicating no detected pesticide) both before and after a **1% baking soda solution soak for 15
minutes**. Result shown on camera: a clear reduction in detected pesticide residue after the soak,
compared to unwashed conventional strawberries and to an organic strawberry control. Proposed
mechanism: baking soda's alkalinity deactivates pesticide residues that tend to be acidic, beyond
what plain water rinsing alone would remove.
