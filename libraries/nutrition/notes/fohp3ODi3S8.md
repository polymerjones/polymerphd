# Why I don't eat fish

## Central claim

**Fish is avoided due to widespread heavy metal contamination (cadmium, mercury, arsenic, lead),
microplastic content shown in animal models to disrupt hormones and fertility, and a cited study
associating fish consumption with increased melanoma rates — with egg yolks, animal fat, meat, and
organs offered as sufficient alternative sources of omega-3s and iodine.** `[00:00]` "Most fish is
highly contaminated with heavy metals like cadmium, mercury, arsenic, lead, and others... In a
recent study, fish consumption was also associated with increased rates of melanoma, probably due
to levels of heavy metals and polychlorinated biphenyls, PCBs, in fish."

## The named contaminants and claims

`[00:00]` — **Heavy metals:** cadmium, mercury, arsenic, lead. **Microplastics:** described as
bioaccumulating in humans, shown in animal models to disrupt hormones and fertility. **PCBs
(polychlorinated biphenyls):** named alongside heavy metals as a probable driver of a cited
melanoma-association study.

## Symptoms and body signals addressed

- **Melanoma rates** — cited as elevated with fish consumption in a referenced study `[00:00]`.
- **Hormone and fertility disruption** — cited as shown in animal models exposed to microplastics
  `[00:00]`.

## Glossary terms introduced

- **PCBs (polychlorinated biphenyls)** — named as a contaminant found in fish, linked to the cited
  melanoma association `[00:00]`.

## Source-stated confidence

Moderate-to-low — heavy metal contamination in fish (particularly larger predatory species) is a
well-documented, mainstream concern; the fish-consumption/melanoma association is described as "a
recent study" without naming the authors, year, or journal, making it unverifiable from this clip
alone; the microplastic/hormone-disruption claim is explicitly scoped to animal models.
