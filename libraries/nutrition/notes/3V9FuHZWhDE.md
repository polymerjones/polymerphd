# Is plant-based meat truly healthy?

## Central claim

**Reacting to factory footage of Impossible Burger production, the host argues plant-based meat
is inferior to regeneratively raised red meat on every axis — health (citing textured soy protein,
seed oils, leghemoglobin, and methylcellulose as its actual ingredients), environmental impact
(carbon positive versus regenerative beef's carbon negative), and, per the host, market perception
(citing stock performance as evidence "people are catching onto this").** `[00:00]` "In every way,
plant-based burgers are inferior. They're horrible for your health. They're not better for the
environment. And they're just a farce."

## The ingredients and the carbon claim

`[00:00]` — Ingredients named on camera: **textured soy protein, sunflower oil, leghemoglobin**
(the genetically engineered "fake hemoglobin" that gives Impossible Burger its red, bleeding
appearance), and **methylcellulose**, which the host again calls "sawdust." On carbon footprint:
plant-based burgers like Impossible are stated flatly as **carbon positive**, contrasted with
regeneratively raised red meat and ground beef as **carbon negative** — no specific figures are
given in this clip, and cell-cultured or 3D-printed burgers are mentioned as unable to make the
carbon-negative claim either.
