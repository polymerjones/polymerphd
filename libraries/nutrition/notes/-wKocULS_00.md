# Pasture-raised vs store-bought eggs

## Central claim

**Cracking a pasture-raised egg and a store-bought egg side by side, the host argues the visible
difference in yolk height and egg-white thickness — not yolk color — is the real quality signal,
and recommends sourcing truly pasture-raised eggs, ideally from a known farmer, while affirming that
any egg is still nutritious.** `[00:00]` "The best metrics that you can use to tell if your egg is
high quality is not the color of the egg, but the height of the yolk and the thickness of the white
around the yolk."

## The comparison

`[00:00]` — **Pasture-raised egg** (from a hen described as eating "a little bit of fermented
bamboo, bugs and worms on the land," laid that morning): a hard shell, thick yolk, thick white with
a visible membrane holding its shape. **Store-bought egg** (from a chicken "eating exclusively corn
and soy"): the white "completely dissolved" through the host's fingers and lay flat, with a visibly
shorter, wider yolk. The host also notes chickens eating their own eggshells as evidence against
treating them as herbivores.

## Source-stated confidence

Low to moderate. The visual test itself (yolk height, white thickness/membrane integrity) is
directly demonstrated rather than merely asserted, though no controlled study is cited connecting
these visual markers to a specific nutrient difference.
