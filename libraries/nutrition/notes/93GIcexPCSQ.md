# Chocolate covered almonds vs. eggs

## Central claim

**The host argues the Food Compass nutrient scoring system, developed at Tufts University with
government funding, rates chocolate-covered almonds healthier than eggs — despite the almonds
containing high-fructose corn syrup, seed oils, and oxalate-rich, poorly digested almonds, while
eggs offer choline, vitamin K2, vitamin A, and highly bioavailable protein — using this as evidence
that official nutrition guidance can't be trusted at face value.** `[00:00]` "I think something is
fishy here. I think we can tell intuitively that eggs are much better for humans."

## The comparison and the scoring system

`[00:00]` — **Food Compass**: described as a nutrient scoring system developed by researchers at
Tufts University over three years, with government funding. **Chocolate-covered almonds**:
contain high-fructose corn syrup, seed oils, and almonds — flagged for oxalates, poor digestibility,
and digestive enzyme inhibitors. **Eggs**: credited with choline, vitamin K2, vitamin A, and highly
bioavailable protein. The host's core objection is that this specific scoring outcome (almonds
rated above eggs) reveals a flawed methodology in an influential, taxpayer-funded guidance system.
