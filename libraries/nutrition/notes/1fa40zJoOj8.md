# Plant-based meat is a scam

## Central claim

**Comparing a regeneratively raised bison ribeye to a mycelium-based "mushroom steak" head to
head, the host argues the animal product wins on nutrients (naming eight specific compounds absent
from the plant product), protein quantity and bioavailability, taste, and carbon footprint —
concluding a regeneratively raised, grass-fed steak is the "sound decision" on every axis
tested.** `[00:00]` "Nutritionally, environmentally, ethically, taste-wise, superior bison ribeye."

## The comparison, by category

`[00:00]` — **Nutrients**: carnosine (transcribed "ancerine"), taurine, creatine, carnitine,
choline, vitamin B12, vitamin K2, heme iron, and selenium are named as present in the bison ribeye
and absent from the mushroom steak. **Protein**: two mushroom steaks together provided **34 grams
of "not very bioavailable" plant protein**, versus **64 grams of bioavailable animal protein** in
the 10-ounce bison ribeye. **Carbon**: the regenerative bison ribeye is described as sequestering
more soil carbon than its production emits, contrasted with the mushroom steak framed as
"carbon-positive." **Taste**: the host eats both on camera, finding the steak "delicious" and
declining to finish the plant-based version — "I don't really want to eat this."
