# The best fat for heart health

## Central claim

**Challenging the mainstream view that butter's saturated fat content makes it bad for the heart,
the host argues grass-fed butter is rich in vitamin K2, and cites the Rotterdam study as showing a
clear, consistent association between higher K2 levels and lower atherosclerotic coronary artery
disease.** `[00:00]` "I bet you didn't know that butter is a heart-healthy food."

## The cited study and the nutrient

`[00:00]` — **The Rotterdam study** is named specifically (a real, well-known Dutch epidemiological
cohort) as showing higher vitamin K2 levels "significantly consistently associated with lower
levels of atherosclerotic coronary artery disease." Vitamin K2 is described as found "essentially
only in animal foods" — liver, meat, and butter named specifically — which the host uses to argue
butter's saturated fat profile shouldn't be evaluated in isolation from its K2 content. Grass-fed
butter's naturally orange color is offered as visual evidence of nutrient density (a color-as-
nutrient framing used elsewhere in this library).
