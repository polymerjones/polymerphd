# Your bacon is BS: Stop eating pork

## Central claim

**Pigs are monogastric animals, and because most are fed corn and soy, their fat becomes "massively
enriched" with linoleic acid** `[00:00]` — a bigger problem than with chicken, the source argues,
"because pigs are fattier than chickens... if you're eating pork you're getting a ton of pork fat."
The wordplay he leans on: "enrich sounds like a good thing but in this case enrich is a bad thing"
`[00:00]`.

## The Tokelau comparison

`[00:00]`–`[01:01]` — The source cites a paper titled (as read on screen) "Cholesterol, coconuts and
diet on Polynesian atolls: a natural experiment — the Tokelau Island studies," noting that despite
"a huge amount of saturated fat in their diet," the population showed no evidence of vascular
disease, which he takes as "further evidence that saturated fat is probably not a harmful thing for
humans." The paper's fatty-acid table, from pigs and chickens raised there "wild" on a mostly
coconut diet, is presented as a baseline: **pork fat measured 2% linoleic acid, chicken fat 2.5%**
`[01:01]`.

## Today's pig and chicken fat, by contrast

`[02:02]` — Against that Tokelau baseline, the source states today's chicken fat runs "15 to 20
percent" linoleic acid, and pork fat "15 to 20 plus percent" — pointing listeners to a prior podcast
with Brad Marshall, described as raising low-linoleic-acid pigs. A second cited study (on dietary
mono- and polyunsaturated fat effects on pig adipose tissue) is summarized as confirming the same
pattern: pigs fed more polyunsaturated fat end up with more polyunsaturated fat in their own tissue,
while pigs fed more saturated or monounsaturated fat end up with less — because, as monogastric
animals, **pigs "store what is present in their diets in their fat... long term, and that is what we
consume as humans"** `[03:04]`.

## What the source does and doesn't eat

`[03:04]`–`[04:05]` — He references a separate podcast with Nina Tayshultz (title paraphrased as
something like "how pork and chicken could be killing you") for more detail, then states his own
practice: he does not eat pork "unless I know exactly what it's fed," would eat pork if he could get
it from Tokelau, and recently ate wild pig killed on a hunt — but does not eat pigs "fed corn and
soy and seed oils because it accumulates in their fat" `[04:05]`.

## Symptoms and body signals addressed

None directly. The video argues from tissue fatty-acid composition and a population-level absence
of vascular disease in the Tokelau data, not from a specific symptom picture.

## Glossary terms introduced

- **Monogastric** — used to describe pigs (and, by comparison, chickens and humans) as animals with
  a single stomach that cannot process dietary polyunsaturated fat the way a ruminant can, so it
  accumulates in their tissue instead `[03:04]`.
- **Linoleic acid** — the omega-6 fat tracked throughout as the marker of what an animal was actually
  fed, from 2% in "wild" Tokelau pork fat to 15–20%+ in today's corn-and-soy-fed pork `[00:00]`,
  `[02:02]`.
