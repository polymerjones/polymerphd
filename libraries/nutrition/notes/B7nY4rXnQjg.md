# Why LED lights are harming your sleep

## Central claim

**Using slowed footage to show LED bulbs flickering, the host argues this imperceptible flicker
stimulates the nervous system, causing a "tired but wired" feeling, headaches, and eye strain, while
also disrupting circadian rhythm — recommending incandescent (Edison) bulbs instead, whose heated
tungsten filament has thermal inertia and produces steady, non-flickering light like natural
sunlight.** `[00:00]` "This is unhealthy processed light. Just like processed food is bad for you,
this is processed light. This is bad for you."

## The mechanism and the analogy

`[00:00]` — **LED flicker effects named**: nervous system stimulation ("tired but wired at night"),
headaches, eye strain, and circadian rhythm disruption. **Incandescent bulb mechanism**: a tungsten
filament heated to 4,000°F, with **thermal inertia** meaning the AC current's on/off cycling doesn't
produce perceptible flicker — the eye perceives it as constant, steady light, comparable to
outdoor sunlight, which the host demonstrates has no flicker. **Framing**: LED light characterized as
"processed light," paralleling processed food as an artificial, unhealthy version of a natural
input.

- **Headaches** — attributed to LED flicker `[00:00]`.

## Glossary terms introduced

- **Thermal inertia** — the property of a heated incandescent filament that keeps it glowing steadily
  between AC power cycles, unlike LEDs `[00:00]`.
- **"Processed light"** — the host's term framing LED light as an artificial, health-degrading
  parallel to processed food `[00:00]`.
