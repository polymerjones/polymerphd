# How to lower glyphosate exposure

## Central claim

**The source's own closing checklist for cutting glyphosate exposure: test your level first, then
address water, meat sourcing, produce, grains, beans/soy, and kale, one lever at a time** `[00:00]`.
"Your food choices, your water choices, your environmental choices, where you live — all of this
will affect your glyphosate exposure" `[03:03]`.

## Testing first, as a baseline

`[00:00]` — The first recommended step: "get your glyphosate level checked" through Great Plains
Labs, working with "a doctor who is savvy," and use that result "as a baseline to start from." The
source admits he personally "haven't done that... but I will do that in the future." He also shares
an anecdote about a Costa Rican grass-fed operation confirming they "never feed hay" and haven't
used herbicides "in the last few years," with plans to test their own meat for glyphosate
`[00:00]`.

## Water, produce, and meat sourcing

`[01:02]` — **"Reverse osmosis is probably the best way to ensure glyphosate-free water"** — the
source flags that ordinary water "could certainly be contaminated with glyphosate." Organic fruit
and vegetables are recommended "if you choose to" eat vegetables at all, alongside knowing the
source of one's meat. Grains are named as a clear source: "avoid the processed foods... Quaker
Oats, anything made with oats, wheat — all of those grains are going to have glyphosate." Beans,
soy, and tofu are grouped together to avoid genistein, described as worsening "the estrogenic
effects of glyphosate in animal models and potentially in humans" `[01:02]`.

## Retesting and a broader toxin panel

`[02:02]` — After making changes, the source suggests repeating the glyphosate test to see if the
level changes, and offers a second test — **GPL-TOX from Great Plains Lab — "to look at all of the
toxins... multiple pesticides, multiple environmental contaminants"** — with the disclosure "I have
no affiliation with them in this podcast."

## Kale as a final lever

`[03:03]` — Avoiding kale specifically is tied to two separate contaminants: **"you'll also have a
lower exposure to these heavy metals like thallium that appear to possibly accumulate in these
leafy greens,"** and, separately, avoiding dacthal "if you're not getting organic kale."

The genistein/glyphosate interaction is attributed to "that one study" without further citation
here — the underlying study is treated more fully elsewhere (see Conflicts). Every recommendation
in this video is stated as the source's own advice ("I would avoid," "I would advise against it")
rather than as settled clinical guidance, and the source is transparent about having no financial
affiliation with either testing lab named `[00:00]`, `[02:02]`.
