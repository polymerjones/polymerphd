# Avoid These Fish at All Costs

## Central claim

**Almost all salmon sold at Costco is farmed Atlantic salmon, dyed to mimic the color wild salmon
gets naturally from its diet, and carrying meaningfully more contamination (PCBs, PFAS, heavy
metals, antibiotics) than wild salmon — while frequently costing more per pound than grass-fed
beef.** `[00:00]`–`[02:04]` Stated flatly: "I would never ever ever eat a piece of farm-raised
salmon" `[02:04]`.

## How wild salmon gets its color, and how farmed salmon fakes it

`[01:01]`–`[03:06]` — Wild salmon's orange/red color comes from astaxanthin, a pigment in krill
that the host calls "probably good for humans" as an antioxidant. Species differ by how much
krill (and therefore astaxanthin) they eat: sockeye is described as the most red, coho as more
orange, and king as sometimes white or light orange because **"king salmon actually has an enzyme
that prevents it from metabolizing astaxanthin."** Farm-raised Atlantic salmon, by contrast, is
naturally "stark white" and only takes on its familiar orange color because it is "fed
betacarotene in the feed" — labels for this state **"farm-raised means color added."** The host
demonstrates this live with an uncolored piece of salmon at the store.

## What's wrong with farm-raised salmon

`[02:04]` — Farmed salmon are described as raised "in pens or open nets in the ocean," crowded
together such that "they share diseases," require antibiotic feeding, and are fed pellets
"contaminated with pesticides, heavy metals." The host names a specific environmental effect —
eutrophication, where waste from penned salmon pollutes surrounding ocean water — and states
**farmed salmon runs higher in PCBs (polychlorinated biphenyls) and PFAS ("forever
chemicals")**, and is "probably" higher in heavy metals due to how it's raised.

## Labeling tricks called out

- **"Pharaoh [Faroe] Island salmon"** at high-end restaurants is, per the host, "just a fancy way
  of saying farm-raised salmon" `[03:06]`.
- Smoked salmon products (Kirkland-brand and others) marketed with decorative Greek-flag branding
  or "honey maple smoked" names are still Atlantic (farmed) salmon with **"color added"** printed
  on the ingredient panel `[07:11]`, `[09:12]`.
- Costco charges **$2 more per pound** for an "antibiotics-free" sticker on otherwise identical
  farmed salmon — called out as evidence of how routine antibiotic use is in the baseline product
  `[14:16]`–`[15:17]`.

## AI tuna and carbon monoxide

`[04:07]`–`[06:10]` — The host describes "AI" tuna (his term for previously frozen, old,
brown/gray tuna) being trimmed, artificially colored, and treated with carbon monoxide gas in
freezers to appear fresh and red. He connects heavy fish consumption of this kind to mercury
toxicity, naming hatterism (his term/spelling for "hatter's disease," referencing the Mad
Hatter) as the mercury-poisoning syndrome and stating he has **"seen diagnosed cases of hatterism
from eating too much AI tuna in sushi restaurants."**

## Heavy metals and microplastics — the general case

`[06:10]`–`[08:12]` — Lead, mercury, cadmium, and arsenic are named as the main heavy-metal
concerns from fish, alongside arsenic in rice specifically. Health effects listed: oxidative
stress, detoxification burden, developmental delay and decreased IQ in children from lead,
hematopoietic issues from mercury, and bone marrow/blood/lymphoma cancers associated with cadmium
— stated as established associations without a specific citation. Microplastics are described as
concentrated in fish and in bottled water (**"hundreds of thousands or millions of particles of
microplastics in a liter"**), with lower levels in beef by comparison, and the host speculates
("I suspect", explicitly hedged) that farmed salmon carries more microplastic contamination
than wild.

## Salmon versus grass-fed beef, on cost

`[13:15]`–`[16:19]` — The host does a direct price comparison at Costco: farmed salmon at roughly
$9–12/lb versus grass-fed ground beef discounted to around $5/lb, concluding **"why are you eating
salmon again?... you've been scoped."**

## The fish market interview: farming has "levels"

`[16:19]`–`[26:33]` — At a separate fish shop, the owner explains that not all farmed salmon is
equivalent: New Zealand king salmon raised in **"offshore pens... completely organic, basically
pristine clean waters... no antibiotics"** and fed a natural pellet (ground krill/shrimp) is
presented as meaningfully cleaner than typical commodity farmed Atlantic salmon, though still
technically "farm-raised." The owner also explains that wild salmon is seasonal (spring–early
summer only), that FDA-permitted harvest-to-sale windows can run up to 14 days for some farmed
fish versus 3–4 days at his shop, and that **wild salmon always carries parasites and legally must
be frozen before sushi use**, whereas farmed salmon, being processed and traceable, is not subject
to that freezing requirement — which the host flags as a loophole: farmed fish can be called
"sushi grade" without the same parasite-safety freezing wild fish requires `[21:24]`–`[23:27]`.

## Symptoms and body signals addressed

- **Mercury toxicity / hatterism** — described as causing altered mental state ("crazy and wild"
  historically; the host says he has seen diagnosed cases) from heavy AI tuna consumption `[05:09]`.
- **Developmental delay, decreased IQ** in children from lead exposure `[06:10]`.
- **Hematopoietic effects** (blood cell formation issues) from mercury; **bone
  marrow/blood/lymphoma cancer associations** from cadmium `[06:10]`.
- **Endocrine disruption and developmental issues** attributed to microplastics and to PCBs
  `[08:12]`, `[09:12]`.

## Glossary terms introduced

- **Astaxanthin** — the carotenoid pigment from krill that gives wild salmon its color, described
  as being added synthetically to farmed salmon feed instead `[01:01]`, `[09:12]`.
- **Eutrophication** — the host's term for ocean-water pollution caused by concentrated waste from
  open-net salmon farming `[02:04]`.
- **Hatterism** — the host's name for mercury-poisoning-induced mental disturbance, referencing the
  historical "mad hatter" `[05:09]`.
- **Benthic organisms** — used to describe bottom-feeding shellfish (mussels, clams, oysters) that
  accumulate heavy metals like cadmium `[04:07]`.

## Analogies worth reusing

- **"It's like pretending a cow is grass-fed just because you're giving it a little bit of green
  pigment"** — used to describe astaxanthin-fed farmed salmon mimicking wild color without the rest
  of the wild diet's benefits `[09:12]`.
- **"You've been scoped"** — the host's framing for consumers paying a premium for farmed salmon
  under the impression it's a health food, when it costs more than grass-fed beef and carries more
  contamination `[16:19]`.

## Source-stated confidence

Most contamination and health-effect claims (PCBs, PFAS, heavy metal disease associations,
microplastic organ accumulation) are stated as established fact with no specific study, agency
report, or figure cited on-screen. Several claims are explicitly self-hedged by the host: the
microplastic comparison between farmed and wild salmon ("I suspect that farm-raised salmon is
going to be worse" `[08:12]`), and his uncertainty about Costco's specific handling practices
("I really have no idea what they're putting out there" `[20:23]`). The fish-market owner's claims
about New Zealand king-salmon farming practices are presented as his own professional account,
not independently verified in the video.
