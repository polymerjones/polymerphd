# Why aren't doctors taught this?

## Central claim

**The host states that across eight years of formal medical training (four years of medical
school, four years of residency), he received essentially zero nutrition education — nothing about
nutrients in meat or organs, or the value of animal foods — attributing this partly to
pharmaceutical funding of medical schools and research, and describes self-educating through
extensive independent reading to eventually earn a physician nutrition specialist board
certification.** `[00:00]` "If there's a question about nutrition, doctors don't know nutrition...
where's the patient left?"

## The claim and the self-education path

`[00:00]` — **Formal training**: four years of medical school plus four years of residency, with
"essentially zero" nutrition content — no coverage of nutrients in meat, unique organ-meat
nutrients, or animal foods' role in the diet, and no coverage of potential food downsides either.
**Funding critique**: "a lot of the funding for medical schools, a lot of the funding for the
studies comes from pharmaceutical companies," which the host says explains the curriculum's
pharma-centric focus. **Self-education**: reading "hundreds, if not thousands" of articles and
books, and consulting experts, eventually earning a **physician nutrition specialist board
certification** — described as making "a huge difference" in his understanding of health and
disease.
