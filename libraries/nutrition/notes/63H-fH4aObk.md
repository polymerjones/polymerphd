# Do seed oils = chronic disease??

## Central claim

**The host argues seed oils are the major driver of obesity, cardiovascular disease, and diabetes
in the US, tracing the trend to Crisco's 1910 introduction and noting that red meat and animal fat
consumption actually declined over the same period chronic disease rates rose — arguing this
inverse correlation undermines blaming red meat, while the seed oil correlation is "too much to
ignore."** `[00:00]` "How can these things, which are inversely correlated with chronic disease, be
the problem?"

## The historical trend argument

`[00:00]` — Before 1900, **99.99% of dietary fat** is stated to have come from animal sources
(tallow, butter), with chronic disease rates "a fraction of what they are today." **Crisco's 1910
introduction** is marked as the start of steadily rising seed oil consumption, paralleled by rising
obesity, cardiovascular disease, and diabetes rates. Over the same century, **red meat and animal
fat consumption decreased** — used as the host's central rebuttal to the idea that red meat drives
chronic disease, since the trend runs in the opposite direction. He adds that "interventional
studies clearly show" seed oils are dangerous, without naming specific studies in this clip.

## Source-stated confidence

Low to moderate. This is explicitly a correlational historical argument (the host doesn't claim
otherwise), built on trend direction rather than a controlled study — the same style of argument
made more self-aware and explicitly hedged ("correlation is not causation") in a related video (see
Conflicts). No specific data source is named for the "99.99%" figure or the consumption trend
lines.

## Conflicts with other sources

"too much to ignore" without the same hedge. `c1kyubykrvA` ("This Is How Canada Convinced You To Eat

its industrial-lubricant origins rather than the Crisco/1910 timeline used here.
