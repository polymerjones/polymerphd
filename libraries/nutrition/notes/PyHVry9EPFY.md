# Vegetables are harming you!

## Central claim

**Rejecting the blanket claim that "all vegetables are good for you," the host names five specific
vegetables he argues are harmful, each tied to a specific defense compound and health effect:
spinach (oxalates), beans (lectins), kale (isothiocyanates), beets (oxalates and a non-protein
amino acid called Aze), and celery (a compound the transcript renders "sorolens").** `[00:00]` "You've been told that all vegetables
are good for you. That is bullshit. I'm going to show you five vegetables that I strongly believe
are hurting you."

## The five vegetables, compound by compound

`[00:00]`:

| Vegetable | Named compound | Claimed harm |
|---|---|---|
| **Spinach** | Oxalates (dicarboxylic acids) | Kidney stones, joint pain, kidney failure (linked to green smoothie overconsumption) |
| **Beans** | Lectins | Gut microbiome disruption, autoimmune disease, and (in animal models) neurologic disease like Alzheimer's |
| **Kale** | Isothiocyanates | Thyroid disruption via inhibited iodine absorption |
| **Beets** | Oxalates + Aze (a non-protein amino acid) | Possible link to autoimmune disease (multiple sclerosis), protein misfolding, possible neurologic disease |
| **Celery** | "Sorolens" (transcript spelling — see Source-stated confidence) | Accumulate in skin, cause photodamage |

## Symptoms and body signals addressed

- **Kidney stones, joint pain, kidney failure** — spinach/beets, via oxalates `[00:00]`.
- **Autoimmune and neurologic disease risk** — beans (lectins) and beets (Aze) `[00:00]`.
- **Thyroid disruption** — kale, via isothiocyanates blocking iodine `[00:00]`.
- **Skin photodamage** — celery, via the compound named "sorolens" in this transcript `[00:00]`.

## Glossary terms introduced

- **Oxalates** — dicarboxylic acids implicated in kidney stone formation `[00:00]`.
- **Aze** — a non-protein amino acid found in beets, linked here to autoimmune disease and protein
  misfolding `[00:00]`.
- **"Sorolens"** — the transcript's spelling for the celery compound said to accumulate in skin and
  cause photodamage; likely a transcription artifact for "psoralens"/furanocoumarins, per
  `bCSjz8TVhTY`'s correctly-spelled coverage of the same claim `[00:00]`.

## Analogies worth reusing

- **"Popeye was full of shit"** — used to reject spinach's strength-food reputation `[00:00]`.

## Source-stated confidence

Low-to-moderate, compound by compound — oxalates causing kidney stones is well documented in
mainstream medicine; the Aze/multiple sclerosis and beans/Alzheimer's links are explicitly scoped
to "animal models" and "may be connected," a hedge the host states directly rather than presenting
as settled human findings; no specific studies are named for any of the five claims.

## Conflicts with other sources

`aDFcCX7v5Yc` ("The problem with vegetables") and its apparent duplicate upload `5hMfRhEGeVw`
(same title, same upload date, same content) both independently develop the spinach/oxalate claim
made here in much greater depth, including documented kidney-failure case studies from smoothie
cleanses. `TKeyH_8s7mA` ("Are beans BS??"), from two days earlier on this channel, gives the fuller
mechanism behind this clip's brief beans/lectins claim, naming phytohemagglutinin specifically.
`5SowFgUc4BI` ("Is broccoli actually good for you??") makes the same isothiocyanate/thyroid/iodine
claim for broccoli that this clip makes for kale — both being cruciferous vegetables, consistent
convergence on the same mechanism across two specific plants. `bCSjz8TVhTY` ("Celery = sunburn??")
independently develops this clip's celery/psoralen claim in much more detail, naming the compound
correctly as furanocoumarins and citing a specific documented case study — that clip's naming
resolves this clip's likely transcription error ("sorolens" for psoralens/furanocoumarins).
