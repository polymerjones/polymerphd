# Is olive oil safe for cooking?

## Central claim

**The host argues the popular claim (attributed to Stephen Gundry) that olive oil oxidizes badly
when cooked is a myth — calling olive oil "the least oxidizable oil," better than avocado or coconut
oil — but separately recommends against cooking with it anyway, citing studies showing it performs
worse than lard or coconut oil under actual cooking-stability comparisons, and favoring butter,
tallow, ghee, or lard from pastured pigs.** `[00:00]` "Don't cook in olive oil, guys. There's clear
evidence for this if you actually look at the studies."

## The two claims, held together

`[00:00]` — **Claim 1 (rebuttal)**: olive oil is "the least oxidizable oil," more resistant to
oxidation than avocado oil or coconut oil, making Gundry's "olive oil oxidizes" framing wrong on its
own terms. **Claim 2 (recommendation)**: despite that, a cited study comparing olive oil, sunflower
oil (a seed oil, called "the worst"), and lard shows olive oil performing worse than both lard and
coconut oil under cooking conditions — leading to the recommendation to cook with tallow, butter,
ghee, coconut oil, or lard from a pig not fed corn and soy, and reserve olive oil for non-cooking
use.
