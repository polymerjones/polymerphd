# The oldest woman's secret to longevity

## Central claim

**Citing Emma Morano (who lived to 117 on a daily diet of two raw eggs and raw steak), the absence
of documented vegetarians among Okinawan centenarians and super-centenarians, and Hong Kong's high
average meat consumption (1.5 lbs/day) alongside a life expectancy over 86 years, the host argues
the notion that animal foods shorten lifespan is false, while explicitly acknowledging correlation
isn't causation.** `[00:00]` "The notion that animal foods shorten our lives is just false."

## The three examples

`[00:00]` — **Emma Morano**: lived to 117, eating very few vegetables or fruit, with a consistent
daily diet of two raw eggs and 3.5oz of raw steak. **Okinawa, Japan**: the host states that among
documented centenarians and super-centenarians there, "not a single person... has been a
vegetarian." **Hong Kong**: cited as averaging 1.5 lbs of meat per day per person, with a life
expectancy over 86 years — offered as a counterexample to low-meat-consumption longevity
narratives. The host explicitly flags **"correlation is not causation"** before presenting this
evidence, treating it as suggestive rather than proof.

## Glossary terms introduced

- **Super-centenarian** — a person who has lived past 110 years old, distinguished from a
  centenarian (past 100) `[00:00]`.

## Source-stated confidence

Low to moderate. The host's explicit "correlation is not causation" caveat is a notable hedge for
this library. Emma Morano's diet is a single, specific, well-documented case (she was a real,
widely reported supercentenarian). The Okinawan and Hong Kong claims are stated as general facts
without a named data source in this clip.
