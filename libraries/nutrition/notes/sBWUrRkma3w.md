# This is how much fruit I eat

## Central claim

**Fruit intake on an animal-based diet is framed as activity-dependent rather than fixed: given
roughly 3 hours of daily surfing plus a 45-minute weightlifting session most days, the host eats a
"moderate" amount of fruit, totaling roughly 150-200g of carbohydrate per day.** `[00:00]` "This
will depend on how active you are... I probably am getting 150 to 200 grams of carbohydrates from
fruit per day... So don't fear fruit."

## The stated amount

`[00:00]` — 3-4 bananas, half a papaya, "a little bit" of watermelon, and half a pineapple, on top
of the rest of the "animal-based food pyramid" (organs, meat, honey, raw dairy).

- **"Animal-based food pyramid"** — organs, meat, fruit, honey, raw dairy, used as a recurring

## Source-stated confidence

Low — self-reported personal intake tied to the host's own activity level, offered as an example
rather than a general prescription; no source is cited for the 150-200g figure itself, which is
presented as the host's own estimate ("I probably am getting").

## Conflicts with other sources

`3KZ6hTSvMW4` ("I had to quit carnivore…") independently describes a similar animal-based diet
including "carbohydrates (fruit, honey, dairy)" reintroduced after a period of strict carnivore
eating, consistent with fruit being a deliberate, non-trivial component of this diet rather than an
afterthought. `BQhI1Q0aEi8` ("USDA carb recommendations vs mine") contrasts this same
fruit-honey-dairy carbohydrate sourcing against USDA grain recommendations, citing phytic acid and
gluten as reasons to prefer fruit over grains as a carbohydrate source — a fuller argument for why
fruit is the preferred carb source this clip quantifies.
