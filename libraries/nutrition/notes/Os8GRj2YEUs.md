# How To Cook Organ Meat With Anya

## Central claim

**Organ meats are approachable if you start with liver, source them clean and fresh, and cook to a
medium-rare doneness rather than to a pathogen-killing standard** — the guest's framing is explicit:
**"my goal is like, [buy] meat that's pathogen free and cook it till it's yummy — don't buy [meat]
that might be dirty and cook it till everything might be dead, which is what the USDA wants you to
do"** `[01:01]`.

## Liver: the starting point

`[00:00]`–`[02:02]` — Chicken liver is recommended as the entry organ: slice the two lobes, remove
connective tissue, and stir-fry in ghee or suet brought to the smoke point with pink salt, served
with a squeeze of lemon or a dollop of crème fraîche or sour cream, cooked to medium-rare (pink in
the middle). Beef or lamb liver gets the same treatment in thin, pan-fried slices. A freshness check
is given for any liver: **it "should smell like an organ... a little dark and clammy," and "any
smells that are off or [acrid] or eggy" mean staying away** `[02:02]`.

## Heart

`[03:03]`–`[04:04]` — The guest's favorite preparation is anticuchos: a marinade of cumin, a mild
chili pepper, oil, garlic, and oregano, kept in the fridge "a couple days," then pan-fried in chunks
for a spice crust (a full recipe is pointed to on the guest's Instagram/IGTV). Without spices, heart
can simply be cut very thin — described as forgiving to slice "any which way" because of its many
muscle directions, unlike an orange that has to be cut along a grain. A post-cook marinade
(chimichurri or similar) after a medium-rare sear is also recommended; chicken hearts specifically
are stir-fried and should not be overcooked.

## Sweetbreads

`[04:04]`–`[05:06]` — Identified as the thymus gland (the guest notes some traditions include other
glands in what's called "sweetbreads" as well). Preparation: cook low and slow over a wood fire down
to embers, about three hours, with no poaching or other pre-treatment — just dry-salted, trimmed of
blood spots, grilled, salted again, sliced thin, and dressed heavily in lemon juice or sherry vinegar
to offset a "slightly eggy" flavor note. Described as **"very nutritionally dense"** and something
"you can actually just feel in your body when you eat it."

## Spleen and kidney

`[06:07]` — The guest has no specific spleen recipes and doesn't cook kidney often, but describes a
default approach for "any kind of unknown organ": trim connective tissue (or render it into broth for
collagen), sear in ghee with heavy salt to a medium doneness, slice thin, and toss with an acid. She
also mentions grinding organs into ground beef to make patties or meatloaf as another option.

- **Sweetbreads** — clarified here as the thymus gland specifically, though the guest notes some
  traditional preparations combine it with other glands `[04:04]`.
- **Anticuchos** — the guest's term for a cumin-chili-garlic-oregano marinated and pan-fried heart
  preparation `[03:03]`.

## Analogies worth reusing

- **Heart as an "avocado," not an "orange"** — used to explain why heart can be sliced in any
  direction without worrying about grain, unlike an orange, which has to be cut along its own
  internal structure `[03:03]`.
