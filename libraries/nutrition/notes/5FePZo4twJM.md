# How much protein for longevity?

## Central claim

**Rebutting a claim (attributed to Dan Buettner) that low-protein, plant-based diets extend
longevity, the host traces it to a 2014 observational study from Walter Longo's lab using
NHANES dietary-recall data, argues the study's own finding reverses illogically after age 65,
notes independent reanalyses haven't replicated it, and states 60g of protein for a 200-lb person
from plant sources alone (tofu, rice, beans) is inadequate — recommending animal foods for their
bioavailability and unique longevity-associated nutrients instead.** `[00:00]` "New research is
showing very clearly... low protein diets [help you live longer]. Baloney."

## The study and its problems

`[00:00]` — The cited claim: **~3g protein per 10 lbs of bodyweight** (60g/day for a 200-lb
person), from plant sources. Traced to a **2014 study from Walter Longo's lab**, described as
observational, using **NHANES data** ("Enhan's data" in the transcript) collected via **dietary
recall**, which the host calls "notoriously inaccurate." The study's own finding: higher protein
intake was associated with longer life in young and middle-aged people, but this pattern
**reversed after age 65** — the host argues this reversal "makes no sense" biologically. He adds
that **independent reanalyses of the same NHANES data using different statistical methods have not
reproduced Longo's finding**, and attributes Dan Buettner's continued promotion of the study to his
prior commitment to veganism and the Blue Zones narrative.

## The Blue Zones critique, and the protein recommendation

`[01:01]` — The host states his own general protein recommendation is **0.6 to 0.8 grams per pound
of bodyweight** "for most people" — lower than a 1g/lb figure he's stated elsewhere (see
Conflicts) — but argues that even 60g/day is unrealistic to obtain adequately from plant sources
alone at 200 lbs: **"tofu and rice and beans are not going to cut it."** He closes by naming Dan
Buettner as "the guy that created the blue zones," stating the Blue Zones framework "is not
supported by science when you actually look into them, and they have been repeatedly debunked" —
without detailing the specific regions or evidence in this clip.
