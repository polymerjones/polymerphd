# Vegan diets and nutrient deficiencies: don’t make this mistake

## Central claim

**The host validates vegans' ethical intentions (reducing animal harm) while arguing their approach
is health-mistaken, because meat provides unique nutrients — creatine, carnosine, carnitine,
anserine, vitamin K2, B12 — that are difficult or impossible to obtain from plants, and because
mineral bioavailability (magnesium, zinc, iron) is generally poorer from plant sources.** `[00:00]`
"Where does a vegan get creatine? Carnosine, carnitine, anserine, taurine, vitamin K2, B12. The list
is so long."

## The nutrient gap argument

`[00:00]` — Named nutrients difficult to obtain on a vegan diet: **creatine, carnosine, carnitine,
anserine, taurine, vitamin K2, and B12**. Separately, mineral **bioavailability** (magnesium, zinc,
iron) is described as lower from plant sources than from meat. The host frames meat prioritization
as an evolutionary default that has been "programmed out of us through vegan documentaries" and
narratives he considers false about meat's healthfulness or environmental impact.

## Source-stated confidence

Moderate. The claim that creatine, carnosine, carnitine, and anserine are found predominantly or
exclusively in animal tissue is well-established in nutritional biochemistry; the claim that vegan
documentaries have specifically "programmed" meat-avoidance is the host's own interpretive framing
rather than a cited claim.
