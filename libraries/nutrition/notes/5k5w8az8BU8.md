# Reacting to Bryan Johnson 100 PILLS supplement protocol

## Central claim

**Reacting to Bryan Johnson's roughly 100-pills-a-day supplement protocol, the host argues it
would be simpler to get many of the same nutrients — taurine, vitamin B12, creatine — by eating red
meat and liver, since these compounds occur predominantly or exclusively in red meat and organs and
are associated with longevity, yet Johnson's diet excludes red meat.** `[00:00]` "Wouldn't it just
be easier to eat things like red meat and liver to get all of these nutrients that you're missing
that are requiring you to take sometimes more than 100 pills a day?"

## The specific critique

`[00:00]` — Johnson's routine is described (secondhand, from his own public content): the "green
giant" drink (collagen peptides, cinnamon, creatine, amino acids) with ~60 pills in the morning,
followed by workout, the "super veggie" meal, then "nutty pudding" and ~40 more pills. The host
notes Johnson takes **taurine** as one of his supplements specifically, and cites evidence that
taurine "extend[s] lifespan across a variety of species" — available naturally in red meat, which
Johnson's diet excludes. Vitamin B12 and creatine are named as two more red-meat nutrients with a
"pro-longevity effect." The host frames Johnson as "smart" and his supplementation as sincere, not
disingenuous, while questioning the underlying dietary choice to exclude the food source of these
nutrients.
