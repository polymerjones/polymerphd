# The Untold Side Effects of Nicotine

## Central claim

**Asked to rank cigars, vapes, marijuana, cigarettes, and nicotine pouches by risk, the source
ranks cigars least risky and cigarettes worst, but explicitly refuses to call any of them "net
positive" — including pouches, despite nicotine's natural occurrence in the body.** **"Nicotine
occurs naturally in the human body... but they're quite addictive... to say that nicotine
supplementation or these pouches is net positive, that's a stretch"** `[01:00]`–`[03:02]`.

## Ranking the five

`[00:00]`–`[01:00]` — In the source's own stated order and reasoning:

| Product | Stated concern |
|---|---|
| Cigars | Least risky *if* "a high quality tobacco leaf without a lot of pesticides" — explicitly **not an endorsement**: "I don't smoke. I don't even smoke cigars" |
| Nicotine pouches | "Quite addictive," called "the next epidemic" of people "messing with their... neurotransmitters and these signaling molecules... in a not great way" |
| Vape | Oils "linked to... certain types of pneumonia in the lungs"; "vaping is gross and people shouldn't be doing it" |
| Marijuana | "Linked to all sorts of hormonal issues," specifically "lowers androgens in men"; called "a very clearly a net negative thing" for most people |
| Cigarettes | "Obviously horrible because the tobacco is full of pesticides and other additives"; named directly as **"the worst"** of the five |

## The COVID anecdote and what it does (and doesn't) show

`[02:02]`–`[03:02]` — A named anecdote: a smoker ("John") argued nicotine is "good for you" because
"people that smoke cigarettes actually didn't get the co virus as much as people who didn't
smoke." Pressed on whether this means smoking is good, John narrows the claim to nicotine
specifically. The source's response keeps the two separate: **"nicotine occurs naturally in the
human body. It is a neurotransmitter... there may be some studies that suggest that it affects the
brain in a positive way, but there's always other side effects."** The response draws an explicit
analogy to cannabinoids — also endogenous, also carrying "downstream effects" when supplemented in
excess — and concludes that calling nicotine supplementation "net positive... is a stretch," citing
mild alertness benefit and unspecified "dementia" data as the only things weighed in its favor
against raised blood pressure, raised heart rate, and addiction. Final line: **"let's be honest,
they're used as a drug... these are drugs."**

## Symptoms and body signals addressed

- **Vaping**: oils reportedly linked to "certain types of pneumonia in the lungs" `[00:00]`.
- **Marijuana**: "lowers androgens in men" `[00:00]`.
- **Nicotine (general)**: raised blood pressure, raised heart rate `[03:02]`.

## Analogies worth reusing

- **Nicotine and cannabinoids as parallel cases** — both occur naturally in the body, and both
  produce "downstream effects" when supplemented in excess beyond what the body makes on its own
  `[03:02]`.

## Source-stated confidence

Consistently hedged rather than absolute: "I think," "I'm not saying cigars are great," "there may
be some studies," "yes, it might make you more alert," "that's a stretch." The one claim delivered
without a hedge is the closing line that nicotine pouches "are absolutely used as a drug."

nicotine "net positive," both name raised blood pressure and heart rate as costs, both allow a

video additionally ranks **cigars as least risky** among five products — a claim `kGqGeYdPxMk`
never addresses, since cigars do not come up there at all, so the two are not in conflict on that
