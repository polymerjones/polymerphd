# Pasteurized milk increases the risk of salmonella?

## Central claim

**Directly rebutting a tweet from Matt Walsh calling raw milk drinkers "morons," the host cites a
1985 salmonella outbreak from pasteurized milk (sickening an estimated 16,000-160,000 people, at
least 10 deaths) and raw milk's associations with lower childhood asthma, eczema, and allergy rates,
arguing raw milk can be produced safely today and offers unique benefits pasteurized milk lacks —
publicly inviting Walsh to Costa Rica to drink raw milk with him.** `[00:00]` "I don't think people
that drink raw milk are morons."

## The cited outbreak and the counter-benefits

`[00:00]` — **The outbreak**: 1985, pasteurized milk, an estimated **16,000 to 160,000 people
sickened**, **at least 10 deaths** — offered specifically to counter the framing that raw milk is
uniquely dangerous. **Raw milk's cited benefits**: consistently lower rates of asthma, eczema, and
allergy in kids; improved respiratory illness outcomes in kids; and beneficial enzymes/compounds
within raw milk itself that limit harmful bacterial growth.

## Symptoms and body signals addressed

- **Asthma, eczema, and allergy in kids** — associated with raw (not pasteurized) milk consumption
  `[00:00]`.
- **Respiratory illness in kids** — described as improved by raw milk consumption `[00:00]`.

## Source-stated confidence

Moderate for the outbreak citation, which gives a specific year and a wide but bounded casualty
range — specific enough to be checkable, though the range itself (16,000-160,000) is unusually
wide, suggesting genuine uncertainty in the underlying source rather than false precision. The
asthma/eczema/allergy and respiratory-illness claims are stated as "consistently associated"
without naming the specific studies here.

## Conflicts with other sources

`Ya-P2K5k6PY` ("What's WRONG With Your Milk") cites the **same outbreak but dates it to 1986**
rather than 1985 — a minor but genuine date discrepancy between two sources in this library
describing what appears to be the same historical event. That note also frames raw-versus-
pasteurized as "a contamination trade-off, not a clean win," a more hedged position than this
short's more confident framing. `2qNty7ZhbhI` ("The healing benefits of raw milk") and
`1lNnLhQwm0w` ("The secret to strong kids?") both independently corroborate the asthma/eczema/
allergy association with early dairy or microbial exposure.
