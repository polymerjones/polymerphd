# Don't Make These Mistakes When Buying Butter

## Central claim

**Butter should be chosen in order of raw first, then grass-fed and organic, and the source argues
it is a health food that will not cause weight gain no matter how much of it is eaten, because
whole-food fats "feed back into your satiety mechanisms."** `[00:00]` "If you fear butter, you
should not fear butter — butter is a health food" `[02:02]`.

## The buying hierarchy

`[00:00]`–`[01:01]` — Raw butter is the first choice because pasteurization "changes the
conformational structure of the proteins," and **"kids who grow up drinking unpasteurized...
milk have lower rates of asthma, eczema, and allergies,"** attributed to unnamed "multiple studies"
`[00:00]`. If raw isn't available, the fallback is grass-fed and organic — acknowledged as hard to
find combined in one product — with pasture-raised organic butter and Irish grass-fed butter given
as practical options `[00:00]`–`[01:01]`. Salted versus unsalted is called a non-issue `[01:01]`.

## Not olive oil, not avocado oil

`[01:01]` — For cooking, the source avoids seed oils, but also olive oil and avocado oil, "because
those oils are also going to oxidize... degrade when you heat them." A specific claim: **"the
majority of olive oils and the majority of avocado oils are also cut with seed oil"** — advice
given elsewhere is to use them unheated and to verify quality if used at all. Preferred cooking
fats, in order given: tallow, butter, and ghee, with ghee's higher smoke point attributed to milk
proteins having been removed.

## A brand scandal, and the plant-based imitation

`[02:02]`–`[03:05]` — Kerrygold (transcribed "KY gold") is named directly: **"recently had a
scandal where they weren't really fully grass-fed, and there were PFAS forever chemicals found in
the packaging."** No further detail (date, source, or testing body) is given in speech. A separate
warning targets plant-based butter substitutes shelved alongside real butter, calling them
"essentially new age margarine," missing vitamin K2 and CLA; one label read on camera lists a
"plant-based oil blend from canola, coconut, and sunflower oils" (two of three being seed oils),
and a second, "Earth Balance," is read as containing four seed oils — palm, canola, soybean, and
flaxseed — called "all garbage."

## Butter's nutrients, and the weight-gain claim

`[03:05]`–`[05:07]` — Butter is credited with stearic acid and odd-chain fatty acids (C15, C17),
said to be "consistently associated with good outcomes in humans," plus CLA, tied to unnamed
"multiple observational trials associating dairy fat with leanness." **The source's stated
"controversial perspective": "you can eat as much butter as you want... I challenge any of you guys
or girls to get fat eating butter,"** reasoned through satiety rather than calorie counting —
explicitly flagged as a position that will make "the calories in, calories out crowd... explode
their minds" `[04:05]`. This is extended to other whole foods (meat, cheese, eggs, oranges) with a
caveat carved out for people who are "diabetic... obese... metabolically unwell," for whom sugar
handling is said to differ, though the source stops short of recommending carbohydrate elimination
even there `[05:07]`.

## Symptoms and body signals addressed

Asthma, eczema, and allergies are named as lower in children who grow up drinking unpasteurized,
unheated milk, attributed to unnamed studies rather than a mechanism `[00:00]`.

## Glossary terms introduced

- **Odd-chain fatty acids (C15, C17)** — named as butter constituents "consistently associated with
  good outcomes in humans," without further detail on the outcomes measured `[02:02]`.
- **CLA (conjugated linoleic acid)** — a compound in butter the source associates with "leanness
  and improved weight loss" `[02:02]`.

## Analogies worth reusing

None distinct — the video argues through a buying checklist and a personal challenge to the viewer
("I challenge any of you... to get fat eating butter") rather than a stated analogy.

## Source-stated confidence

Largely unsourced in speech: the raw-milk/allergy claim, the dairy-fat/leanness trials, and the
Kerrygold PFAS finding are each attributed to unnamed "studies" or a "scandal" with no date, lab, or
publication given. **The weight-gain claim is the most explicitly self-labeled as opinion — "this
is my controversial perspective," "I know a lot of people debate me on that"** `[04:05]` — a rare
case in this library of a confident claim also being flagged by its own source as contested.

## Conflicts with other sources

Converges with `JpW1utNfQM0`, `d4mYLeFxONQ`, and `mV58zXMBco4` on two points also made
independently in this video: that much retail olive and avocado oil is "cut with seed oil," and
that saturated fats (tallow, butter, ghee) are more heat-stable and therefore better cooking
choices than olive, avocado, or seed oil. This video adds the Kerrygold PFAS/grass-fed claim, a
specific brand-contamination finding none of those three olive-oil-focused videos raise, since none
of them discuss butter. It also pairs directly with `2KMaKJqU5Gs`'s caution that raw dairy is only
as good as the animal's diet — this video's raw-butter preference doesn't address whether the
source animals are grass-fed, the exact gap `2KMaKJqU5Gs` flags when it warns that raw milk "from a
cow that's fed grains... is still a problem."
