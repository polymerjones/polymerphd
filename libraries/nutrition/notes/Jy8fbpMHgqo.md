# Why PEANUT BUTTER is one of the worst foods with Dave Asprey

## Central claim

**Peanut butter has "no argument" in its favor, according to Dave Asprey: it is "the number one
source of aflatoxin, the most cancer-causing molecule we know of," carries problematic lectins and
phytic acid, and contains very long-chain fatty acids (VLCFA) that are "overrepresented in the
brains of people with Alzheimer's disease."** `[30:28]` His summary line: **"there's not an argument
for peanut butter... why is it good for you? I don't know. I don't know."** `[30:28]`–`[31:28]`

## The specific case against peanut butter

`[30:28]`–`[32:29]` — Beyond aflatoxin, Asprey names **VLCFA** as the item "a lot of people don't
know about": saturated fatty acids so long that they don't fit properly into a cell membrane and go
in "sideways." He also dismisses the "high protein" defense: **"the protein in there isn't very good
quality protein,"** comparing it to gluten as an example of a technically-present but
low-value plant protein. He shares a personal anecdote of eating "the biggest, thickest peanut
butter and jelly sandwich" as a kid and feeling unwell afterward, and recommends almond butter as a
lesser evil — "still high in lectins... still high in phytic acid... also very high in oxalates."

## Plant defense chemicals and personalized tolerance

`[06:05]`–`[19:20]` — Asprey frames lectins, phytic acid, oxalates, and histamines as the main
categories of "plant compounds that cause troubles," stating "different people have different
kryptonite vegetables." He personally avoids oxalate-heavy foods (raspberries, blackberries,
spinach, kale) after developing what he calls "oxalate toxicity" from a period as "a dumbass raw
vegan." His central metaphor for phytic acid is anti-nutrient framing taken to its extreme: **"when
you eat plants, they basically have condoms wrapped around their minerals. You will not get those
minerals."** He argues the effects of phytic acid are harder to notice than lectins or histamines
because the damage (poor bone density) surfaces only much later: "you get the feedback when you
break your hip." He also criticizes the **aggregate nutrient density index (ANDI)** as a "farce"
because it ignores food volume, weight, and bioavailability, calling out its use to justify pricey,
low-calorie leafy greens `[36:34]`–`[37:34]`.

## Peanuts as beans, with a caveat

`[35:33]`–`[36:34]` — Both speakers frame peanuts as legumes sharing the same defense-chemical
profile as beans generally, and jointly conclude grains, nuts, beans, and legumes are "best left out
of people's diets" where possible. Asprey adds an explicit exception: **"if the people actually have
food insecurity, these belong in their diets before ramen"** — preferring peanut butter or wheat
over "fat-free ramen and canola oil" for someone with genuine financial constraints.

## Fructose, honey, and fruit — a real-time disagreement

`[42:39]`–`[54:51]` — Asprey states he keeps fructose under **"25 grams of fructose per day"**
because fructose, even from fruit, "increases fatty liver" and advanced glycation end products; he
allows more (up to 50g) for someone getting heavy sun exposure and exercise. Saladino pushes back
directly, saying he hasn't seen human evidence that fruit causes non-alcoholic fatty liver disease
and critiquing the methodology of the one Iranian study Asprey references, since it lumped "fruit,
dried fruit, and... fruit products" together. Saladino cites his own hemoglobin A1c (5.1–5.2) and a
mouse study confirming A1c reflects fructose glycation; Asprey's own A1c is "5.2." Both independently
reference the **same diabetic honey study** — an 8-week trial giving diabetics 125–150 grams of
honey per day, showing improved insulin sensitivity — as a data point they find striking `[47:44]`.

## Seed oils: a measurement critique

`[55:52]`–`[60:56]` — Saladino argues that a widely-shared 2019 meta-analysis defending seed oils
relied mostly on blood linoleic acid levels, a poor proxy for consumption, and that of the 30
cohort studies included, **only three measured adipose tissue linoleic acid** — the better proxy —
and those three showed no benefit or a worse cardiovascular signal. Asprey adds that seed oil
consumption shows up first in white adipose tissue ("love handles") and describes the brain as
roughly 45% saturated fat by necessity, with the remainder split among mono- and polyunsaturated
fats; he singles out black cumin seed oil as a beneficial exception for mast-cell stabilization.

## Symptoms and body signals addressed

- **Poor bone density / fracture risk**, attributed by Asprey to chronic phytic acid intake, framed
  around his own recent foot surgery where a surgeon reportedly struggled to saw through his bone
  `[12:13]`–`[13:14]`.
- **Oxalate toxicity** (frequent urination as an early sign) from Asprey's own raw-vegan period
  `[10:12]`.
- **Leaky gut and disrupted sleep** reported by Asprey after roughly three months of an all-animal
  diet, offered as a caution against zero-carbohydrate eating long-term `[05:04]`.
- **Migraines**, discussed as one of the most food-responsive conditions in medicine, addressed with
  caffeine, aspirin, and glucose rather than fructose `[51:47]`–`[52:49]`.

## Glossary terms introduced

- **VLCFA (very long-chain fatty acids)** — saturated fats long enough that they don't fit properly
  into a cell membrane, described as overrepresented in Alzheimer's-affected brain tissue `[30:28]`.
- **Aggregate nutrient density index (ANDI)** — a nutrient-per-calorie ranking metric Asprey
  criticizes for ignoring food volume, water content, and bioavailability `[36:34]`.

## Analogies worth reusing

- **"They basically have condoms wrapped around their minerals"** — Asprey's extended metaphor for
  phytic acid blocking mineral absorption, including the image of "swallow[ing] two pounds of
  heroin" that "magically wasn't bioavailable" `[17:17]`–`[18:19]`.
- **"Saran Wrap gas is a plant-based protein"** — Asprey's sarcastic aside undercutting the "peanut
  butter is high protein" defense `[31:28]`.

## Source-stated confidence

Asprey states the peanut butter case with full confidence and no on-screen citation for the VLCFA/
Alzheimer's claim beyond "they're overrepresented in the brains of people with Alzheimer's disease"
`[30:28]`. By contrast, the fructose/fruit disagreement is handled as an open, hedged question by
both speakers: Saladino says **"I'm not aware of any studies in humans that... show that fruit
contributes to NAFLD"** while inviting Asprey to "send me the studies offline" `[44:40]`, and Asprey
responds "there probably is" a level at which fructose becomes harmful without naming one
`[49:45]`. Both explicitly flag the same honey study's findings as "striking" rather than
conclusive `[47:44]`.
