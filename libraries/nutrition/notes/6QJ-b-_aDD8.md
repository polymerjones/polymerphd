# Why are you using sunscreen?

## Central claim

**Citing a 2018 meta-analysis of 29 studies covering over 300,000 people finding no proof
traditional sunscreens protect against skin cancer, the host questions why anyone would apply
sunscreen chemicals linked to hormonal disruption, points out that melanoma rates are rising even
as sunscreen use has increased and time spent outdoors has fallen below 10%, and argues diet
quality is more linked to skin cancer risk than sun exposure itself — while still endorsing a
tallow-based, non-nano zinc mineral sunscreen for photoprotection and skin aging specifically.**
`[00:00]` "By all means don't fear the sun. The sun is good for you."

## The cited evidence

`[00:00]` — **2018 meta-analysis**: 29 studies, over 300,000 people, found no proof traditional
sunscreen protects against skin cancer — a specific, checkable citation. **Chemical concerns**:
avobenzone, oxybenzone, octocrylene, and homosalate named as linked to hormonal disruption,
absorbed through skin and excreted in urine/feces. **The paradox**: melanoma rates are rising even
as people spend "90 plus percent" of their lives indoors and use sunscreen more than ever,
suggesting sun exposure alone doesn't explain the trend. **The nuanced recommendation**: chronic
low-level sun exposure is described as protective against melanoma; a mineral sunscreen (tallow-
based, non-nano zinc oxide) is still endorsed for photoprotection, skin aging, and wrinkles
specifically — a different goal than cancer prevention. Practical advice: build sun exposure
gradually over the summer, use a hat, and don't avoid the sun out of fear.

## Symptoms and body signals addressed

- **Melanoma** — its rising rate despite falling sun exposure and rising sunscreen use is used as
  evidence against a simple sun-exposure-causes-cancer model `[00:00]`.
