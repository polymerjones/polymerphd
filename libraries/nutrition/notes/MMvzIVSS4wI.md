# Stop eating soy and beans (Genistein + Glyphosate)

## Central claim

**"The EPA says that glyphosate is not an endocrine disruptor," but a 2013 study found it
"promoted the growth of T47D cells via estrogen receptors" and had "an additive effect with
genistein" on that same pathway** `[00:00]`. Genistein is the source's name for the polyphenol
found in soy and beans `[01:00]`, so the practical takeaway argued here is to avoid both.

## The 2013 breast-cancer cell-line study

`[00:00]` — At concentrations of "10 to the minus 12 or 10 to the minus 6 molar," glyphosate is
reported to have promoted T47D cell growth via estrogen receptors, producing "alteration of
estrogen receptors which can be blocked." **The mechanism given: glyphosate altered the receptors
"by increasing the expression ratio of estrogen receptor alpha and estrogen receptor beta."**
Combined with genistein, the effect is described as extending to "estrogen response element
transcription" `[00:00]` and subsequent "activation and cell growth" `[01:00]`.

## Genistein in soy and beans

`[01:00]`–`[02:03]` — Genistein is called "a polyphenol found in soy and beans," and elsewhere "an
isoflavonoid found in a plethora of plant-based foods." A cited meta-analysis of preclinical
studies ("from the 27th of june 2022") reported genistein "has detrimental effects on male
reproductive system and on the progression and sustenance of pregnancy," with "more pronounced
adverse impact in males particularly when exposed in utero." Its largest food sources are named as
"all beans black beans white beans etc and soybeans," with smaller amounts in peanuts and beer.
**The source's stated position: there isn't "sufficient literature to suggest that we get any
benefits from these polyphenols like genistein... that you can't get other places with less
detrimental side effects"** `[02:03]`.

## A supporting epidemiology study on lymphoma

`[03:03]`–`[04:05]` — A cited systematic review/meta-analysis, "non-hodgkin lymphoma occupational
exposure to agricultural pesticide chemical groups," reported "b-cell lymphoma was positively
associated with phenoxy herbicides and the organophosphorous herbicide glyphosate" — with the
explicit hedge that "not all studies have showed this but some epidemiology does show this." This
is named as one input into the IARC's decision to classify glyphosate as a "class ii carcinogen"
`[04:05]`.

## Glossary terms introduced

- **Genistein** — "a polyphenol found in soy and beans," elsewhere "an isoflavonoid found in a
  plethora of plant-based foods," used here as the compound whose fertility/reproductive effects
  compound with glyphosate's estrogenic effects `[01:00]`–`[02:03]`.
- **Estrogen receptor alpha/beta expression ratio** — the source's description of how glyphosate is
  said to alter estrogen receptors, by shifting "the expression ratio of estrogen receptor alpha
  and estrogen receptor beta" `[00:00]`.

## Analogies worth reusing

None — the video argues from study findings directly rather than through analogy.

## Source-stated confidence

Mixed. The 2013 breast-cancer cell-line finding and the genistein meta-analysis are each
attributed to a specific, dated study. The non-Hodgkin lymphoma claim is explicitly hedged: "not
all studies have showed this but some epidemiology does show this" `[03:03]`. No dosing context is
given for how the cell-line molar concentrations relate to realistic human exposure.
