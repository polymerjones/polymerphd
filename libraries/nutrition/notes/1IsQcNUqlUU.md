# Eating cheese as lactose intolerant? 🤯

## Central claim

**The host argues lactose-intolerant people don't need to switch to plant-based milks like oat or
almond milk, because aged cheeses (Parmigiano-Reggiano, Pecorino-Romano, aged Swiss) and fermented
dairy (kefir, yogurt) are naturally low enough in lactose to eat without GI symptoms, while still
providing dairy's nutrient advantages — calcium, vitamin K2, odd-chain fatty acids, bioavailable
protein.** `[00:00]` "You can eat Parmigiano-Reggiano cheese, Pecorino-Romano cheese... or an aged
Swiss cheese. Completely safely, you will not end up in the bathroom from those cheeses."

## The recommended foods, and how to start

`[00:00]` — Named safe options: **Parmigiano-Reggiano and Pecorino-Romano** (both "always raw"),
and **aged Swiss cheese**. Preference given to **raw** versions of aged cheese where possible. For
easing in: start with a small amount — "a spoonful or a small glass of kefir or yogurt" — and
assess tolerance from there.

## Symptoms and body signals addressed

- **GI distress ("ending up in the bathroom")** — the specific lactose-intolerance symptom this
  short addresses, framed as avoidable via aged/fermented dairy rather than by avoiding dairy
  entirely `[00:00]`.
