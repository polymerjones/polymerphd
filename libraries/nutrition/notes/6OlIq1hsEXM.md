# What are the best FATS to cook with?

## Central claim

**The host ranks cooking fats while grocery shopping: avoid seed oils (sunflower, grapeseed)
entirely; avoid cooking with olive or avocado oil specifically (though they're better than seed
oils and usable uncooked with careful sourcing) because of oxidation and linoleic acid content;
use virgin organic coconut oil as an acceptable plant-oil option; and prefer animal fats — tallow
or ghee — for their fat-soluble vitamins and, in butter/ghee specifically, butyric acid.** `[00:00]`
"If you're going to cook in oil, use tallow or ghee."

## The ranking

`[00:00]` — **Avoid entirely**: sunflower and grapeseed oil (seed oils). **Not for cooking**:
avocado and olive oil — "too much linoleic acid," prone to oxidizing when heated, and "very often
cut with vegetable oils" or otherwise low quality; usable uncooked but only with careful sourcing.
**Acceptable plant option**: virgin organic coconut oil, ranked third overall. **Preferred**:
animal fats (tallow, ghee), credited with fat-soluble vitamins A, D, E, K2, and — specifically in
butter and ghee — **butyric acid**, described as an anti-inflammatory compound "even used to treat
inflammatory bowel disease."

## Symptoms and body signals addressed

- **Inflammatory bowel disease** — named as a condition butyric acid (found in butter/ghee) is used
  to treat, though this is stated about butyric acid as a therapeutic compound generally, not
  specifically about eating butter/ghee as treatment `[00:00]`.

## Glossary terms introduced

- **Butyric acid** — a short-chain fatty acid found in butter and ghee, described as anti-
  inflammatory and used in IBD treatment `[00:00]`.
