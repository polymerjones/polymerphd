# Is this the biggest driver of chronic illness?

## Central claim

**Reacting to a CBS News segment, the host highlights the reported statistic that ultra-processed
foods now make up 73% of the U.S. food supply and over 60% of average diets, linked to diabetes,
obesity, cancer, and depression — and argues that simply maximizing unprocessed animal and plant
foods while avoiding ultra-processed foods is the single highest-impact health change available.**
`[00:00]` "I believe there is no single thing that you can do that will have a more positive impact
on your health than that."

## The cited figures and the mechanism

`[00:00]` — Attributed to CBS News/researchers: **ultra-processed foods make up 73% of the US food
supply**, and **over 60% of the average diet** is ultra-processed. These foods are characterized as
high in fats, starches, sugars, and additives, "full of seed oils... refined grains... emulsifiers,
dyes, and other coloring and flavor-enhancing ingredients," and are said to increase hunger — a
claim the host says "has been shown multiple times" without naming the specific research. Linked
health issues cited: **diabetes, obesity, cancer, and depression**. The recommended fix: maximize
unprocessed animal foods (meat, eggs, dairy) and unprocessed plant foods (fruit, and vegetables "if
you want").

## Symptoms and body signals addressed

- **Increased hunger** — attributed to ultra-processed food consumption specifically `[00:00]`.

## Source-stated confidence

Moderate. The 73%/60%+ figures are attributed to a named news source (CBS News) reporting on
unnamed "researchers," which is more specific sourcing than an unattributed claim, though the
underlying study isn't named. The hunger-increasing claim is stated as repeatedly shown without a
specific citation.
