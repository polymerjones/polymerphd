# This is why obesity rates are skyrocketing

## Central claim

**Comparing two US maps — one showing obesity rates, the other showing ultra-processed fast food
consumption rates — the host argues the near-identical geographic patterns are not coincidental, and
that with ultra-processed foods making up over 60% of the American diet, this is strongly related to
climbing obesity, diabetes rates, and record-low fertility.** `[00:00]` "Those charts are almost
exactly the same... is this any coincidence? I doubt it."

## The comparison

`[00:00]` — Two maps shown: US obesity rates by region, and US ultra-processed fast-food consumption
rates by region, described as closely matching geographically. Cited figure: ultra-processed foods
make up "more than 60%... of the American diet." Named trends the host connects to this: climbing
obesity rates, climbing diabetes rates, and record-low fertility. Recommendation: reducing
ultra-processed food consumption as the single most impactful health change available.

## Source-stated confidence

Low to moderate. The correlational map comparison and the 60% ultra-processed-food figure are
consistent with published US dietary and obesity-prevalence research, though the host explicitly
frames the connection as his own inference from correlation ("I doubt it [is a coincidence]") rather
than presenting a controlled causal study — an appropriately hedged, though still directionally
strong, claim.
