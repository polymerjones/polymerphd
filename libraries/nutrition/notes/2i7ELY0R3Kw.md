# Is Dementia Preventable?

## Central claim

**The host argues Alzheimer's disease and dementia are largely preventable because they're
strongly connected to insulin resistance — sometimes called "type 3 diabetes" — and since insulin
resistance is itself preventable and reversible through diet, eliminating seed oils and processed
sugars should sharply reduce dementia risk.** `[00:00]` "I believe dementia, Alzheimer's, are
completely preventable... if more people did that, I believe our epidemic of dementia would
vanish."

## The argument's chain

`[00:00]` — The reasoning: Alzheimer's is "strongly connected with insulin resistance," which the
host says is sometimes called **"type 3 diabetes."** Since standard type 2 diabetes and insulin
resistance are "preventable and reversible," and Alzheimer's is causally linked to the same
underlying metabolic dysfunction, the host argues Alzheimer's prevention should follow the same
levers: **eliminate seed oils, eliminate processed sugars**. He frames the absence of mainstream
dietary-intervention conversation around Alzheimer's as a notable gap.

## Symptoms and body signals addressed

- **Memory loss and loss of recognition of loved ones** — named as the feared outcome of dementia
  that motivates the prevention argument `[00:00]`.

## Glossary terms introduced

- **Type 3 diabetes** — used here as an alternate name for Alzheimer's disease, framing it as a
  form of insulin resistance specifically affecting the brain `[00:00]`.

## Source-stated confidence

Low to moderate. The insulin-resistance/Alzheimer's connection reflects a real, actively researched
hypothesis in the scientific literature (the "type 3 diabetes" framing is a genuine if contested
term used in some research), but no specific study is cited here, and the causal leap from
"connected with" to "completely preventable" is stated with more confidence than the underlying
research area currently supports.
