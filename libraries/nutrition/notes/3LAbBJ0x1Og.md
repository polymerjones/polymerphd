# Pasture-raised vs regular eggs

## Central claim

**Comparing two egg yolks side by side, the host argues the darker, more orange color of a
farmer's-market pasture-raised egg directly reflects higher vitamin A, beta-carotene, vitamin E,
vitamin D, and vitamin K2 content compared to a lighter grocery-store egg from a chicken fed mostly
corn and soy — using yolk color itself as the quality signal.** `[00:00]` "You can tell that the
nutrition going into this egg is much better than the nutrition going into this egg."

## The comparison

`[00:00]` — Pasture-raised egg (Costa Rica farmer's market): chickens outside eating bugs, worms,
and "very small amounts of feed" — yolk described as "much darker... much more orange." Grocery
store egg: chicken likely eating "100% of its feed as corn and soy" — yolk lighter colored. The
host frames the color difference itself as the evidence of higher vitamin A, beta-carotene, vitamin
E, vitamin D, and vitamin K2 content, and extends it as "a metaphor for what you eat also."

## Conflicts with other sources

This is a direct tension within the library, not just a missing citation: `-wKocULS_00` ("Pasture-
raised vs store-bought eggs") has the same host state explicitly, **"the best metrics that you can
use to tell if your egg is high quality is not the color of the egg, but the height of the yolk and
the thickness of the white."** `SPaqnDv-qmQ` ("Never Buy These Egg Brands in 2026") independently
corroborates that caution — "yolk color alone is not a reliable quality indicator" — noting two
eggs of different real quality can "look about the same color." This short does the opposite,
using color itself as the direct evidence of nutrient quality, with neither yolk height nor white
thickness mentioned. Worth flagging when citing any of these three: this host's stated position on
whether color is a reliable quality signal is inconsistent across videos.
