# Animal-based diets will eliminate cravings

## Central claim

**Three specific causes of persistent carb cravings are named, each with a mechanism and fix:
methylxanthines in coffee/chocolate causing mineral loss and resulting cravings; weekend junk food
eating driving weekday cravings; and insufficient organ meat intake undermining nutrient adequacy
and satiety — with the fix being to eliminate the first two and add more organs.** `[00:00]` "Here
are three reasons you are still having carb cravings... All of these have methylxanthines like
caffeine or theobromine which cause mineral loss. Mineral loss will lead to cravings because you
are nutrient deficient... To get rid of your carb cravings, avoid those three things and just focus
on meat and organs."

## The three named causes

`[00:00]`:
1. **Caffeine/theobromine (coffee, chocolate):** methylxanthines causing mineral loss, driving
   cravings via nutrient deficiency.
2. **Weekend junk food** (cookies, cakes, candies, pizza): stated to cause weekday cravings.
3. **Insufficient organ intake**: undermines nutrient adequacy, which affects satiety.

**Named product reference:** desiccated organs from **Heart and Soil Supplements** offered as a
fallback when fresh organs aren't available.

## Symptoms and body signals addressed

- **Carb cravings** — the central symptom, attributed to mineral loss from methylxanthines, weekend
  junk food patterns, and organ-meat nutrient inadequacy `[00:00]`.

## Glossary terms introduced

- **Methylxanthines** — used without full definition; named as the class covering caffeine and
  theobromine, said to cause mineral loss `[00:00]`.

## Source-stated confidence

Low — the methylxanthine/mineral-loss/craving chain, the weekend-junk-food/weekday-craving link,
and the organ-meat/satiety claim are all stated without a named study in this clip; caffeine does
have a mild diuretic effect that can contribute to mineral loss in the broader literature, though
the magnitude and craving-causation link stated here is not independently sourced.
