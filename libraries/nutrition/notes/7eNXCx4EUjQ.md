# The infant formula/seed oil scandal

## Central claim

**Every infant formula sold in the United States is legally required to contain seed oils — a
rule the speaker attributes to soybean-industry lobbying rather than infant-health evidence — and
the source connects this to a measurable, decades-long rise in the linoleic acid content of
American breast milk.** `[00:00]` "The soybean lobby actually successfully... made it law that
every baby formula in the United States has to have seed oils. That is a requirement" `[00:00]`.

## The lobbying history

`[00:00]`–`[01:02]` — Traced to World War II-era subsidies for "stable crops like soybeans," meant
to feed the world under the Marshall Plan; the resulting industries and lobbies are said to have
"kept this thing in place" long after the original justification. Formula ingredients are called
"highly, highly regulated," with **"only been one new formulation of any kind approved" in the
past ten years, and all approved formulations require... essentially GMO-laden seed oil"** — stated
as fact, with no named agency or approval record cited in speech `[00:00]`. This is said to have
produced a "huge black market" for imported European formula, which the US has "cracked down" on —
a crackdown the speaker frames ambiguously, hedging that it happened "probably at the... behest of
the soybean farmers" while also allowing it may be "for health reasons" `[01:02]`.

## Linoleic acid in breast milk

`[02:03]`–`[03:04]` — Linoleic acid is introduced as "an 18-carbon polyunsaturated fatty acid found
in seed oils in high amounts," present at roughly "2% of the fat" in meat versus "25 to 65%" in
seed oils. **"If you look at the linoleic acid composition of breast milk, it's gone up steadily in
the last 100 years"** — tied explicitly to rising seed-oil consumption over the same period, with
the claim that breast milk linoleic acid is "significantly higher... than it was 50 years ago"
`[02:03]`. The inference offered, hedged as belief rather than fact: formula makers are "trying to
mimic the amount of linoleic acid... found in the breast milk of mothers who are eating excess
amounts of seed oils" `[03:04]`. A co-founder ("Justin") is credited with research suggesting the
original benchmark population used to formulate infant formula "was even... worse than the national
average" — again hedged: "I think" `[03:04]`.

## A takedown, and a caution about overreach

`[03:04]`–`[04:05]` — The speaker describes having "a couple of videos taken down from YouTube,"
one for proposing a non-seed-oil infant formula recipe for mothers who couldn't breastfeed. The
claim is qualified rather than escalated: "I won't give any radical suggestions... my research was
by heart was the best US-based formulation," and the speaker explicitly declines to speak to
breastfeeding decisions themselves, calling it "a monumental undertaking" `[04:05]`.

## Breastfeeding support and formula-industry PR

`[04:05]`–`[06:07]` — A claim, attributed to a named contact working on breastfeeding advocacy
(transcribed as "Rachel Camp Ofelia Fox"), that "there's actually a lot of PR from the formula
makers to actually denigrate breastfeeding" `[05:06]`. A personal anecdote: a friend's Instagram
was suspended for a week after adults jokingly tried her breast milk in front of her. The segment
closes on the observation that roughly 40% of US births are by C-section, attributed to "medical
incentives" rather than medical necessity, framed as a related sign that "our underlying biology...
is being messed with" `[06:07]`.

## Symptoms and body signals addressed

None at the level of an individual case; the discussion stays at population statistics (linoleic

## Source-stated confidence

Mixed and often self-hedged. The legal-requirement claim and the "only one new formulation
approved" claim are stated flatly, without a named regulation or agency `[00:00]`. The formula
manufacturers' motive ("trying to mimic... breast milk of mothers eating excess seed oils") is
explicitly framed as belief: "I believe that what they're trying to do" `[03:04]`. **The
crackdown-on-imported-formula claim is the most visibly uncertain moment in the video, hedging
between an industry-protection motive and a genuine health rationale in the same breath**
`[01:02]`. The "Rachel Camp Ofelia Fox" attribution and the formula-industry-PR claim are both
asserted without further sourcing.

## Conflicts with other sources

Converges with `lDQofCXuYik` and `FFwAgyTDSH8` on the general claim that seed oils are a recent
addition to the human diet tied to a specific industrial history, though this video is the only one
in the library to trace that history specifically to soybean-lobby influence on infant-formula law
rather than to fast-food frying practices. It also shares `c1kyubykrvA`'s framing of a single
crop-oil industry reshaping the food supply after a wartime origin, though `c1kyubykrvA` traces
canola's origin to a lubricant surplus rather than a food-subsidy program — two different
mechanisms for a similar outcome, not the same claim. No other source in this library discusses
infant formula, breastfeeding, or C-section rates, so those claims stand without corroboration or
contradiction here.
