# It's sprayed all over your food…

## Central claim

**The host argues glyphosate is a proven carcinogen, not a safe, "world-feeding" input, pointing to
protective equipment worn by applicators, a specific European lifespan rat study finding increased
cancer at the EU's own maximum allowable human dose, and the scale of Bayer/Monsanto litigation as
evidence — concluding non-organic produce should be avoided where possible.** `[00:00]` "To suggest
that these chemicals, especially glyphosate, are not carcinogens is crazy."

## The cited evidence

`[00:00]` — Three specific pieces of evidence: **application gear** — crop-dusting planes and
workers in hazmat suits, offered as circumstantial evidence against the "it's safe" framing;
**litigation** — over **$4 billion in pending lawsuits** against Bayer/Monsanto over glyphosate
(Roundup), with **over $611 million already awarded** by courts; and a **European lifespan rat
study**, where even the lowest tested dose — stated as **0.5 mg/kg, the highest allowable dose for
humans in Europe** — "caused increased cancers" over the animals' lifespan. The study is described
but not named by author, journal, or year.

## Symptoms and body signals addressed

None named as personal symptoms — the concern here is population-level cancer risk from chronic
low-dose exposure, not an individually reportable signal.

## Glossary terms introduced

None distinct.

## Analogies worth reusing

None distinct — the argument is evidentiary (litigation, a study, protective equipment) rather than
built around a metaphor.

## Source-stated confidence

Mixed. The litigation figures are specific and checkable in principle (dollar amounts, "pending"
versus "already awarded" distinguished). The rat study is described with a specific dose figure
(0.5 mg/kg) but not named, so it can't be independently verified from this source alone. The
central causal claim — that this study proves glyphosate is a human carcinogen at real-world
exposure levels — is stated as settled rather than debated, despite regulatory bodies disagreeing
on this question.

## Conflicts with other sources

`nRN8yhYmyVc` ("Glyphosate: how it's harming you") covers the same subject in far more depth,
including IARC's Group 2 carcinogen classification (which that note's source says also covers red
meat and acrylamide, adding "I would debate" the classification's meaningfulness) and a *different*
cited rat study — an ultra-low-dose (0.1 ppb) two-year study finding liver and kidney damage, not
the 0.5 mg/kg cancer study cited here. The two videos cite different studies for related but
distinct endpoints (cancer here, organ damage there), which is worth knowing when citing either one
specifically. `_Cb2NJ_C1Mg` ("Your Protein Bar Is Made Of WHAT?!?"), filmed roughly eight months
after this short, cites a larger, later Monsanto settlement figure ($11 billion, versus this short's
$611 million awarded / $4 billion pending) — consistent with ongoing litigation growing over that
period rather than a contradiction between the two sources.
