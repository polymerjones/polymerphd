# What is he eating? 😳

## Central claim

**Reacting in real time to footage of a stranger's day of eating at an office job, the host flags
a series of specific concerns at each meal — aluminum foil storage, a plastic-lined paper coffee
cup, brown rice's arsenic content, a cited cohort study linking artificial sweeteners to cancer and
cardiovascular disease, non-organic "dirty dozen" produce, and a cited Columbia study on
nanoplastics in bottled sparkling water — while expressing sympathy for the person's food
environment ("depressed just watching this under these fluorescent lights").** `[00:00]` "I'm not
stoked about how he ended the day."

## The claims, meal by meal

`[00:00]` — **Aluminum foil**: said to increase aluminum content in refrigerated food. **Coffee**:
in a paper cup "lined with plastic," called "a microplastic bomb." **Lunch**: chicken curry with
rice (flagged as likely brown rice, "much higher in arsenic than white rice") and salad. **Diet
Coke**: sweetened with Ace-K and aspartame, cited to the **2022 NutriNet-Santé cohort**, which
found increased cancer and cardiovascular disease rates in the highest artificial-sweetener
consumers — the host adds an important hedge: **"hazard ratios were not massive, but still
concerning."** **Apple**: flagged as being on "the dirty dozen" (a pesticide-residue watch list),
hoped to be organic. **Water sources**: green tea and glass-served water assumed reverse-osmosis
filtered (approved of), versus **bottled sparkling water** at day's end, cited to a **Columbia
study** finding "hundreds of thousands of nanoplastics per liter."
