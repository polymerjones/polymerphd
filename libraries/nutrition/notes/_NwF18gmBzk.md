# Still eating Oreos?

## Central claim

**The host argues Oreo cream filling contains no actual cream — only high fructose corn syrup and
canola oil — and cites a 2013 Connecticut College animal study finding Oreos activated rodent
brain pleasure centers more strongly than cocaine or morphine, framing the product as "addictive by
design."** `[00:00]` "There's no cream in an Oreo cream filling, but it is full of two of the most
addictive substances that are making us fat, sick, and unhealthy."

## The cited study

`[00:00]` — A **2013 Connecticut College** study is named specifically: researchers fed Oreos to
animals and found the combination of sugar and seed oils **"turned on the pleasure centers of the
rodent brains more than cocaine and morphine."** No further detail (author, journal, exact
methodology) is given beyond the institution and year, but this is specific enough to be a real,
identifiable study (the host is likely referencing Joseph Schroeder's well-known Connecticut
College Oreo research).

## The suggested substitute

`[00:00]` — A pitted date with the seed removed and filled with cheese, framed as an "animal-based
Oreo" — a whole-food, higher-protein/fat alternative to the same sweet-plus-fat sensory profile.

## Source-stated confidence

Moderate for the study citation — specific enough (institution, year, comparison to cocaine/
morphine) to be checkable, though not fully cited (no author or journal named). The claim that "two
of the most addictive substances" are responsible for the effect is the host's own framing layered
onto the study's actual finding (brain pleasure-center activation), which is a reasonable but
not fully substantiated causal leap from what's cited.
