# Are LED lights disrupting your circadian rhythm?

## Central claim

**Using slowed-down footage to show LED bulbs fully switching on and off every cycle, the host
argues this imperceptible flicker stresses the nervous system and eyes, and that LEDs' blue light
content interferes with sleep and circadian rhythm — recommending incandescent bulbs instead, whose
heated tungsten filament (thermal inertia) never fully dims, producing perceived steady light.**
`[00:00]` "If you wanna feel better and you want your eyes to be less stressed, get rid of garbage
LEDs like this and put incandescent in your home."

## The mechanism, LED vs. incandescent

`[00:00]` — **LED flicker**: LEDs turn fully on and off every cycle due to how a light-emitting diode
works, described as "stressful for your nervous system" though imperceptible without slowed-down
video. **LED blue light**: named as interfering with sleep and circadian rhythm. **Incandescent
bulbs**: also technically flicker (from AC current), but their tungsten filament — heated to "over
3000 degrees Fahrenheit," described as "a fire in a bottle" — has **thermal inertia**, so it never
fully dims between cycles, and the eye perceives it as constant, steady light. The host notes
incandescent bulbs are legal again and available on Amazon.

## Symptoms and body signals addressed

- **Nervous system and eye stress** — attributed to imperceptible LED flicker `[00:00]`.
- **Sleep and circadian rhythm disruption** — attributed to LED blue light content `[00:00]`.

## Glossary terms introduced

- **Thermal inertia** — the property of a heated incandescent filament that keeps it glowing between
  AC power cycles, preventing perceptible flicker `[00:00]`.

## Analogies worth reusing

- **"A fire in a bottle"** — the host's description of an incandescent filament's extreme heat,
  making its steady-glow property vivid `[00:00]`.
