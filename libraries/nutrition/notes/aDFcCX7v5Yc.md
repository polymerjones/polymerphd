# The problem with vegetables

## Central claim

**The host argues vegetables don't serve everyone well — specifically, people with unresolved
autoimmune issues, gut issues, skin issues (psoriasis, eczema), or mood/sleep issues (insomnia) may
be reacting to oxalates, using spinach as an example of a food high in oxalates, which accumulate in
the body's joints, thyroid, and breast tissue, form the most common type of kidney stone, and have
caused documented kidney failure cases via smoothie cleanses.** `[00:00]` "Very few physicians are
going to say to a patient, the spinach you're eating might be causing your problem."

## The oxalate mechanism and case evidence

`[00:00]` — **Framing**: "if you're thriving... eat a salad, eat some kale, eat some broccoli" — this
is targeted at people with unresolved symptoms, not a universal prohibition. **Oxalates**: named as
accumulating in the human body, forming **calcium oxalate kidney stones** (the most common kidney
stone type in humans), and separately accumulating in joints, thyroid tissue, and breast tissue.
**Case evidence**: "clear case studies" of kidney failure from smoothie cleanses involving high
oxalate absorption. **Anecdote**: the host describes a friend with recent kidney stones whose diet
is "traditionally healthy," implying high vegetable/oxalate intake as the overlooked cause.

in tissues, are established points in the medical literature; the broader claim linking
undiagnosed autoimmune, gut, skin, mood, and sleep issues to oxalate sensitivity is presented with
appropriate hedging ("very few physicians are going to say...") rather than as settled clinical
consensus, and the smoothie-cleanse kidney failure cases are referenced generally without specific
