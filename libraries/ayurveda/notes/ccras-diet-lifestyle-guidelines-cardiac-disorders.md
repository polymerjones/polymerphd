# Ayurveda Based Diet and Life Style Guidelines for Prevention of Cardiac Disorders

*This is a government clinical guideline (Central Council for Research in Ayurvedic Sciences,
Ministry of AYUSH, Government of India; 1st edition 2018; ISBN 978-93-83864-31-7), not a popular
or influencer source. It shares extensive structural and word-for-word overlap with the companion
CCRAS skin-diseases guideline in this library (`` `ccras-diet-lifestyle-guidelines-skin-diseases` ``)
— see Conflicts below. Several of the source's ingredient tables are image-heavy and did not
extract cleanly from the PDF; food names and their Latin/botanical identifications are preserved
as printed, but visual layout (which foods were grouped in which image) could not be reconstructed
reliably.*

## Central claim

**Cardiovascular disease is framed here as substantially preventable through Ayurvedic diet and
lifestyle, operating on a specific proposed digestive mechanism** rather than treated as primarily
a mechanical or genetic condition. "It is estimated that 90% of CVD is preventable. Prevention of
CVD involves improving risk factors through: healthy eating, exercise, avoidance of tobacco smoke
and limiting alcohol intake." `[p.10]` The Ayurvedic causal chain offered: impaired **Koshtagni**
(digestive fire) → poor-quality **Ahararasa** → vitiated **Rasa Dhatu** (circulated by Vyana Vayu
through the **Rasavaha Srotas**, whose main organ is the **Hridaya**/heart) → metabolic disorders
(Medoroga, Sthoulya, Prameha) → **Hrid-vikara** (heart disease). `[p.11]` "The indiscretions
followed by human beings in terms of Ahara (diet) and Vihara (lifestyle) can directly cause
impairment of Agni and thus leads to formation of vitiated rasa dhatu." `[p.11]`

## Cardiac risk factors: modern epidemiology framing

The source opens with conventional epidemiological claims before layering Ayurvedic explanation
onto them: "Cardiovascular disorders continue to be the major cause of mortality representing
about 30% of all deaths worldwide," with prevalence "reached alarming proportions among Indians in
recent years" amid urbanization. `[p.6]` It separately lists:

- **Non-modifiable risk factors**: advancing age, genetic predisposition. `[p.20]`
- **Modifiable risk factors**: sedentary lifestyle, hypertension, type-II diabetes, dyslipidemia,
  overweight/obesity, metabolic syndrome, smoking/alcohol. `[p.20]`
- **Emerging risk factors**: H. pylori and Chlamydia pneumoniae infection, calcium supplementation,
  vitamin D deficiency, antiphospholipid syndrome, premature birth/low birth weight. `[p.20]`

It also states, attributing the framing to a cited study rather than to classical Ayurvedic
sources: "It is not what you eat that kills you; it is what eats you (negative thoughts) that
kills you," citing Hippsley-Cox, Fielding, and Pringle on depression as an IHD risk factor. `[p.8]`

## The 11 named causative/predisposing factors for Hridroga (heart disease)

Attributed to "authoritative texts of Ayurveda": excess hot/oily/spicy/fried food; excess
astringent/irritant food; incompatible diet (viruddhaahara); excessive or absent exercise;
excessive stress/fear/anger; retention of natural urges; excessive fasting; overeating/irregular
food habits; improper vasti/purgative use; excess alcohol/tobacco/cigarette; and chest injury.
`[p.11]`–`[p.12]`

## Vihara (lifestyle): dinacharya, ritucharya, sadvritta, and urge suppression

Structurally and often verbatim identical to the companion skin-diseases guideline's equivalent
sections (see Conflicts below):

- **Dinacharya**: waking in Brahma Muhurta (4:00–5:30am), nature's calls, tooth/tongue cleaning,
  betel-leaf chewing, daily Abhyanga (oil massage), daily Vyayama (exercise), Udvartana (dry powder
  massage with yava/kola/kulath), Snana (bathing), grooming. `[p.8]`–`[p.9]`
- **Ritucharya**: the same six-season, dosha-matched dietary shift scheme as the skin-diseases
  guideline. `[p.8]`–`[p.9]`
- **Sadvritta**: the same ~20-item ethical conduct list. `[p.9]`–`[p.10]`
- **Urge suppression**: the same 13 non-suppressible urges with the same named consequences (e.g.,
  suppressing vomiting → "urticaria, giddiness, anaemia, hyperacidity, skin diseases and fever"),
  plus the same suppressible-urge list (greed, grief, fear, fury, pride, envy). `[p.10]`

## Three named conditions: causes, symptoms, and pathya/apathya

| Condition | Ayurvedic classification | Notable causes cited | Notable symptoms cited |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Madhumeha** (Diabetes) `[p.21]`–`[p.22]` | Special variety of Vataja Prameha; also Ojomeha/Kshaudrameha; a Santarpanjanya vyadhi (overeating/sedentary-caused disease) | Excess sleep/inactivity, excess sweet/sugar/dairy, excess new-harvest carbohydrate, fried/oily food, fear/grief/anger, parental Madhumeha | Frequent/turbid urination, excess thirst and hunger, burning/tingling in hands/feet, tiredness |
| **Uchcharaktachapa** (Hypertension) `[p.23]`–`[p.24]` | Vataja, attributed to Vyana Vayu imbalance; overlaps with Raktagatavata/Raktavritavata/Pittavritavata | Obesity, sedentary lifestyle, excess oily/salty/sour/spicy food, alcohol/smoking, stress/anxiety/anger | Headache, vertigo, breathlessness, chest discomfort, irritability, nausea, nosebleed, blurred vision, leg/eye swelling, burning/numbness in hands and feet |
| **Sthaulya** (Obesity) `[p.25]`–`[p.26]` | One of Charaka's "Ashta ninditiya purusha" (8 despicable conditions); a Santarpanottha Vikara (over-calorie disease); Medodushti named as an IHD risk factor | Sedentary lifestyle, frequent/excess intake, oily/sweet/cold/heavy food, inactivity, day-sleeping, parental obesity, mental exhaustion | Breathlessness on little exertion, disinterest in work, profuse malodorous sweating, excess hunger, tiredness, excess sleep |

Each condition's guideline includes a specific Pathya (Do's) and Apathya (Don'ts) list — e.g., for
Uchcharaktachapa: pathya includes barley, sorghum, wheat, green gram, moringa, bitter gourd, bottle
gourd, turnip, carrot, radish, amla, cucumber, black grapes, pomegranate, apple, pineapple, cold
milk, weight reduction, timely sleep, daily brisk walking, and Yoga/meditation under supervision;
apathya includes excess salt, butter, ghee, chilies, pickles, sour fruits, curd, tea/coffee, animal
fat, processed/oily food, alcohol, smoking, and day-sleeping. `[p.23]`–`[p.24]`

## Diet charts by Prakriti (constitution)

Three full-day menus are given — for Vata, Pitta, and Kapha Prakriti Purush — each specifying
exact foods and quantities across six meal slots (early morning, breakfast, lunch, evening,
dinner, bedtime). `[p.27]`–`[p.29]` Differences are specific rather than general: e.g., bedtime
is plain milk (1–250ml) for Vata Prakriti, milk with Mishri/Khand (sugar) for Pitta Prakriti, and
milk *without* sugar for Kapha Prakriti; the Kapha chart adds tea/coffee in the evening slot, which
neither other chart includes. `[p.27]`–`[p.29]`

## Symptoms and body signals addressed

- Diabetes-associated: frequent/turbid urination, excess thirst/hunger, hand/foot
  burning/tingling, tiredness `[p.21]`
- Hypertension-associated: headache, vertigo, breathlessness, chest discomfort/pain, irritability,
  nausea/vomiting, appetite loss, nosebleed, vision haziness, leg/eye swelling, hand/foot
  burning/numbness, restlessness `[p.23]`
- Obesity-associated: exertional breathlessness, work disinterest, malodorous sweating, excess
  hunger, tiredness, excess sleep `[p.25]`
- General Hridroga symptoms named via Sanskrit terms with modern gloss: Aruchi (anorexia),
  Mukhavairasya (mouth dis-taste), Kaphotklesh (nausea), Trishna (increased thirst), Shwas
  (dyspnoea), Murchha (fainting), Hikka (hiccough), UrahShool (chest pain), Kasa (cough), Chhardi
  (vomiting), Vivamata (pallor/cyanosis), Pramoh (stupor), Galoplepa (throat coating), Jwar
  (fever), KaphaShthivan (productive cough) `[p.12]`

## Glossary terms introduced

- **Hridaya** — the heart, described as the Mula (main seat) of Rasavaha Srotas. `[p.6]`
- **Rasavaha Srotas** — the Ayurvedic circulatory system through which Rasa Dhatu (the first
  tissue formed from digested food) circulates, driven by Vyana Vayu. `[p.11]`
- **Viruddhahara** — dietetic incompatibility; here explicitly linked to cardiac and dermatologic
  risk via Tridosha aggravation. `[p.7]`
- **Madhumeha, Uchcharaktachapa, Sthaulya** — this source's Ayurvedic names for diabetes,
  hypertension, and obesity respectively, each treated as a distinct named disease entity with its
  own causes and remedies rather than as a lab-value threshold. `[p.20]`–`[p.26]`

## Analogies worth reusing

The source offers little in the way of everyday analogy — its style is closer to a clinical
protocol document than a teaching text. The one notable rhetorical device is the reframing of a
cited public-health aphorism for a cardiac-prevention audience: "It is not what you eat that kills
you; it is what eats you (negative thoughts) that kills you." `[p.8]`

## Source-stated confidence

This is a citation-backed government guideline (35 numbered references mixing classical Ayurvedic
texts — Charaka Samhita, Sushruta Samhita, Astanga Hridaya/Sangraha — with modern peer-reviewed
citations on turmeric, garlic, ginger, fenugreek, and pomegranate). It states its Ayurvedic
causal claims (the Koshtagni→Rasa Dhatu→Hrid-vikara chain; dosha classifications of Madhumeha,
Uchcharaktachapa, Sthaulya) as established doctrine without hedging, while explicitly labeling
statistical claims (90% of CVD preventable, 30% of world deaths) and its "negative thoughts" claim
as coming from cited external sources rather than classical Ayurvedic text. Like the companion
skin-diseases guideline, it opens with the same explicit caveat that these are "general guidelines
& advocacies" requiring supervision by "a qualified registered Ayurveda practitioner." `[p.3]`

## Conflicts with other sources

**Strong convergence** with the companion guideline `` `ccras-diet-lifestyle-guidelines-skin-diseases` ``
— published by the same body (CCRAS, Ministry of AYUSH) in the same guideline series, sharing
near-identical Ahara/Vihara framing, an identical Ashta ahara vidhi visesha ayatana factor list, and
word-for-word overlapping Dinacharya, Ritucharya, Sadvritta, and urge-suppression sections down to
matching phrasing ("It is advisable to wake up during Brahma Muhurta (preferably between 4.00 a.m.
to 5.30 a.m.)"). Both guidelines also independently list Viruddhahara as a cause of skin disease —
this document names it alongside Gulma and allergies as a cardiac-adjacent concern `[p.7]`, while
the skin-diseases guideline treats it as a direct Kushta cause — a point of genuine overlap rather
than disagreement. Flagging for future cross-referencing against `` `-BEdhS9-cno` `` (the
HealthyGamerGG video note already in this library): that source's claim of "ayurgenomics" research
linking Kapha to type-2-diabetes susceptibility runs parallel to, but is not sourced from, this
document's clinical Madhumeha guidance — the two make compatible but independently derived claims
about diabetes and dosha that have not been reconciled against each other by either source.
