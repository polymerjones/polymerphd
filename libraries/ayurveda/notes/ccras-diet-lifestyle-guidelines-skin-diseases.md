# Ayurveda Based Diet & Life Style Guidelines for Prevention and Management of Skin Diseases

*This is a government clinical guideline (Central Council for Research in Ayurvedic Sciences,
Ministry of AYUSH, Government of India; ISBN 978-93-83864-32-4), not a popular or influencer
source — its register and authority differ from the rest of this library, and it should be weighed
accordingly. Some tables in the source PDF did not extract cleanly (image-based ingredient icons
were dropped, occasionally scrambling row order); where reconstructed as a table below, the food
name and its stated modern-day example are preserved exactly as printed, even where the source
table's own layout was disordered.*

## Central claim

**Skin disease (grouped under the umbrella term Kushta) is, in this source's framework, primarily
a disease of diet and lifestyle** — not an external or purely dermatological condition — arising
from specific named categories of incompatible or excessive food intake (Aharaja nidana) compounded
by lifestyle and psychological factors (Viharaja, Manasika nidana). `[p.7]`–`[p.14]` "Ayurveda has
included skin diseases under the umbrella of Kushta and has pointed at its multifactorial etiology.
Erroneous Diet and lifestyle have been implicated as chief factors for the onset, progression and
recurrence of many skin diseases." `[p.4]` The guideline's stated purpose is prevention and
management through "dietary and lifestyle advocacies based on authentic Ayurvedic commendations."
`[p.4]`

## The concept of Ahara (diet) and its eight governing factors

Diet is framed as one of Ayurveda's three sub-pillars (thrayo-upasthamba), alongside vihara
(lifestyle) and aushadha (medicaments), with food itself classified as pathya (wholesome,
congenial to the individual) or apathya (unwholesome). `[p.8]` The source lists 18 general
Ayurvedic dietary guidelines (eating at the right time, per one's constitution, with proper
hygiene, warm, easy to digest, with six taste components, calmly, silently, etc.) `[p.8]`, plus a
specific quantity rule attributed to Susruta and Vagbhata: divide stomach capacity into four parts
— two solid food, one liquid, one left empty "for easy movement of Vata." `[p.9]`

The eight-factor framework (Ashta ahara vidhi visesha ayatana) for evaluating any food:

| Factor | Meaning (as stated) |
|---|---|
| Prakriti | Nature of the food itself (heavy/light, hot/cold potency, etc.) |
| Karana | Modification of food properties by processing (e.g., heating dadhi/curd is prohibited) |
| Samyoga | Combinations that augment or nullify each other's effects |
| Raashi | Quantity required per person |
| Desha | Habitat/origin of the food relative to the person |
| Kaala | Time — climate, digestive phase, time of day, disease stage |
| Upayoga Samstha | Following the rules of dietetics in how food is taken |
| Upayokta | The consumer's personal preference and habituation (okasatmya) |

`[p.9]`

## Virudha ahara (dietetic incompatibility)

Defined via Acharya Charaka as diet combinations that "interrupt the metabolism," inhibit tissue
formation, or have properties opposite to the tissue being nourished. `[p.10]` The source states
Ayurvedic literature compiles roughly 18 types of virudha ahara, with named complications ranging
from skin disease (kushta) and visarpa (erysipelas) to unmada (insanity) and marana (death).
`[p.10]`

## Vihara (lifestyle): dinacharya, ritucharya, sadvritta, and urge suppression

Presented as near-identical in structure to the same sections in the companion CCRAS cardiac
guideline (`` `ccras-diet-lifestyle-guidelines-cardiac-disorders` `` — see Conflicts below),
down to shared wording in several places:

- **Dinacharya (daily regimen)**: waking in Brahma Muhurta (4:00–5:30am), drinking water on
  waking, attending nature's calls, tongue/mouth cleaning, betel-leaf chewing, daily oil massage
  (abhyanga), daily exercise, bathing, grooming, and avoiding extramarital sex or sex during a
  partner's menses, pregnancy, or postpartum period. `[p.10]`–`[p.11]`
- **Ritucharya (seasonal regimen)**: six named seasons, each with a specific dosha aggravation and
  a matched dietary shift — e.g., "In summer season due to hot climate aggravation of pitta occurs.
  Hence pitta pacifying cold, liquid, sweet and oily diet is advised." `[p.11]`
- **Sadvritta (ethical regimen)**: a 20-item list of conduct rules (truthfulness, temper control,
  avoiding sensory addiction, meditation, cleanliness, patience, self-control, kindness,
  regularity). `[p.11]`–`[p.12]`
- **Suppression of natural urges**: 13 non-suppressible urges are named, each with a specific
  stated consequence of suppression (e.g., "Suppression of vomiting produces different types of
  diseases like urticaria, giddiness, anaemia, hyperacidity, **skin diseases** and fever")
  `[p.12]`, plus a shorter list of urges (greed, grief, fear, fury, pride, envy) that should
  instead be actively suppressed. `[p.13]`

## Etiology and pathogenesis of Kushta

The source distinguishes **Sannikrishta Nidana** (proximal causative factors: the three doshas —
Vata, Pitta, Kapha — plus four Dushyas: Tvak/Rasa, Rakta, Mansa, Ambu/Lasika) from **Viprakrishta
Nidana** (remote factors that sensitize the body without directly causing disease), the latter
split into **Kulaja Nidana** (hereditary/karmic — Kushta is described as transmissible "from past
lives," and as arising in a fetus "from the day of conception") and **Janmottarakalaja** (present-
life: Aharaja/diet, Viharaja/lifestyle, Manasika/psychological). `[p.13]`–`[p.14]` **Papakarma**
(sinful acts) is separately named as an implicated cause, and Kushta is described as a
**samsargaja vyadhi** — a communicable disease, spreading via physical contact (including sexual
contact), exhaled air, shared dining/sleeping/sitting, or shared personal items like clothing,
ornaments, and cosmetics. `[p.13]`, `[p.18]`–`[p.19]`

Pathogenesis is summarized as: Aharaja/Viharaja/Manasika/Agantuka factors → Agni Vaisamya
(digestive fire imbalance) and Raktadushti (blood vitiation) → deeper tissue (dhatu) involvement →
vitiation of Vata, Pitta, and Kapha manifesting in skin, blood, muscle, and lymph — described as
"seven-fold pathogenic substances" collectively producing the 18 named Kushta subtypes (7 major +
11 minor). `[p.19]`

### Food-related causative factors (Aharaja nidana)

The source lists specific foods and combinations implicated in skin disease, each with a named
Ayurvedic category and a modern-day equivalent example:

| Ayurvedic category | Modern-day example given |
|---|---|
| Atilavana (excess salt) | Pickles, bhelpuri, papad, chips, namkeen |
| Atiamla (excess sour) | Fermented products, pickles, sour juices, tomato sauce, curd, buttermilk, lemon juice, vinegar, alcohol |
| Virudhahara (incompatible combos) | Sprouted vegetables/grains with meat, milk with meat, honey with meat, curd with chicken, fish with jaggery |
| Guru annapana (hard to digest) | Pizza, cheese-mixed foods, bakery products, kidney beans, paneer, dosa/idli/vada, beef, pork |
| Snigdha annapana (oily/unctuous) | Biryani, fried oily foods, meat soups, ghee-heavy sweets |
| Ati dravapana (excess liquid) | Excess water/juice/soft drinks/milk/rasam beyond bodily need |
| Asatmya ahara (uncongenial to person/place) | Foreign-origin fruits, Chinese food, pasta, pizza, soybean |
| Dadhi | Curd (raita, lassi, dahivada), especially heated or in autumn/spring |
| Excessive fish, or fish + milk | — |
| Masha | Black gram preparations (vada, dosa) in excess |
| Mulaka | Regular radish use |
| Excess tila/guda | Sesame preparations; jaggery products |
| Ati madyapana | Excess alcohol — named cause of visarpa (erysipelas) |
| Praklinna anna | Putrefied food |
| Kurchika, kilata | Excess dairy products like paneer |

`[p.15]`–`[p.22]` Lifestyle factors listed alongside diet: irregular/improper panchakarma
practice, entering cold water after fear/exhaustion/grief, daytime sleep outside summer,
suppressing natural urges, and abrupt hot/cold exposure shifts. `[p.23]`–`[p.24]`

## Pathogenesis stages and symptoms (Poorvarupa)

The source lists prodromal signs (poorvarupa) attributed to multiple named classical authors
(Charaka, Sushruta, Vagbhata): excessive or absent perspiration, itching, skin roughness or
excessive smoothness, wound-size increase despite treatment, horripilation, numbness, blood
discoloration, coarseness, hot flushes, heaviness, recurrent oedema/spreading, and severe wound
pain. `[p.26]`–`[p.28]`

## Symptoms and body signals addressed

- Skin lesions classified into 7 Maha Kushta (major) types — Kapala, Udumbara, Mandala,
  Rusyajihva, Pundarika, Sidhma, Kakanaka — and 11 Kshudra Kushta (minor) types — Ekakusta,
  Carmakya, Kitibha, Vipadika, Alasaka, Dadru, Carmadala, Pama, Visphota, Sataru, Vicaracika —
  each given a specific visual/tactile description (e.g., "Kapala Kusta: patches...like black and
  reddish pieces of Kapala [broken earthen pot]...un-unctuous, rough and thick to touch...
  associated with excessive pain and...difficult to cure") `[p.29]`–`[p.30]`
- A further list of related dermatologic conditions and their descriptions: Shwitra
  (depigmentation), Ajagallika, Andhalaji, Indralupta (hair loss), Yuvanapidika (acne-like),
  Chippa (nail-bed suppuration), Kunaka, Charmakeela, Padadarika (cracked soles), Vyanga (facial
  discoloration), Darunaka (scalp dryness with itching/flaking) `[p.33]`–`[p.34]`
- Itching, burning sensation, discharge/suppuration, and pain are recurring features across nearly
  every named Kushta subtype

## Glossary terms introduced

- **Kushta** — the umbrella Ayurvedic term for skin disease used throughout this source.
- **Pathya / Apathya** — wholesome vs. unwholesome diet and conduct, congenial or non-congenial to
  a given individual's constitution. `[p.8]`
- **Virudha ahara** — dietetic incompatibility; combinations of food deemed mutually contradictory
  or harmful when taken together or at the wrong time. `[p.10]`
- **Samsargaja roga** — the source's term for communicable disease, spread via contact, shared air,
  or shared objects; skin disease is explicitly classed here. `[p.18]`
- **Poorvarupa** — prodromal signs indicating a forthcoming disease. `[p.20]`

## Analogies worth reusing

The source's own diagnostic method for Kushta subtypes is built entirely on comparative imagery —
each skin condition is named and recognized by resemblance to a familiar object:

- **Kapala Kusta** — patches resembling "black and reddish pieces of Kapala (broken pieces of
  earthen pot)." `[p.30]`
- **Udumbara Kusta** — coppery lesions "resembling like a ripe fig fruit (Udumbara phala)."
  `[p.30]`
- **Rusyajihva Kusta** — patches "resembling the tongue of rushya (a type of antelope)." `[p.30]`
- **Pundarika Kusta** — white lesions "resembling the petals of lotus flower." `[p.30]`
- **Carma Kusta** — lesion resembling "skin of elephant." `[p.31]`
- **Dadru** — lesion resembling "atasi flower (Linum usitatissimum)." `[p.32]`

## Source-stated confidence

This is a formal, citation-backed government clinical guideline (42 numbered classical and modern
references), and it states its core claims — Kushta's dosha-based etiology, the classical dietary
incompatibility framework — as established Ayurvedic doctrine without hedging. It does, however,
carry its own explicit caveat at the top of the document: **"These are general guidelines &
advocacies and should only be followed under supervision of qualified registered Ayurveda
practitioner"** `[p.2]`, and a disclaimer that the publisher "shall not be accountable for any
inadvertent error in the content." `[p.2]` The claim that past-life conduct (Poorva Janmakrata) can
directly cause a person to be reborn with Kushta `[p.13]` is presented as classical doctrine
(citing Sushruta) rather than as the source's own clinical observation — a category distinct from
the diet/lifestyle claims that make up the bulk of the document.

## Conflicts with other sources

No prior notes exist in this library to compare against except the companion government guideline
`` `ccras-diet-lifestyle-guidelines-cardiac-disorders` ``, published by the same body (CCRAS,
Ministry of AYUSH) in the same series. The two **converge closely** — both open with near-identical
Ahara/Vihara framing, an identical Ashta ahara vidhi visesha ayatana list, and word-for-word
overlapping Dinacharya, Ritucharya, Sadvritta, and urge-suppression sections (down to shared
phrasing like "It is advisable to wake up during Brahma Muhurta (preferably between 4.00 a.m. to
5.30 a.m.)"). They diverge only in their disease-specific sections: this document's Kushta
etiology/classification and pathya/apathya lists versus the cardiac guideline's risk-factor and
diet-chart content. Also flagging for future cross-referencing against `` `-BEdhS9-cno` `` (the
library's HealthyGamerGG video note): that source's dosha-personality framework (cognitive
fingerprint, RPG-stat analogies) describes Vata/Pitta/Kapha in psychological/constitutional terms
very different from this document's strictly clinical/dermatological framing — both describe the
same three doshas, but apply them to different domains without directly overlapping claims to
compare.
