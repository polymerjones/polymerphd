# DJI Air 3s: Every Feature & Set up Explained - [FULL FLIGHT DEMO]

## Central claim

**This is a physical setup and full-menu walkthrough filmed in the field, going from unboxing
(props, filters, charging) through controller binding to every automated shot mode the Air 3S
offers, before landing on the presenter's own recommended button and exposure setup.** `[00:00]`
"I'm going to go through absolutely everything in this video, from fixing the props, charging the
battery, going through how to fit the ND filters, which ND filter to use, all the features on
the [RC] controller, how to set it up, how to program the buttons, and how to use tracking, quick
shots, master shots, cruise control, and the best settings to use for video and photography — and
we're going to do it all out in the field."

## Physical setup: props, binding, ND filters (01:01–09:11)

Props are keyed to prevent misassembly: `[02:02]` "there's A and B props and it's clearly marked
on the packet here, and it shows you which prop arm to connect them to, and they go diagonally."
Controller-to-aircraft binding requires an explicit hold sequence: `[05:06]` "you hold your
finger down on that and then it'll ask you to hold down the button for 4 seconds on the drone, it
would make a high-pitched bleep a couple of times and they will connect together."

ND filter choice is derived from a specific stop calculation rather than a rule of thumb: `[09:11]`
"I've set the ISO at 100 and we know it needs to be at f2.8 because that's what the fixed focal
length is... [at 400th shutter] to work out which filter you use you would move it one stop up
or down." Following the 180-degree shutter rule at 25 fps: `[10:13]` "180 rule says I want my
shutter to be [1/50th]... I've got to come down three stops... an nd2 is one stop, nd4, nd8 — so
I need to put an nd8 on to get the shutter where I want it to be... I will leave the ISO in auto
and let that just adjust according to the light, but that will give me a better sense of motion
blur in the movements."

## Button customization: C1/C2 (07:09–08:10)

The presenter states his own working assignment rather than a manufacturer default: `[07:09]`
"the C1 button, I've set as cruise control." For C2: `[08:10]` "I've got auto exposure lock on or
off, because I find that super helpful... that's really handy when you're flying — you've got a
good exposure, you don't want it to fluctuate — you can just click that C2 button and it will
hold that exposure throughout any movements you're doing."

## Pre-flight and exposure setup in the field (10:13–13:14)

Satellite count before this flight was lower than the presenter's usual target, stated as
acceptable rather than a hard minimum: `[10:13]` "checking my satellites, it got to about 11
satellites... it usually likes about 16, of course you only need four to really position where
you are, but one might drop off — that's why it will give you the benefit of having [a] desire
[for] more satellite connections." Active-track geometry is set to specific, stated values before
flying: `[11:13]` "I've changed the inner radius to 5 m, outer radius to 10 m, and the inner
height to 5 m... [and] outer height 10 m, so I know exactly where it's going to be."

## Waypoint flight with point-of-interest lock (20:50–22:56)

The waypoint workflow ties recording start/stop to specific waypoints and can force the gimbal to
hold on a fixed subject throughout: `[20:50]`–`[21:53]` "we're going to go to our waypoint one and
say this is where we're going to start recording, and then we're going to go to waypoint two and
this is where it's going to stop recording... if we get to our point of interest [and set]
altitude... to zero, that's going to make it look at me [as a] point of interest... the gimbal
should always be looking at me."

## Glossary terms introduced

- **AE lock (C2 shortcut)** — a programmable button assignment that freezes auto-exposure at its
  current reading for the duration of a shot `[08:10]`.
- **Quick Shots** — a named set of six pre-programmed automated camera movements (Dronie, Rocket,
  Circle, Helix, Boomerang, Asteroid), each launched from a single subject selection `[25:02]`.
- **Master Shots** — an auto-generated sequence of varied movements around one selected subject,
  built from presenter-set parameters (width, distance, start point) `[24:00]`.
