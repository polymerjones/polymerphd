# You can't shame yourself into a new nervous system | Nicole LePera

## Central claim

**Lasting behavior change comes from "reparenting" — consciously noticing a stress reaction as it
builds and deliberately responding differently in the moment — rather than from coping (relieving
discomfort quickly without changing what drives it) or from shaming lapses, and the practice
requires small, repeated steps rather than sweeping reinvention, because the nervous system
actively prefers familiar patterns.** `[03:05]` "Coping is a way, a means to get through each
moment... without actually changing what's driving a lot of these dysfunctional habits or patterns.
And that's what reparenting actually allows us to do. It allows us to show up in the moment in a new
way."

## Reparenting versus coping, illustrated with an unanswered text

`[03:05]`–`[04:06]` LePera contrasts the two responses to the same trigger — waiting on a reply to a
text or email. **Coping**: the body's urgency rises, and the person copes by acting on it
immediately — firing off another message, re-reading their own last text, convincing themselves the
other person is upset. **Reparenting**: noticing the urgency building in the chest, deliberately
relaxing tensed muscles and slowing quickened breath, and pausing before reacting — teaching the
nervous system a new association ("distance doesn't mean disconnection") instead of just soothing
the discomfort as fast as possible.

## Three misconceptions about reparenting, corrected directly

`[05:07]`–`[06:09]` LePera names and corrects three specific misunderstandings: reparenting is not
about **blaming parents** ("it's simply acknowledging the impact that our past is still having on
our present moment"); it does not **keep a person stuck in childhood** ("our nervous system is
already doing that" — reparenting is the intervention, not the trap); and it is **not immediate** —
"it took years for us... for all of these patterns to form, so change equally is going to take
time."

## Why shame backfires, and the "small daily promise" instead

`[14:18]`–`[15:20]` LePera is explicit that struggling to act on new self-knowledge is expected, not
a personal failing: **"it is so difficult to translate insight into action because we're
challenging our body, our nervous system's capacity."** The stated practical response is to break an
intended change down to "the smallest most accessible choice possible," anticipate that it will be
uncomfortable, and — critically — avoid shaming a lapse back into the old pattern, since shame
itself is framed as counterproductive to the nervous-system change being attempted. This is named
in her community practice as **"a small daily promise."**

## Talking to parents: managing the expectation, not just the conversation

`[11:15]`–`[12:17]` LePera addresses a common next step directly: wanting parents to validate the
impact of their past actions. She's candid that many parents, "human too," will respond
defensively rather than with the hoped-for validation, and reframes the goal: **"is it enough for me
to validate my own experience even if what I'm hearing on the other side isn't what I had wanted to
hear from my parents?"** — treating the act of voicing the experience, not the parent's reaction, as
the point.

## Signals and internal states addressed

- **A stress reaction building — tightening chest, quickening breath, urgency** — named as the
  specific moment where reparenting practice applies, distinct from already having reacted.
  `[04:06]`
- **Shame or self-criticism after falling back into an old pattern** — explicitly named as
  counterproductive, to be replaced with acknowledgment and continued small steps. `[15:20]`
- **Frustration when loved ones don't adopt new insights or tools as enthusiastically as expected**
  — reframed as the nervous system's ordinary preference for familiar patterns, not a personal
  rejection. `[13:18]`
- **A sense that awareness of one's own patterns has come "too late"** — explicitly countered:
  "there's never a concept of too late... awareness is something to be celebrated, not to be
  shamed." `[16:20]`

## Glossary terms introduced

- **Reparenting** — consciously stepping in as a compassionate, regulating adult presence for
  oneself in the present moment, as distinct from simply coping with discomfort. `[00:00]`
- **Small daily promise** — a SelfHealers Circle community practice of committing to the smallest
  possible version of an intended change, expecting discomfort, and continuing without self-shame
  after a lapse. `[15:20]`
