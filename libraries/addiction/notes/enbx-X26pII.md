# Your goals are optimized for the wrong thing | Chris Bailey

## Central claim

**A goal only reliably gets attained when it's aligned through the full "intention stack" — values,
priorities, goals, plans, and present intentions — because a goal pursued in isolation, disconnected
from what a person actually values, is optimized for the wrong thing even if it looks identical to
an aligned goal from the outside.** `[00:00]` "When it comes to the goals that we attain, intention
matters more than any other factor in determining whether we'll attain our goals." `[02:03]` "A goal
is not enough. We need our values, we need our daily actions as well... it's about the priorities
that it fits with, the broader values that are in your life that it fits with, too."

## The five-layer intention stack

`[01:01]`–`[02:03]` From top to bottom: **values** (broad, deep motivations), **priorities**
(e.g. "becoming healthier"), **goals** (a "story of change," e.g. running a marathon), **plans**
(e.g. "run this many miles this week"), and **present intentions** (e.g. "go for a run after
lunch today"). Bailey's stated point: a goal is not self-contained — it should connect upward to a
priority and value, and downward to a concrete plan and daily action, or it risks being pursued
without real motivational backing.

## Default versus deliberate intentions

`[04:04]`–`[06:07]` Bailey distinguishes **default intentions** — habitual, conditioned, often
unnoticed (e.g. picking up a phone and "bouncing around between a stable of apps" on waking) —
from **deliberate intentions**, consciously chosen after a specific "moment of awakening" where a
person steps back and asks **"what is the most meaningful thing I could be doing right now."**
Both are described as legitimate and constant; the goal isn't to eliminate default intentions but
to notice the choice points where deliberate ones can be substituted.

## Four attributes of every intention

`[07:08]`–`[10:12]` **Source** — biology, social conditioning, past lessons, or deeper self-
reflection. **Duration** — spans from a tiny intention (tying shoelaces) nested inside a larger one
(training for a marathon) nested inside a value (feeling secure in one's body). **Strength** — "a
function of how much we desire doing the intention minus the aversion" to it, which fluctuates
across a goal's timeline rather than staying constant. **Depth** — how connected an intention is to
a person's deepest values; Bailey gives his own example (self-direction and pleasure as his top
values) to show why some goals feel motivating and others feel like effortful obligation even when
comparably difficult.

## Signals and internal states addressed

- **Acting for long stretches without noticing a choice was available** — named as time spent in
  default/autopilot intention rather than deliberate choice. `[04:04]`
- **A goal that "should" be motivating but consistently feels like effortful obligation** — named
  as a sign of low depth-alignment between that goal and a person's actual top values (Bailey's own
  post-surgery cardiovascular-fitness example). `[10:12]`
- **Desire for a goal fading over its timeline even when the goal hasn't changed** — named as a
  normal, expected fluctuation in intention strength, not evidence the goal was wrong. `[09:10]`

## Glossary terms introduced

- **The intention stack** — Bailey's five-layer model: values, priorities, goals, plans, present
  intentions. `[01:01]`
- **Default intention** vs. **deliberate intention** — habitual/conditioned action versus
  consciously chosen action. `[04:04]`
