# 04 — Signals and Self-Assessment

Internal states and observable signals this collection's sources say to watch for, gathered across
all eleven notes and grouped by what they indicate. Matches this library's "Signals and internal
states addressed" section, aggregated here rather than left scattered per-note.

---

## Signs a habit or compulsion is running on autopilot, not choice

- A craving that persists despite satisfying the assumed reward (sugar, caffeine, a snack) — a sign
  the actual reward hasn't been correctly identified yet.
- Reflexive, repeated urges to check a device — more than once every five minutes when it's put
  away is offered as one source's own rough self-test for behavioral dependence.
- Feeling restless, anxious, or in need of a "reset" after an absorbing digital session — a
  retrospective marker that the immersion ran counter to actual values, distinct from a fulfilling
  kind of absorption.
- Long stretches of acting without noticing a choice point was ever available — described as time
  spent in default, conditioned intention rather than deliberate choice.

## Signs of identity-protective avoidance

- Avoiding a *good* opportunity, not just a bad one, or sabotaging a relationship that's going
  well — the counterintuitive signature of identity-threat avoidance, which applies symmetrically
  to beneficial and harmful things.
- A disproportionate, "irrational" anxiety response to an objectively low-stakes change — reframed
  as a positive diagnostic (a keystone habit or identity-linked pattern worth deliberately
  confronting) rather than a reason to avoid the change.
- A visceral, "get this out of my face" resistance to a specific goal or task — named as a values
  conflict, not a motivation or willpower deficiency.

## Signs of values misalignment

- Claiming to value something (honesty, health) while consistently acting against it — described
  as the ordinary, unremarkable way self-deception about one's own values shows up, not a special
  kind of hypocrisy.
- A goal that sounded appealing when set but is now actively resented in daily practice — a
  mismatch between the idea of a goal and its lived reality.
- Consistently not doing something despite genuinely intending to — treated as the signal that the
  goal itself (not the person's discipline) needs to be revised.
- Feeling paralyzed or adrift when facing many possible paths — named as a symptom of values
  unclarity that gets *harder*, not easier, as available options increase.

## Signs of the "comfort trap"

- Feeling that an external device's score or metric should override one's own judgment about
  readiness or capability — named as a source of fragility rather than reliability.
- Exhaustion, apathy, and dread despite a low or moderate workload — distinguished from
  conventional overwork burnout, and attributed instead to insufficient meaningful engagement.
- Judging oneself for not feeling "happy enough," or for feeling discomfort during otherwise
  meaningful work — reframed as a symptom of treating happiness itself as the goal, rather than a
  sign something is wrong.

## Signs of productive struggle, not failure

- Progress stalling after an initial period of fast, visible gains — reframed as the ordinary
  transition into a plateau, itself often a necessary precondition for a breakthrough, not a sign
  the approach has failed.
- Feeling disoriented or unfulfilled immediately after achieving a long-pursued goal — a named,
  common reaction ("the arrival fallacy"), not evidence the goal was the wrong one.
- Difficulty translating a new insight into consistent action — framed as an expected friction
  between insight and an already-established nervous-system or habitual pattern, not a personal
  failing.
- A sense that self-awareness about one's own patterns arrived "too late" — explicitly countered in
  this collection as a false framing; awareness is treated as always usable, not time-limited.
