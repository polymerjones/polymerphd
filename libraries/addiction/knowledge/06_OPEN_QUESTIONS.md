# 06 — Open Questions

Claims in this collection that are stated without a named source, hedged by the source itself, or
otherwise worth treating with more caution than the collection's more directly cited material.
Nothing here is resolved — the point is to keep the derived-versus-stated distinction visible
rather than smoothing it away.

---

## Claims stated without a named source

- A prefrontal-cortex mechanism for how repeated in-the-moment self-regulation "rewires" a reactive
  nervous-system pattern is stated as fact but without naming a specific study.
- A critique that SMART goals "don't work" per unspecified research, and a "research waste" claim
  around the SMART framework generally, are both relayed without naming the underlying research.
- Specific procrastination statistics (15-20% of adults chronically procrastinate; a near-universal
  99% admission rate in one survey) are attributed to an unnamed "procrastination researcher" and an
  unnamed survey.
- A claim that a particular thought-technique (visualizations, affirmations, mirror self-talk)
  measurably works better for people who don't need it than for people who do is attributed to
  unnamed research.

## Claims explicitly flagged by the source itself as uncertain or speculative

- One source is explicit that a chain of reasoning connecting lactate to a "precursor to happiness"
  is "not a substantiated theory" — offered as the speaker's own extrapolation, not a settled
  finding, and flagged as such directly in the source material.
- Cross-culturally lowest-rated status of a specific human value, despite the same value's
  structural necessity in some social roles, is presented descriptively without methodological
  detail on how the ranking was established.

## Places where this collection's coverage is thin

- No source in this collection addresses diagnosable, clinical substance-use disorder directly —
  every treatment of "addiction" here is either about a specific non-substance behavior (phones,
  screens, status-chasing) or uses addiction/substance-dependence as an illustrative comparison
  rather than its actual subject. A user asking about clinical addiction treatment, withdrawal
  management, or diagnosis would not be well served by this collection alone.
- Commercial interest is disclosed directly by more than one source (a paid certification, program,
  book, or community tied to the framework being taught) — worth surfacing when relaying a claim
  from a source that also monetizes the framework it's describing, consistent with how each
  individual note already flags this where it's stated.
