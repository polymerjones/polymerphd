# Plain-English Glossary

Terms introduced across this library's notes, defined in plain English. Alphabetical. No citations
here — see the individual note for the source of each term.

**Anticipation of reward** — what dopamine actually signals: not pleasure itself, but the
expectation of a reward, which is what drives seeking and checking behavior.

**Arrival fallacy** — the mistaken expectation that reaching a goal will itself produce lasting
satisfaction, rather than the pursuit of it.

**Aversion triggers** — six qualities (boring, frustrating, unpleasant, unstructured, far-off,
meaningless) that make a task or goal feel resistant to start.

**The backwards law** — chasing a positive experience amplifies its negative opposite, and
accepting a negative experience produces a positive one.

**Default intention / deliberate intention** — habitual, conditioned action versus consciously
chosen action.

**Disevolution** — the mismatch between cravings evolved under historical scarcity and a modern
environment engineered for abundance.

**Dorsal anterior cingulate cortex** — a brain region tied to social-exclusion pain and grief.

**Flow state** — full, pleasurable immersion in a task that's challenging enough to hold attention
without being so hard it becomes aversive.

**Goal inventory** — a regularly reviewed list of every goal a person is currently pursuing.

**"Going to see God"** — the rare moments of maximal, all-out effort a sustained practice
occasionally calls for, distinct from routine training intensity.

**Golden rule of habit change** — a habit must be changed (new routine, same cue and reward) rather
than extinguished, since its neural connection can't be erased.

**Grandiose narcissism / vulnerable narcissism** — two opposite-seeming self-narratives (superior
vs. uniquely victimized) that produce the identical demand for special treatment.

**Habit loop** — the three-part cue/routine/reward structure the brain uses to automate behavior.

**Happiness industrial complex** — cultural pressure to treat happiness itself, rather than meaning
or satisfaction, as life's central goal.

**Hedonia / eudaimonia** — two forms of happiness: hedonia is pleasure, comfort, and short-term
satisfaction; eudaimonia is deeper, purpose-driven life satisfaction.

**The intention stack** — a five-layer model connecting values, priorities, goals, plans, and
present intentions, used to explain why a goal pursued in isolation often doesn't stick.

**Keystone habit** — a single habit whose change triggers unrelated cascading changes elsewhere in
a person's life, via an update to self-image.

**Manson's Law of Avoidance** — the more something threatens a person's identity-certainty, the
more it gets avoided, regardless of whether the thing itself is good or bad.

**Monkey mind** — a restless, constantly task-switching mental state, unable to settle on one
thing — the opposite of flow.

**"Newton's three laws of emotion"** — an allegory (not a scientific law) describing proportional
emotional reaction to identity-relevant events, cumulative identity formation from a life's
emotional experiences, and identity's inertia against change.

**"Psychological vegetables"** — unglamorous but functionally necessary truths, contrasted with the
"sugar and desserts" of chasing constant validation and positive feeling.

**Reparenting** — consciously stepping in as a compassionate, regulating adult presence for
oneself in the present moment, distinct from simply coping with discomfort.

**Schwartz's 12 fundamental human values** — self-direction, stimulation, pleasure, achievement,
power, face, security, tradition, conformity, humility, universalism, and benevolence, held by
everyone in different proportions.

**Sepia-toned goal / value** — something that looks appealing as an idea but proves disliked or
unlivable once actually pursued day to day.

**"Shitty flow"** — an immersive, flow-like state that is values-discordant, identifiable by
post-experience regret rather than fulfillment.

**Small daily promise** — a practice of committing to the smallest possible version of an intended
change, expecting discomfort, and continuing without self-shame after a lapse.

**Zombie burnout** — burnout arising from insufficient meaningful engagement rather than from
overwork.
