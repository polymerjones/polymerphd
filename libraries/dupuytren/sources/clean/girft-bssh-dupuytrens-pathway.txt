[p.1] This source is a single-page clinical care pathway flowchart, "Dupuytren's disease — Hand surgery,
December 2023," co-branded GIRFT (Getting It Right First Time) and BSSH (British Society for Surgery of the
Hand), with BAPRAS and the British Orthopaedic Association also credited. It has five stages, left to right:
Presentation, Referral, Surgical review, Operative procedure, Recovery and rehabilitation. What follows
transcribes the flowchart's boxes and decision branches in their actual connected order, not the order text
extraction happened to produce.

STAGE 1 — PRESENTATION

Entry point: "Presentation in Primary Care." Next box: "Take history and perform clinical examination (see
note 1)."

Note 1 — Clinical history and examination (footnote, applies to this box):
History: hand dominance, patient's occupation, interference with normal daily activities/occupation/
recreational activities, location/duration/history of symptoms in hand or fingers, trauma, previous hand
surgery, recurrence, co-morbidities including diabetes/smoking, family history of Dupuytren's contracture.
"Ring finger most commonly affected followed by little and middle fingers."
Examination: loss of hand function; skin thickening or pitting in the palm; firm fixed (to skin/deep fascia)
nodules; fibrous tendon-like cords; flexion deformity of the MCPJs and IPJs; reduction of the span between
thumb and index finger webspace; unable to lay palm and fingers flat on table top. Ectopic lesions: nodules
overlying dorsal PIPJs, soles of feet, or penile fibromatosis.
Differential diagnosis to consider: callus, ganglion cyst, giant cell tumour, inclusion cyst, ulnar nerve
palsy, trigger finger, post trauma/infection, joint disease, diabetic cheiroarthropathy, camptodactyly,
Volkmann's contracture, epithelioid sarcoma (rare).

From "Take history and perform clinical examination," the pathway branches on a decision diamond, "Threshold
for surgery":
- "Flexion contracture of 30° or more at the MCPJ or 20° at the PIPJ."
- "Thumb contracture impairing function."
- "Previously failed non-operative management."
- "Rapidly progressive disease."

If the threshold for surgery is NOT met (No): the pathway goes to a second decision diamond — "Diagnostic
uncertainty?" / "Intolerably painful nodules and not trained to give cortisone injections?"
  - If Yes to that: "If available, referral to community MSK service for confirmation of diagnosis +/-
    steroid injection," which then rejoins the Referral-stage assessment box.
  - If No: "Inject intolerably painful nodules with cortisone," then "Provision of BSSH approved patient
    information and Primary Care contact details," then "Reassure tender nodules likely to become less
    tender over time" — an exit point (no surgical referral).
  There is also a separate exit from the "Threshold for surgery: No" branch to "Consider referral for
  radiotherapy as part of local audit or research if available."

If the threshold for surgery IS met (Yes): the pathway proceeds directly into Stage 2, "Hand surgery referral
with full patient history."

STAGE 2 — REFERRAL

Box: "Hand surgery referral with full patient history," followed by "Provision of BSSH patient information"
and a "Decision support tool" link.
Decision diamond: "Any of: Contracture meets threshold for surgery. Persistent painful nodules."
  - Yes leads into Stage 3.
  - No loops back to the community-MSK-service referral box described above.

STAGE 3 — SURGICAL REVIEW

Box: "Assessment in clinic by/supervised by surgeon with appropriate training in Hand Surgery. Confirm
diagnosis and that surgery is appropriate. Informed Consent process."
Then: "Choice of procedure based on surgeon preference / patient factors / disease characteristics," which
splits into two parallel paths:
  - "Perform or list for percutaneous fasciotomy in OPD."
  - "List for daycase surgery. Complete locally agreed pre-operative assessment processes."
Both paths converge into Stage 4.

STAGE 4 — OPERATIVE PROCEDURE

"Admission on day of surgery" → "Reaffirmation of consent process" → "Surgery by or supervised by surgeon
with appropriate training in Hand Surgery, sterile operating or approved procedures room, with field
sterility." → "Advice given to patient: driving, analgesia, managing ADLs, range of movement exercises. Hand
therapy referral as per locally agreed protocols."

STAGE 5 — RECOVERY AND REHABILITATION

"Nurse led discharge: As per local protocols, analgesia, elevation, follow-up arrangements" leads to a
decision diamond that is not spelled out with a label beyond Yes/No branches:
  - No → "Surgical review" (loops back into surgical review).
  - Yes → "Provide patient with written information. Discharge from inpatient services."
Then, "At 3–5 days post surgery": "Consider reducing/changing dressings, wound check, range of motion
exercises and night splint."
Then, "At 10–14 days post surgery": "Suture removal if required in Treatment room, Community Services or
Primary Care as per local arrangements." Also: "Ongoing hand therapy/splint provision/scar management as
required. Consider one virtual or face to face review with surgeon or Extended Scope Practitioner (locally
agreed)."
Final box: "Discharge with Patient Initiated Follow-up (PIFU)."
