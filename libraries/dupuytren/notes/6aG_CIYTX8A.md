# Dr. David O'Gorman "The Extracellular Matrix in Dupuytren's Disease" 2015 Dupuytren Symposium

## Central claim

**The extracellular matrix isn't a passive scaffold in Dupuytren's — it's a potent, independent regulator of
disease gene expression, driven substantially by its own physical stiffness, not just its chemical
signals — and this has a direct methodological consequence: cells cultured on standard plastic dishes may
give misleading data because plastic is a billion times stiffer than the actual tissue.** O'Gorman states his
central finding directly: **"the ECM regulates gene expression in Dupuytren's disease — they're clearly
regulated by stiffness and the type of culture matrix, [and] even a modest change can enhance gene
expression levels"** `[18:21]`.

## What's actually in the matrix

`[02:01]`–`[04:02]` — Beyond the well-known type I and III collagens, Dupuytren's cord tissue contains **at
least 10 additional collagen types**, fibronectin (including high levels of **oncofetal fibronectin**),
heat shock proteins, proteoglycans and glycosaminoglycans, multiple growth factors (TGF-beta, IGF-2, and
others), and a full complement of proteases and their inhibitors (MMPs, ADAMTS-family enzymes, TIMPs).
**Matricellular molecules** (periostin, tenascin, the CCN/WISP family) are singled out as a distinct,
important category: proteins that **only function when associated with the matrix**, not structural
components themselves, but **"molecular bridges"** mediating signals between the matrix and the cells living
in it.

## The positive feedback loop, and why the matrix isn't passive

`[01:00]`–`[02:01]` — Myofibroblasts secrete the matrix; the matrix, in turn, **promotes further
myofibroblast formation** — a self-sustaining loop O'Gorman identifies as the basis of fibrotic conditions
generally. His central thesis: **"what it is not is a passive bystander in the pathogenesis of Dupuytren's
disease — the matrix is actually a major driver of disease progression, in my opinion,"** and, importantly,
**the Dupuytren's-affected ECM itself "is certainly not normal"** — not just abnormal cells sitting in
ordinary matrix.

## Beta-catenin: an independent discovery later corroborated by genetics

`[06:05]`–`[08:07]` — O'Gorman's group was **the first to identify beta-catenin as upregulated in
Dupuytren's disease** — a finding made years before genome-wide association studies (see `-3a_yiOu_3I` and
`-0JtjUrrU4o` elsewhere in this library) independently identified **SNPs in Wnt signaling genes** as disease-
associated from the opposite direction (genetics first, mechanism later), converging on the same pathway.
Mechanistically: beta-catenin is normally kept at low levels through **continuous degradation**; when a
signaling event interrupts that degradation — **canonical Wnt signaling, or, separately, TGF-beta acting
through an adjunct pathway** — beta-catenin accumulates in the cytoplasm, translocates to the nucleus, and
**transcriptionally regulates thousands of genes** (his group's ongoing ChIP-sequencing work is actively
cataloguing them). The same pathway is implicated in colon tumors, aggressive fibromatosis, and hypertrophic
scarring. Immunohistochemistry directly confirmed the pattern in patient tissue: **marked beta-catenin
upregulation in contracted tissue, compared to visually unaffected palmar fascia taken from the same patient
only about a centimeter away.**

## A surprising negative result, and its resolution

`[09:09]`–`[11:10]` — Attempting to replicate the tissue-level beta-catenin difference in **cultured cells**
(comparing Dupuytren's-derived "DD" cells, matched normal palmar fascia "PF" cells from the same patients,
and true normal controls from carpal tunnel patients) produced **no difference at all** — beta-catenin,
fibronectin, and HSP47 levels came out essentially the same across all three cell types once cultured. The
group's diagnosis: **culture substrate stiffness itself was the confound.** Using Young's modulus as the
comparison: healing-wound provisional matrix measures under **1,000 Pascals**; Dupuytren's cord tissue
measures around **50,000 Pascals**; a standard tissue-culture plastic dish measures **around 3 billion
Pascals** — a difference of many orders of magnitude from either. For cells this biomechanically sensitive,
O'Gorman argues, **standard plastic-dish culture is simply not physiologically informative.**

## Growing cells on physiologically relevant collagen instead

`[11:10]`–`[15:13]` — Switching to **soft type I collagen matrices** (still allowing normal proliferation and
adhesion), the group used the **fibroblast-populated collagen lattice (FPCL)** contraction assay — confirming
DD cells' enhanced contractility relative to controls, consistent with prior published work. The key
experiment: **beta-catenin levels transiently spiked and then dropped within six hours when cells were kept
under mechanical stress** in the lattice; but when cells were cultured on **soft, unstressed collagen** for
72 hours, **beta-catenin levels dropped significantly** (confirmed by both densitometry and
immunofluorescence) — in cells that had shown clearly elevated beta-catenin in the original diseased tissue.
**Conclusion: matrix mechanics — not biochemistry alone — regulates beta-catenin levels in Dupuytren's
cells.**

## Mechanical stretch produces dramatic gene expression changes

`[15:13]`–`[18:21]` — Using a "tissue train" system — three-dimensional collagen constructs mounted on
nylon anchors, subjected to vacuum-driven cyclic or static stretch to approximate physiological loading —
the group tested a panel of genes including alpha-SMA, periostin, IGF-2, **WISP1** (a beta-catenin-driven
CCN-family matricellular gene), and **NRG1** (described as a **newly discovered beta-catenin-driven gene in
Dupuytren's**, identified through their own ChIP work). Result: imposing **just a 1% increase in stretch per
day, cumulating to only 14% over seven days**, produced **2- to 8-fold upregulation** in expression of these
genes — an extraordinary degree of mechanosensitivity from a genuinely modest mechanical stimulus.

## Therapeutic implications

`[18:21]`–`[20:22]` — O'Gorman reframes an already-approved treatment in matrix-centric terms: **collagenase
is, functionally, an existing ECM-targeting therapy** — it removes type I and III collagen, major matrix
components. His proposed next step: **combine matrix-targeting treatment (collagenase) with
myofibroblast-targeting therapy simultaneously** — hitting both the cell producing the matrix and the matrix
itself — as the most promising direction for reducing progression and recurrence, rather than treating either
target alone.

## Symptoms and body signals addressed

None directly — this is molecular and biomechanical laboratory science, not a discussion of patient-facing

## Glossary terms introduced

- **Matricellular molecules** — proteins (periostin, tenascin, the CCN/WISP family) that function only in
  association with the matrix, mediating signals between matrix and cells without being structural
  components themselves `[04:02]`.
- **Beta-catenin / canonical Wnt signaling** — the pathway O'Gorman's group first identified as upregulated
  in Dupuytren's, later independently corroborated by GWAS findings in Wnt-pathway genes `[06:05]`.
- **Young's modulus** — the stiffness measure used to quantify the enormous mismatch between physiological
  tissue stiffness and standard tissue-culture plastic `[10:09]`.
- **WISP1 and NRG1** — specific beta-catenin-driven genes identified as responsive to mechanical stretch in
  this study `[16:16]`.

## Analogies worth reusing

## Source-stated confidence

O'Gorman is explicit about crediting his students and collaborating surgeons for generating the data
presented, rather than claiming personal authorship. He states the negative culture-replication finding
plainly rather than omitting it, and frames the methodological conclusion — that plastic-dish culture may be
producing uninterpretable data — as his own considered opinion (**"in my personal opinion"**) while still
grounding it in a specific, quantified stiffness comparison. He also flags his beta-catenin-driving-disease-
progression claim as **"somewhat difficult to prove conclusively"** even while stating it as his working
position.
