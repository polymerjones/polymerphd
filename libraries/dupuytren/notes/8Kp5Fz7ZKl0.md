# Christina Raykha MSc Candidate, "IGFBP6 as a Potential Mediator of Myofibroblast Differentiation" 2010 Miami Dupuytren Symposium

## Central claim

**TGF-beta appears to drive Dupuytren's contraction partly by suppressing IGFBP6, a protein that normally
keeps IGF2 in check — and directly testing both molecules confirms IGFBP6 inhibits contraction while IGF2
promotes it, making IGFBP6 a candidate non-surgical therapeutic target.** Raykha's own summary: **"TGF-beta
is repressing IGFBP6 transcription and protein secretion... and IGFBP6 is inhibiting TGF-beta-induced
contraction of [diseased] and [normal] cells, while IGF2 is inducing cell contraction"** `[08:17]`.

## What IGFBP6 is

`[01:07]`–`[02:10]` — Insulin-like growth factor binding protein 6 is a **secreted glycoprotein associated
with the ECM**, whose main function is binding IGFs to regulate their availability to receptors — notably
**binding IGF2 with 20–100 times more affinity than IGF1**, making it a specific IGF2 regulator within the
larger IGF family (two ligands, IGF1 and IGF2; six binding proteins, IGFBP1–6; two receptors, with the type 1
receptor doing the main signaling driving proliferation and differentiation).

## The finding: downregulated, and worsened by TGF-beta

`[02:10]`–`[04:13]` — Microarray analysis (echoed across multiple labs represented at this symposium)
identified **IGFBP6 as significantly downregulated in Dupuytren's cord tissue compared to patient-matched
control tissue** — in direct contrast to periostin, described as markedly *upregulated* in the same
comparison. This was confirmed at the mRNA level by PCR (lower IGFBP6 mRNA in diseased "DD" cells versus
matched normal palmar fascia "PF" cells), and **treating cells with TGF-beta produced further suppression of
IGFBP6 mRNA** beyond the baseline disease-associated reduction. The same pattern held at the **protein
level**: IGFBP6 secretion into conditioned media was lower from DD cells than PF cells, and **TGF-beta
treatment further repressed IGFBP6 secretion** in both cell types.

## The functional test: IGFBP6 blocks contraction, IGF2 drives it

`[05:14]`–`[08:17]` — Using dose-response testing in stressed fibroblast-populated collagen lattices (FPCLs):
adding **IGFBP6 at physiologically relevant concentrations (200–400 ng/mL)** produced a **dose-dependent
reduction in TGF-beta-induced contraction**, with **400 ng/mL producing a much more pronounced reduction in
DD cells than in PF cells.** Conversely, treating cells with **IGF2 (100 ng/mL)** produced **clear induction
of contraction** in both cell types, again **more exaggerated in DD cells.** Together, this confirms the
proposed mechanism directly: **IGFBP6 sequesters IGF2 away from its receptor, and less IGFBP6 means more
IGF2 signaling reaches the cell — driving the myofibroblast contraction and differentiation characteristic
of the disease.** This combinatorial TGF-beta/IGF2 signaling toward myofibroblast differentiation and
collagen production is noted as previously demonstrated in a different (non-Dupuytren's) experimental system.

## Glossary terms introduced

- **IGFBP6 (insulin-like growth factor binding protein 6)** — a secreted glycoprotein specifically
  sequestering IGF2, found downregulated in diseased tissue and further suppressed by TGF-beta `[01:07]`.
- **Combinatorial TGF-beta/IGF2 signaling** — the proposed joint pathway by which reduced IGFBP6 (via TGF-
  beta repression) increases IGF2 bioavailability, together driving myofibroblast differentiation `[04:13]`.

## Analogies worth reusing

None stated — the talk presents molecular mechanism and dose-response data directly.

## Source-stated confidence

Raykha explicitly frames herself as a student presenter without financial disclosures to make, and is
careful to distinguish confirmed findings (the microarray, PCR, and protein-secretion results; the direct
dose-response contraction data) from stated future work (effects on apoptosis, proliferation, and collagen
production; identifying the specific signaling pathway responsible for TGF-beta's repression of IGFBP6
transcription) — the mechanism proposed here is a working hypothesis supported by the data shown, not
presented as a fully resolved pathway.

## Conflicts with other sources

**Directly complements `aL-aMj2B1hY`** (O'Gorman's Basic Research talk, Raykha's own supervisor, delivered
five years later) — that talk separately reports **loss of IGF2 genomic imprinting** in Dupuytren's tissue,
causing IGF2 to be transcribed from both parental alleles instead of one. Read together, these are **two
independent mechanisms converging on the same outcome**: increased IGF2 bioavailability and signaling in
diseased tissue — one epigenetic (imprinting loss, increasing IGF2 production), the other post-translational
(reduced IGFBP6, increasing the fraction of IGF2 free to signal). **Directly relevant to `6aG_CIYTX8A`**
(O'Gorman's ECM talk) — periostin's upregulation, mentioned here only in passing as a contrast to IGFBP6's
downregulation, is treated there as a matricellular signaling molecule in its own right. **Extends
`dsPwSUDrz1Y`** (Phan's mechanism talk) and **`UB71XI_3fxA`** (Morris's MMP/TIMP talk) — all describe
TGF-beta as a central upstream driver of Dupuytren's biology; this talk adds a specific downstream mechanism
(IGFBP6 repression, IGF2 release) through which TGF-beta's effects on contraction may be partly mediated.
