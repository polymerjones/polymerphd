# Dr. Ruud Selles: "Consensus Definition of Recurrence" 2015 Dupuytren Symposium

## Central claim

**A single dataset's reported recurrence rate can be tuned to almost anything — from 2% to 86% — simply by
choosing a different definition, and Selles's group ran a formal international consensus process
specifically to close that gap with one standardized definition.** He states the demonstration directly:
citing his own group's 2013 literature review, **"you can find more than forty different definitions of what
a recurrence [is]... and if you apply these different definitions to your single dataset, you can basically
tweak your reported recurrence rate anywhere between... two and eighty-six percent, just by picking a
different definition"** `[01:01]`.

## The Delphi process

`[02:03]`–`[03:05]` — A **Delphi study**: a structured, anonymous, multi-round method for building expert
consensus, where feedback from each round is fed back to the group before the next round. Experts were
selected based on research contribution to the Dupuytren's field (mostly senior or first authors of relevant
publications); **43 were invited, 21 agreed to participate**. **Five rounds** were needed to reach final
consensus, with the fifth round being formal approval of the constructed definition.

## Four dimensions debated, and where consensus landed

`[03:05]`–`[04:05]`:

1. **Scope**: should recurrence assessment cover all affected joints, or a statistically convenient subset
   (as some trials, including the presenters' own, had previously done by looking at only one digit)?
   **Consensus: all treated joints should be evaluated.**
2. **What counts as recurrence — nodules/cords, or angular measurement?** Described as **"the biggest
   discussion point."** **Consensus: an angular contracture measure only** — nodules and cords were
   explicitly **excluded** from the definition, because (as discussed) their presence alone isn't itself an
   indication for surgery, many minimally invasive techniques don't remove them regardless of outcome, and
   **no generally accepted measurement standard yet exists** for grading nodules or cords consistently.
3. **Threshold**: **20 degrees** of increased contracture was set as the binary yes/no recurrence cutoff.
4. **Timing/baseline**: consensus rejected using a **perioperative baseline** (used in some prior studies)
   in favor of **six weeks post-treatment** as the reference point, compared against **one-year
   follow-up.** Patient-reported recurrence and percentage-based recurrence measures were explicitly
   **not** included in the final definition.

## The resulting consensus definition

`[04:05]`–`[05:07]` — **"Recurrence is any increase in joint contracture, in any treated joint, of at least
20 degrees at one year post-treatment compared to six weeks post-treatment."** The expert panel additionally
recommended **repeating measurement over time** (since how recurrence evolves across different treatments
and time periods isn't yet well understood) and **reporting recurrence separately by joint type** (e.g. MCP
versus PIP) in addition to a combined figure.

## Applying it in practice: a worked example

`[05:07]`–`[06:08]` — Using a hypothetical patient with MCP joints 4 and 5 affected in one hand: contracture
is measured pre-treatment, at 6 weeks, and at 1 year; any joint crossing the 20-degree threshold between
those two later time points counts as a recurrence. Aggregated across a group of patients and joints (the
example uses 14 total treated joints), the recurrence rate is simply the proportion crossing that threshold
— **5 of 14 in the worked example, or 36%.**

## Why nodules and cords were excluded — the strongest pushback

`[06:08]` — Selles notes some experts specifically opposed including nodules and cords, and states the
reasoning plainly: **their mere presence isn't an indication for surgery on its own**, many treatments
(minimally invasive ones especially) leave residual nodules/cords without necessarily representing treatment
failure, and — pointedly — **there isn't yet a generally accepted way to measure them consistently**, even
though other talks at the same symposium have proposed specific grading approaches.

## Glossary terms introduced

- **Delphi consensus method** — the structured, anonymous, multi-round expert-agreement process used to
  construct this definition `[02:03]`.
- **Consensus recurrence definition** — the specific resulting standard: a 20-degree contracture increase in
  any treated joint, measured at one year against a six-week baseline `[05:07]`.

## Source-stated confidence

Selles is explicit about the limits of even this consensus effort — closing directly with **"it's impossible
to really catch all the finer details of recurrence in one measure"** and framing the proposed definition as
**"the minimal [standard] to report in future studies,"** not a replacement for other outcome measures
entirely. He also flags that a formal disclosure ("consultant... not related to this study") was mentioned up
front, and that the panel's recommendation to track recurrence over time reflects genuine current uncertainty
about how recurrence evolves, rather than a settled understanding.

gap. **Directly relevant to `oRdE0UKeJF0`** (Van Rijssen's randomized PNF-vs-fasciectomy trial), whose own
