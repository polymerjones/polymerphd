# Dr. Charles Eaton "Dupuytren Data Bank (IDDB)" 2015 Dupuytren Symposium

## Central claim

**Dupuytren's needs to be studied like a chronic disease, not a surgical procedure, and the field currently
lacks the basic tool that would let it: a biomarker.** **"Chronic diseases are chronic diseases because
there's not an effective treatment, and that's often because the core biology is not understood... we don't
have a unique biomarker — we don't have a way to definitively tell, by something other than clinical
examination, whether a person has or does not have Dupuytren's disease"** `[00:00]`–`[01:00]`. The
International Dupuytren Data Bank (IDDB) is presented as the project built to fix that gap.

## Two subsets, one hypothesis

`[03:03]`–`[04:03]` — Eaton proposes, without yet having a biomarker to confirm it, that clinically similar
early Dupuytren's changes may split into two biologically distinct groups: **"the genetic version of
Dupuytren's disease tends to affect younger people, tends to have associated conditions because it's a
systemic disorder — people get frozen shoulder, Ledderhose, Peyronie's disease — and tends to be more
aggressive and treatment-resistant,"** versus an **"exposure factors, epigenetic changes... old age, trauma,
diabetes, tobacco, medications"** version that tends to be more benign and often **never needs treatment at
all** — a point he underscores with a specific stated figure: **"25% of white men over the age of 70 have
Dupuytren's disease. Well, what Dupuytren's disease do they have? We need to work this out."**

## What his own 3,400-patient intake data shows

`[05:05]`–`[09:13]` — Eaton describes independently recording a single question — **"how much does
Dupuytren's interfere with the use of your hand?"** — on new-patient intake during his percutaneous
fasciotomy practice, before he had heard of the formal Patient Evaluation Measure. From roughly 3,400
searchable intakes, several correlations with more interference emerged:

- **Age of onset under 50** `[06:08]`.
- **A family history of Dupuytren's** `[07:10]`.
- **Concurrent Ledderhose disease** — which he separately notes correlates with early age of onset,
  **"which clearly suggests to me that there's a biologic difference in people who have early age onset
  that tend to have these systemic associated diseases"** `[07:10]`.
- **Reported pain** — present in **20–35% of people presenting for treatment**, and itself correlated with
  earlier age of onset `[07:10]`–`[08:11]`.
- **Reported itching** in nodular disease — also correlated with age of onset, which Eaton connects to a
  colleague's point about immune cells: **"as Jagdeep has said, there is a relationship of immune cells, in
  addition to myofibroblasts, and itching is an immunologically related thing"** `[08:11]`.

He frames the cleanest split as: **people with both a late age of onset and no itching report the least
interference; everyone with either itching or an early age of onset reports more** — proposed as a possible
marker of two biologically distinct groups `[09:13]`.

## A possible disease-modifying signal from statins

`[09:13]`–`[11:14]` — Thirty percent of his Dupuytren's patients were on statins, and **patients not on
statins reported more hand interference than those on statins**. Eaton flags the confound directly rather
than overselling it: **"it's not a simple question, though, because statins have an anti-inflammatory
component to them, and it might be that hyperlipidemia is one of those factors that is kind of an exposure
factor that predisposes to more benign Dupuytren's disease."** To control for age (younger patients are less
likely to be on statins regardless of disease biology), he restricted the comparison to patients already
returning for a second treatment — **"a fairly more clear spread"** persisted in that subgroup, which he
says **"points to possibly disease-modifying therapy."** Calcium channel blockers showed a smaller trend in
the same direction (small numbers); glucosamine — despite "internet buzz" linking it to Dupuytren's — showed
no trend in his data; aspirin showed a possible helpful trend, "but that may be for reasons other than
Dupuytren's biology."

## The IDDB's design

`[12:16]`–`[17:21]` — Three design choices, each stated with its rationale:

1. **Direct-to-patient online enrollment**, built as an added module on the National Data Bank for Rheumatic
   Diseases' existing 18-year, ~20,000-patient infrastructure — **"by going direct to patients we can dodge
   the whole issue of having a physician-practice-based registry... at big cost savings"** and can capture
   people who never see a hand surgeon at all `[12:16]`–`[13:16]`.
2. **A validated photo-based self-diagnosis tool** (attributed to a researcher whose name the auto-captions
   render as "Pervalesco," not confidently recoverable), reported at 95% specificity, paired with a
   photo-matching contracture-pattern selector following "Jo Dias's work with the BSSH" — chosen explicitly
   to avoid **"trying to get goniometric measurements at a distance, which is just a very difficult
   problem"** `[12:16]`, `[13:16]`.
3. **The Patient Evaluation Measure**, incorporated for longitudinal tracking of any treatment type,
   **"not... limited to any one treatment pattern"** `[14:18]`.

Recruitment target: **10,000 enrolled patients plus 1,000 disease-free controls**, drawing on the
foundation's existing ~6,000-email mailing list, clinic waiting-room brochures, and wallet cards directing
patients to dupestudy.com `[16:20]`–`[17:21]`.

## Symptoms and body signals addressed

- **Hand interference correlating with early age of onset, family history, concurrent Ledderhose disease,
  pain, and itching** — proposed markers of a more aggressive, systemic disease subset `[06:08]`–`[09:13]`.
- **Pain in Dupuytren's disease, reported by 20–35% of patients presenting for treatment** `[08:11]`.
- **Itching in nodular Dupuytren's disease** — linked to immune-cell activity alongside myofibroblasts
  `[08:11]`.

## Glossary terms introduced

- **International Dupuytren Data Bank (IDDB)** — the Dupuytren Foundation's longitudinal, direct-to-patient
  research registry `[00:00]`.
- **Patient Evaluation Measure (PEM)** — the questionnaire used to track hand interference and treatment
  outcomes over time `[05:05]`.
- **Aggressive/systemic subset vs. exposure/benign subset** — Eaton's working hypothesis for splitting
  clinically similar presentations by underlying biology `[03:03]`.

## Analogies worth reusing

None stated — this talk presents intake data and study design directly rather than through illustrative
comparison.

## Source-stated confidence

Eaton is explicit throughout about what is established versus what is a working hypothesis awaiting a
biomarker: the genetic/exposure subset split is stated as unconfirmed (**"since we don't have a
biomarker"** `[03:03]`), the statin finding is qualified with its own confound before being called
suggestive rather than conclusive `[10:13]`, and the calcium-channel-blocker trend is flagged as
underpowered ("the numbers are smaller on this... needs to be looked at further") `[11:14]`. The self-report
data itself comes from a single surgeon's practice, not the multi-center registry being proposed.
