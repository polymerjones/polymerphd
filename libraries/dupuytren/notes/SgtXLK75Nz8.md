# Dr. Charles Eaton: "2017 Dupuytren Awareness" Campaign Video

## Central claim

**Eaton argues that lack of public and government awareness — not lack of scientific effort — is the primary
obstacle blocking Dupuytren's disease research, and asks viewers for five minutes of advocacy effort rather
than money, specifically to help secure a state (and eventually national) Dupuytren Disease Awareness Day
that could open the door to federal research funding.** He states this directly: **"our team is working hard,
but we're limited by a major obstacle, which is lack of awareness... this is why right now there's no federal
funding assigned specifically for Dupuytren's disease research"** `[00:00]`.

## The problem, stated plainly

`[00:00]`–`[01:01]` — Eaton, executive director of the Dupuytren Research Group, describes the disease as a
gradual tightening of the palm's tissues affecting millions in the US and many more worldwide, capable of
causing **crippling hand deformities** that severely limit daily living. He states plainly: **there is
currently no cure and no medicine that can stop it**; surgery treats contractures, but for many patients the
disease **returns over time**, and severe disease **cannot always be controlled surgically**, potentially
leading to **loss of hand use.** He frames the research group's central obstacle not as a scientific one but
an awareness one: **most people — including government officials — have never heard of Dupuytren's disease**,
directly explaining the **absence of dedicated federal research funding.**

## The campaign strategy

`[01:01]`–`[02:03]` — Eaton describes his own template action: as a Florida resident, he contacted his state's
members of Congress, who agreed to submit a resolution designating **April 1st as Dupuytren Disease Awareness
Day in Florida** — noting the disease **"crosses all party lines,"** making it an easy, non-partisan ask. He
lays out a simple process for viewers to replicate this in their own states: visit a website (given in the
video as **"KnowDUP.com"**), which provides **an automatic email link to Congress members in the viewer's own
state**, plus a **template letter** that can be copied directly into that email.

## Reclaiming April Fools' Day

`[02:03]` — Eaton directly addresses the seemingly odd choice of April 1st: **"Dupuytren's disease has fooled
too many people for too long — it's time for us to turn things around."** The stated longer-term goal: if
enough states adopt their own awareness-day resolutions, the campaign can push for a **national** Dupuytren
Disease Awareness Day, which Eaton frames as a concrete step toward unlocking government research funding.

## Symptoms and body signals addressed

- **Crippling hand deformities severely limiting normal daily living**, and eventual **loss of hand use** in
  severe, surgically uncontrollable cases — named as the disease's most serious downstream consequence
  `[00:00]`.

## Source-stated confidence

This is an advocacy and public-awareness video, not a research presentation — its claims about disease
prevalence and severity are stated as general public-facing background rather than data with individual
citations, consistent with its purpose of mobilizing grassroots political action. Eaton is explicit and
transparent about the campaign's actual mechanism (a state legislative resolution, not a binding federal
action) and about what he is and isn't asking viewers for (time, not money).
