# Dr. Paul Werker: "Debate: PNF is Best" 2015 Dupuytren Symposium

## Central claim

**Arguing PNF is the best Dupuytren's treatment in a formal debate, Werker opens by suggesting collagenase's
appeal partly reflects novelty and marketing rather than demonstrated superiority, then shows that once
recurrence is measured using the same definition as the collagenase literature, PNF and collagenase produce
strikingly similar recurrence rates at both the MCP and PIP joint.** He states the punchline of his comparison
directly: **"MCP joint recurrence rate at three years, twenty percent for PNF, twenty-seven percent for
collagenase... and for the PIP, sixty-five percent for PNF, fifty-six percent for collagenase — very similar,
isn't it?"** `[03:08]`.

## A playful opening argument

`[00:01]`–`[01:03]` — Werker opens with an extended, humorous character sketch of a colleague ("David") known
for embracing thrill-seeking hobbies — a sports car, cycling, skiing, dirt-bike riding, RAF flight training,
and a charity ride raising £7,000 through South Africa. His conclusion: **"David loves his toys, he seeks
thrills — and that is, of course, the reason why David embraces collagenase."** He grants collagenase's real
appeal honestly: **minimally invasive, "miraculous, maybe even sexy," beautifully developed, strongly
investigated, and superbly marketed** — but immediately lists its downsides: **expensive, priced differently
across currencies/markets, PIP contractures harder to treat, and real adverse events.** He shows a case from
his own practice — an unusual complication following collagenase injection in one finger that he states he'd
**"never heard of before,"** suggesting it might be worth writing up as a case report. He also cites a 2010
statement from Thomas [surname unclear in the transcript], **himself a firm believer in collagenase**, who
had stated the comparative advantages of collagenase over needle aponeurotomy were **"yet to be fully
elucidated"** — a position Werker says he fully agrees with.

## PNF's stated case

`[01:03]`–`[02:04]` — Werker's key points for PNF: it is the **least invasive** treatment modality; he is
**convinced it is highly effective**, with effectiveness **as high as limited fasciectomy** and **similar to
collagenase**; its **complication rate is low**; patients resume most activities **within hours to days**;
**satisfaction is high**; **recurrence rate is relatively high, but retreatment is always possible and often
as effective as the first treatment**; and it is **cheap** compared to both limited fasciectomy and
collagenase.

## Why recurrence definition changes the picture

`[02:04]`–`[03:08]` — Werker's own randomized trial recurrence figures are similar to those separately reported
by **Van Rijssen** (see `oRdE0UKeJF0`): **65% at three years, 85% at five years.** But he flags a critical
methodological caveat: this figure uses a **different recurrence definition** than the "30-degree" definition
referenced by a co-presenter earlier in the session — his own definition is an **increase in total passive
extension deficit compared to earlier results across the whole ray**, not a single joint. To make a genuinely
comparable measurement, **Gary Pess** re-analyzed outcomes using the **same definition used in the collagenase
literature**, finding: **20% MCP recurrence and 65% PIP recurrence in successfully released joints**; for
joints that were **not** successfully released, **25% MCP and 67% PIP at three years.**

## The matched comparison

`[03:08]` — Using Pess's collagenase-matched definition, Werker presents recurrence side-by-side: **MCP joint
at three years — 20% for PNF versus 27% for collagenase; PIP joint — 65% for PNF versus 56% for
collagenase** — numbers he characterizes as **"very similar."**

## Conclusion

`[03:08]`–`[04:10]` — Werker's closing summary: PNF is **quick and cheap**, recovery is **fast**, satisfaction
is **high**, efficacy is **similar to limited fasciectomy and similar to collagenase**, durability is
**comparable to collagenase**, and it **can be repeated** — with a playful promise that the following day's
session would cover exactly how often retreatment can reasonably be performed.

## Analogies worth reusing

- **"David loves his toys, he seeks thrills"** — Werker's extended, deliberately playful analogy suggesting

## Source-stated confidence

Werker is explicit this is a **debate** talk, opening by noting "bashing is allowed" — signaling the framing
is deliberately one-sided rather than a neutral review. He is careful, however, to flag a genuine methodological
point rather than simply asserting PNF's superiority: that recurrence figures are **not directly comparable
across studies unless the same definition is used**, and he credits Gary Pess's re-analysis using a matched
definition as the basis for his closing comparison, rather than presenting his own trial's raw recurrence
figures as if they were already comparable to the collagenase literature.

## Conflicts with other sources

**Directly relevant to `oRdE0UKeJF0`** (Van Rijssen's randomized PNF-vs-fasciectomy trial) — Werker cites this
trial's recurrence figures directly as consistent with his own group's RCT results. **Directly relevant to

know" talk) — this talk is a concrete, worked example of exactly the definition-dependent recurrence-reporting
problem both of those talks address in the abstract; Werker's own resolution (re-analyzing with a matched
definition) is a practical response to Warwick's broader skepticism about cross-treatment comparability.

alongside the three-year figures Werker presents here from Pess's re-analysis.
