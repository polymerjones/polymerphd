# Paul Smith MD, "A Logical Approach to the PIP Joint" 2010 Miami Dupuytren Symposium

## Central claim

**Most PIP joint surgery in Dupuytren's is practiced like Bertrand Russell's unfalsifiable teapot rather
than like real science — and Smith proposes a stepwise algorithm instead, where each step has an
observable, testable consequence before moving to the next.** His framing: **"a lot of the clinical work in
Dupuytren's is up there with the teapots"** rather than being put to observable test like the search for the
Higgs boson `[01:05]`–`[02:06]`. His single most counterintuitive finding: **"if you start at the top with
manipulation alone... [that means] taking the most severe patients and getting the best results — and the
more aggressive we became, the less successful and effective we were"** `[11:27]`.

## The algorithm, step by step

`[03:07]`–`[10:24]` — Presented as a strict sequence, each step attempted only if the prior one fails to
achieve full extension:

1. **Digital fasciectomy** — limited fasciectomy via linear incision converted to multiple Z-plasties,
   combined with open palm technique; care taken around the **spiral cord's displacement of the nerve**;
   **lateral bands are freed from adhesions** (always present, both to the dorsum of the proximal phalanx
   and to dorsal skin) and tested by pulling to see if they extend the joint.
2. **Gentle passive manipulation** — flexing the MP joint, then gently leaning on the PIP joint for about
   two minutes. **Periarticular adhesions reliably respond to this alone.**
3. **Volar plate release** (± tendon sheath) — if a volar contracture remains, released through the
   posterior surface of the volar plate with a Beaver blade, turned 90 degrees, cut to bone; a
   low-exposure technique.
4. **Accessory collateral ligament release** — checked next by testing joint stability: a finger that
   "snaps" into flexion or extension rather than holding position mid-range indicates a tight accessory
   collateral ligament over the condyle, treated by releasing it.
5. **Central slip check and treatment** — via what Smith calls testing for central slip attenuation
   (flexing the wrist and MP joints together and checking for passive PIP extension) — if the digit still
   "lies in flexion" despite being otherwise fully correctable, this is the cause.
6. **Conjoined lateral band check** — full passive DIP flexion is confirmed last, to rule out tightness in
   the conjoined lateral bands (seen as DIP hyperextension), treated by Fowler's procedure or, occasionally,
   gentle manipulation alone.

## Central slip attenuation is far more common than expected

`[07:14]`–`[09:18]` — Across **188 PIP joint contractures** studied: **113 resolved with digital fasciectomy
alone**; **75 required an additional PIP joint procedure**; and of those 75, **54 (72%) had central slip
attenuation.** Severity predicted this strongly: **if the preoperative joint angle exceeded 60 degrees, 80%
of those patients had central slip attenuation.** Smith is direct about the consequence of missing this:
**"if you ignore that, your patients will just immediately go into that position after operation."**
Treatment for an intact central slip allows immediate mobilization and standard night splinting; an
attenuated central slip instead requires **three weeks of static splinting followed by three weeks of
[dynamic/tapered] splinting.**

## What actually got used, and what they abandoned

`[10:24]`–`[11:27]` — Outcome breakdown from their patient series: the **vast majority required no PIP
joint procedure at all**; **8% responded to gentle passive manipulation**; **5% required accessory
collateral ligament release**; **10% required volar plate tenotomy**; **9% required tendon sheath release**;
a small number required joint capsule release. Notably, **3% had extensor tendon repair and 2% had the PIP
joint K-wired** — procedures Smith now explicitly disavows: **"these are things I would never do now,"**
describing that period candidly as **"a reflection of early insanity."** Restated with current practice, the
manipulation-alone group grows to roughly **13%** once the abandoned procedures' former candidates are
redirected to gentler management. Reviewing outcomes by contracture severity confirmed the core finding: **the
gentlest technique (manipulation alone) produced the best results even on the most severe patients**, while
escalating aggressiveness correlated with worse outcomes.

## Symptoms and body signals addressed

- **A PIP joint that fails to fully extend despite apparently complete fasciectomy**, worked through via
  the staged algorithm rather than treated as a single undifferentiated problem `[05:10]`.
- **A "snapping" finger** — instability flicking between flexion and extension rather than holding
  mid-range position — as the specific sign of a tight accessory collateral ligament `[06:13]`.
- **A digit that passively straightens but persistently "lies in flexion"** — the specific sign of central
  slip attenuation, distinguished from other causes of incomplete correction `[07:14]`.
- **DIP joint hyperextension with tight conjoined lateral bands**, limiting full DIP flexion `[09:18]`.

## Glossary terms introduced

- **Central slip attenuation** — stretching/weakening of the central extensor slip from prolonged PIP
  flexion, tested for by flexing the wrist and MP joints and checking passive PIP extension `[07:14]`.
- **Conjoined lateral bands (tightness)** — a cause of DIP extension limitation distinct from the cord
  itself, checked last in the algorithm and treated by Fowler's procedure `[09:18]`.

## Analogies worth reusing

- **Bertrand Russell's orbiting teapot versus the search for the Higgs boson** — an extended, memorable
  framing for the difference between an unfalsifiable claim and a properly testable one, applied directly to
  surgical decision-making `[00:03]`–`[02:06]`.

## Source-stated confidence

Unusually candid about past mistakes: Smith names two abandoned procedures directly (extensor tendon repair,
K-wiring) and calls the period he used them **"early insanity,"** rather than omitting or softening this
history. The central slip attenuation figures (72% of PIP-procedure patients, 80% above 60 degrees) are
presented as measured findings from a defined patient series (188 joints), not estimates.

## Conflicts with other sources

**Directly corroborates and extends `iD3mYMmfN-Q`** (Eaton's anatomy talk), which separately identifies
central slip stretching and lateral band changes as causes of early lost correction — this talk supplies the
specific stepwise clinical algorithm and quantified attenuation rates (72%/80%) behind that anatomical
description. **Directly relevant to `1BURdqs3a4U`** (Pess's technique talk), whose dynamic splinting practice
for "an active extension lag... probably a stretched central slip" matches this talk's specific splinting
protocol (three weeks static, three weeks dynamic) for the same finding. **Complements `L3kjpUIwHIQ`**
(Leclercq's severe PIP contracture talk), which uses a similarly named "Tubiana disease test" for extensor
slackening — likely testing the same underlying central slip problem from a related angle, worth treating as
convergent evidence rather than two separate findings.
