# Dupuytren's Disease: Hand Surgery Pathway

## Central claim

**This is a single-page NHS clinical care pathway, not a narrative source — it specifies exactly which
patients should be referred for surgery, on what criteria, and what happens at every stage from a GP
appointment through to discharge.** `[p.1]`. It is co-branded GIRFT (Getting It Right First Time) and BSSH
(British Society for Surgery of the Hand), dated December 2023, with BAPRAS and the British Orthopaedic
Association also credited.

## The five stages

`[p.1]`

The pathway runs Presentation → Referral → Surgical review → Operative procedure → Recovery and
rehabilitation. **Presentation in primary care leads to a specific history-and-examination protocol** (hand
dominance, occupation, interference with daily activities, duration of symptoms, trauma, previous surgery,
recurrence, co-morbidities including diabetes/smoking, and family history) **and a specific examination**
(loss of hand function; skin thickening or pitting in the palm; firm nodules fixed to skin or deep fascia;
fibrous tendon-like cords; flexion deformity of the MCPJs and IPJs; reduced thumb–index webspace; inability
to lay the palm and fingers flat on a table top; and ectopic lesions — nodules over the dorsal PIPJs, soles
of the feet, or penile tissue).

**The pathway names a specific differential diagnosis list to rule out first**: callus, ganglion cyst, giant
cell tumour, inclusion cyst, ulnar nerve palsy, trigger finger, post-trauma or infection, joint disease,
diabetic cheiroarthropathy, camptodactyly, Volkmann's contracture, and (rarely) epithelioid sarcoma.

## The referral threshold

`[p.1]`

**The pathway sets an explicit numeric bar for surgical referral**: "flexion contracture of 30° or more at
the MCPJ or 20° at the PIPJ," or a thumb contracture that impairs function, or previously failed
non-operative management, or rapidly progressive disease. Below that threshold, the pathway branches
differently depending on whether there is diagnostic uncertainty or intolerably painful nodules the primary
clinician isn't trained to inject — either routed to a community MSK service for confirmation and possible
steroid injection, or (if neither applies) simply reassured that "tender nodules [are] likely to become less
tender over time," with cortisone injection offered for pain and radiotherapy noted only as something to
"consider... as part of local audit or research if available" — not a standard offer.

## Surgical review, procedure, and recovery

`[p.1]`

Once referred, the pathway requires **assessment by, or supervised by, a surgeon with appropriate training
in Hand Surgery**, confirmation of diagnosis, and a formal informed-consent process, before choice of
procedure — **percutaneous fasciotomy in outpatients, or listing for daycase surgery** — based on surgeon
preference, patient factors, and disease characteristics.

Recovery is staged explicitly: **at 3–5 days post-surgery**, dressing changes, wound checks, range-of-motion
exercises and a night splint are considered; **at 10–14 days**, suture removal (if needed) and ongoing hand
therapy, splinting, and scar management, with one review by the surgeon or an Extended Scope Practitioner.
The pathway's endpoint is **"Discharge with Patient Initiated Follow-up (PIFU)"** — the patient, not a
scheduled appointment, triggers any further contact.

## Symptoms and body signals addressed

- **Firm nodules fixed to the skin or deep fascia, with skin thickening or pitting in the palm** `[p.1]`.
- **Fibrous, tendon-like cords, and flexion deformity at the MCPJ and IPJ** `[p.1]`.
- **Reduced span between thumb and index finger webspace; inability to lay the palm flat on a table** `[p.1]`.
- **Ectopic nodules over the dorsal PIPJs, soles of the feet, or penile tissue** `[p.1]`.
- **Persistent painful nodules**, distinguished in the pathway from nodules that are simply present but not
  painful `[p.1]`.

## Glossary terms introduced

- **MCPJ / PIPJ** — metacarpophalangeal joint / proximal interphalangeal joint, the two joint locations the
  pathway's surgical threshold is defined against `[p.1]`.
- **Patient Initiated Follow-up (PIFU)** — a discharge model where the patient contacts the service if
  needed, rather than being given a routine follow-up appointment `[p.1]`.

## Analogies worth reusing

None — this source is a procedural flowchart, not explanatory prose, and doesn't use illustrative analogies.

## Source-stated confidence

This is an operational care pathway, not a graded evidence review — it states what should happen clinically,
not how strong the evidence is for each step. Two things are worth preserving as-is: the pathway explicitly
demotes radiotherapy to something to "consider... as part of local audit or research if available" rather
than a standard-offer treatment `[p.1]`, and it explicitly tells clinicians to reassure patients below the
surgical threshold that their nodules are "likely to become less tender over time" rather than promising
resolution `[p.1]`.
