# Effectiveness and Safety of Dupuytren Contracture Treatments: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis Using the GRADE Approach

## Central claim

**No treatment wins outright: PNF, CCH, and LF reduce contracture by a similar amount long-term, but LF holds
that reduction better at the cost of a higher risk of rare serious complications** `[¶4]`. The review states
its own motivation directly: **"There is currently no consensus on the optimal treatment for Dupuytren
contracture. Prior meta-analyses have been limited by suboptimal data synthesis methodologies."**

## The evidence base and what it found

`[¶4]`

Built from **17 publications and 1,010 patients**, comparing PNF, CCH, and LF against outcomes at 3 months, 1
year, and 2 to 3 years — residual contracture, recurrence rate, hand function, pain, global satisfaction, and
adverse events. On long-term contracture reduction, the review found **"no clinically important difference"**
between treatments, graded high-to-moderate certainty: PNF versus LF, mean difference 7.6° (95% CI 1.8 to
13.4°); CCH versus LF, mean difference 4.8° (95% CI −1.3 to 10.9°). But recurrence and safety diverge sharply:
**LF carries the lowest long-term recurrence risk** (PNF versus LF relative risk 12.3, 95% CI 1.6 to 92.4; CCH
versus LF relative risk 9.5, 95% CI 1.2 to 73.4) — while **LF also carries a higher risk of serious adverse
events than PNF** (relative risk 0.5, 95% CI 0.3 to 0.9), and **CCH carries a higher risk of overall adverse
events than PNF** (relative risk 4.8, 95% CI 2.9 to 7.0).

## Symptoms and body signals addressed

- **Residual contracture angle after treatment**, tracked separately from whether the contracture later
  returns `[¶4]`.
- **Recurrence of contracture** as its own outcome, distinct from initial correction `[¶4]`.
- **Pain, hand function, and global satisfaction** named as outcomes the review tracked, though the specific
  figures for these were not given in the excerpt available here `[¶4]`.

## Glossary terms introduced

- **GRADE approach** — the Grading of Recommendations Assessment, Development and Evaluation framework used
  to rate how certain each finding is `[¶4]`.
- **PNF / CCH / LF** — percutaneous needle fasciotomy, collagenase clostridium histolyticum injection, and
  limited fasciectomy — the three treatments compared throughout `[¶4]`.

## Analogies worth reusing

None — this is a quantitative meta-analysis reported in effect sizes and confidence intervals, not
illustrative language.

## Source-stated confidence

The review itself grades its findings by certainty rather than stating them all at one confidence level: the
"no important difference in contracture reduction" finding is **"high- to moderate-certainty evidence,"**
while the recurrence and durability findings are explicitly **"moderate-certainty evidence"** `[¶4]`. The
authors' own conclusion resists a single "best" answer: **"CCH, PNF, and LF are equally effective in
long-term contracture reduction. However, LF yields more durable results at a higher risk of rare but serious
adverse events... ultimate treatment decisions should be tailored to individual patient preferences"**
`[¶4]`.

## Conflicts with other sources

**Converges closely with `nocek-2026-recurrence-systematic-review`**, which independently quantifies the same
recurrence ordering with hard percentages (LF 16.5%, CCH 32.5%, NA 46.4%) rather than this review's relative
risks — the two sources reinforce each other on which treatment recurs least, from different meta-analytic
methods. **Also converges with `nice-htg21-needle-fasciotomy`**, whose stated ~50%-at-3-to-5-years recurrence
figure for needle fasciotomy sits consistently above collagenase and well above the low end this review
implies for LF. **Relevant to `detect-2026-progression-rct`**, a single RCT (not included in this review,
published after it) that reaches a similar directional conclusion — surgery more durable than the
percutaneous options — from primary trial data rather than pooled studies.
