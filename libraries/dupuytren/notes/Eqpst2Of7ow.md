# Biomarkers and the IDDB: 2021 Symposium

## Central claim

**Dupuytren's isn't unpredictable because the disease itself is erratic — it's unpredictable because the
field's measurements are inadequate, and until that changes, preventive treatment can't be developed or
tested.** Eaton states this reframe directly: **"it's not that Dupuytren's is persistent — it's that we
don't have an effective disease-modifying treatment. It's not that Dupuytren's is unpredictable — it's that
we don't have useful disease measurements"** `[02:04]`.

## Failed treatment can mean amputation

`[00:01]`–`[02:04]` — Eaton opens with two documented cases rather than abstractions: a man whose fingers
feel "not his" after three minimally invasive procedures and are worse now than before treatment, and an
anesthesiologist who had an **unplanned amputation because his finger couldn't be revascularized at the end
of a redo fasciectomy.** He states a specific figure: **"eight percent amputation rate for revision
fasciectomy after dermofasciectomy"** — for what he calls **"a benign, slowly progressive, usually painless
condition."** A third case, a physician diagnosed in 2006, underwent fasciectomy, radiotherapy, ongoing
TNF-blocker treatment, and finally dermofasciectomy with a radial forearm flap and skin graft — a
technically excellent early result that nonetheless left her with active disease and a functional amputation
three years later. **"Failed Dupuytren's treatment is the most common reason for elective finger
amputation."**

## Why angle measurements mislead: the geometry

`[04:07]`–`[06:09]` — The talk's most technical and novel section. Angular contracture from a cord depends
on **how close the cord runs to the joint's axis of rotation, not just how much it shortens**: the same
length of shortening produces a much larger angular change the closer the cord sits to that axis. Concretely,
at the PIP joint a **lateral cord roughly 6mm from the axis, shortened by 3mm, produces more than 25 degrees
of angular change**, while a **central cord roughly 10mm from the axis, shortened the same amount, produces
less than 15 degrees.** This explains what Eaton calls **"the curse of the pinky finger PIP joint"**: three
independent factors compound there — PIP joints are smaller than MCP joints, pinky-finger joints are smaller
than the other fingers', and lateral PIP cords are more common in the pinky specifically. **"It's not a
curse — it's just geometry."** A model built from this predicts **lateral-cord contractures progress faster
than central-cord contractures, especially at the PIP joint** — a 40% larger angular effect from the same
shortening, by his model. Separately, he flags that patients **unconsciously flex the CMC joint** to
compensate for and mask more distal contracture, meaning an unconstrained measurement can substantially
understate the true contracture. **Take-home: "individual angle measurements don't directly represent
Dupuytren's anatomy or disease progression."**

## Age of diagnosis is not age of onset

`[06:09]`–`[08:10]` — Two stated problems. First, **Dupuytren's onset is invisible** — tissue mechanics,
histology, and gene expression changes are believed to precede any visible sign. Second, and more
concretely demonstrated: reviewing his own multi-thousand-patient, no-prior-treatment practice data, Eaton
found that computing "rate of contracture" from **self-reported disease duration** produced the
counterintuitive result that patients *without* a family history appeared to progress faster than those
*with* one — the opposite of biological expectation. His explanation: **family history biases patients
toward noticing and being diagnosed earlier**, not toward faster true progression — confirmed when,
normalized for contracture severity at first treatment, family-history-positive patients reported *longer*
disease duration than family-history-negative patients. Switching the denominator from **self-reported
duration to patient age** made the family-history effect on rate of contraction largely disappear. **Take-
home: age of diagnosis (or self-reported duration) is not a reliable proxy for biologic severity or disease
progression rate — patient age is a cleaner measure.**

## What the IDDB looks like in 2021

`[12:13]`–`[16:19]` — Launched 2015, **now (2021) approaching 6,000 enrolled Dupuytren's patients plus over
700 controls from 61 countries.** Demographics: just over half women; average self-reported disease
duration ~8 years; **1 in 5 report Ledderhose disease; 1 in 5 report frozen shoulder (women roughly twice as
often); 1 in 7 report knuckle pads; 1 in 14 men report Peyronie's disease**; half report a family history of
Dupuytren's (and are a few years younger on average than those without). Treatment burden, among the
subset with a corrective procedure (~40% of enrollees): **almost half have had two or more procedures,
almost a quarter three or more, one in eight four or more, and one in thirty more than six** — and **one in
36 of those treated report at least one amputation.** Eaton draws a pointed inference from the sheer variety
of procedures tried: **"if a disease has many treatments, it means either they all work, or none of them
work well — in the latter case, people shop for better options."**

## The biomarker panel design

`[16:19]`–`[18:21]` — The stated goal is to intervene **before myofibroblasts arrive, before fibrosis
begins** — not merely to catch contracture earlier. Whole exome sequencing DNA analysis is explicitly
**deprioritized**, for two reasons: it is not dynamic (doesn't change with disease activity or treatment
response), and a polygenic disease like Dupuytren's would require a cohort of **10,000–20,000 affected
patients plus a comparable control group** to produce useful DNA-level findings. Instead, IDDB focuses on
**dynamic blood markers collected simultaneously**: small RNA, messenger RNA, whole-genome bisulfite
sequencing (epigenetic changes), and broad proteomics — described as **exploratory**, with **no existing
short list of candidate biomarkers awaiting validation.**

## Symptoms and body signals addressed

- **Fingers feeling "not one's own" — strange, stiff, numb, or tender — after multiple minimally invasive
  procedures, described as a real and worsening outcome, not merely subjective dissatisfaction** `[00:01]`.
- **Loss of finger circulation during a redo procedure, severe enough to require amputation** `[00:01]`.
- **Peau d'orange-like dermal ridging over long-standing palm nodules**, compared explicitly to the skin
  changes of inflammatory breast cancer `[08:10]`.
- **Unconscious CMC joint flexion masking the true severity of a more distal contracture** `[05:08]`.

## Glossary terms introduced

- **Diathesis (reframed)** — described here as "not a measurement, it's an educated guess," compared to
  baking a cake from a recipe of risk-factor ingredients, useful but imprecise `[03:06]`.
- **Cord-to-joint-axis distance** — the geometric variable Eaton argues actually determines how much a
  given amount of cord shortening translates into angular contracture `[04:07]`.
- **Age of diagnosis vs. age of onset** — a distinction Eaton argues the field routinely collapses,
  producing biased severity/progression comparisons `[06:09]`.

## Analogies worth reusing

- **"It's not a curse, it's just geometry"** — explaining the pinky-finger PIP joint's outsized vulnerability
  to contracture through three compounding size/anatomy factors rather than any special biological cause
  `[04:07]`.
- **Diathesis as a cake recipe** — a blend of ingredients (age of onset, family history, disease location)
  that's informative but not a real measurement `[03:06]`.
- **"If a disease has many treatments, it means either they all work, or none of them work well"**
  `[16:19]`.

## Source-stated confidence

Unusually rigorous about distinguishing measured fact from inference throughout: the geometric cord-axis
model is explicitly labeled a model ("we can't measure this geometry in the office, but we can model it and
measure the model") rather than direct clinical measurement `[05:08]`; the biomarker panel is called
**"exploratory"** with no confirmed candidates yet `[18:21]`; and Eaton flags the IDDB's self-enrollment
methodology as carrying **expected self-selection bias**, stated openly rather than minimized `[13:16]`.
