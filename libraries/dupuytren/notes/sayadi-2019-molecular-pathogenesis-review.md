# The Molecular Pathogenesis of Dupuytren Disease: Review of the Literature and Suggested New Approaches to Treatment

## Central claim

**This is a systematic review of the basic-science (not clinical-treatment) literature on Dupuytren's,
organizing 77 included studies into molecular, structural, and genetic categories, with the explicit goal of
pointing toward future biologic treatments that target the disease process rather than just cutting or
dissolving the resulting cord** `[¶7]`.

## Framing and scope

`[¶4]`

The review opens from an existing staging framework: **"Ever since the classification of Dupuytren disease
into the proliferative, involutional, and residual stages, extensive research has been performed to uncover
the molecular underpinnings of the disease."** Its stated goal is two-part — **"to systematically review the
basic science literature pertaining to Dupuytren disease and suggest a new approach to treatment"** — meaning
this source is explicitly reaching beyond a pure literature summary toward a treatment proposal.

## Method and yield

`[¶5]`, `[¶6]`

The review followed **PRISMA (Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analysis)
guidelines**, searching MEDLINE for basic-science literature falling into one or more of three categories:
**"(1) Molecular alterations, (2) Structural alterations, and (3) Genetic predisposition."** Of **177
articles screened, 77 met inclusion criteria** and were sorted into those three categories for the review's
body.

## Conclusion

`[¶7]`

**"The pathophysiological changes involved in Dupuytren's disease can be divided into a number of molecular
and structural alterations with genetic predisposition playing a contributory role."** The review's stated
payoff for this categorization is treatment-directed: **"Understanding these changes can allow for the
development of biologics which may disrupt and halt the disease process"** — a research direction, not a
treatment that exists yet.

## Symptoms and body signals addressed

Not directly addressed — like `layton-2023-fibrotic-disorder-review`, this is a mechanism-level review rather
than a clinical-presentation source.

## Glossary terms introduced

- **Proliferative, involutional, and residual stages** — the disease-staging framework this review says
  motivated the underlying research it's summarizing `[¶4]`.
- **PRISMA** — the reporting standard this systematic review followed for how studies were searched and
  screened `[¶5]`.

## Analogies worth reusing

None — this source reports its method and findings in review-methodology language rather than illustrative
comparisons.

## Source-stated confidence

The review is explicit about its own screening funnel (177 screened, 77 included), which lets a reader judge
how selective it was, rather than simply asserting a conclusion `[¶6]`. Its forward-looking claim about
biologics is stated as a possibility opened up by the science, not a therapy already validated: **"may
disrupt and halt the disease process,"** not "does" `[¶7]`.

## Conflicts with other sources

**No direct conflict with `layton-2023-fibrotic-disorder-review`** — both are basic-science reviews of the
same disease reaching the same general shape of conclusion (multiple molecular/structural drivers, genetics
playing a contributory rather than sole role), though this review is broader in scope (77 studies across
three categories) while Layton's is narrower and more recent, with a specific, named drug target (TNF) this
review's available excerpt doesn't mention by name. **No conflict with the clinical-treatment sources** in
this library — this review's territory (why the disease happens at a cellular level) doesn't overlap with
their territory (which existing procedure works best on an already-formed cord).
