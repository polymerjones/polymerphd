# Dupuytren Foundation: Surgeons' Perspectives 2

## Central claim

**Interviewing multiple hand surgeons at the American Society for Surgery of the Hand's annual meeting about
what they would personally do if they developed Dupuytren's disease, Eaton finds a near-unanimous preference
for minimally invasive treatment (needle aponeurotomy or collagenase) over open surgery, with most surgeons
favoring a watch-and-measure approach until the disease becomes functionally limiting.** One surgeon frames
this as a genuine shift in the field: **"I think that's the biggest change that's happened in the last 10, 15
years — the change from 'wait, wait, wait, big operation' to intervening a little earlier with minimally
invasive approaches, either needle aponeurotomy or Xiaflex"** `[03:10]`.

Note: this is a montage-style interview video — Eaton poses the same question to a series of surgeons on
camera, identified visually rather than by name in the narration, so individual answers below are attributed
to "one surgeon" or "another surgeon" rather than to named individuals.

## The range of personal treatment plans

`[00:06]`–`[02:09]` — Asked what their own "battle plan" would be, surgeons' answers varied in emphasis but
converged strongly on minimally invasive treatment as the eventual choice:

- One would start with **massage and vitamin E cream** in early stages, escalating to **needle aponeurotomy**
  (performed by a colleague he trained) once functionally disabling.
- One, given unlimited resources, would treat an early **nodule** aggressively with **collagenase (Xiaflex)**
  even before a contracture developed.
- Several stressed **regular measurement** as central to their plan — tracking a "tangible record" of
  progression specifically so the disease wouldn't "creep up" unnoticed, and stopping or switching a treatment
  if it wasn't working rather than persisting with it.
- One would begin treatment the moment they **lost passive MCP joint hyperextension**, favoring **steroid
  injection** first, reasoning it works well specifically in the inflammatory, nodular phase.
- Several explicitly favored **early intervention over delay**, reasoning that **the later contracture-stage
  disease is tackled, the worse the outcome and the more complex the secondary anatomical changes become.**

## The shift toward minimally invasive treatment

`[02:09]`–`[03:10]` — One surgeon frames watching-and-waiting differently — willing to observe a nodule
without treatment, planning to start with an **enzyme (collagenase) injection** once treatment became
necessary, while acknowledging **a newer development might exist by the time they needed it.** Several
describe personally practicing needle aponeurotomy and would simply "keep an eye on" a nodule, treating only
once secondary changes (particularly at the **PIP joint**) or functional interference became a real concern.
One surgeon explicitly names this pattern as **a broader field-wide shift over the past 10–15 years**: away
from delaying until a "big operation" was needed, toward earlier minimally invasive intervention.

## Caution at specific sites

`[04:10]`–`[05:11]` — Several surgeons specifically flagged **thumb or DIP-adjacent PIP joint involvement** as
a source of added caution — one stated they would be "very nervous" operating in that location even while
comfortable with needle aponeurotomy elsewhere. The overwhelming final preference, restated by multiple
surgeons independently in closing, was for **needle aponeurotomy** as the least-invasive option once treatment
became functionally necessary.

## Symptoms and body signals addressed

- **Loss of passive MCP joint hyperextension**, named directly by one surgeon as their personal trigger point
  for starting treatment `[01:08]`.
- **Difficulty putting on gloves**, cited by more than one surgeon as the practical, functional threshold that
  would prompt them to seek treatment `[00:06]`, `[04:10]`.

## Analogies worth reusing

- **"Wait, wait, wait, big operation" versus early minimally invasive intervention** — one surgeon's own

## Source-stated confidence

This is a first-person perspective/interview format rather than a data-driven talk — each surgeon's answer is
explicitly their own stated personal preference for a hypothetical scenario (their own disease), not a
description of published outcome data or a formal treatment guideline. Eaton does not editorialize or rank the
answers; the near-unanimous preference for minimally invasive treatment emerges from the surgeons' own answers
rather than from any claim Eaton makes himself.

## Conflicts with other sources

outcome and safety data reported in those talks. **Relevant to `93IWNYYqCjs`** (Warwick's "what we may never

and `2-Ad4vp1EOA` (the splinting debate talks) — several surgeons here mention "watching" a nodule without
treatment, a strategy that doesn't involve splinting at all, consistent with both debate talks' shared
