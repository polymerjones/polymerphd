# Drs. Paul Werker and Jos Aartsen "Introductory Remarks" 2015 Dupuytren Symposium

## A note on this source

This is primarily a ceremonial welcome address (conference logistics, host-city facts about Groningen,
hospital statistics) opening the 2015 symposium — most of it is not substantive Dupuytren's content and is
not covered in this note. Two specific, substantive pieces of research history are extracted below.

## Central claim

**A hospital CEO's own unusual, non-hereditary Dupuytren's case — combined with his hunch about a specific
recreational vibration exposure — led directly to a real research finding, illustrating how patient-driven
observation can start formal research.** Aartsen states his reasoning directly: **"I am the only person in
my family to be afflicted. I suggested... that there could be a link between developing Dupuytren's and
playing hockey with a stick — anyone who plays hockey will immediately recognize the vibration you feel
through the stick when you hit the ball"** `[09:20]`.

## Werker's path to the international genetics collaboration

`[00:04]`–`[01:06]` — Werker describes learning needle fasciotomy (percutaneous needle aponeurotomy) directly
from its French popularizer in Paris (the name renders in auto-captions as "Lermusiaux," not confidently
recoverable) in the late 1990s, then bringing the technique back to his own hospital and beginning formal
research on it: **first a pilot study, then a randomized clinical trial that lasted five years.** Early in
the following decade, he began a genetics collaboration with **Professor Roel Ophoff**, which by **2010,
meeting in Miami, grew into an international collaboration** with Dominic Furniss and Hans Hennies —
**published, Werker notes with evident pride, in the New England Journal of Medicine.**

## The hockey-vibration finding

`[09:20]`–`[10:21]` — Aartsen, the hosting hospital's CEO, describes himself as **"a hands-on expert not only
as a patient but also as an instigator of research"** into Dupuytren's. Being the only affected member of his
family cast doubt, in his own mind, on the disease being purely hereditary, prompting his vibration-exposure
hypothesis to Werker. The resulting research reportedly found Dupuytren's disease **"significant among male
hockey players at the Dutch veterans tournament"** — a specific, occupational/recreational vibration-exposure
finding arising directly from one patient's informal observation reaching a receptive researcher.

## Symptoms and body signals addressed

- **Dupuytren's occurring in a patient with no other affected family members**, used here as the prompt for
  questioning a purely hereditary explanation in that specific case `[09:20]`.

## Source-stated confidence

Both accounts are told as personal history/anecdote rather than presented with data in this talk itself —
Werker references the NEJM publication as an external, verifiable outcome of the collaboration he describes,
while Aartsen's hockey-vibration finding is summarized without given sample size or statistical detail in
this excerpt, though it is described as a completed, findings-bearing study rather than mere speculation.
