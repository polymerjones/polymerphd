# Dr. Gary Manley "The Patient's View: Ledderhose Disease" 2015 Dupuytren Symposium

## Central claim

**In the Ledderhose-specific portion of the joint patient survey, women outnumbered men among Ledderhose
respondents — the reverse of Dupuytren's usual male predominance — and patients rated radiotherapy far above
every other treatment option, with roughly 60% satisfied versus under 15% dissatisfied.** Manley states the
treatment-preference finding directly: **"radiotherapy for Ledderhose shows by far the best results from the
patient's point of view, and nearly 60% being happy with the treatment and less than 15% being unhappy with
the treatment"** `[04:06]`.

## The gender skew

`[00:00]`–`[01:01]` — This talk reports the Ledderhose-specific results of the same global survey described in
`LVxj5lEVnz0`. Geographic distribution matched the broader survey (majority US and European, with Germany and
the UK dominant in Europe). The striking finding: **in the 40–59 age range specifically, the number of women
with Ledderhose disease rose sharply, ultimately outweighing the number of men** — a reversal of the typical
Dupuytren's pattern. In absolute survey numbers: **about 1,300 men participated, of whom 373 had Ledderhose;
fewer than 1,000 women participated, of whom over 400 had Ledderhose** — meaning a notably higher proportion
of female respondents reported Ledderhose than male respondents. Manley is explicit this could reflect either
**a genuine gender-prevalence difference or a bias in survey recruitment** — the data can't distinguish the
two. A similar pattern (women more frequently affected than men) was also seen among younger respondents.

## Risk factors and age of onset

`[01:01]`–`[03:06]` — Consistent with expectation, a large proportion of Ledderhose patients also reported
**frozen shoulder**, a high percentage had **knuckle pads**, and there was a slight increase in **thyroid
conditions** — though Manley cautions the thyroid numbers are too small to draw strong conclusions from. On
age of onset: patients who **already had Dupuytren's disease tended to develop Ledderhose later**; a similar,
milder pattern held for knuckle pads. **Alcohol consumption showed no difference by amount**, but drinkers as
a group showed a younger age of onset than non-drinkers (of uncertain statistical significance); **smokers
showed a marginally earlier onset.** The male/female onset-age difference seen broadly in Dupuytren's disease
held here too, with **female patients developing Ledderhose considerably later than males** — and within that
group, DD-positive female non-drinkers specifically showed an even later age of onset.

## Treatment satisfaction ratings

`[03:06]`–`[04:06]` — Using the same 1–10 patient satisfaction rating scale described in the companion
Dupuytren's-focused survey talk: **surgery** left a majority of patients not particularly happy, though some
tolerated it; **PNF rated somewhat better**; **cryosurgery** had very limited respondent numbers but few
strongly unhappy patients; **orthotics** rated in the middle, with many unhappy; **steroid injections** left
most patients unhappy; **enzyme (collagenase) injections**, based on only about 10 respondents, also skewed
toward dissatisfaction. **Radiotherapy stood out clearly as the best-rated treatment**, with the strongest
satisfaction and lowest dissatisfaction of any option reported.

## Perceived medical knowledge

`[04:06]`–`[05:09]` — Asked to rate the **broader medical community's knowledge of Ledderhose disease**, a
majority of patients rated it **5 or less out of 10** — a low figure Manley notes isn't surprising given how
little the condition had come up over the symposium's own two days. However, when asked specifically about
**their own treating doctor's** knowledge of treatment options and success rates, patients reported **much
higher satisfaction** — a clear gap between perceived general awareness of the disease and confidence in one's
own individual care. A large volume of free-text patient comments was collected but deferred to the
symposium's planned book chapter rather than presented in detail here.

## Symptoms and body signals addressed

- **Frozen shoulder and knuckle pads**, reported as commonly co-occurring conditions among Ledderhose survey
  respondents, paralleling the same associations found for Dupuytren's disease `[01:01]`.

## Source-stated confidence

Manley is explicit that the striking gender skew found in this survey **cannot be distinguished from a
recruitment/sampling bias**, stating this uncertainty directly rather than asserting a true prevalence finding.
He similarly flags several other findings (thyroid association, alcohol's effect on age of onset) as based on
sample sizes too small to draw firm conclusions from, and notes the enzyme-injection treatment rating is based
on only about ten respondents.

## Conflicts with other sources

**Directly complements `LVxj5lEVnz0`** (Wach's companion survey talk) — both talks report results from the
same underlying patient survey, this one specifically isolating the Ledderhose disease subset; together they
give a fuller picture of the survey's scope than either talk alone. **Relevant to `S2k9bDzxSOU`** (van Driel's
Peyronie's disease talk) — all three of Ledderhose, Peyronie's, and Dupuytren's disease are related fibrotic
conditions discussed via patient comorbidity and survey data across this library, worth cross-referencing
together when discussing Dupuytren's diathesis broadly. **Relevant to any note reporting radiotherapy outcomes
for Ledderhose or Dupuytren's disease from a clinical (not patient-satisfaction) perspective** — this survey's
strongly favorable patient rating for radiotherapy is a self-reported perception measure, useful to compare
against clinically measured outcome data reported elsewhere in this library for the same treatment.
