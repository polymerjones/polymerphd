# Davor Jurisic PhD MD, "Screening of Potential Prodrugs on Cells Derived from Dupuytren's Patients" 2010 Miami Dupuytren Symposium

## Central claim

**Of four newly synthesized compounds tested against cells cultured directly from patients' own diseased
tissue, one — "compound 4" — showed genuine selectivity, suppressing diseased cells specifically while
sparing healthy tissue, unlike the most potent compound tested, which killed cells indiscriminately.**
Jurisic's framing of that distinction: **"the strongest compound... has no differential activity on those...
cell cultures, so it was highly cytotoxic. The one being mostly interesting for us is compound 4, because...
it has shown to be highly specific for the [diseased] cell lines... preserving the [near-diseased] and the
control cells"** `[05:10]`.

## Study design

`[02:06]`–`[04:09]` — Eight male patients (six primary cases, two recurrences), with tissue samples marked
**D** (microscopically diseased tissue), **ND** (tissue taken near the primary incision, without visible
phenotypic disease expression), and true healthy controls from **carpal tunnel release patients.** Four
compounds tested: **5-fluorouracil (5-FU)** as the reference control (chosen for its known antiproliferative
effect in ophthalmologic surgery), plus three newly synthesized compounds (numbered 1–3, with 3 and 4 already
patented at the time of the talk — compound 4 being tested alongside, not among, the patented pair as best
understood from the transcript). A practical technical note: for **advanced (Tubiana stage 3–4) disease**,
excessive tissue manipulation and exposure during a long dissection degraded cell culture quality — solved
by **removing and handling palm tissue first, before continuing the dissection into the finger**, to keep the
sample viable.

## Results

`[04:09]`–`[06:10]` — Tested at day 3, 6, and 13 of culture. **Compound 3 was the most potent
antiproliferative agent overall, but showed no selectivity** — equally toxic to diseased, near-diseased, and
healthy control cells, making it clinically unsuitable. **Compound 4, by contrast, was highly specific to
diseased (D) cell lines at tested concentrations, while preserving ND and control cells.** IC50/LC50 values
were determined specifically to identify this kind of differential effect between diseased and healthy
tissue. Immunofluorescence tracking alpha-smooth-muscle-actin expression alongside a fluorescent signal
confirmed the pattern; for compounds 1–3, treated cells showed **enlargement interpreted as cell-cycle
arrest.**

## What compound 4 actually is

`[07:11]`–`[08:13]` — Belongs to a class of compounds that **specifically inhibit mammalian topoisomerase
activity**, binding DNA and causing DNA damage, and additionally **interferes with tubulin polymerization**
— a described, real mechanism class (topoisomerase inhibition plus microtubule disruption). Given its
selectivity for diseased cells and **low toxicity across all tested cell cultures**, Jurisic proposes two
potential applications: **selective treatment during the early, highly proliferative disease phase**
specifically, and **postoperative use aimed at the wound-healing process**, given its low toxicity profile.

## An honest self-critique about disease phase

`[08:13]` — Jurisic notes candidly that his own surgical case selection likely captured tissue in a
**residual or evolutional phase** of disease rather than the truly early, highly proliferative phase this
compound would be best suited to target — **"I didn't catch the patients with the proliferative phase"** —
flagging this as something to actively consider when designing future treatment-timing studies, rather than
glossing over the mismatch between his sample and his own proposed application.

## Glossary terms introduced

- **Differential (selective) antiproliferative activity** — a drug's ability to suppress diseased cells
  specifically while sparing healthy cells, the key criterion distinguishing compound 4 from the more potent
  but non-selective compound 3 `[04:09]`.
- **Topoisomerase inhibition / tubulin interference** — compound 4's described mechanism class `[07:11]`.

## Analogies worth reusing

None stated — the talk presents laboratory screening methodology and results directly.

## Source-stated confidence

Jurisic is direct about which compound failed and why (compound 3's lack of selectivity, despite its raw
potency, disqualifying it), and explicitly names a mismatch between his own patient sample (residual/
evolutional-phase disease) and the disease phase his proposed treatment application would actually target —
flagging a real limitation of his own study design rather than glossing past it. He also states outright that
this is preclinical work: compound 4 is proposed for **"further preclinical studies and optimization,"** not
presented as ready for clinical testing yet.

## Conflicts with other sources

directly on assembled actin filaments, and this compound acts through DNA topoisomerase inhibition and
tubulin disruption, each targeting the disease process at a different molecular level. **Relevant to

studies rely on the same core methodology of comparing patient-derived diseased cells against matched
healthy-tissue controls from the same patients, a recurring experimental design choice across multiple
independent labs represented in this symposium.
