# Dr. Jagdeep Nanchahal "TNF as a Therapeutic Target" 2015 Dupuytren Symposium

## Central claim

**Nanchahal's group found that TNF makes Dupuytren's palmar fibroblasts more contractile at the same
concentration the cells themselves secrete it — a cell-type-specific effect not seen in normal or non-palmar
fibroblasts — and that anti-TNF exposure reverses this contractility and downregulates the myofibroblast
phenotype, motivating a funded clinical trial injecting anti-TNF directly into early-disease nodules.** He
states the key divergence directly: **"palmar fibroblasts from Dupuytren's patients became more contractile
at a hundred picograms per ml... [while] normal palmar fibroblasts and non-palmar fibroblasts from Dupuytren's
patients became less contractile, but at much higher levels — so there is this separation in the behavior of
the cell types"** `[02:02]`.

## Beyond the myofibroblast: macrophages in the nodule

`[00:00]`–`[01:01]` — Disaggregating Dupuytren's nodules taken from fasciectomy patients and running FACS
analysis found roughly **90% myofibroblasts**, but also a substantial population of **classically and
alternatively activated macrophages**, confirmed present throughout the nodule by histological staining.
Rather than targeting these cells directly, Nanchahal's group cultured the **whole freshly disaggregated cell
mixture** (myofibroblasts plus inflammatory cells) for 24 hours to study what the mixture collectively
secretes — explicitly modeled on the same type of experiment used by colleagues at his institute to identify
TNF as a therapeutic target in rheumatoid arthritis.

## Cytokine levels and TGF-beta's predictable effect

`[01:01]`–`[02:02]` — The 24-hour culture supernatant showed variable **TGF-beta (mean ~250 pg/mL)**, **TNF
(~80 pg/mL)**, substantial **IL-6**, and enough **GM-CSF** to plausibly account for the macrophage population.
Testing TGF-beta's effect: added to Dupuytren's palmar fibroblasts, they became more contractile — but this
was **not a Dupuytren's-specific effect**: normal palmar fibroblasts and non-palmar fibroblasts from
Dupuytren's patients responded the same way, consistent with TGF-beta being a general driver of myofibroblast
transformation across fibroblast types. Inhibiting TGF-beta decreased contractility and downregulated the
myofibroblast phenotype at both mRNA and protein level, as expected.

## TNF's divergent, cell-type-specific effect

`[02:02]`–`[03:04]` — TNF's effect was different and more interesting: **Dupuytren's palmar fibroblasts became
more contractile at ~100 pg/mL** — close to the level found secreted in the ex vivo nodule culture — while
**normal palmar fibroblasts and non-palmar Dupuytren's fibroblasts became less contractile, but only at much
higher TNF concentrations**, tested across a log-range dose series. Exposing Dupuytren's myofibroblasts to
**anti-TNF** reversed this: cells became less contractile, expressed less mRNA for **alpha-smooth-muscle actin
and collagen I**, produced less of the corresponding protein, and visibly **disassembled their alpha-smooth-
muscle actin** stress fibers.

## TNF-Wnt signaling crosstalk

`[03:04]` — Nanchahal's group found TNF signaling crosstalk with the Wnt pathway — but **specifically in
Dupuytren's palmar fibroblasts, not in the other cell types tested**: TNF was found to **inhibit GSK-3 beta**,
which preserves **beta-catenin**, allowing its translocation to the nucleus where it participates in gene
transcription within the canonical Wnt signaling pathway; beta-catenin is also involved in cytoskeletal
structure at adherens junctions. Testing **clinically approved anti-TNF agents** on Dupuytren's myofibroblasts,
all were effective, with the most effective being two fully human IgG antibody-class agents, tested across a
dose-response range.

## Why primary cultures matter — and a proposed explanation for prior trial failures

`[04:04]` — A key methodological point: by **passage 2** in culture, the inflammatory cells present in the
original tissue have disappeared and TNF becomes nearly undetectable — but the remaining myofibroblasts show a
**three-fold increase in autocrine TGF-beta secretion**. Nanchahal proposes this culture-passage artifact may
help explain **why late-phase clinical trials targeting TGF-beta have failed across essentially every form of
fibrosis studied** — a passaged-cell system may be dominated by TGF-beta signaling that doesn't reflect the
original tissue's actual cytokine environment, where TNF (from the now-vanished inflammatory cells) played a
more central early role.

## Moving to a clinical trial

`[04:04]`–`[05:05]` — Anti-TNF is highlighted as having an **excellent, well-characterized safety profile**,
with rare adverse effects that can be mitigated through patient prescreening (for infection risk), and
subcutaneous administration described as well tolerated. The stated plan: inject **early-disease nodules**
directly with anti-TNF. The resulting trial is **funded by the Wellcome Trust and the UK Department of
Health**, with drug supply initially funded by a biotech Nanchahal identifies as **180 Therapeutics** —
structured as a **dose-ranging study followed by a randomized, placebo-controlled trial** in early-disease
patients.

## Glossary terms introduced

- **GSK-3 beta / beta-catenin crosstalk** — the specific mechanism proposed by which TNF signaling connects to
  canonical Wnt pathway activity in Dupuytren's palmar fibroblasts `[03:04]`.

## Source-stated confidence

Nanchahal discloses a specific financial conflict upfront (support from the biotech funding the drug for the
planned trial). He is explicit that TGF-beta's contractility effect is **not cell-type-specific** ("predictable
... [in] any fibroblast from any system"), directly contrasting it with TNF's divergent, Dupuytren's-specific
effect, rather than overstating either finding. He frames the culture-passage/TGF-beta-autocrine finding as a
proposed explanation for prior trial failures, not a proven mechanism, and is clear the anti-TNF nodule
injection approach is still moving into early-phase dose-ranging trial work, not yet an established treatment.
