# Hans Hennies PhD, "Characterization of the Epidemiology and Genetics of Dupuytren's" 2010 Miami Dupuytren Symposium

## Central claim

**A large German/Swiss cohort found the expected diabetes association, but no association at all with
smoking, alcohol, or rheumatoid arthritis — challenging several commonly assumed risk factors with actual
comparison data against national population rates.** Hennies states the smoking finding plainly: **"40% in
male patients and 20% in female patients are or were smokers, which is even less than in the general
population"** according to German Federal Ministry of Health figures `[05:11]`, and the same held for
alcohol consumption.

## Study design and cohort

`[01:05]`–`[02:07]` — Based in Cologne, framed under a university "Cluster of Excellence on age-related
diseases," with clinical centers across Germany plus partners in Zurich. At the time of the talk: **560
patients registered**, with **~500 blood samples, 280 cultured cell lines, and 230 tissue samples**
collected. Hennies is explicit these are **not registry-grade epidemiological data**, only what was
collected from the actual patients enrolled.

## Basic demographics

`[02:07]`–`[04:11]` — Roughly an **80:19 male:female ratio** among affected patients (matching the strong
male skew reported throughout this library); **mean age at first surgery around 51**, with familial cases
presenting somewhat earlier (**around 55**) than the overall average. **About 40% of cases were familial** —
with the caveat that this figure carries real limitations in how family history is actually confirmed.
**Ectopic disease occurred in 12% of patients: 6% with knuckle pads, 5% with plantar (Ledderhose)
involvement.**

## Disease associations, including two genuine surprises

`[04:11]`–`[05:11]` — **Diabetes**: present in 10% of patients, versus a general German prevalence of 5.8% —
a real association, consistent with findings reported elsewhere in this library. **Rheumatoid arthritis**:
present in only 3% of patients, versus a 4% general population rate — **no positive association, and if
anything a slightly lower rate**, consistent with rheumatoid arthritis appearing as a risk-*reducing* factor
elsewhere in this library. **Epilepsy**: 1%, matching the general population rate exactly — no association.
**Smoking and alcohol use, checked against national baseline data, both showed no increased — and for
smoking, a lower — rate among Dupuytren's patients** than the general population. Trauma and manual work
were examined too, but Hennies states the numbers were **still too small for a reliable conclusion.**

## Preliminary cell contraction data

`[06:12]`–`[07:13]` — Myofibroblasts cultured from patient tissue, seeded into a collagen matrix and tracked
over 10 days (of a planned two-month study), showed **markedly higher contractility in patient-derived
cells than in control cells** — consistent with similar fibroblast-populated collagen lattice findings
reported elsewhere in this library.

## The genetic study's two-track design

`[07:13]`–`[12:24]` — Framed around the standard **multifactorial disease model**: a combination of
environmental risk factors and genetic risk alleles together producing disease manifestation, with the
genetic study aimed at identifying which risk alleles are involved. Two parallel approaches:

1. **Gene expression profiling**, using Illumina microarray chips (**~50,000 probes**), comparing RNA from
   **tissue samples** against RNA from **cultured cells** (with an explicit caveat that culturing itself
   changes expression patterns) — intended to let disease stage, culture condition, and age-related changes
   all be compared.
2. **Whole-genome association study**, using the Affymetrix SNP 6.0 chip (capturing both SNPs and copy
   number variants), in a **case-control design (400–500 patients versus ~1,700 controls)**, following a
   **multi-stage design** (citing a review by Pearson and Manolio): starting with a smaller sample and many
   markers, then validating findings in progressively larger cohorts with fewer markers — a design chosen
   partly for practical funding reasons, since the more markers tested per sample, the more expensive each
   sample becomes.

At the time of the talk, the **first genome scan was not yet complete** — no genetic results were presented,
only the study design.

## Broader aims beyond Dupuytren's itself

`[12:24]` — Hennies frames the project's ambitions past a single disease: characterizing **myofibroblast
proliferation and differentiation generally**, and specifically **stress-induced and age-dependent
molecular changes in connective tissue** — positioning Dupuytren's as a useful model for broader aging and
connective-tissue biology, alongside the hope of eventually contributing to a curative treatment.

## Symptoms and body signals addressed

- **Knuckle pads and plantar (Ledderhose) involvement**, quantified at 6% and 5% respectively in this
  cohort `[04:11]`.
- **No elevated diabetes-style association with rheumatoid arthritis, smoking, or alcohol use** — presented
  as reassuring negative findings against commonly assumed risk factors, checked against real national
  baseline rates rather than assumed `[04:11]`–`[05:11]`.

## Glossary terms introduced

- **Multi-stage GWAS design** — a genome-wide association study structure that starts with fewer patients
  and more markers, then validates in larger cohorts with fewer markers, partly for cost efficiency
  `[10:19]`.

## Analogies worth reusing

None stated — the talk presents epidemiology and study design directly.

## Source-stated confidence

Hennies is careful to qualify his own numbers: the 40%-familial figure is flagged with "limitations in
obtaining this number"; the trauma/manual-work risk factor data is explicitly called too small to draw
conclusions from; and he states plainly that **no genetic results are available yet**, only the completed
study design — resisting the temptation to over-promise ahead of the actual genome scan results.

## Conflicts with other sources

**Is very likely a precursor cohort to the same international collaboration described in `hRJZEQzxy4U`**
(Werker and Aartsen's introductory remarks), which names Hennies directly as part of the group whose genetics
work, alongside Ophoff's and Furniss's, was published in the New England Journal of Medicine — this talk
shows Hennies's own German/Swiss cohort and study design prior to that international merger. **Extends
`-3a_yiOu_3I`** (Dolmans's Dutch GWAS talk) — both run parallel, independently designed whole-genome
association studies around the same period, likely feeding into the same eventual combined publication.
**Directly corroborates `W_Tbwi613Qw`** (Finsen's Bosnia epidemiology talk) on the diabetes association, and
**adds a genuine counterpoint to any assumption that smoking or alcohol are Dupuytren's risk factors** —
neither showed an association here, worth flagging explicitly wherever those factors are mentioned casually
elsewhere in this library.
