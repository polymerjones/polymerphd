# Sem Phan PhD MD, "Mechanisms of Myofibroblast Differentiation" 2010 Miami Dupuytren Symposium

## Central claim

**This talk is basic molecular biology from pulmonary, renal, and hepatic fibrosis research, presented at
the symposium as mechanism science likely to generalize to myofibroblast differentiation in Dupuytren's —
not as Dupuytren-specific findings.** The speaker states this framing himself at the outset: **"it's
clearly impossible to talk about mechanisms of differentiation in 15 minutes, so I'll just highlight some
recent exciting events in this area"** `[00:09]`, working entirely from lung/kidney/liver fibrosis models.
The single explicit point of connection to Dupuytren's disease itself is a passing remark about a signaling
molecule, Fizz1, **"which is also called resistin-like molecule alpha... considered to be an adipokine
initially... and potentially may be relevant even in Dupuytren's, where there are fatty or fat deposits
around this fascia"** `[12:26]` — offered as a hypothesis, not a finding.

## Where myofibroblasts come from

`[00:09]`–`[08:19]` — Phan reviews competing origin hypotheses for myofibroblasts in fibrosis, using
lineage-tracing evidence (not from Dupuytren's tissue) to rule two of them down:

- **Circulating/bone-marrow origin**: using a bone-marrow transplant model with GFP-tagged donor cells, his
  group found the tagged cells expressed collagen but **did not become myofibroblasts, even when treated
  with TGF-beta** — "the classical signal for inducing differentiation" `[02:11]`–`[04:16]`. His conclusion:
  this is "probably an unlikely contribution," though these cells may still act indirectly, in a paracrine
  role, by releasing TGF-beta that induces differentiation locally.
- **Epithelial-to-mesenchymal transition (EMT)**: described as "rather controversial." The most definitive
  cited study used genetic lineage-tracking to tag kidney epithelial cells directly and found **"no evidence
  of colocalization... they concluded from this study that there's no evidence for epithelial-mesenchymal
  transition in vivo"** (in vitro evidence existed, but not in vivo) `[05:16]`–`[07:18]`.
- **What's left**: the working model — attributed to a review by his department chair — is that
  myofibroblasts **"predominantly arise from... the perivascular adventitial fibroblast, and primarily due
  to stimulation by TGF-beta"** `[07:18]`.

## TGF-beta drives both differentiation and survival

`[08:19]` — Phan states his own group's earlier finding that TGF-beta does two jobs, not one: **"in addition
to inducing differentiation, they can also inhibit apoptosis in the myofibroblast"** (shown against nitric
oxide-induced death), making it **"a potent signal for both the differentiation as well as the persistence,
or survival, of the myofibroblast."** He then locates this at the promoter level: two SMAD binding elements
were identified on the alpha-smooth-muscle-actin gene promoter, and mutating the upstream element (SBE1)
disrupted TGF-beta responsiveness, while mutating the downstream element (SBE2) had no effect — **direct
evidence for SMAD-mediated transcriptional control at a specific site** `[10:23]`–`[11:24]`.

## The Fizz1 → Jagged1 → Notch pathway

`[12:26]`–`[15:32]` — presented as a TGF-beta-*independent* route to the same endpoint. Fizz1/RELMα, induced
in a lung fibrosis model, activates Notch signaling through the ligand Jagged1; overexpressing Jagged1
enhanced alpha-actin expression beyond Fizz1 alone, and blocking Jagged1 suppressed it — **"indicating that
Fizz1 induction of alpha-actin expression, or myofibroblast differentiation, is dependent on Jagged1."** A
CSL-complex binding element was located on the alpha-actin promoter close to the SMAD site described above.

## Active repression as a second, independent layer

`[16:34]`–`[19:35]` — beyond induction, Phan describes active suppression keeping fibroblasts undifferentiated
at rest: Krüppel-like Factor 4 (KLF4) competes for promoter binding sites and inhibits SMAD3 binding to the
SMAD element; a short-form CBP/beta-catenin also inhibits. These are described as **induced** repressors,
not present at baseline. Separately, his lab identified **NKX2.5** — normally a cardiomyocyte marker — as a
baseline repressor specific to fibroblasts (opposite its role in vascular smooth muscle): mutating an
upstream NKX2.5 binding site increased promoter activity, suppressing NKX2.5 was sufficient to promote
differentiation, and **fibrotic tissue in vivo showed reduced NKX2.5 expression** — consistent with a
repressor being switched off as fibrosis proceeds `[18:34]`–`[19:35]`.

## Epigenetic gatekeeping

`[20:36]`–`[26:44]` — Two mechanisms, both shown to matter more than expected: **histone deacetylase
inhibition unexpectedly blocked differentiation** (counterintuitive, since deacetylation normally silences
genes — Phan attributes this to an indirect route through another suppressor molecule). **DNA methylation**
at three CpG islands on the alpha-actin promoter was found to differ sharply between fibroblasts (low
methylation downstream, permissive) and alveolar epithelial cells that never express alpha-actin (high
methylation) — and a demethylating agent produced a dose-dependent increase in alpha-actin expression,
**"confirming that methylation is an important mechanism by which these cells are being kept
undifferentiated."** A separate liver-fibrosis study (Mann's group, UK) is cited converging on a related
pathway through MeCP2 and microRNA-132, ultimately suppressing PPAR-gamma to permit alpha-actin expression.

## Glossary terms introduced

- **Myofibroblast differentiation** — the process by which fibroblasts acquire contractile,
  alpha-smooth-muscle-actin-expressing phenotype `[00:09]`.
- **Perivascular adventitial fibroblast** — the cell type identified as the likely local precursor to
  myofibroblasts `[07:18]`.
- **SMAD binding element (SBE)** — a specific promoter DNA sequence through which TGF-beta signaling
  activates alpha-actin gene expression `[10:23]`.
- **Fizz1 (RELMα)** — an adipokine-derived signal shown here to activate Notch/Jagged1 signaling and
  induce differentiation, with a stated (unconfirmed) hypothesis of relevance to fat deposits near
  Dupuytren's-affected fascia `[12:26]`.
- **NKX2.5** — a transcription factor identified here as a fibroblast-specific repressor of
  differentiation, distinct from its known role in cardiac and vascular smooth muscle `[17:34]`.

## Source-stated confidence

The speaker is explicit that this is a partial, fast-moving review rather than a settled account: **"it may
sound a little bit disjointed... that's the limitation of the time"** `[00:09]`. He separately flags EMT's
contribution as **"rather controversial"** with the level of contribution "not entirely clear," notes the
cited lineage-tracing study is **"not the final word,"** and explicitly labels the Dupuytren's connection
to fat deposits as a stated possibility ("potentially may be relevant"), not a tested finding `[05:16]`,
`[12:26]`. The histone-deacetylase result is flagged by the speaker himself as "somewhat counterintuitive"
and only partially explained.
