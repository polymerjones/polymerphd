# Anne Ouellette MD MBA, "Outcomes of Bioengineered Skin Graft (Apligraf) Use in Dupuytren's Surgery" 2010 Miami Dupuytren Symposium

## Central claim

**Covering open McCash-technique fasciectomy wounds with bioengineered (tissue-engineered) skin graft,
instead of leaving them open or using autologous skin, showed zero recurrences in a small long-followed
subgroup and reduced post-operative pain — but it also, counterintuitively, healed more slowly than simply
leaving the wound open.** Ouellette states this tension directly: **"the wounds covered with bioengineered
skin healed at an average of about 40 days, but the uncovered ones healed at 27 days... so did the engineered
skin actually delay the healing, and if so, why?"** `[07:17]`.

## What bioengineered skin graft actually does

`[00:06]`–`[02:09]` — Originally developed for diabetic foot and venous leg ulcers, where it's established as
safe and effective, reducing healing time and infection risk. Ouellette emphasizes it is **not merely a
physical barrier**: it delivers **living, active fibroblasts and keratinocytes**, shown to **persist in the
wound for up to about six weeks** even after the initial applied sheet sloughs off. It functions as a **cell
therapy delivery system** for growth factors, cytokines, natural antibiotics, matrix proteins, and
proteoglycans, and stimulates **neovascularization** and new matrix (fibronectin and related proteins)
formation at the wound edge.

## A prior precedent: epidermolysis bullosa hand surgery

`[02:09]`–`[03:09]` — Before applying this to Dupuytren's, Ouellette's group used bioengineered skin
off-label in **epidermolysis bullosa (EB)** hand surgery — releasing the characteristic "mitten hand"
contractures common in EB, where skin is extremely fragile. In **5 of 6 patients**, they achieved a
**100% take rate**, **all wounds healed by six weeks**, no autologous skin was needed, all patients regained
functional hand use, and there were no surgical complications — a result she describes as directly
motivating the attempt to apply the same approach to Dupuytren's.

## Applying it to Dupuytren's, and how

`[03:09]`–`[05:13]` — The rationale draws on the same logic covered elsewhere in this library: removing skin
over a fasciectomy is believed to reduce recurrence (she cites **~10% recurrence after dermatofasciectomy**),
used specifically for **aggressive, diffuse-type disease with well-defined cords**, typically in redo cases.
Technique: perform the fasciectomy via McCash-style incisions, then instead of leaving the wound open (the
traditional McCash approach) or applying autologous skin, **suture bioengineered skin directly over the
wound**, using non-adherent dressing (adaptic plus antibiotic ophthalmic drops, changed by the therapist,
with the underlying graft itself left undisturbed). The graft **dries out and acts like a scab by 7–10
days** with the antibiotic-drop protocol (or sloughs off sooner, around 4–5 days, without it), after which
care proceeds as a routine open McCash wound.

## Results from a small, long-followed cohort

`[06:15]`–`[09:20]` — A retrospective review of patients treated **1999–2007**; of 36 total patients, **11
had adequate follow-up data with bioengineered skin graft**, at a **mean follow-up of 76 months (~6.3
years).** In this subgroup: **average DASH score of 8, with zero recurrences.** Ouellette is explicit the
sample is too small to draw firm conclusions from. Beyond the slower healing time noted above, **post-
operative pain was reduced** compared to standard donor-site wound care (citing a separate comparative
pain study on split-thickness graft donor sites), and **range of motion improved substantially** in most
patients — **PIP joint improvement averaging about 29%, MCP joint improvement about 47%** — notable given
this early cohort skewed toward severe or redo cases. A minority of patients (two for PIP, one for MCP)
showed little improvement.

## Symptoms and body signals addressed

- **Post-operative wound pain**, reported as reduced with bioengineered skin coverage compared to standard
  open-wound or donor-site care `[08:19]`.
- **Delayed wound closure time (average 40 vs. 27 days)** as an observed tradeoff against the pain benefit
  `[07:17]`.
- **Recurrence versus disease progression at long-term follow-up**, acknowledged as sometimes genuinely hard
  to distinguish on exam `[08:19]`.

## Glossary terms introduced

- **Bioengineered (tissue-engineered) skin graft** — a living skin substitute delivering active
  fibroblasts and keratinocytes as a cell-therapy vehicle, not just a mechanical wound cover `[00:06]`.
- **McCash open-palm technique** — the baseline open-wound fasciectomy approach this study modifies by
  covering the wound with bioengineered skin instead of leaving it open `[00:06]`.

None stated — the talk presents technique and outcomes data directly.

## Source-stated confidence

Unusually candid about the study's limits: Ouellette calls the work **"like a focus group"** rather than a
real trial, notes data collection became harder after she left the institution mid-review, and explicitly
states the zero-recurrence finding shouldn't be overinterpreted given the small sample (11 patients). She
proposes the necessary next step herself — **a prospective, randomized, controlled comparison** against
standard open McCash technique with pre- and post-operative range-of-motion measurement and near-5-year
follow-up — rather than presenting the current retrospective data as sufficient.
