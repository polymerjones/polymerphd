# Dr. Philip Blazar: "Collagenase US Dupuytren Treatment Patterns" 2015 Dupuytren Symposium

## Central claim

**Analyzing US insurance claims from 2007–2013, Blazar's group found their own null hypothesis — that FDA
approval of collagenase would not significantly change national Dupuytren's treatment patterns — was clearly
disproven: open surgery fell by roughly 20%, collagenase grew to about 30% of US treatment by the end of 2013,
and needle aponeurotomy use remained comparatively flat throughout.** He states the topline conclusion
directly: **"by the end of 2013, forty-two percent of treatment was no longer open surgery — CCH use
increased significantly, and this correlated with the decrease in open surgery, but the needle aponeurotomy
procedures remained relatively steady"** `[05:11]`.

## Data source and method

`[00:00]`–`[02:07]` — Using **IMS Health**, a claims-catalog database covering roughly **75% of the US
insurance market** (public and private payers), the group queried **ICD-9** diagnosis codes for Dupuytren's
contracture (contrasted explicitly with UK colleagues' use of ICD-10) alongside procedural codes for
**fasciotomy and fasciectomy**, covering **January 2007 through the end of 2013** — collagenase (CCH) had been
FDA-approved in **February 2010**, giving a comparable period both before and after approval. The 75%-coverage
data was **extrapolated to 100% of the US population** using actual collagenase vial sales figures obtained
from the manufacturer's clearinghouse, assuming all sold vials were used. Blazar discloses much of the data
came from the manufacturer (Auxilium/Endo Pharmaceuticals) but states the group received no financial support
for the analysis itself.

## A large rise in diagnosed disease

`[02:07]`–`[03:08]` — Diagnoses of Dupuytren's contracture rose by **almost 70%** over the study period —
Blazar attributes this to a broad increase in disease awareness, though the data couldn't distinguish whether
this rise was driven more by specialists or primary care physicians. **Total procedures for Dupuytren's
contracture** rose too, but by a smaller margin — **roughly 25%.**

## Treatment mix shifted substantially

`[03:08]`–`[04:10]` — Breaking down what specific treatment was performed: **open surgery (fasciotomy/
fasciectomy) declined by almost 20%** over the period. **Collagenase, introduced at zero in early 2010,
increased significantly** following FDA approval. **Needle aponeurotomy, by contrast, did not increase
significantly** over the same period — remaining comparatively flat. By percentage of total US Dupuytren's
treatment at the end of 2013: **collagenase had grown to roughly 30%**, **needle aponeurotomy remained in the
low teens (~12%)**, leaving open surgery as the majority but declining share.

## A seasonal pattern unique to open surgery

`[04:10]`–`[05:11]` — A **statistically significant seasonal variation** was found specifically in open
surgery volume, concentrated in the **winter months (November through February)** — a pattern Blazar notes
will not surprise practicing hand surgeons in the audience, attributing it to patients' reluctance to accept
the longer recovery and reduced hand function of open surgery during a period they want to remain manually
active. Critically, **this seasonal pattern was not observed** in the volume of the "less invasive" treatments
— needle aponeurotomy or collagenase.

## Source-stated confidence

Blazar frames the study explicitly around a stated **null hypothesis** — that FDA approval would not
significantly change treatment patterns — and reports that the data clearly rejected it, rather than framing
the result as an expected or foregone conclusion. He is candid about a specific data limitation: the large rise
in diagnoses couldn't be attributed to a specific physician type (specialist versus primary care) with the
data available, and he explicitly frames "less invasive" in quotation marks when describing needle aponeurotomy
and collagenase together, signaling that framing as a category label rather than an unqualified clinical
claim.

## Conflicts with other sources

**Directly contrasted by `3PcPMAWaUDM`** (Reichert's German hand surgeon preference talk) — that talk reports
collagenase as practically unavailable in Germany over this same general period, due to a 2011 German health-
authority (GBA) decision not to reimburse it, a striking real-world contrast to this talk's finding of
collagenase reaching roughly 30% US market share by 2013; together the two talks show national payer policy,
not just clinical evidence, driving very different treatment adoption curves for the identical drug. **Relevant to `mUFigJz8r-U`** (Badalamente's clinical research talk) —
this talk's national-scale treatment-pattern shift is a large, real-world downstream consequence of exactly the
translational research process that talk describes from the bench-to-bedside development side. **Relevant to
`bKXguv2CscM`** (Badalamente's concurrent collagenase injections talk) and other collagenase trial-data notes —
this talk supplies the population-level adoption context (rising to ~30% market share by 2013) for the
individual-trial efficacy and safety data reported in those notes.
