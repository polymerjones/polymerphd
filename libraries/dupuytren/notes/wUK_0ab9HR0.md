# Dr. Joe Dias "The Epidemiology of Surgical Intervention" 2015 Dupuytren Symposium

## Central claim

**Analyzing English national hospital data covering 34,000 Dupuytren's contracture release procedures over
two years, Dias found an eight-fold variation in surgical rate between hospitals — with larger units
operating less than expected and smaller units operating considerably more — a pattern he attributes to
practice preference rather than evidence, and not explained by geography.** He gives a striking local example
directly: **"the biggest unit... provides care for half the population of [my] unit, and yet it is doing three
times the amount of surgery... just 20 miles apart"** `[03:00]`.

## Data source

`[00:00]`–`[01:00]` — English hospitals code every admitted patient's diagnosis using **ICD-10** (roughly
10,000 codes, including a unique code for Dupuytren's) and every procedure using **OPCS 4.5** codes (the UK
equivalent of DRG-style procedure coding), with each procedure assigned a cost tariff — alongside other
routinely collected data such as length of stay. Dias notes this system is broadly similar to what's used in
the Netherlands and Germany.

## An eight-fold variation between hospitals

`[01:00]`–`[03:00]` — Covering **2010–2011, 34,000 patients underwent Dupuytren's contracture release** across
English hospitals. Using a **funnel plot** (hospital population served on the x-axis, procedure rate per
100,000 population per year on the y-axis, with control lines at two and three standard deviations from the
national mean), Dias shows the **national mean rate is 56 per 100,000 population per year**, but a large
number of hospitals fall **outside the control limits** — with a clear, consistent pattern: **larger units
operate less** (lower rate than expected) and **smaller units operate considerably more** than expected.
Geography does not explain this: his own unit and the highest-volume unit in the dataset are **only 20 miles
apart**, yet the larger unit — serving half his unit's population — performs **three times the surgical
rate.**

## A shift in procedure type

`[03:00]` — Looking at specific procedures, dominated by **palmar fasciotomy and digital fasciotomy**: overall
fasciotomy rate is **increasing**, but this is driven almost entirely by a **decreasing palmar fasciotomy
rate** offset by an **increasing digital fasciotomy rate** — the overall volume shift masks a real change in
*which* procedure is being chosen. **Revision rate has remained essentially unchanged** over the same period.

## National and EU-wide scale

`[04:03]` — Using the standard method employed by the UK Department of Health, the overall variation across
hospitals measures **8.2-fold**. Scaling up: England performs **17,000 Dupuytren's cases per year**, costing
**£60 million (roughly €81 million) per year**; extrapolated across the **whole European Union**, this implies
roughly **160,000 cases per year, costing approximately three-quarters of a billion euros.** The **revision
rate holds steady around 7%** — a figure Dias notes does **not match what's commonly reported in the
literature** — and **dermofasciectomy remains a very small share** of overall procedures performed.

## Closing framing

`[04:03]` — Dias closes by explicitly reiterating a point made earlier in the same session (attributed to a
co-presenter): this scale of unexplained variation **"tends to reflect preference rather than evidence"** —
framing the eight-fold rate variation as a practice-pattern problem rather than one driven by differences in
underlying disease burden or clinical need across regions.

## Symptoms and body signals addressed

None directly clinical — this is a health-services/administrative epidemiology talk tracking procedure rates
and costs, not individual patient symptoms.

## Glossary terms introduced

- **Funnel plot** — a statistical visualization plotting an outcome rate against a unit's population/sample
  size, with control limits used to flag units performing outside expected statistical variation `[01:00]`.
- **OPCS 4.5** — the UK procedure coding system used to record which specific surgical procedure was performed,
  referenced as roughly analogous to DRG-style coding elsewhere `[00:00]`.

## Analogies worth reusing

None stated — the talk presents national administrative data directly.

## Source-stated confidence

Dias discloses no conflicts of interest. He directly names a striking, hard-to-explain-away example (two units
20 miles apart with a 3x rate difference) rather than only presenting the abstract funnel plot, and explicitly
states this national dataset's 7% revision rate does not match commonly reported literature figures without
attempting to reconcile the discrepancy — flagging it as an open inconsistency rather than resolving it in
favor of either source.

revealing substantial, hard-to-fully-explain variation in how the disease is actually treated in practice.
**Relevant to `-PUJvVwlJis`** and `EAU0emyHHOg` (Dias's own PEM/URAM outcome measure talks, same presenter) —

procedure choice already vary this widely. **Relevant to `93IWNYYqCjs`** (Warwick's "what we may never know"
talk) — this talk's finding that an 8-fold rate variation reflects preference rather than evidence is a

Warwick argues makes randomized comparison across techniques so difficult to design cleanly.
