# Dr. Marike van Beuge: "Wnt Pathway Signals" 2015 Dupuytren Symposium

## Central claim

**Screening 84 Wnt pathway genes across Dupuytren's nodules versus matched control tissue, van Beuge's group
found widespread dysregulation at multiple pathway levels — including strongly downregulated endogenous Wnt
antagonists — and showed that this antagonist downregulation, itself driven by TGF-beta, sensitizes normal
fibroblasts to canonical Wnt/beta-catenin signaling, evidence of direct crosstalk between the two major
profibrotic pathways.** She summarizes the throughline directly: **"there are many, many levels of Wnt
signaling that are dysregulated in Dupuytren's disease, and there's also a strong case for there being
crosstalk between [these] different profibrotic pathways"** `[05:08]`.

## Building on prior genetic findings

`[00:00]`–`[01:03]` — Prior genetic studies (referenced earlier in the same symposium session) had already
identified the **Wnt pathway as dysregulated**, with mutations found across a striking number of Wnt-related
genes and increased **beta-catenin expression**. But those findings left gaps: **no correlation was found
between beta-catenin expression and disease recurrence**, and **little correlation between expression of the
genes coding for Wnt proteins themselves and beta-catenin dysregulation** — motivating this study's decision to
examine **more levels of the pathway**: receptors and downstream mediators, not just the initial ligand genes.

## Screening design

`[01:03]`–`[02:05]` — **84 genes** were checked across **12 patients' nodule tissue versus matched, unaffected
control fascia**. An unsupervised cluster analysis based on this gene panel **cleanly distinguished nodule from
control tissue**, confirming real, systematic expression differences exist across the pathway. No single
canonical Wnt activator gene reached significance except **WNT7B**, which was upregulated but with high
variability across patients (not statistically significant) — consistent with a similar, earlier finding from
genome-wide studies. Some **non-canonical Wnt pathway components** were also found upregulated, verified at the
protein level (data not shown in the talk for time).

## WISP1 — clearly dysregulated, but no demonstrated function in culture

`[02:05]`–`[04:07]` — **WISP1**, previously shown to be upregulated, was confirmed here at the protein level
too, with consistent expression shown across representative images and quantified in **10 patients**. To test
whether WISP1 plays a functional role, van Beuge's group **knocked down WISP1 with siRNA** in cells isolated
directly from nodules (myofibroblasts), achieving a clean knockdown at both mRNA and protein level. However,
this knockdown **did not affect fibrotic marker expression**: no change in **ACTA2** (encoding alpha-smooth-
muscle actin), an actual **increase in collagen III**, and no change in a further fibrotic marker gene. Van
Beuge's stated interpretation: despite WISP1's clear dysregulation in tissue, **"we were not able in vitro to
show a functional role for WISP1 in this setting — it might just be a marker, [or] it might be the [culture]
system that we used"** `[04:07]` — explicitly leaving open whether the negative result reflects biology or a
limitation of the in vitro model.

## Endogenous antagonists and TGF-beta crosstalk

`[04:07]`–`[05:08]` — Among the most strongly downregulated genes: **four endogenous antagonists of Wnt
signaling**. This downregulation could be **reproduced in normal fibroblasts simply by adding TGF-beta1** —
itself a known profibrotic compound present in diseased tissue — suggesting direct crosstalk between the TGF-
beta and Wnt signaling pathways. Testing this further: normal fibroblasts stimulated with a **low dose of
TGF-beta alone** showed **no nuclear beta-catenin expression** (beta-catenin remained present elsewhere in the
cell). But when the four antagonist genes were **knocked down with siRNA first**, that same low TGF-beta dose
was now **sufficient to cause nuclear beta-catenin translocation** — indicating that TGF-beta-mediated
antagonist downregulation is what sensitizes cells to Wnt/beta-catenin signaling, describing a **positive
feedback loop** between the two pathways.

## Glossary terms introduced

- **WISP1** — a Wnt/beta-catenin-driven gene found consistently upregulated in Dupuytren's nodule tissue at
  both mRNA and protein level `[02:05]` (also discussed in `6aG_CIYTX8A` as mechanically responsive).
- **Endogenous Wnt antagonists** — a set of four genes found strongly downregulated in nodule tissue,
  themselves shown to be suppressed by TGF-beta exposure `[04:07]`.

## Source-stated confidence

Van Beuge discloses no financial conflicts. She explicitly credits the prior session's speaker for framing
context and is transparent about a genuine negative result — WISP1's clear tissue-level dysregulation did not
translate into a demonstrable functional effect when knocked down in cultured cells — offering two possible,
unresolved explanations (WISP1 as a passive marker, or a limitation of the culture system) rather than forcing
an interpretation. She frames the TGF-beta/Wnt crosstalk finding as a "strong case," appropriately confident
language for a result demonstrated through a specific, controlled knockdown-plus-stimulation experiment.

## Conflicts with other sources

**Directly relevant to `6aG_CIYTX8A`** (the mechanical-stretch talk discussing WISP1 and NRG1 as beta-catenin-
driven, mechanosensitive genes) — that talk shows WISP1 is upregulated by mechanical stretch; this talk adds
that WISP1's upregulation in actual diseased tissue does not, on its own, appear to drive fibrotic marker
expression in cultured nodule cells, together painting WISP1 as a robust disease marker whose precise causal
role remains unresolved. **Directly relevant to `T7VnrFtGnfY`** (Nanchahal's TNF talk), which separately
identifies TNF-driven GSK-3-beta inhibition and beta-catenin preservation as a crosstalk mechanism specific to
Dupuytren's palmar fibroblasts — together these two talks describe **two independent profibrotic pathways
(TNF and TGF-beta) each converging on canonical Wnt/beta-catenin signaling** through different molecular
routes, a notable point of convergence between the two labs' findings worth surfacing wherever Wnt signaling is
discussed elsewhere in this library.
