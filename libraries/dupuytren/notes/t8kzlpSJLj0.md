# Dr. Chao Zhou "Collagenase Injection versus Limited Fasciectomy" 2015 Dupuytren Symposium

## Central claim

**Using propensity score matching on a real-world cohort, Zhou's group found collagenase and limited
fasciectomy produced similar MP joint contracture correction, while limited fasciectomy was more effective at
the PIP joint — but collagenase patients reported significantly larger functional and satisfaction gains, and
every serious adverse event in the study occurred in the surgical group.** Zhou states the joint-specific
finding directly: **"for affected MP joints, both techniques were similar in terms of contracture correction,
but for affected PIP joints, LF was slightly more effective — however, the larger increase[s] following
collagenase show[] that the treatment has [an] effect [on] best recovery"** `[04:06]`.

## Answering a challenge from the previous day

`[00:00]` — Zhou opens by directly acknowledging a point raised the day before by Dr. Warwick (see
`93IWNYYqCjs`): that a genuinely valid comparison between Dupuytren's treatments, via randomization or clever
study design, is difficult to achieve — but states plainly, **"I think we have to at least try."** His stated
purpose: while collagenase's efficacy is already established through high-quality multinational randomized
trials, **direct comparisons against established surgical technique in actual clinical practice remain rare**
— this study is an attempt to fill that specific gap using a non-randomized, real-world cohort.

## Study design

`[01:03]`–`[02:03]` — A **prospective cohort, 2011–2014**, including both **primary and recurrent** cases;
excluding **multiple-digit involvement** and any **concomitant confounding hand condition.** Collagenase was
dosed per manufacturer instruction (0.25mL for MP contractures, 0.20mL for PIP contractures), with manipulation
attempted 24–72 hours later. **Primary outcome**: residual contracture, assessed 6–12 weeks post-treatment.
**Secondary outcomes**: patient-reported hand function via the **Michigan Hand Questionnaire (MHQ)** and
adverse events.

## Propensity score matching

`[02:03]`–`[03:04]` — Anticipating baseline differences between the two non-randomized treatment groups,
Zhou's team used **propensity score matching (PSM)** — a statistical method for balancing groups on many
observed confounders simultaneously, particularly useful where true randomization is impractical. From an
initial **397 patients**, applying eligibility criteria left **218 patients** (roughly half fasciectomy, half
collagenase). Before matching, baseline groups differed meaningfully: **MP contractures were on average more
advanced in the collagenase group**, while **PIP and DIP contractures were less advanced** in that same group
relative to the fasciectomy group. After computing individual propensity scores, **66 matched patients per
group** were identified — closely comparable on all baseline characteristics except which treatment they
received.

## Results

`[03:04]`–`[04:06]` — In the matched cohort:

- **MP joint contracture correction**: **no significant difference** between treatments.
- **PIP joint contracture correction**: **significant difference favoring limited fasciectomy.**
- **MHQ functional subscores, activities-of-daily-living satisfaction, and work performance**: **significantly
  larger in the collagenase group**; other MHQ domains showed no significant difference.
- **Serious adverse events**: **all occurred in the fasciectomy group** — three cases of tenosynovitis
  requiring intervention and one nerve injury, versus **zero serious adverse events in the collagenase
  group.** Mild complications were **not compared**, since many are inherently specific to one treatment
  (collagenase) and wouldn't offer a fair like-for-like comparison.

## Conclusion

`[04:06]`–`[05:08]` — Zhou frames this as one of the first studies to use propensity score matching
specifically to compare collagenase against limited fasciectomy directly. Summary: **similar MP correction,
slightly better PIP correction with LF, but larger functional and satisfaction gains with collagenase** —
suggesting collagenase may offer a more favorable overall recovery experience even where its raw contracture
correction at the PIP joint falls short of surgery. He explicitly frames the study's methodological
contribution as demonstrating the value of propensity score matching for comparative-effectiveness research in
Dupuytren's disease, where true randomized comparison remains difficult.

## Source-stated confidence

Zhou is explicit about the study's core methodological trade-off: it directly addresses Warwick's argument that
randomization is impractical, while being transparent this is an observational, not randomized, comparison,
relying on propensity score matching to approximate a fair comparison rather than achieving one through study
design directly. He reports the diverging results candidly — LF's PIP-joint advantage alongside collagenase's
functional/satisfaction advantage — rather than favoring either treatment in the framing, and explicitly notes
mild complications were excluded from comparison because they aren't meaningfully comparable across treatment
types.

## Conflicts with other sources

**Directly answers `93IWNYYqCjs`** (Warwick's "what we may never know" talk) — Zhou opens by explicitly naming
Warwick's argument that no valid comparative trial can be designed, and offers propensity score matching as a
practical, if imperfect, alternative approach rather than accepting Warwick's conclusion as final. **Directly
comparable to `nUVjZI2XCg0`** (Hovius's randomized PALF-vs-LF trial) — both compare a minimally invasive
technique against limited fasciectomy; Hovius's randomized design found no significant MP-joint difference and
a non-significant PIP trend favoring surgery, broadly consistent with this study's matched-cohort finding of
similar MP correction and an LF advantage at the PIP joint. **Relevant to `SZEmawrCaK8`** (Zhou's own hand
function satisfaction talk, same presenter) — that talk's finding that preoperative hand appearance (not
treatment type) drives satisfaction after fasciectomy is a useful complement to this talk's finding that
collagenase patients report higher functional satisfaction despite comparable or lesser correction, together
suggesting patient-reported outcomes and objective contracture correction can diverge for more than one reason.
