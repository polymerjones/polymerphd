# Heinrich Seegenschmiedt MD, "Review of Radiotherapy for Morbus Dupuytren and Ledderhose" 2010 Miami Dupuytren Symposium

## Central claim

**Radiotherapy given early, before any extension deficit, produces roughly three times better long-term
control of Dupuytren's than no treatment — and a widely cited textbook image supposedly showing
radiation-caused tissue damage is, according to Seegenschmiedt, a misdiagnosis of ordinary failed surgical
healing.** His own quantified conclusion from long-term follow-up: **"we would say control probably three
times better... with regard to surgery, and probably 2.5 times better... with regard to any [type of]
progression"** `[09:16]`–`[10:18]`.

## Correcting a specific textbook error

`[02:08]` — Seegenschmiedt directly challenges images labeled "radionecrosis" in textbooks (he names
Brenner's): **"I have seen this picture. None of them is a radionecrosis... this was tissue never healing
after operation... a result of surgery [possibly] interfering with radiation."** True radionecrosis, he
clarifies, requires irradiation followed by tissue death — not failed post-surgical wound healing being
misattributed to a radiation treatment the patient may have separately received at some point. He calls this
**"a complete misinterpretation of radiotherapy data."**

## Why timing is everything

`[02:08]`–`[06:12]` — Citing work from Rodemann's group in Tübingen, Seegenschmiedt frames the biological
window precisely: fibroblasts and myofibroblasts have **roughly 50 lifetime replication cycles**; in
Dupuytren's, damaged-system fibroblasts continue proliferating, differentiate into myofibroblasts, and once
they reach the **post-mitotic phase, the contracture is set.** Radiation works by interfering with this
proliferation-to-differentiation cascade — **"the only stage amenable to radiotherapy is the phase when
fibroblasts are proliferating."** He proposes a **radiotherapy-specific staging threshold distinct from
Tubiana's surgical staging**: **10 degrees as the practical limit** — nodules and early extension deficit
only, never established 30–40 degree contracture, which is "already scarred tissue" where "radiation plays
no role." He criticizes the standard clinical advice to simply wait for surgical-threshold contracture
before intervening at all: **"why should patients wait, when you have a target cell amenable to
treatment?"**

## The Erlangen/Essen trial numbers

`[07:12]`–`[09:16]` — More than **600 patients over 14 years**, with **412 followed for over 5 years**
(the data presented is restricted to that 5-year-plus group). Patients self-selected radiotherapy versus
observation; those choosing radiotherapy were randomized between **7×3 Gray (lower dose)** and **10×3 Gray
(traditional higher dose)**, with treatment fields extending 2cm longitudinally and 1cm laterally beyond
palpable disease. At final follow-up (end of 2009, minimum 5 years, longest 14):

| Group | Progression rate | Required surgery |
|---|---|---|
| **Control (no treatment)** | 61% | 31% |
| **Lower-dose radiotherapy (7×3Gy)** | 23% | 12% |
| **Higher-dose radiotherapy (10×3Gy)** | 18% | 7% |

Multivariate analysis found **radiation-versus-control status was the single strongest predictor** (highest
odds ratio), with **disease stage at treatment** the second-strongest — even 10–20 degrees of existing
deficit reduced effectiveness compared to treating at zero. **Smoking and multi-finger involvement** were
weaker, univariate-only risk factors for worse outcomes. Toxicity was low, with **chronic effects limited to
mild dry skin.**

## Ledderhose disease: separate, dedicated data

`[11:20]`–`[15:26]` — Seegenschmiedt reports the **comorbidity rate directly: about 50% of Ledderhose
patients have Dupuytren's disease, and about 20% of Dupuytren's patients have Ledderhose disease** — and
notes the anatomical site differs mechanically from the hand: **plantar disease centers on the first and
second digits, not the fourth**, attributed to different mechanical forces at the foot. Standard surgical
management of Ledderhose is described as poor: **local excision carries about an 85% relapse rate**, often
progressing to subtotal excision, which itself still carries **about a 50% relapse rate** — "you don't see
large [surgical case] series [reported] because of this dismal result."

His own Ledderhose radiotherapy cohort: **143 patients (about 20–25% of his total Dupuytren's-era
population), 138 with over one year of follow-up**, evenly split by sex, **222 feet treated, 54 untreated
feet serving as internal controls** (patients who declined radiation). Treatment: **two courses of 5×3
Gray, 8–12 weeks apart**, chosen specifically to accommodate the disease's slow progression. Cost is
explicitly stated as a practical advantage: **about €300 (orthovoltage) to €600 (linear accelerator) in
Germany — "1/10 of the cost you have with any type of surgery plus recurrence surgery."** Results: **grade
1 toxicity in 21%** (same mild dry skin as hand treatment, no ulceration, no observed carcinogenesis);
**11% progression in the treated group versus 34% in controls** (three times more progression untreated);
**objective remission (nodule reduction, sometimes complete) in about 50%**; **symptomatic relief — itching,
tension, pressure sensation, pain — in more than two-thirds of patients**, including regained normal
walking in previously painful cases; **roughly 50% of otherwise likely surgical candidates avoided surgery**
via radiotherapy first; and **85% of patients reported better quality of life at one-year follow-up.**

## Symptoms and body signals addressed

- **Itching, tension sensation, pressure sensation, and pain in plantar (Ledderhose) nodules**, with
  specific symptomatic relief rates from radiotherapy `[14:23]`.
- **Difficulty walking normally due to plantar pain**, reported as reversible with treatment in a majority
  of cases `[14:23]`.
- **Progressive nodule and cord formation as the proliferative signal indicating radiotherapy candidacy**,
  distinguished sharply from established, already-scarred contracture `[02:08]`.
- **Dry skin as the primary chronic side effect of radiotherapy**, consistent across both hand and foot
  treatment `[10:18]`, `[13:22]`.

## Glossary terms introduced

- **Post-mitotic phase (as a disease endpoint)** — the point at which proliferating fibroblasts stop
  dividing and the contracture becomes fixed, framed as the process radiotherapy aims to interrupt before it
  is reached `[02:08]`.
- **Radiotherapy-specific staging (10-degree threshold)** — Seegenschmiedt's proposed practical cutoff for
  radiotherapy candidacy, distinct from surgical (Tubiana) staging `[04:10]`.

## Analogies worth reusing

None stated — the talk presents mechanism and trial data directly throughout.

## Source-stated confidence

Seegenschmiedt is explicit about study design limitations: the hand cohort was **not randomized between
treatment and control** (patients self-selected, only the dose arm was randomized), and the Ledderhose data
is labeled **"a first evaluation study,"** also not randomized against control. He directly credits a
collaborating surgeon's independent assessment that difficult post-irradiation surgical cases mostly reflect
difficult disease, not radiation damage, rather than asserting this himself alone. He closes by calling for
a **future controlled, multi-center study** as the next necessary step, rather than presenting current data
as final.
