# Dr. Michael Ng: "Genome-wide Association Study" 2015 Dupuytren Symposium

## Central claim

**Expanding an earlier nine-locus GWAS to a meta-analysis of roughly 8,000 cases and 12,000 controls, Ng's
group replicated all nine original regions and identified 15 new associated regions — and showed, through a
functional follow-up in patient-derived myofibroblasts, that the single most significant variant produces
roughly a five-fold difference in SFRP4 gene expression between risk and protective allele carriers.** Ng
states the functional result directly: **"there's almost a five-fold difference between the risk allele and
the protective allele... [indicating] the allele variant affects the expression of SFRP4"** `[05:04]`.

## Why genetics matters — the heritability evidence

`[00:00]`–`[01:00]` — Ng cites a sibling study published roughly three years earlier (attributed to Capstick)
comparing siblings of affected patients against the general population, finding siblings **4.5 times more
likely** to develop Dupuytren's, with an estimated **heritability of approximately 80%**. He frames this
alongside the roughly 20% of disease risk not attributable to genetic factors as clear evidence Dupuytren's is
a **complex, multifactorial, polygenic disease** — genetics playing a dominant but not exclusive role.

## Building on the earlier nine-locus study

`[01:00]`–`[03:01]` — A prior GWAS (published 2011, matching the study presented in `-3a_yiOu_3I`) had
identified **nine associated regions, six of them within the Wnt signaling pathway**. This study expanded the
approach with a larger sample specifically to find more functional variants, following the general statistical
principle that larger sample sizes are needed to detect additional true associations. **Discovery phase**:
roughly 4,000 cases and a large control population, genotyped on a chip assessing about half a million SNPs
(Illumina platform) — successfully **replicating 8 of the original 9 regions**, identifying **4 new regions
at genome-wide significance**, and **20 further suggestive regions**. A **replication cohort** of roughly
4,000 additional samples was then collected from the UK, Germany, and the Netherlands (with collaborator Paul
Werker credited by name), and combined with the discovery cohort in a **meta-analysis totaling roughly 8,000
cases and 12,000 controls.**

## Meta-analysis results

`[03:01]`–`[04:04]` — The meta-analysis **replicated all nine original regions** and identified **15 further
new associated regions** — work Ng states was published in a leading medical journal. The newly associated
genes were grouped into four functional categories:

1. **Cell differentiation and proliferation** — including the expected classic Wnt signaling pathway genes.
2. Genes including **BOP1** and **DDR2** (discoidin domain receptor 2) — DDR2 specifically noted as a receptor
   that binds collagen and regulates cell proliferation.
3. **Matrix remodeling regulation** — including an aggrecan-related gene, **MMP14**, and DDR2 again.
4. **Inflammation-related genes** — including a TGF-beta pathway gene and **GCKR**, noted as playing a role in
   the TNF-alpha pathway, alongside a broader set of transcription factor genes.

QQ-plot and Manhattan-plot analyses of the discovery cohort were shown to confirm no confounding bias in the
association results.

## Functional follow-up: SFRP4 and MMP14

`[04:04]`–`[06:07]` — Two specific hits were highlighted. The most statistically significant association
(p ≈ 3.35 × 10⁻⁹¹) was located near **SFRP4** (secreted frizzled-related protein 4, a Wnt pathway gene) and a
neighboring gene. To test whether this variant has a functional effect, the group assessed RNA expression
directly in **myofibroblasts disaggregated from palmar fascia of patients undergoing fasciectomy**, finding
**roughly a five-fold expression difference between risk-allele and protective-allele carriers** — direct
functional evidence the variant acts as an expression-affecting locus (an eQTL) for SFRP4. The second
highlighted hit, noted with a direct aside to a fellow researcher in the audience, was located in or near
**MMP14** on chromosome 14.

## Glossary terms introduced

- **SFRP4** — secreted frizzled-related protein 4, a Wnt pathway gene identified as the site of this study's
  most statistically significant genetic association, with confirmed differential expression by genotype
  `[05:04]`.
- **eQTL (expression quantitative trait locus)** — a genetic variant shown to affect the expression level of a
  nearby gene; the concept underlying the SFRP4 functional follow-up `[05:04]`.

## Source-stated confidence

Ng frames the sibling-study heritability figures (4.5x risk, ~80% heritability) as drawn from a separately
published study, not his own group's finding, and is explicit that roughly 20% of disease risk remains
attributable to non-genetic factors — avoiding an overstated genetic-determinism framing. He presents the
statistical confound checks (QQ plot) directly as part of validating the discovery-phase results, and
distinguishes the SFRP4 functional follow-up (direct expression data from patient myofibroblasts) as stronger,
more direct evidence than the association signal alone.

## Conflicts with other sources

**Directly extends `-3a_yiOu_3I`** (Dolmans's earlier GWAS talk, presenting the original nine-locus study this
work builds on) and **`-0JtjUrrU4o`** (a related GWAS talk) — this talk reports the same research program's
large-scale meta-analysis follow-up, replicating those earlier findings and adding 15 new loci with direct
functional validation for the top hit. **Directly relevant to `2Q5gm6FDZb0`** (Clark's biomarkers talk) — MMP14
appears independently in both talks: Clark's group found MMP14 protein correlating with preoperative total
extension deficit, while this study finds a genetic association near the MMP14 locus itself, two independent
lines of evidence converging on the same gene. **Directly relevant to `NjNR2dDZO4Q`** (van Beuge's Wnt pathway
talk) — SFRP4 is itself a Wnt pathway antagonist gene, adding a specific, genetically-validated candidate to
that talk's broader finding that endogenous Wnt antagonist genes are strongly downregulated in nodule tissue.
