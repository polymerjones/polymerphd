## Central claim

**Ectopic Dupuytren's disease — presentations outside the palm — and a documented case series of the
disease in non-Caucasian patients together argue against treating Dupuytren's as an exclusively Northern
European, hand-only condition.** Rayan describes ectopic sites including the sole of the foot (**Ledderhose
disease**) and the penis (**dorsal curvature, also known as Peyronie's disease**) `[00:04]`–`[01:05]`, and
separately reports a case series: **"we've done a study on 39 patients... only six patients [were] of
Northern European descent; the majority were of mixed ethnicity, including Hispanics, African-Americans,
and Native Americans"** `[04:11]`. He cites the non-Caucasian occurrence of Dupuytren's as **"not a new
concept," described by Anderson in 1891"** `[04:11]`.

## Risk factors and disease patterns

`[02:07]`–`[06:15]` — Legible fragments establish that Rayan distinguishes Dupuytren's **diathesis** —
described as a more aggressive pattern — from typical disease, and separately identifies **trauma and prior
surgery as a primary risk factor**, with **distal radius fracture accompanied by palmar ecchymosis (bruising)**
named specifically `[05:13]`. He also references a staging classification (the presenter's name for it is
not reliably legible in the captions) distinguishing early, intermediate, and late-stage disease by degree
of joint contracture `[02:07]`–`[03:09]`. A genetic association study is mentioned — described as mapping
association across multiple chromosomes and identifying candidate susceptibility genes — but the specific
genes and chromosome numbers are not confidently recoverable from this transcript `[07:16]`–`[08:18]`.

## Symptoms and body signals addressed

- **Nodules and cords in the palm, described as the defining clinical and pathologic findings of the
  disease** `[00:04]`.
- **Plantar nodules/cords (Ledderhose disease) as an ectopic presentation** `[00:04]`.
- **Penile curvature (Peyronie's disease) as an ectopic presentation**, described as a related but distinct
  condition with its own, non-surgical treatment approach `[01:05]`, `[06:15]`.
- **Palmar bruising following distal radius fracture, associated with later Dupuytren's-like changes**
  `[05:13]`.

## Glossary terms introduced

- **Ectopic Dupuytren's disease** — presentations of the disease process outside the palm, including
  plantar and penile sites `[00:04]`.
- **Dupuytren's diathesis** — referenced as a distinct, more aggressive disease pattern, though the
  transcript does not clearly capture its defining criteria `[06:15]`.
