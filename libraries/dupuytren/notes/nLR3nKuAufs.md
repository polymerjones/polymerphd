# Wayne Hellstrom MD, "Medical Management of Peyronie's Disease" 2010 Miami Dupuytren Symposium

## Central claim

**Peyronie's disease is far more common, more heterogeneous, and more psychologically damaging than the
field long assumed — and, like Dupuytren's, incomplete understanding of its pathophysiology is what limits
treatment.** Hellstrom draws the parallel explicitly in his close: **"we do not have a full understanding
of the pathophysiology, and this in itself compromises our evolution of treatment strategies — and I think
we all recognize that it's very similar with Dupuytren's contracture"** `[13:21]`.

## How common it actually is, and its overlap with Dupuytren's

`[01:03]`–`[03:07]` — Long assumed to affect **under 1% of men**, a late-1990s Cologne, Germany male-aging
study found actual prevalence around **3.2%**, and **"this was quite a traumatic finding for many of us
urologists."** Subsequent studies in Europe, South America, and the US have found rates **up to 9%.**
Clinical presentation in the Cologne cohort: **angulation in 84%, painful erections in 46%, erectile
dysfunction in 40%, and all three together in only 1%.** Most directly relevant to this library: **"up to
20% of men who have Peyronie's disease may have Dupuytren's contracture"** — a specific figure quantifying
the association other sources in this collection mention only qualitatively.

## Why patients don't talk about it

`[03:07]`–`[05:09]` — Hellstrom frames this explicitly as a psychological disease as much as a physical one:
**"it impinges upon men's self-image... many of them are afraid to confront the opposite sex, or to talk
about it to anybody... they suffer in silence."** A patient-run advocacy site (peyroniesassociation.org)
receives **400,000 hits a month worldwide**, evidence of the demand for information patients aren't getting
from clinical encounters. He also stresses the condition is **genuinely heterogeneous** — presentation,
curvature direction, plaque location, and severity vary enough between patients that he cautions **"you
shouldn't think of [Peyronie's] as the same type of condition"** from patient to patient, drawing an
explicit parallel to the same caution he'd extend to Dupuytren's. A major, under-recognized complaint: **loss
of penile length**, which he describes as a genuine loss of self-identity for many patients — "some of the
most unhappy patients in the world even after surgery."

## Mechanism and natural history

`[05:09]`–`[07:13]` — Proposed pathway: **minor penile microtrauma during intercourse → microhemorrhage →
fibrin deposition → scar (plaque) formation**, with **Devine's "rib theory"** proposing the dorsal septum is
specifically prone to this kind of micro-fracture and bleeding — consistent with **about 70% of men
presenting with a dorsal plaque.** The disease has a defined **early/active phase lasting roughly 6–16
months**, during which surgery is avoided (**"we don't typically operate... before 12 months"**); pain
specifically tends to resolve within **5–7 months.** Natural history without treatment: **about 12% improve
within a year, 40–45% get worse, and 40% remain stable.** Average age at presentation: **57.**

## Non-surgical treatments, rated by evidence quality

`[07:13]`–`[11:17]`:

| Treatment | Evidence |
|---|---|
| **Oral therapies** | Few placebo-controlled studies; **"for the most part they don't work,"** despite marketing claims — studies are generally poorly designed, often lacking a placebo arm. |
| **Interlesional steroids** | **Not recommended** — causes tissue atrophy and damage to surrounding structures; operating afterward "is a disaster." |
| **Interlesional verapamil** | Plausible rationale (reduces fibroblast proliferation), but **only one placebo-controlled study (14 patients) showed benefit.** Inexpensive and widely insurance-covered. |
| **Interlesional interferon** (Hellstrom's own research area) | Inhibits fibroblast proliferation and modulates collagen activity. His own multicenter placebo-controlled trial found **significant curvature reduction** and **significantly better subjective improvement** in the treated group. Rated **"between a grade A and grade B recommendation."** |
| **Interlesional collagenase (Xiaflex)** | Phase 2 trial (completed shortly before this talk) showed **significant curvature improvement and bother-score improvement** versus placebo at 36 weeks — but the benefit was concentrated in patients who also received **"modeling"** (mechanical straightening by the surgeon after injection, sometimes producing an audible crack/pop); **without modeling, the improvement was not significantly different from placebo.** Safety profile was consistent with prior pilot studies — mainly pain and bruising, no significant systemic or local complications. Phase 3 trials were about to begin at the time of this talk. |

## Symptoms and body signals addressed

- **Penile angulation, painful erections, and erectile dysfunction**, with specific co-occurrence rates
  given `[02:05]`.
- **Loss of penile length**, named specifically as an under-recognized driver of patient distress `[05:09]`.
- **Dorsal plaque formation**, the most common presentation pattern `[06:10]`.
- **Concurrent Dupuytren's contracture, in up to 20% of Peyronie's patients** `[01:03]`.

## Glossary terms introduced

- **Devine's rib theory** — the proposed mechanism explaining why the dorsal penile septum is especially
  prone to the microtrauma believed to initiate plaque formation `[05:09]`.
- **Modeling** — mechanical straightening of the penis by the surgeon shortly after an interlesional
  injection, shown here to be necessary for collagenase's benefit to reach statistical significance
  `[10:17]`.
- **Bother score** — a patient-reported outcome measure used in the collagenase trial, distinct from the
  physical curvature measurement `[10:17]`.

## Analogies worth reusing

None stated — the talk presents epidemiology and trial data directly.

## Source-stated confidence

Hellstrom is explicit about evidence quality throughout, rating treatments individually rather than
presenting them as uniformly promising: oral therapies are stated to not work despite marketing claims,
steroids are stated as actively contraindicated, verapamil's evidence is called out as resting on a single
small study, and even collagenase's phase 2 benefit is qualified as dependent on the modeling technique
being used alongside it. He closes by naming the field's shared problem with Dupuytren's directly rather
than overstating either condition's current treatment maturity.

## Conflicts with other sources

**Directly relevant to `-jLfWXX3YUM`** (Seven Questions), which dismisses **topical** verapamil for
Peyronie's/Ledderhose partly on the grounds that it likely doesn't penetrate skin thickness — this talk
describes a different delivery route, **interlesional injection**, with its own (still weak, one-study)
evidence base; the two aren't in conflict, but should not be conflated, since the route of administration
changes the whole argument about whether the drug reaches its target. **Extends the Peyronie's/Ledderhose
associations mentioned in `Y6IZZLF-erw`, `AbY1KVhTVAo`, and `HE6nBpteAAo`** — this talk supplies the first
quantified overlap figure (20% of Peyronie's patients having Dupuytren's) behind those qualitative
mentions. **Relevant to `9LY5fyLw618`** (Hurst's collagenase talk) — the same drug (collagenase/Xiaflex)
appears here in a second, distinct clinical application, with its own separate trial pipeline and its own
dependency on a technique-specific step (modeling) for full benefit.
