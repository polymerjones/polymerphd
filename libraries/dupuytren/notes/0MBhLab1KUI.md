# Dr. Clayton Peimer "Five-Year CORDLESS Data" 2015 Dupuytren Symposium

## Central claim

**Across a 950-patient, five-year prospective follow-up of collagenase-treated joints, 84% of successfully
treated joints needed no additional treatment at all — and no serious long-term safety risks emerged, even
though recurrence by strict criteria still affected nearly half of joints treated.** Peimer's closing
summary states both facts plainly, without softening either: **"eighty-four percent of successfully treated
joints had no additional treatment, which is encouraging"** alongside recurrence figures of **47% (20-degree
criteria) or 32% (30-degree criteria)** `[08:15]`.

## What CORDLESS is, and why the definitions matter

`[00:00]`–`[02:04]` — **CORDLESS** is a prospective five-year follow-up of patients drawn from **five prior
collagenase trials** (CORD I, CORD II, Joint I, Joint II, and a CORD I extension study), covering anyone who
received one or more collagenase injections and at least one post-treatment assessment in those studies,
with **annual reevaluation starting two years after treatment.** Peimer notes the general literature's
recurrence figures vary enormously (roughly 12–100% depending on the technique and combination of
procedures compared) — attributed to **inconsistent follow-up periods and inconsistent recurrence
definitions across studies**, which is exactly why this study adopts strict, explicit criteria:

- **Successfully treated joint**: 0–5 degrees of contracture within 30 days.
- **Measurably improved joint**: at least 20 degrees of contracture reduction.
- **Recurrence (after successful correction)**: a contracture increase of 20 degrees or more, *plus* either
  a palpable cord or any secondary medical/surgical intervention.
- **Non-durable response**: the same recurrence pattern, but specifically in joints that only achieved
  partial (not full 0–5-degree) correction to begin with.
- A **post-hoc 30-degree threshold** was also calculated, specifically to allow comparison against the more
  commonly reported figure in older published literature.

## The cohort and five-year recurrence figures

`[03:07]`–`[04:08]` — **950 eligible patients, over 1,000 joints**, each receiving three or fewer
injections; **156 joints (about 12%) were not effectively treated** at baseline. Five-year recurrence in
successfully treated joints, by joint and by threshold:

| Joint | 20° recurrence | 30° recurrence |
|---|---|---|
| **All joints** | 47% | 32% |
| **MP (MCP)** | 40% | 26% |
| **PIP** | 66% | 46% |

## A counterintuitive severity finding, flagged honestly

`[04:08]` — Looking at pretreatment contracture severity: **at the PIP joint, more severe cases had higher
recurrence, as expected.** But **at the MP joint, the pattern reversed — higher-severity joints appeared to
do *better* (lower recurrence) than lower-severity joints.** Peimer doesn't try to explain this away,
stating plainly: **"the MP joints are counterintuitive... maybe we need an even larger study"** — treating it
as an open finding rather than forcing a narrative onto it.

## When recurrence actually happens

`[05:10]` — The time course, not just the endpoint, is informative: recurrence risk **rises most sharply in
years 2–3 (about a 15% increase)**, then **drops to about 7% the following year**, and continues flattening
toward year 5 — the same general pattern held for both MP and PIP joints. Practical implication: **most
patients who are going to recur do so within the second and third year**, not steadily throughout follow-up.

## Safety data

`[06:11]`–`[07:14]` — **365 patients (57%) had an adverse event** of some kind — Peimer notes this figure is
inflated in part because the **FDA counts swelling itself as a reportable adverse event.** One specific,
almost amusing observation: a **decrease in measured circumference** was recorded as an adverse event in
some cases, which Peimer flags may actually reflect a **measurement artifact** (a change in how skin-to-skin
distance was measured) rather than a genuine physical change, referencing a colleague's separate presentation
on thumb web-space outcomes. **181 serious adverse events occurred, but none were judged treatment-related**
— they included unrelated incidents like car accidents and broken bones, reported per protocol regardless of
cause. **No allergic reactions occurred in this specific series**, though Peimer notes the general
possibility of an allergic reaction to any drug should still be anticipated, with resuscitation equipment
kept available. **Tendon rupture: 1 case in this series, 3 across the full clinical trial program, and 26
reported worldwide** out of roughly **50,000 total treated cases** at the time of the analysis — plus one
pulley injury and one ligament injury. These tendon-related events were **"late"** — occurring within the
first 30 days post-injection, but not at the time of manipulation itself.

## Symptoms and body signals addressed

- **Contracture recurrence timing concentrated in years 2–3**, with a specific, quantified drop-off pattern
  afterward `[05:10]`.
- **Swelling as a formally counted adverse event** under FDA reporting criteria, distinct from more clinically
  significant complications `[06:11]`.
- **A possible measurement artifact (decreased circumference) mistaken for a genuine physical change** —
  flagged as worth reconsidering rather than accepted at face value `[06:11]`.

## Glossary terms introduced

- **CORDLESS** — the five-year prospective follow-up study pooling patients across the original CORD/Joint
  collagenase trial cohorts `[01:01]`.
- **Non-durable response** — recurrence-pattern deterioration specifically in joints that never achieved
  full initial correction, distinguished from recurrence after a genuinely successful correction `[02:04]`.

## Analogies worth reusing

None stated — the talk presents trial data directly.

## Source-stated confidence

Peimer states upfront that this talk is **"data-driven and on-label,"** distinguishing it from a separate,
explicitly off-label talk he was giving the following day — a clear, stated boundary around what evidence
supports what claim. He reports the counterintuitive MP-joint severity finding without forcing an
explanation, and is direct that the elevated overall adverse-event percentage partly reflects a broad FDA
reporting definition (counting swelling) rather than necessarily reflecting clinically significant harm.
