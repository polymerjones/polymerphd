# Dr. Marianna Kruithof de Julio "PDGFR-β Targeted Interferon Gamma" 2015 Dupuytren Symposium

## Central claim

**Kruithof de Julio's group built a cyclic peptide that links part of interferon-gamma to a PDGF receptor
beta-targeting sequence, activating interferon-gamma's anti-fibrotic signaling specifically on myofibroblasts
while avoiding macrophages — and, tested in a purpose-built ex vivo tissue model, this compound reduced
fibrotic load by day 7, performing comparably to pirfenidone, the reference anti-fibrotic drug used as a
benchmark.** She frames the compound's central design logic directly: using a cyclic peptide **"to target the
activation of the interferon gamma signaling only on the myofibroblasts, and not on the macrophages, in order
to not have an [unwanted] anti-inflammatory response"** `[00:00]`.

## Why target interferon-gamma this way

`[00:00]`–`[01:01]` — **Interferon-gamma (IFN-γ)** is described as a major cytokine playing a dual role: both
**anti-fibrotic** and **pro-inflammatory.** Because of its anti-fibrotic importance, a research group
developed (in 2011) a **cyclic peptide** — combining a portion of interferon-gamma with a sequence targeting
**PDGF receptor beta (PDGFR-β)** — specifically so the compound's anti-fibrotic signaling activates only on
cells expressing that receptor (myofibroblasts), rather than triggering interferon-gamma's separate,
unwanted pro-inflammatory effect on macrophages.

## A purpose-built 3D ex vivo tissue model

`[01:01]`–`[02:02]` — Echoing a concern raised by an earlier speaker at the same symposium about the difficulty
of reproducing results in standard cell culture, Kruithof de Julio's group (crediting PhD student Sofia's work
directly) developed a **three-dimensional ex vivo culture system**: a plastic surgeon excises Dupuytren's
tissue, which is sectioned and placed on a nitrocellulose membrane, where it can be grown for **up to 21
days.** This system allows both compound/functional testing directly on real diseased tissue, and — in a
technique run in the reverse direction from another speaker's approach — applying **mechanical tension** to
the tissue and measuring its release response.

## Findings

`[02:02]`–`[03:02]` — Using **second harmonic generation live imaging** of collagen, the group tracked how the
extracellular matrix rearranges over time following compound treatment. RNA sequencing of their tissue
database confirmed **sufficient PDGF receptor expression** compared to control tissue, as hoped. Imaging
showed **PDGFR co-expressed with alpha-smooth-muscle actin** — but critically, while alpha-smooth-muscle actin
is also expressed on blood vessels (making it a less specific marker), **PDGFR is not**, making it a
**more specific target for myofibroblasts** specifically. Applying the compound and comparing day 3 to day 7:
a measurable **reduction in "fibrotic load"** (quantified via smooth-muscle-actin, collagen type I, and
collagen type III expression), performing comparably to the group's chosen **"gold standard" comparator,
pirfenidone**, which also showed an effect on extracellular matrix rearrangement in this model.

## Glossary terms introduced

- **PDGFR-β (platelet-derived growth factor receptor beta)** — the receptor used to specifically target
  myofibroblasts for interferon-gamma-based anti-fibrotic signaling `[00:00]`.
- **Second harmonic generation imaging** — the live-imaging technique used to visualize collagen and track
  extracellular matrix rearrangement over time in the ex vivo tissue model `[02:02]`.
- **Fibrotic load** — the group's composite measure (smooth-muscle-actin, collagen type I, collagen type III
  expression) used to quantify treatment effect `[03:02]`.

## Source-stated confidence

Kruithof de Julio discloses a specific collaboration with a small pharmaceutical company based in Groningen.
She presents this as ongoing, early-stage work — explicitly describing the tissue model as still being
developed and refined (crediting a current collaborator, Rajeev, as actively working on it) rather than a
finished or validated therapeutic pipeline. The comparison against pirfenidone is offered as a benchmark
against an existing anti-fibrotic reference compound, not as evidence the new compound outperforms it.
