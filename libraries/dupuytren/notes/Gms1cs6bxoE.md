# Dr. Stephen Coleman: "The Australian Research Perspective" 2015 Dupuytren Symposium

## Central claim

**International multi-site trials are the only practical way to reach the sample sizes Dupuytren's research
needs, but the coordination overhead is substantial and mostly invisible in the published results.**
Coleman's own comparison: **"the five-year follow-up... presented this meeting was 950 subjects [and] 1,081
joints — it'd take us a long while to do that in Australia"** alone `[03:03]`.

## Why go international at all

`[01:02]`–`[04:05]` — Australia's population and geographic isolation make large single-country studies
impractical; Coleman notes wryly that Antarctica is closer to Australia than most of the rest of the world,
and **"if I want to do a study on Dupuytren's, I can't go to Antarctica — I've got to go somewhere else."**
Since joining collagenase trials in 2006, he's participated in **multiple international studies**, mostly
spanning Australia, the EU, and the US. Stated advantages: much larger achievable sample sizes, population
diversity that reduces genetic/country-specific bias, and shared cost burden across countries with different
funding sources (industry, foundations, charities). He also flags a **patient-population matching
consideration**: Dupuytren's is rare in Asian populations, so an Asia-based trial site would be a poor
choice, just as a melanoma trial would favor Australia over Scandinavia.

## The real costs of running one

`[02:03]`–`[08:11]` — **Data collection is straightforward; tissue collection is not** — Coleman describes
a genetics collaboration where shipping frozen tissue to Los Angeles became cost-prohibitive to sustain.
Other concrete friction points: **standardizing instruments across sites** (goniometers shipped from the US
so every site used the same device), **refrigerated drug transport** (collagenase imported specifically from
the US), **blood/urine/tissue sample shipping and preservation**, **customs risk**, and **regulatory
variation** — collagenase's US approval significantly preceded its availability in Australia and parts of
Europe, meaning a trial's home country might not even have access to the resulting drug for years after
contributing data. He also describes needing in-person alignment meetings despite the cost: a 2008 trip from
Australia to Chicago (over 20 hours flying each way) purely to review initial trial results face-to-face.

## The ethics and oversight infrastructure required

`[08:11]`–`[11:12]` — All trials Coleman describes were run under the **Declaration of Helsinki's 35
principles**, with patient welfare treated as taking precedence over everything else. He strongly recommends
using a **contract research organization (CRO)** with international accreditation for logistics, drug
import, recruitment, and recordkeeping — quoting the FDA's own standard directly: **"if it isn't written
down, it didn't happen — and similarly, if it isn't written down correctly, it didn't happen."** Local
**human research ethics committees (HRCs)** are required and vary in approval speed by country; using an
established one (he names Bellberry) sped up his own approvals. All participants require full informed
consent covering risks, benefits, funding sources, and confidentiality, with **every adverse event —
"if they sneeze, you've got to write it down"** — logged. Each site needs a **principal investigator**
personally accountable for protocol adherence, who — as a practical trade-off — typically ends up as lead
author on resulting publications.

## A first-hand account: the first double-dose injection

`[05:08]`–`[06:09]` — In January 2010, Coleman performed the **first double-dose collagenase injection** in
a proof-of-concept trial, with genuine uncertainty about outcome severity: **"we didn't know what was going
to happen when we put double the dose — where the hand was going to fall off, or what was going to
happen."** Auxilium's vice president flew from Philadelphia and the company's chief pharmacist flew from
the UK specifically to observe the result in person. The trial proceeded safely and was later published,
followed by the full multi-dose trial series.

## Glossary terms introduced

- **Contract research organization (CRO)** — a specialized third-party organization handling trial
  logistics, drug import, recruitment, and data retention `[09:11]`.
- **Human research ethics committee (HRC)** — the required local ethics review body for any human trial
  `[10:12]`.
- **Principal investigator** — the single, personally accountable role at each trial site `[12:14]`.

## Analogies worth reusing

- **Antarctica being closer to Australia than most of the world** — a light, memorable illustration of why
  Australian researchers default to international collaboration out of geographic necessity `[01:02]`.

## Source-stated confidence

Presented as a personal, practical account of firsthand trial experience rather than a formal methods
paper — Coleman states his own financial relationships with the sponsoring companies upfront and frames his
recommendations ("I strongly recommend...") as his own conclusions from direct involvement, not as
universal prescription.

countries. **Useful operational context for any note citing multi-site trial data** in this library (e.g.
