# David O'Gorman PhD, "Primary Dupuytren's Disease Cell Biology" 2010 Miami Dupuytren Symposium

## Central claim

**Dupuytren's disease appears to spread not because nodule cells physically migrate along the fascia, but
because they secrete factors into the extracellular matrix that convert adjacent, genetically normal
fascia cells into disease cells.** O'Gorman states the reframe directly at the outset: **"the extracellular
matrix in Dupuytren's disease is not some kind of passive environment in which Dupuytren's disease cells
grow — it's in fact an active component of this disease... a mediator of disease progression and
potentially recurrence"** `[00:04]`, arguing this points toward **targeting the ECM itself, not just the
disease cells, as a treatment strategy.**

## Two competing hypotheses, tested directly

`[03:07]`–`[09:12]` — O'Gorman's lab compared Dupuytren's disease (DD) cells against **patient-matched,
genetically identical control cells** — "phenotypically normal" fascia taken from the same patient,
roughly a centimeter away from affected tissue — to isolate disease-driven differences from ordinary genetic
variation. Two hypotheses, and their testable predictions:

1. **Cell migration hypothesis**: a nodule forms, its cells acquire migratory ability, move along the
   fascia, differentiate, and contract it into a cord. Predicts: nodule cells are inherently more migratory;
   cord cells are direct descendants of nodule cells; ECM changes should facilitate that migration.
2. **ECM-mediated disease hypothesis**: nodule cells instead secrete factors into the surrounding matrix
   that induce adjacent, otherwise-normal fascia cells to differentiate into disease cells themselves,
   spreading disease through the tissue without any cell relocation. Predicts: a matrix "conditioned" by
   disease cells alone should be able to convert normal cells, with no migration required.

## Testing migration: the hypothesis loses

`[05:09]`–`[08:10]` — Using fibroblast-populated collagen lattices (FPCLs), stressed for 72 hours and
allowed to differentiate into myofibroblasts, then transferred onto cell-free collagen (with or without
specific ECM molecules) to measure how far cells migrated out, assessed by 3D stereo microscopy. Findings,
tested across multiple patients:

- **DD cells were, if anything, *less* migratory than their matched normal fascia controls** — the opposite
  of what the migration hypothesis predicts.
- **TGF-beta** had no consistent migration effect on DD cells, but **inhibited migration of normal fascia
  cells** when added to the surrounding collagen.
- **ADAM-12**, a metalloprotease consistently found upregulated in Dupuytren's ECM in prior gene-expression
  studies, **inhibited migration in both cell types.**
- **Periostin** (a matricellular signaling molecule, distinct from a structural ECM protein) had **no
  significant effect on migration** in either cell type, though it selectively drove normal fascia cell
  *proliferation* and DD-cell *myofibroblast differentiation* — a cell-type-specific effect, just not a
  migration effect.

O'Gorman's summary: **"3D migration of Dupuytren's disease cells is either unaffected or inhibited by at
least the factors we've checked so far... that doesn't really support the [migration] hypothesis."**

## Testing conditioned matrix: the hypothesis holds

`[09:12]`–`[12:15]` — A transwell design let DD or normal fascia cells secrete freely into surrounding
collagen for seven days without ever contacting the collagen substrate directly, after which the medium was
replaced and **fresh, patient-matched normal fascia cells were grown on that pre-conditioned collagen alone**
— testing whether secreted factors, with no cells or cell contact involved, could alter normal cells.
Result: **normal fascia cells grown on collagen conditioned by DD cells showed marked upregulation of
cytoplasmic beta-catenin** — the same marker O'Gorman's group had earlier identified as elevated in disease
tissue itself — **compared to normal cells grown on collagen conditioned by other normal cells, which showed
"virtually no" beta-catenin accumulation.** The same DD-conditioned substrate also produced visibly altered
**stress fiber/actin condensation** in the exposed normal cells, consistent with a shift toward a disease-like
phenotype. **Conclusion: a Dupuytren's-conditioned extracellular matrix alone can induce disease-associated
changes in genetically normal fascia cells — support for the ECM-mediated disease hypothesis, not the
migration hypothesis.**

## Glossary terms introduced

- **Cell migration hypothesis** — the idea that Dupuytren's spreads because nodule cells physically move
  along the fascia `[03:07]`.
- **ECM-mediated disease hypothesis** — the idea that Dupuytren's spreads because disease cells condition
  the surrounding matrix, converting adjacent normal cells in place `[09:12]`.
- **Matricellular molecules** — a category the talk defines explicitly: **"secreted into the ECM but [not]
  structural components... they act as interacting components between the cells and the ECM,"** modifying
  the signals disease cells receive from their environment — periostin is given as the lab's working example
  `[01:06]`.
- **Fibroblast-populated collagen lattice (FPCL)** — the in vitro model used to assay cell migration and
  contraction `[05:09]`.

## Source-stated confidence

O'Gorman flags his own confusion openly at one point: pre-treating cells to stop proliferation unexpectedly
normalized their migration rate to match controls, and he states plainly, **"I've been scratching my head
about that one for quite a while — if anyone can come up with a good reason for that, please let me
know"** `[08:10]`. He also notes the TGF-beta migration data was **"much more variable than we would have
liked"** given the labor-intensive 3D counting method `[06:09]`, and describes downstream gene-expression
follow-up work as just-started, with an equipment failure ("a real-time PCR machine went down for a month")
explicitly cited as the reason no further data was available yet `[12:15]`.

## Conflicts with other sources

**Extends `dsPwSUDrz1Y`** (Phan's mechanism talk) and **`fvmRS3OiWic`** (Hinz's contraction-mechanics talk)
— both describe myofibroblast biology and TGF-beta signaling from other fibrotic-disease models without
direct Dupuytren's tissue data; this talk supplies a genuinely Dupuytren-specific experimental test using
patient-matched cells, and its migration-hypothesis-versus-ECM-hypothesis framing adds a disease-spread
mechanism neither of those talks addresses. **Consistent with `QRUnLzXLyjE`** (Nanchahal's "active
contraction" debate talk), which also treats the ECM as an active, not passive, participant in disease
progression — though via a different mechanism (mechanical TGF-beta activation there, versus conditioned
paracrine signaling here); the two are complementary rather than overlapping claims about ECM activity.
**Directly updated by `6aG_CIYTX8A`** (O'Gorman's own 2015 talk) — that note adds the critical methodological
refinement (physiologically relevant culture substrate stiffness) this 2010 talk's design doesn't yet
incorporate, plus substantial new beta-catenin/matrix-stiffness findings.
