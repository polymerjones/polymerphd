# Marie Badalamente PhD, "Injectable Collagenase Clostridium Histolyticum — the CORD I Trial" 2010 Miami Dupuytren Symposium

## Central claim

**The CORD I trial set a deliberately strict bar for success — full correction to within 5 degrees of
normal extension, not just meaningful improvement — and collagenase still cleared it in the majority of
treated joints, with MP joints responding far more reliably than PIP joints.** Badalamente states the bar
explicitly: **"that's the very high bar that we set for ourselves initially with the FDA — and maybe in
hindsight that was too high a bar, but that's the bar we set for all of Phase 2 and Phase 3"** `[02:08]`.
Result: **"64% of all the primary joints reached that very critical 0-to-5-degrees-of-normal-extension
[endpoint] 30 days after injection"** `[08:19]`.

## Trial design

`[02:08]`–`[05:15]` — CORD I ("Collagenase Option for Reduction of Dupuytren's") was **randomized,
double-blind, placebo-controlled, with an open-label extension**, run across **16 US centers** (a companion
CORD II study, not reported here, ran five Australian centers). Enrollment: **MP contracture 20–100
degrees, PIP contracture 20–80 degrees**, stratified by severity (≤50 degrees for MP, ≤40 degrees for PIP).
**Randomization favored drug over placebo 2:1**, a ratio chosen based on prior Phase 2 experience.
**Dose: 0.58mg**, injected in a small volume directly into the cord — **0.25mL near an MP joint, 0.20mL near
a PIP joint** — with up to **three injections per cord, 30 days apart.** The open-label extension allowed
**up to five further injections** for treatment failures or other affected joints, meaning a drug-randomized
patient could receive **up to eight injections total.** All patients were followed to one year, with
**annual follow-up planned out to five years.**

## Overall and joint-specific results

`[07:16]`–`[09:19]` — Intent-to-treat population: **306 primary joints analyzed (203 drug, 103 placebo)**,
mean age ~63, 80% male — noted as consistent with the broader Dupuytren's literature, with a notably high
proportion of little-finger involvement. Headline results: **64% of all primary joints reached full clinical
success (0–5 degrees); 85% achieved ≥50% contracture reduction; mean reduction 79%.** Broken down by joint:

| Joint | Full clinical success (0–5°) | ≥50% contracture reduction |
|---|---|---|
| **MP** | 77% | 94% |
| **PIP** | 40% | 67% |

Severity mattered specifically at the PIP joint: **MP joints responded well regardless of severity, and
less-severe PIP joints (below the ≤40-degree stratification cutoff) responded well — but more severe PIP
joints responded meaningfully worse.** This directly quantifies the joint- and severity-dependent treatment
window described more generally elsewhere in this library.

## Safety data

`[10:20]`–`[11:22]` — Adverse events were **mild to moderate, resolving without intervention at a median of
10 days.** Collagenase-treated patients had more injection- and manipulation-related events than placebo, as
expected, most commonly **edema, contusion, injection-site hemorrhage, and pain.** **Three serious adverse
events occurred: two tendon ruptures and one case of complex regional pain syndrome (CRPS).** **No vessel or
nerve injuries and no systemic immune events** were reported. Among joints that achieved clinical success,
the **mean number of injections needed was 1.5** — most successful joints required only a single injection.

## Symptoms and body signals addressed

- **MP versus PIP joint contracture responding very differently to the same drug and dose**, with PIP joints
  — especially more severe ones — responding meaningfully less well `[08:19]`.
- **Tendon rupture and complex regional pain syndrome**, named as the specific serious complications observed
  in the trial, rare but real `[11:22]`.
- **Immediate, audible cord rupture on manipulation the day after injection**, described as the clinical
  event defining treatment success `[05:15]`.

## Glossary terms introduced

- **CORD I / CORD II** — the pivotal Phase 3 trial names ("Collagenase Option for Reduction of
  Dupuytren's"), US and Australian arms respectively `[02:08]`, `[03:10]`.
- **Complex regional pain syndrome (CRPS)** — named as one of the trial's three serious adverse events
  `[11:22]`.

## Source-stated confidence

Badalamente discloses her financial relationships upfront (research investigator and consultant compensation,
partial royalty rights via a named company) before presenting results. She's explicit that the trial's own
primary endpoint threshold may have been set too strictly in hindsight, rather than defending it as
obviously correct, and reports the PIP-joint underperformance candidly rather than folding it into the more
favorable overall averages.
