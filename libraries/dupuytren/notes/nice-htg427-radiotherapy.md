# Radiation Therapy for Early Dupuytren's Disease

## Central claim

**NICE's verdict is conditional and cautious: radiotherapy "raises no major safety concerns," but efficacy
evidence is "inadequate in quantity and quality" and "difficult to interpret because of uncertainty about
the natural history of Dupuytren's disease" — so the procedure "should only be used with special
arrangements for clinical governance, consent and audit or research"** `[¶1]`. This is a materially more
cautious stance than NICE takes on needle fasciotomy for the same disease.

## What "early disease" means

`[¶2]`

NICE is explicit that there's no formal line: **"There is no formal clinical definition of early disease but
the term is generally used for patients with contractures of 30 degrees or less, with or without palmar
disease."** The guidance also states plainly that **"not all patients have progressive disease, and the
natural history of the disease is not well understood"** — the same uncertainty that limits how confidently
efficacy can be judged. The stated aim of radiotherapy is **"to prevent or postpone disease progression, and
reduce the need for surgical intervention,"** with a mechanism that is itself uncertain: **"thought to affect
the development and growth rate of fibroblasts in the palmar fascia"** `[¶3]`.

## Efficacy, across four cohorts

`[¶6]`, `[¶8]`, `[¶10]`, `[¶12]`

Four separate patient groups supply the efficacy evidence, and NICE keeps their numbers distinct rather than
pooling them:

| Cohort | Dose | Follow-up | Outcome |
|---|---|---|---|
| RCT, 129 patients / 198 hands | 30 Gy vs 21 Gy | 1 year | Regression in 56% (30 Gy) / 53% (21 Gy) of hands (objective assessment); stable in a further 37%/38%; overall progression 8% |
| Case series, 206 patients | 32 Gy | median 40 months | Regression in 45% (93/206); no further progression in 80% |
| Case series, 135 patients / 208 hands | 30 Gy | median 13 years | Regression in 10% of hands, stable in 59%, progression in 31% |
| Case series, 33 patients / 60 sites | (dose not stated in this excerpt) | median 31 months | Progression at any location in 61% of patients; in-field progression 23% of sites, with 4 sites successfully re-irradiated |

**The 13-year cohort's own patient-reported relief breakdown is worth keeping distinct from the "regression"
figure above**: complete relief in 16%, good relief in 18%, minor relief in 32%, unchanged in 14%, and
progression of symptoms in 20% of patients `[¶10]`. Subsequent surgery was needed in 3% of the RCT patients
within 1 year, 20% of the 13-year cohort's hands, and 6% of the 33-patient cohort within 31 months `[¶14]`.
Patient satisfaction, where measured, was high: a mean 7.9±2.7 out of 10 in the 206-patient cohort, and 94%
of the 33-patient cohort considered the treatment successful `[¶16]`.

## Safety — acute and chronic toxicity

`[¶24]`–`[¶53]`

**Acute effects** (within about 4 weeks) reported across the cohorts include skin tenderness, redness,
peeling, or mild pain (50% in a 17-patient cohort); dry skin or redness (38% of hands in the RCT); dry
desquamation (5% of hands) and wet desquamation (2%); extensive erythema (6% of hands in the RCT, 20% of
patients in the 206-patient cohort); and pronounced swelling (2% of hands).

**Chronic effects** reported include mild tightness, dryness, skin thickening, mild swelling, and decreased
sensation (31% of the 17-patient cohort at a mean 35 months); skin dryness, increased desquamation, mild
atrophy or slight subcutaneous fibrosis needing topical treatment (16% of hands at 30 Gy and 11% at 21 Gy
within 3 months, falling to 4–5% by 12 months, in the RCT); lack of sweating (4% of the 206-patient cohort);
skin atrophy and telangiectasia (3% each in the 206-patient cohort; mild atrophy with occasional
telangiectasia in 7% of the 13-year cohort's hands); altered heat/pain sensation (4% of RCT hands); and, in
the 33-patient/60-site cohort specifically, subjective weakness (3% of sites), reduced nail health (3%), and
hyperpigmentation (3%).

**No radiation-induced malignancy was reported in the literature NICE reviewed**, despite specialist advisers
naming it as a theoretical (not observed) risk alongside poor wound healing from surgery on irradiated skin
`[¶55]`.

## Symptoms and body signals addressed

- **Regression, stabilisation, or progression of existing nodules and cords, tracked as the three primary
  outcome categories across every cohort in this guidance** `[¶6]`, `[¶10]`.
- **New nodules, new cords, or increased flexion deformity as markers of on-treatment progression** — 6%,
  4%, and 6% of hands respectively in the RCT `[¶6]`.
- **Post-treatment skin symptoms**: tenderness, dryness, thickening, reduced sweating, altered sensation, or
  weakness at the treated site `[¶24]`–`[¶49]`.

## Glossary terms introduced

- **Gy (Gray)** — the radiation dose unit this guidance's cohorts are grouped by (21, 30, or 32 Gy) `[¶6]`.
- **Acute versus chronic toxicity** — NICE's own split for organizing side-effect data, roughly at the
  4-week mark `[¶21]`, `[¶35]`.
- **In-field versus out-of-field progression** — whether the disease worsens inside the area that was
  actually irradiated, or elsewhere `[¶12]`.

## Source-stated confidence

This is the most explicitly hedged source in the library so far. NICE states directly that the efficacy
evidence is **"inadequate in quantity and quality"** and that interpreting it is complicated by not knowing
**"the natural history of Dupuytren's disease"** in the first place `[¶1]` — meaning even a genuine
"regression" rate can't cleanly be separated from cases that might have improved on their own. The committee
also flagged, without resolving, a structural problem: **"despite the recommendations in the previous
guidance for audit, and encouragement for further research, data collection since the guidance was published
had been disappointing"** `[¶58]`. That combination — real numbers, explicitly stated as too weak to found a
confident recommendation on — is the reason this guidance lands on "special arrangements for governance,
consent and audit," not a routine recommendation.

## Conflicts with other sources

**Converges with `girft-bssh-dupuytrens-pathway`**, which independently treats radiotherapy as something to
"consider... as part of local audit or research if available" rather than a standard offer — the same
caution this guidance's own evidence grading explains in detail. **No sources yet in this library report a
recurrence figure specifically for radiotherapy comparable to the treatment-recurrence figures
`nocek-2026-recurrence-systematic-review` gives for LF/CCH/NA** — radiotherapy targets early, pre-cord
disease and isn't directly comparable to those cord-release procedures, and this library has no source yet
that states that distinction explicitly.
