# Dr. Atanu Biswas "Soft Tissue Distraction versus Fasciectomy" 2015 Dupuytren Symposium

## Central claim

**In a retrospective review of severe PIP joint contractures, a dynamic external soft-tissue distraction
device ("digit widget") achieved a larger mean improvement than fasciectomy with check-rein ligament
release — 53.4 versus 31.4 degrees at 18-month follow-up — positioning it as an alternative or adjunct
specifically for severe contractures beyond the range where needle aponeurotomy or collagenase are typically
effective.** Biswas states the comparative result directly: **"the fasciectomy and check-rein ligament
cohort had a mean improvement of 31.4 degrees, compared to the digit widget cohort at the same interval
follow-up — 53.4 [degrees]"** `[05:04]`.

## Why acute release is risky for severe PIP contracture

`[00:00]`–`[01:00]` — Biswas frames the underlying clinical problem: severe PIP joint contracture has no
strong treatment consensus, and acute surgical release of contracted volar joint structures (the check-rein
ligaments) carries two specific risks — **digital artery spasm and possible ischemia** from acute stretching
of the neurovascular bundle, and **skin loss** from the contracted soft-tissue envelope, which then requires
skin grafting onto a flexor tendon sheet bed he describes as "not the ideal bed" for a graft to take.

## The device and treatment algorithm

`[01:00]`–`[02:00]` — The **digit widget** is a dynamic external distraction device applying continuous
extension force across the PIP joint, used **preliminarily, before any surgical release**, to gradually
lengthen the soft-tissue envelope over time rather than releasing it acutely. This avoids acutely stretching
the neurovascular bundle and expands the available skin envelope before any subsequent surgery. Standard
protocol: the device is worn roughly **six weeks**, removed once the joint approaches full extension, with
**no postoperative splinting typically required** in their observed cases. Its limitation: correction can
**plateau before reaching full extension**, at which point operative release is still pursued.

## Case example

`[02:00]`–`[04:03]` — A 76-year-old right-hand-dominant male with a small-finger MCP flexion deformity of 55
degrees and PIP contracture of 90 degrees, no prior surgery. The device was applied under fluoroscopic
guidance via proximal and distal pins placed through a guiding jig, with rubber-band elastics (similar to
dental elastics) providing graduated tension via a boom-arm attachment. At six weeks, a **secondary
boutonnière deformity had developed** — flagged as a complication requiring separate management. Three months
after device removal, since the patient had not achieved full correction, he was offered **surgical
contracture release**, and — because DIP hyperextension remained inflexible — a **Fowler central slip
tenotomy** was performed at the same time; the patient was followed to five years.

## Cohort comparison and complications

`[04:03]`–`[06:04]` — Retrospective review, **30 patients / 37 digits**: 20 digits treated with fasciectomy
plus check-rein ligament release (which also used K-wires as an internal splint during healing), 17 with the
digit widget prior to fasciectomy. Outcome measures: overall improvement relative to preoperative contracture,
stratified by prior surgery history, and complications.

| Cohort | Mean improvement (18-mo follow-up) | Complications |
|---|---|---|
| **Fasciectomy + check-rein release** | 31.4° | 2 partial full-thickness skin graft loss; 1 K-wire removal |
| **Digit widget** | 53.4° | 2 pin-tract infections (1 requiring removal for adjacent-digit impingement); 1 MP joint hyperextension |

The MP hyperextension complication led the team to add an **MP flexion strap** to their protocol going
forward — noted as a known risk specifically when an isolated PIP contracture coexists with a supple MP
joint, possible with either treatment approach. **No significant recurrence or loss of gained extension** was
observed in either cohort after 18 months.

## Where this fits in the treatment landscape

`[06:04]` — Biswas frames the device as an **alternative or adjunct specifically for severe contractures**,
explicitly deferring to other modalities for milder disease: **"the other modalities, for a lesser degree of
contractures, are probably best served with either needle aponeurotomy or [collagenase]. But once you get
beyond 60 degrees, or severe contractures approaching 90 degrees, I think this is a very good alternative
modality."**

## Symptoms and body signals addressed

- **Digital artery spasm and possible ischemia from acute neurovascular bundle stretching**, the specific
  vascular risk motivating a gradual-distraction approach `[01:00]`.
- **A secondary boutonnière deformity developing mid-treatment**, shown as an evolving complication requiring
  its own management rather than a fixed starting condition `[03:02]`.
- **MP joint hyperextension**, identified as a device-specific complication that led to a protocol change
  (adding a flexion strap) `[05:04]`.

## Glossary terms introduced

- **Digit widget** — the dynamic external soft-tissue distraction device used preliminarily to gradually
  lengthen contracted PIP joint tissue before any surgical release `[01:00]`.
- **Check-rein ligaments** — the contracted volar PIP joint structures targeted by the surgical comparator
  procedure `[00:00]`.

## Source-stated confidence

Biswas discloses no financial relationships or conflicts. He is explicit this is a **retrospective review**,
not a randomized or prospective comparison, and frames the conclusion modestly as identifying the device as
"an alternative, or I guess an adjunct tool," specifically for severe contractures — not a general
replacement for other treatments. He credits the study's primary investigator, Dr. Anthony Smith, by name and
notes ongoing discussion about a future prospective study.
