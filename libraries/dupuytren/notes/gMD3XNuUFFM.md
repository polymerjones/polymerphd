# Robert Choa MD, "Digital Nerves in Dupuytren's Surgery" 2010 Miami Dupuytren Symposium

## Central claim

**A consistent, previously undescribed pair of nerve branches — found in every single finger examined —
arises from the proper digital nerve at a predictable location and could be mistaken for the proper digital
nerve itself during Dupuytren's surgery, especially once distorted by a spiral cord.** Choa's summary states
the safety stakes directly: **"there are constant pairs of nerve branches which could be mistaken for the
proper digital nerves... we found it's quite a predictable location — this hopefully will serve to aid
surgical dissection and facilitate protection of the proper digital nerve"** `[07:11]`.

## Why this mattered

`[00:03]`–`[01:04]` — Nerve injury is a recognized complication of Dupuytren's surgery, with **reported
rates of 1.4–7.8%** varying across surgeons and units in the literature. The study originated from an
informal observation by Choa's consultant colleague, who noticed **large-caliber branches diverging from the
proper digital nerves**, colloquially nicknamed **"nerves to the disease."** The concern: these branches
could be **large enough to be mistaken for the proper digital nerve itself**, particularly once normal
anatomy is distorted by a spiral cord — and a literature search found **no prior formal description of these
specific branches.**

## Study design

`[01:04]`–`[02:04]` — **40 fingers** dissected from cadaveric hands (10 pairs from male specimens, plus
additional specimens from a female body), all **screened and confirmed free of hand pathology or
Dupuytren's disease** before dissection. Skin was removed from the proximal transverse palmar crease to the
distal joint crease, and each specimen was examined for the A1 pulley, the common and proper digital
nerves, and the branch pattern in question.

## The "Chevron pair" finding

`[03:07]`–`[05:09]` — Photographic documentation showed **paired branches diverging from the proper digital
nerves and meeting at the finger's midline**, forming a shape the consultant colleague described as a
**"chevron."** Measuring from the **midpoint of the A1 pulley's proximal edge**, the branch origin distance
was recorded with calipers alongside total finger length, allowing the location to be expressed as **a
percentage of finger length** for generalizability across different-sized fingers. Result: **a constant
chevron pair was found in every finger from index through little finger**, occurring predictably at
**15–16mm from the A1 pulley**, which works out to **approximately 20% (roughly one-fifth) of the total
distance from the A1 pulley to the fingertip.** A **dense concentration of Pacinian corpuscles** was found
near these branch points, and the branches were confirmed **anatomically distinct from the dorsal digital
nerve branches** — ruling out that this was simply a case of dorsal branches being misidentified.

## Making the finding usable in surgery

`[06:11]` — Since the A1 pulley isn't routinely dissected or directly identified during standard
Dupuytren's surgery, Choa's group combined their chevron-branch location data with **an existing published
surface-marking technique** (attributed to Wilhelmy) for locating the A1 pulley from external landmarks —
allowing surgeons to **predict roughly where these at-risk nerve branches will be**, without needing to
formally expose the pulley itself, as an aid to safer dissection.

## Symptoms and body signals addressed

- **Nerve injury as a recognized surgical complication**, with a specific cited rate range (1.4–7.8%) from
  the existing literature `[00:03]`.
- **Risk of mistaking a chevron-pair branch for the proper digital nerve itself**, specifically flagged as
  more likely once the anatomy is distorted by a spiral cord `[03:07]`.

## Glossary terms introduced

- **Chevron pair** — the paired nerve branches from the proper digital nerve, meeting at the finger midline
  in a V-shaped pattern, found consistently across all examined fingers `[03:07]`.
- **"Nerves to the disease"** — the informal nickname the branches were given prior to formal study,
  reflecting their apparent association with the diseased tissue region `[01:04]`.

## Analogies worth reusing

- **The chevron (V) shape** — the visual/geometric description used throughout to describe how the paired
  branches meet at the finger midline `[03:07]`.

## Source-stated confidence

Choa is direct about the study's limitations: **only 40 specimens**, though he notes this is comparable to
other similarly scaled cadaveric nerve studies in the literature, and no directly comparable prior
description of this specific branch pattern could be found despite a search (distinguishing it from separate,
existing work on dorsal digital nerve branching patterns by other named authors). He also notes candidly that
he personally hadn't performed many Dupuytren's operations himself, relaying the clinical concern as reported
by his more experienced consultant colleague rather than from his own direct surgical experience.
