# Lynn Ketchum MD, "Dermofasciectomies and Full-Thickness Skin Grafts" 2010 Miami Dupuytren Symposium

## Central claim

**Across a 25-year, 204-hand series, expanded dermofasciectomy with a full-thickness skin graft produced
zero recurrence beneath the graft — a result Ketchum states directly is "a real phenomenon," not a
statistical fluke, and specifically recommends for patients with strong Dupuytren's diathesis.** His closing
line: **"the decrease in recurrence and extension rates following expanded dermofasciectomy and full-
thickness grafts is a real phenomenon, and it's a feasible treatment for patients with a strong diathesis for
the disease and consequent tendency to develop recurrent [disease]"** `[15:34]`.

## Learning from a technique that didn't quite work

`[00:03]`–`[02:07]` — Ketchum opens with his own early frustration: a 1967 case released via limited
fasciectomy recurred within a year, with findings at reoperation **"virtually identical"** to the original.
Trying Conolly's 1970 technique (transverse incisions resurfaced with full-thickness grafts, **the grafts
coming to a point at the mid-lateral line**), Ketchum found **no recurrence beneath the graft itself — but
new contracture developed right along the graft's edge**, extending into the digit. This specific failure
mode motivated his own modification.

## The expanded dermofasciectomy technique

`[02:07]`–`[06:16]` — The key change: **grafts squared off at the mid-lateral line rather than pointed**,
extending **at least 2cm**, from the mid-lateral line of the affected digit to the mid-lateral line of the
adjacent digit, and from the mid-lateral line into the second webspace in the palm — carried further to the
radial or ulnar border, or even including the thumb, if disease extended there. Graft harvested transversely
or obliquely from the medial upper arm. A specific, hard-won technical point: **the bolus dressing must stay
on for a full 21 days** — early graft loss occurred when dressings were removed at two weeks, before
capillary ingrowth from the recipient bed had matured enough to withstand hydrostatic pressure when the hand
was in a dependent position.

## Outcomes across two decades, two phases

`[08:19]`–`[11:25]` — **Phase 1 (1979–1985): 36 hands, 24 patients.** Result: **zero recurrence beneath the
graft; an 8.3% extension rate** (new disease appearing just outside the graft margin, explicitly
distinguished from recurrence under it). Compared against contemporaneous literature cited at the same
symposium: **limited fasciectomy recurrence around 50%**; the **McCash technique (per Snyder's data) showing
42% recurrence, a 48% extension rate, and 41% flexion loss in one or more digits.** To test whether the 8.3%
figure was a fluke, the **study continued through 2005**: **Phase 2 added 168 hands, 129 patients**, on the
same protocol. **Combined total: 204 hands, 153 patients, 1970–2005.** Demographics: 74.5% men, 25.5% women,
notably **young average age of onset (51.6 years)**, bilateral disease in 76%, plantar (Ledderhose)
involvement in 22.4%, positive family history in 48.7%. One notable deviation from most other series: **the
fifth digit, not the ring finger, was the most commonly affected in this cohort.** Combined result: **still
zero recurrence beneath the graft; extension rate rose slightly to 9.3%** overall. Complications:
hyperpigmentation, occasional hair growth within the graft, decreased sensibility (not reported as
bothersome by patients), no flare reactions, occasional suture abscess, and hematoma under the graft in
under 5% of cases.

## Four proposed reasons grafting works

`[12:27]`–`[14:32]` — Ketchum cites several independent lines of evidence converging on why removing skin
specifically (not just fascia) reduces recurrence:

1. **Collagen distribution**: Fitzgerald's group found **type III collagen concentrated in the upper
   dermis, decreasing with depth** — meaning cords themselves contain relatively little of it, but the
   dermis above them is collagen-rich tissue left behind by fasciectomy alone.
2. **Myofibroblast location**: a separate group found myofibroblasts concentrated **in the dermis and
   nodules, with minimal amounts in cords** — again implying the dermis, not just the cord, harbors the
   cells driving recurrence.
3. **Diseased fat/skin signaling**: diseased skin and subcutaneous fat may contain **cytokines, growth
   factors, and arachidonic acid metabolites** capable of stimulating recurrence if left in place after a
   limited fasciectomy that removes only the cord.
4. **Mechanical stress removal**: expanding the dermofasciectomy **breaks the longitudinal mechanical
   stress** that, combined with TGF-beta, drives fibroblast-to-myofibroblast differentiation — removing the
   diseased dermis along with the fascia interrupts this pathway directly. Separate work (attributed to
   "Gordon, Tonkin and others") found **graft "anchors" reduce the area available for redundant tissue to
   return**, and experimental work (attributed to "Rudolf") showed full-thickness grafts **decrease the
   myofibroblast population** during wound healing generally.

## Houston's prophylactic rationale

`[14:32]`–`[15:34]` — Citing Houston and McFarland's work on the **diathesis concept**: the more diathesis
risk factors a patient has, the greater their recurrence risk after any treatment. Houston's reasoning,
endorsed here: **if a patient has multiple diathesis factors, why not use the full-thickness graft
prophylactically** — proactively choosing the more extensive, recurrence-resistant procedure specifically
because the patient's risk profile predicts they'll need it eventually anyway.

## Symptoms and body signals addressed

- **Recurrence beneath a graft versus extension of disease at the graft's margin**, explicitly distinguished
  as two different failure modes requiring different technical fixes `[01:05]`.
- **Graft loss from premature dressing removal**, tied to a specific healing-time mechanism (capillary
  ingrowth maturity) rather than treated as a generic complication `[04:11]`.
- **Decreased sensibility in grafted skin**, noted as a real but not clinically bothersome finding for
  patients `[12:27]`.

## Glossary terms introduced

- **Expanded dermofasciectomy** — Ketchum's squared-off-margin modification of Conolly's original pointed-
  graft technique `[02:07]`.
- **Recurrence versus extension** — a load-bearing distinction throughout this talk: recurrence is disease
  reappearing under the graft itself; extension is new disease appearing adjacent to, but outside, the graft
  margin `[01:05]`.

## Analogies worth reusing

None stated — the talk presents technique and long-term outcome data directly.

## Source-stated confidence

Ketchum is explicit about testing his own initial result for robustness rather than accepting it at face
value — the entire Phase 2 extension (1985–2005) exists specifically to answer **"was the 8.3% recurrence and
extension rate... a real phenomenon... or was it an aberration [artifact]?"** He also flags the atypical
finger-distribution finding (fifth digit over ring finger) as a genuine deviation from most other series
without explaining it away.

## Conflicts with other sources

**Directly extends and substantially strengthens `L3kjpUIwHIQ`** (Leclercq's severe PIP contracture talk),
whose own dermofasciectomy series (34 patients, 21% recurrence at 5.6 years) is much smaller and shorter than
this 204-hand, 25-year series reporting literally zero recurrence beneath the graft — this talk should be
treated as the stronger evidence for the graft-protective effect where the two overlap. **Directly relevant
to `1Xqmh80rYPQ`** (Degreef's surgical outcome talk), whose bias-adjusted analysis found no clear recurrence
advantage for more extensive surgery in a general population — this talk's result is specific to patients
selected for **strong diathesis**, exactly the subgroup Degreef's own indication-bias finding suggests
surgeons already select for bigger surgery; the two are not necessarily in conflict once that population
difference is accounted for. **Directly corroborates `-jLfWXX3YUM`** (Seven Questions) and **`9LY5fyLw618`**
(Hurst's collagenase talk) on the diathesis concept — Houston's prophylactic-grafting rationale cited here is
the same diathesis-driven treatment-selection logic Eaton describes as current practice guidance.
