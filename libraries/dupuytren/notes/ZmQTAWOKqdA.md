# Dr. Zsolt Szabo: "Treatment of Dupuytren in Different Countries" 2015 Dupuytren Symposium

## Central claim

**Practice around Dupuytren's varies so much by country, specialty, and individual conviction that
Szabo's informal survey reveals something closer to institutional habit than evidence-based consensus — and
his proposed fix is a decision-making framework, not a single correct protocol.** His own summary: **"if
everything is so unclear, so controversial, and so different, and everybody is convinced that his own method
he's used to do is the best... how to decide? It's very, very difficult"** `[09:11]`–`[10:13]`.

## What his (self-described "non-scientific") survey found

`[02:02]`–`[08:10]` — Drawing on years of international meetings as a former FESSH/IFSSH secretary general,
Szabo reports genuine disagreement at every stage of care: **etiology** (some doctors still believe it's a
flexor tendon contracture; others know the histology precisely); **diagnosis** (some diagnose purely by
sight, others insist on histology, ultrasound, and MRI); **who treats it** (rheumatologists in some
countries, GPs in others, specialized hand surgeons elsewhere); **treatment setting** (same-day discharge in
some countries, one-to-two-week hospitalization in others for the same disease); **timing** ("as early as
possible" versus "don't touch it while it's young — wait for real contracture"); and **complication and
recurrence rates**, which some surgeons report as essentially zero and others report as near-universal —
for, notionally, the same procedures.

## A geographic tour, told with dry wit

`[08:10]`–`[10:13]` — Szabo's country-by-country characterization, offered as caricature but grounded in
real practice patterns: **the US "produces and sells the collagenase";** wealthier Scandinavian centers
**buy and use it;** in **France**, rheumatologists dominate needle aponeurotomy treatment, leaving hand
surgeons mainly the severe, recurrent cases requiring fasciectomy ("the only way to prevent recurrences," as
he characterizes the French surgeon's view); **Germany** favors **radiotherapy**, attributed to existing
X-ray expertise and cost concerns about collagenase; the **UK** publishes heavily; the **Netherlands** uses
**lipofilling** (a wry aside about the country having "a lot of fat"); and **Eastern Europe** tends to follow
whichever larger neighboring country's practice is cheapest and available. He's explicit this is not a
formal study — **"a non-randomized and absolutely non-scientific review."**

## The three-pillar decision-making model

`[13:15]`–`[14:16]` — Presented as **"the most important slide of my presentation"**: medical
decision-making rests on three pillars, none sufficient alone — **evidence-based data**, **patient values
and expectations**, and **individual clinical expertise** (how a surgeon was trained and what their own
experience has taught them). His point in raising this explicitly: given how thin the evidence base is for
much of Dupuytren's treatment (discussed throughout the talk), the other two pillars are carrying
disproportionate weight in real-world decisions right now, whether or not that's openly acknowledged.

## Beliefs, knowledge, and hope

`[10:13]`–`[15:17]` — Szabo frames his own talk honestly as **belief** (his collected impressions, not
data), separately naming what he actually **knows**: that the field lacks evidence-based data, that even
existing evidence-based data isn't consistently used properly, that more research is needed, and that the
field lacks a shared vocabulary. He credits the collagenase era specifically with an indirect benefit
unrelated to the drug's own clinical merits: **"the development and appearance of collagenase [meant] a lot
of money was invested in courses and congresses and teaching all around the world"** covering etiology,
diagnosis, and treatment broadly, not just the drug itself — alongside a stated shift in FESSH/IFSSH's own
institutional priorities, redirecting congress-generated funds toward research and education rather than
accumulation. He closes with **hope**: drawing an explicit parallel to duodenal ulcer treatment, which
shifted from routine surgery to a pill within a single surgical career — **"today, young surgeons have no
idea what it is [to operate one]. Only because of a pill."** He states his hope for the same outcome for
Dupuytren's, while noting candidly that hand surgery would lose "one of the most interesting chapters" if it
happened.

## Glossary terms introduced

- **The three pillars of medical decision-making** — evidence-based data, patient values/expectations, and
  individual clinical expertise, none sufficient alone `[13:15]`.

## Analogies worth reusing

- **Duodenal ulcer treatment's shift from routine surgery to a single pill within one surgical career** — a
  concrete historical precedent for how quickly a "surgical disease" can stop being one `[14:16]`.
- **"Dupuytren was something [of a] jolly joker, just like football and politics — everybody's an
  expert"** — on how a lack of consensus lets everyone feel confident regardless `[01:01]`.

## Source-stated confidence

Szabo repeatedly and explicitly labels his own talk as impressionistic rather than rigorous — **"a
non-randomized and absolutely non-scientific review"** based on informal international conversations, not a
systematic survey. He separates his own stated beliefs from what he says he actually knows, a distinction
he draws deliberately rather than blurring for rhetorical effect.
