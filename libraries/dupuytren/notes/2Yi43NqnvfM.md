# Chrishan Samuel PhD, "Relaxin: An Emerging Anti-Fibrotic Therapy" 2010 Miami Dupuytren Symposium

## Central claim

**Relaxin is a naturally occurring hormone with a specifically disease-selective anti-fibrotic action — it
blocks pathological fibrosis without disturbing normal collagen turnover — and has already reduced or
reversed fibrosis in every in vivo disease model tested so far, across multiple organs and species.**
Samuel's summary of that track record: **"H2 relaxin has been demonstrated to reduce or reverse the
fibrosis associated with every in vivo model examined so far"** `[08:15]`–`[09:18]`, while **"studies have
shown that it does not affect basal matrix or collagen turnover, highlighting its safety as a potential
therapy"** `[10:22]`.

## What relaxin is

`[00:03]`–`[02:05]` — A small (6 kDa), two-chain peptide hormone structurally related to insulin, produced
mainly by the corpus luteum in pregnant mammals (and, to a lesser degree, the prostate and testes in males,
with more recent identification in heart, lung, kidney, and brain tissue in both sexes). Humans and higher
primates carry three relaxin peptides (H1, H2, H3); most other species, including rodents, carry the
species-equivalent of just two. **H2 relaxin is the major circulating form in humans** and the one the talk
focuses on. Its actions — anti-fibrotic, anti-inflammatory, anti-apoptotic, pro-angiogenic, and
vasodilatory — are mediated through a specific receptor, **RXFP1**.

## Already in human trials, for a different disease

`[02:05]` — Samuel cites completed Phase 1/2 clinical trials of H2 relaxin in **over 250 acute heart failure
patients**: a 24–48 hour infusion was well tolerated and **improved dyspnea, cardiac output, and other
cardiac/renal function measures**, with **lower rehospitalization and death rates at 3–6 month follow-up**
compared to placebo. A Phase 3 trial in over 2,000 acute heart failure patients had recently begun at the
time of this talk — evidence, independent of any Dupuytren's-specific data, that the drug's basic safety
profile is already being established in humans.

## The mechanism: selectively blocking TGF-beta through SMAD2 and nitric oxide

`[03:07]`–`[06:11]` — Across human and rat fibroblast culture models (skin, including scleroderma patients;
lung, uterus, kidney, liver, heart), relaxin **does not affect basal fibroblast function or matrix
turnover**, but **significantly inhibits the influence of fibrotic cytokines — TGF-beta1, angiotensin II,
and interleukin-1 beta** — blocking their downstream effect on fibroblast proliferation, myofibroblast
differentiation, and collagen synthesis. It also **promotes MMP-1, -2, -9, and -13 expression/activation**
while **inhibiting TIMPs**, shifting the MMP:TIMP ratio toward net collagen degradation — directly relevant
to the imbalance mechanism described elsewhere in this library.

Mechanistically, of the nine characterized SMAD proteins involved in TGF-beta signaling, **SMAD2, 3, and 4
promote TGF-beta's effects; SMAD7 opposes them.** A 2005 study (Heeg and colleagues, on human renal
fibroblasts) showed relaxin **specifically inhibits SMAD2 phosphorylation**, blocking the SMAD2/3 complexing
needed for TGF-beta signaling — **without directly affecting SMAD3, 4, or 7.** Samuel's own lab extended
this in vivo (renal fibroblasts from obstructed rat kidneys, and a mouse tubulointerstitial scarring model),
showing relaxin's inhibitory action also runs through **the nitric oxide pathway** — specifically
**endothelial and neuronal nitric oxide synthase and cyclic GMP** — since general nitric oxide inhibitors
(L-NMMA, L-NAME), an nNOS-specific inhibitor, and a cyclic GMP inhibitor all blocked relaxin's suppression of
SMAD2 phosphorylation and myofibroblast differentiation markers, while an iNOS-specific inhibitor did not.

## Synergy, and a long list of animal models

`[07:13]`–`[09:18]` — In scleroderma-patient dermal fibroblasts, **relaxin acted synergistically with
interferon-gamma** — lower combined doses of both outperformed high doses of either alone at reducing
collagen. In vivo, **as little as two weeks of H2 relaxin (at concentrations found in pregnant rodents)**
dose-dependently reduced fibrous-capsule collagen in two dermal scarring models; broader testing across
**fibrotic cardiomyopathy, ischemic/hypertensive/diabetic heart disease, renal injury and obstruction, CCl4-
induced liver injury, and bleomycin-induced lung injury** consistently showed reduced or reversed fibrosis.

## Genetic evidence: knockout mice confirm a causal role

`[11:25]` — **Relaxin gene knockout mice** (lacking the mouse-equivalent circulating form) develop
**age-dependent, progressive collagen accumulation and dermal thickening** (elevated type I and III
collagen) compared to age-matched wild-type mice — a phenotype **reversible with as little as two weeks of
exogenous H2 relaxin replacement**, again via reduced myofibroblast differentiation. Samuel's conclusion:
**relaxin functions as a naturally occurring endogenous regulator of myofibroblast activity**, joining a
short list of other known myofibroblast antagonists (hepatocyte growth factor, bone morphogenic protein 7,
SMAD7, interferon-gamma) — with **potential application to Dupuytren's disease specifically flagged as the
open question**, not yet directly tested at the time of this talk.

## Glossary terms introduced

- **RXFP1** — relaxin's primary G-protein-coupled receptor, mediating its anti-fibrotic signaling `[02:05]`.
- **SMAD2/3/4 versus SMAD7** — the pro-fibrotic versus anti-fibrotic arms of TGF-beta's intracellular
  signaling pathway `[04:10]`.
- **Disease-selective anti-fibrotic action** — Samuel's own framing for relaxin's key safety property: it
  suppresses pathological fibrosis without disturbing normal (basal) matrix turnover `[10:22]`.

## Source-stated confidence

Samuel is explicit that Dupuytren's-specific application is speculative at this point — the talk's closing
line frames it as something relaxin "may have potential application" for, not a tested finding. All the
anti-fibrotic evidence presented comes from other organs and disease models (heart, kidney, liver, lung,
skin generally), and the clinical trial data cited is for acute heart failure, not any fibrotic-scarring
indication. This is candidly a "here's a promising candidate mechanism" talk, not a Dupuytren's results
talk.
