# The Likelihood of Future Dupuytren Disease Intervention After Initial Treatment in the Same Digit, Another Digit, and Contralateral Hand

## Central claim

**Within about 5 years of an initial procedure, more than 4 in 10 patients needed some further treatment for
Dupuytren's — split roughly into recurrence in the same digit, spread to another digit in the same hand, or
new disease in the opposite hand, each with its own timeline and risk factors** `[¶6]`.

## Study design

`[¶5]`

A **single-institution database review**, starting from 658 patients coded for collagenase treatment or
surgical fasciectomy, narrowed to **341 eligible patients** — 141 initially treated with fasciectomy and 200
with collagenase, treated between 2015 and 2019. The primary outcome was simply whether the patient needed
subsequent treatment, and where: the same digit, another digit in the same hand, or the contralateral hand.

## What it found

`[¶6]`, `[¶7]`

At a **mean follow-up of 5.3 years, 142 of 341 patients (42%) underwent additional treatment.** That 42%
splits unevenly by location and by timeline: **same-digit revision in 88 patients (26%) at a mean of 3.2
years; another digit in the same hand in 35 patients (10%) at a mean of 3.6 years; and the contralateral hand
in 79 patients (23%) at a mean of 2.3 years** — meaning the opposite hand was, on average, the *fastest* site
to need treatment, even though same-digit recurrence was the single most common outcome. Different factors
predicted each location: **procedure type, multiple digits treated, and initial nontreated contracture**
predicted same-digit retreatment; **younger age, active smoking, and initial nontreated contracture**
predicted spread to another digit in the same hand; and **the dominant hand being the one initially treated,
plus initial nontreated contracture,** predicted treatment later being needed in the other hand.

## Symptoms and body signals addressed

- **Recurrence of contracture in the originally treated digit** `[¶6]`.
- **New Dupuytren's intervention needed in a digit that was not initially treated**, whether in the same hand
  or the opposite one `[¶6]`.
- **Pre-existing, untreated contracture in a digit other than the one initially addressed**, named repeatedly
  as a risk factor for further treatment regardless of location `[¶6]`.

## Glossary terms introduced

- **Contralateral hand** — the hand not initially treated; this study tracks it as a distinct outcome
  location from the treated hand's other digits `[¶4]`.

## Analogies worth reusing

None — outcomes are reported as percentages and follow-up years.

## Source-stated confidence

The authors translate their findings directly into a counseling recommendation rather than leaving the reader
to infer it: **"Within 5 years of initial treatment for Dupuytren contracture in one digit, patients have
over 40% likelihood of future intervention for contracture... Key risk factors include age, multiple digits
treated, smoking, and the presence of initially nontreated fingers, which should be incorporated into patient
counseling"** `[¶7]`. The study labels its own evidence tier explicitly: **"3-Retrospective cohort study,
prognostic"** — a lower evidence tier than a randomized trial, stated as such rather than implied `[¶8]`. A
conflict of interest is disclosed directly: **"Dr Blazar is a consultant for Endo Pharmaceuticals"** (a
collagenase manufacturer), with the other authors reporting no related benefits `[¶8]`.

## Conflicts with other sources

**Relevant to, but not the same measurement as, `nocek-2026-recurrence-systematic-review` and
`detect-2026-progression-rct`**: those sources measure recurrence or progression within the treated joint;
this cohort is the only source in the library so far that tracks the disease appearing or needing treatment
somewhere *else* — another digit, or the other hand entirely — after an initial procedure. **No conflict in
direction**: all three sources agree that further treatment after an initial procedure is common, not rare.
