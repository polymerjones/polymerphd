# Dr. Chao Zhou "Hand Function Satisfaction Following Fasciectomy" 2015 Dupuytren Symposium

## Central claim

**In a 194-patient prospective study, the single significant independent predictor of postoperative
satisfaction with hand function after limited fasciectomy was the patient's *preoperative* Michigan Hand
Outcomes Questionnaire (MHQ) hand-appearance subscore, not any demographic or clinical factor typically
assumed to matter — with gender approaching, but not reaching, significance.** Zhou states the effect size
directly: **"a one-point higher preoperative score in hand appearance corresponded to a 1.4 times higher
likelihood of being satisfied with hand function, and males, as compared to females, were about 2.5 times as
likely to be satisfied with their hand function"** `[03:02]`.

## Why patient satisfaction, and what was measured

`[00:01]`–`[02:02]` — Zhou frames patient satisfaction as an increasingly used quality-of-care marker,
particularly apt for Dupuytren's treatment since the goal is restoring function and quality of life — yet what
actually determines satisfaction (versus dissatisfaction) after treatment remains poorly understood. The
study's specific aim: identify **preoperative** predictors, since these could be identified early enough for
clinicians to intervene and potentially improve outcomes. Data came from **six practice sites in the
Netherlands**, prospectively gathered, including both **primary and recurrent** limited fasciectomy cases, with
patients having a **concomitant confounding hand condition excluded.** The **Michigan Hand Outcomes
Questionnaire (MHQ)** was administered before surgery, at 6 months, and at 1 year. Satisfaction itself was
measured with a single item asking overall hand function satisfaction on a **5-point Likert scale**, with
"very satisfied" or "somewhat satisfied" coded as **satisfied**, everything else as **unsatisfied.**

## Study design

`[02:02]`–`[03:02]` — Since postoperative outcomes themselves could confound satisfaction, **residual
contracture and complications** were also assessed and controlled for. Bivariate analysis first tested
demographics, clinical characteristics, and preoperative MHQ subscores against satisfaction; factors showing
an association were carried into a multivariate model. After excluding 42 patients, **194 remained** in the
final study population; **84% were satisfied** with their hand function at an average of **10 months**
post-fasciectomy.

## Results

`[03:02]`–`[03:02]` — Bivariate analysis: proportionally **more men than women** were satisfied; patients
satisfied at follow-up had **higher preoperative satisfaction scores and higher preoperative hand-appearance
MHQ subscores**; other measured factors showed no association. As expected, postoperative outcomes also
mattered — satisfied patients had **less residual contracture and a lower complication rate.** In the final
**multivariate model**, controlling for those postoperative outcome differences, only **preoperative
hand-appearance subscore** remained a significant independent predictor, with **gender approaching but not
reaching significance.**

## Interpreting the hand-appearance finding

`[04:03]` — Zhou notes prior studies had already established fasciectomy's effectiveness from a **clinician's**
perspective, but frames this study as among the first to focus specifically on **patient satisfaction and its
determinants.** The 84% satisfaction rate is consistent with prior literature. Because the MHQ hand-appearance
subscore specifically asks whether patients feel **hampered in hand use, or feel depressed, by their hand's
appearance**, Zhou interprets the finding as evidence that **hand deformity carries real functional
significance to patients** — not merely a cosmetic concern, but one intertwined with how patients experience
their hand's actual usability and their own emotional wellbeing.

## The unresolved gender gap

`[04:03]`–`[05:03]` — Zhou is explicit that the underlying mechanism behind the male/female satisfaction
disparity is **not understood** — possible explanations offered only speculatively include differing
physiological response to surgery or differing patient expectations between genders, neither confirmed by this
study. The practical takeaway offered: **future satisfaction studies should account for the gender composition
of their sample**, since this gap is real and substantial even if its cause is unknown. A meaningful amount of
**unexplained variance in satisfaction remains** even after accounting for the identified predictors, which
Zhou frames as motivation for further research to identify additional, ideally modifiable, factors clinicians
could address.

## Symptoms and body signals addressed

- **Feeling hampered in hand use, or feeling depressed, specifically due to hand appearance** — the exact
  MHQ subscore items found to predict overall satisfaction, linking cosmetic perception to functional and
  emotional impact `[04:03]`.
- **Residual contracture and postoperative complications**, confirmed as outcome-level factors influencing
  satisfaction alongside the identified preoperative predictor `[02:02]`.
