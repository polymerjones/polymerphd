# Veronique Moulin PhD, "Tissue engineering and the myofibroblasts" 2010 Miami Dupuytren Symposium

## Central claim

**Using a tissue-engineered skin model, Moulin's lab found that keratinocytes from hypertrophic scar tissue
secrete a factor — identified as tissue inhibitor of metalloproteinase 1 (TIMP-1) — that by itself is enough
to make the underlying dermis grow thicker, and separately, that myofibroblasts (more than ordinary
fibroblasts) actively promote new capillary network formation, a role not previously appreciated for this
cell type.** Her closing summary states the throughline directly: **"interactions between numerous cell
populations contribute to normal and pathological tissue formation, and these parameters have to be thought
[of] when we study this different pathology"** `[09:18]`.

Note: this talk is framed throughout around **hypertrophic scarring**, not Dupuytren's disease by name — its
relevance to this library is that myofibroblasts are the shared pathological cell type central to both
conditions, and the talk was presented at this symposium on that basis.

## The tissue-engineering method

`[00:06]`–`[02:09]` — Moulin's lab uses a **self-assembly tissue engineering model**: dermal fibroblasts are
cultured with ascorbate so they build their own extracellular matrix into a sheet; keratinocytes are then
added on top, and once confluent, the skin/dermis interface is raised to air to mature the epidermis. The
stated advantage over standard cell culture: **cells experience conditions closer to those found in vivo**
`[00:06]`.

## Keratinocyte-driven dermal thickening

`[02:09]`–`[06:15]` — Two myofibroblast populations were compared (isolated from hypertrophic scar versus
normal wound), each paired with either hypertrophic-scar-derived or normal keratinocytes. Trichrome staining
showed: **regardless of which dermal (myofibroblast) population was used, adding hypertrophic-scar
keratinocytes produced a measurably thicker dermis than adding normal keratinocytes** `[03:10]`. Screening
keratinocyte culture supernatants by two-dimensional electrophoresis identified a protein spot present
strongly in hypertrophic-scar keratinocyte secretions and weak or absent in normal keratinocyte secretions;
sequencing identified it as **tissue inhibitor of metalloproteinase 1 (TIMP1)** — a protein that inhibits
matrix metalloproteinases/collagenase and has separately been shown to stimulate cell growth and inhibit
apoptosis `[04:12]`. ELISA confirmed **elevated TIMP1 in hypertrophic-scar keratinocyte supernatant**, both in
monolayer culture and in the full skin-equivalent model. Adding TIMP1 directly to a normal-keratinocyte skin
equivalent **reproduced the thickened-dermis effect seen with hypertrophic-scar keratinocytes**, and biopsy
findings from actual hypertrophic scar versus normal skin confirmed **higher keratinocyte-secreted TIMP1
in vivo** as well. Moulin's stated takeaway: hypertrophic scarring is usually thought of as primarily a dermal
pathology, but this shows **keratinocytes play a significant role in driving fibrosis formation** `[06:15]`.

## Myofibroblasts and angiogenesis

`[06:15]`–`[09:18]` — A second model used a **fibrin gel** (representing the earliest wound-healing
provisional matrix), combining endothelial cells with either normal fibroblasts or myofibroblasts (normal- or
hypertrophic-scar-derived), assessed at 40 days for capillary-like network formation and quantified as an
**angiogenic index**. Results: endothelial cells alone formed only a weak capillary-like network; adding
mesenchymal cells (fibroblasts or myofibroblasts) increased the angiogenic index; **adding myofibroblasts —
and especially hypertrophic-scar-derived myofibroblasts — produced a significantly stronger increase than
plain fibroblasts.** Moulin frames the novelty explicitly: it was already known fibroblasts could support
capillary formation, but **"it's very new that myofibroblasts can have a role during the angiogenesis"**
`[08:17]` — myofibroblasts had previously been understood mainly for their role in matrix contraction, not
in promoting new vessel growth.

## Symptoms and body signals addressed

- **Dermal thickening driven by a keratinocyte-secreted factor**, demonstrated to be reproducible by adding
  that single factor (TIMP1) alone `[05:14]`.
- **Capillary/angiogenic network formation** as a previously unrecognized function of myofibroblasts,
  quantified via an angiogenic index `[08:17]`.

## Glossary terms introduced

- **Self-assembly tissue engineering model** — the ascorbate-driven method of growing a fibroblast-built
  dermal matrix, then adding keratinocytes and raising to air, to model skin in vitro `[00:06]`.
- **TIMP1 (tissue inhibitor of metalloproteinase 1)** — the keratinocyte-secreted protein identified as
  driving dermal thickening; also noted to inhibit apoptosis and stimulate cell growth independent of its
  matrix-inhibiting role `[04:12]`.
- **Angiogenic index** — the quantified measure used to compare capillary-like network strength across cell
  combinations in the fibrin gel model `[07:16]`.

## Source-stated confidence

Moulin discloses no financial relationships. She presents the TIMP1 finding as validated across three
independent methods (2D electrophoresis/sequencing, ELISA, and direct biopsy comparison), and is explicit that
the angiogenic role for myofibroblasts is a genuinely new finding relative to prior literature, distinguishing
it clearly from the already-established role of fibroblasts (not myofibroblasts specifically) in capillary
support.

## Conflicts with other sources

**Complements the myofibroblast biology described in this library's other cellular-mechanism notes** — this
talk supplies laboratory evidence for two specific myofibroblast/keratinocyte functions (TIMP1-mediated dermal
thickening, angiogenesis support) at a level of mechanistic detail not covered by the clinical/surgical talks
in this library. Its scope is explicitly hypertrophic scarring rather than Dupuytren's cord tissue directly —
readers should treat the angiogenesis and TIMP1 findings as evidence from a related fibrotic condition sharing
myofibroblast biology, not as direct findings about Dupuytren's cords themselves.
