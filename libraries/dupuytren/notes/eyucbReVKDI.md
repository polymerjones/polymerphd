# Dr. Jesper Nordenskjold "Using Local Anesthesia Before Collagenase" 2015 Dupuytren Symposium

## Central claim

**In a randomized comparison, giving local anesthesia 20 minutes before the collagenase injection itself
significantly reduced patient-reported injection pain — mean VAS 2.3 versus 4.3, a difference of 2.1 points —
though pain experienced at home in the following one to two days before manipulation was the same in both
groups, and the department has since adopted local anesthesia as routine practice as a direct result.**
Nordenskjold states the finding directly: **"we can see significantly lower VAS mean scores for the group
that got local anesthesia 20 minutes before the collagenase injection — 2.3, in contrast to a mean of 4.3 for
the no-LA group, with a mean difference of minus 2.1"** `[04:03]`.

## The question behind the study

`[00:00]`–`[01:01]` — At Nordenskjold's department (a hospital in southern Sweden), collagenase is the
**first-line treatment** for Dupuytren's contracture, with **475 patients treated since 2011.** It was already
standard practice to give local anesthesia before the **finger manipulation/extension** step, since that
procedure is known to be painful. The open question this study addresses: is the **collagenase injection
itself** — separate from the later manipulation — also painful enough to warrant local anesthesia
beforehand? Clinical observation suggested patients experienced substantial pain specifically at the time of
injection, motivating this trial.

## Study design

`[01:01]`–`[03:03]` — **162 consecutive patients** meeting standard inclusion criteria (extension deficit of
at least 20 degrees at the MCP or PIP joint, palpable cord) were **alternately assigned** (quasi-randomized) to
a **local anesthesia (LA) group**, receiving anesthetic **20 minutes before the collagenase injection**, or a
**no-LA group.** A single surgeon performed all injections using a modified technique, delivering collagenase
into multiple spots along the cord. **Mepivacaine, diluted with sodium bicarbonate** (to reduce the pain of
the local anesthetic itself, per prior literature), was the anesthetic used. Pain was measured via the
**visual analog scale (VAS, 0–10)** at the time of the first injection (whichever came first, LA or
collagenase), and again when patients returned **one to two days later** for the manipulation procedure, at
which point they also rated the pain experienced **at home** in the interim.

## Sample size planning

`[03:03]`–`[04:03]` — The team reviewed the literature and identified **a 1.3-point difference in VAS score as
the established minimum clinically important difference**, drawn from two large prior studies, and used this
to calculate a required sample size of **50 patients per group.** Analysis used mean VAS scores and standard
deviation, with a covariance-adjusted (ANCOVA) approach.

## Results

`[04:03]`–`[05:05]` — **Injection-time pain**: LA group mean VAS **2.3** versus no-LA group mean **4.3** — a
**statistically significant** difference of 2.1 points, comfortably exceeding the pre-specified 1.3-point
MCID. **Pain at home between visits** (before the manipulation step): **essentially identical between groups,
2.9 for both** — the anesthesia given before injection had no detectable carryover effect on pain experienced
in the following days.

## Symptoms and body signals addressed

- **Pain specifically at the moment of collagenase injection**, quantified and distinguished from
  manipulation-related pain and from pain experienced at home in the days between visits `[04:03]`.

## Source-stated confidence

Nordenskjold, presenting as a new PhD student and incoming resident, discloses he personally has no conflicts
but is transparent that his co-author does (shown directly on the slide). He is candid that the study used
**alternating rather than true randomization** across scheduled treatment sessions, and reports a clean null
result for the home-pain comparison rather than selectively emphasizing only the positive injection-pain
finding. The department's resulting practice change (adopting routine local anesthesia before collagenase
injection) is presented as a direct, stated consequence of this specific study's result.
