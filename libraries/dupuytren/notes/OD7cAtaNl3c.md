# Dr. Gary M Pess "Collagenase Clostridium Histolyticum Treatment" 2015 Dupuytren Symposium

## Central claim

**In the same 714-patient concurrent double-dose collagenase trial reported from the statistical side in
`bKXguv2CscM`, Pess found that delaying the finger manipulation step to 48 or 72 hours (instead of the standard
24) produced no difference in efficacy or overall safety — and the skin laceration rate was actually *lower*
at 72 hours than at 24 or 48.** He states this directly: **"timing of the finger extension procedure did not
influence the clinical response in terms of efficacy or safety, and the rate of lacerations was lower at 72
hours compared to 24 and 48 hours"** `[05:04]`.

## Why test delayed manipulation

`[00:01]`–`[01:03]` — Collagenase's standard protocol involves injecting the drug to lyse the cord, followed
traditionally by manipulation/finger extension **24 hours later** — a fixed window Pess notes **may not be
convenient for patient or physician** scheduling. This talk reports a subset of a larger study (the same
concurrent double-dose trial covered from the efficacy-data side in `bKXguv2CscM`) specifically examining
whether manipulation could be **safely delayed** past 24 hours.

## Study design

`[01:03]`–`[02:03]` — A large, multi-center, worldwide study: **714 subjects analyzed for efficacy and
safety, across 724 joint pairs.** All patients had **two or more contractures of at least 20 degrees**, each
receiving **two full 0.58mg injections** (separate vials) into two affected joints in the same hand during one
visit. The finger extension procedure was performed at **24, 48, or 72+ hours** after injection, with outcomes
assessed at **31 days.**

## Results — consistent across manipulation timing

`[02:03]`–`[03:04]` — Fixed flexion contracture improvement was **similar regardless of whether manipulation
occurred at 24, 48, or 72 hours**:

| Joint | 24h | 48h | 72h |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Total joints** | 75% | 75% | 73% |
| **MP** | 84% | 81% | 84% |
| **PIP** | 64% | 66% | 60% |

Range of motion improvement was likewise consistent across timing groups (roughly 68% for total joints, 36%
for MP, 30% for PIP). **Clinical efficacy** (defined as correction to 5 degrees or less): **65% for MP joints,
29% for PIP joints** — again with no meaningful difference by manipulation timing.

## Safety

`[03:04]`–`[05:04]` — Common adverse events were similar to those reported in the original CORD I and CORD II
trials. **Laceration rate was higher overall** than in CORD I/II, but notably **lower specifically at the
72-hour timepoint** than at 24 or 48 hours — Pess offers the interpretation that a little extra time may allow
the treated area to "calm down" before manipulation. **No deaths** occurred; **one anaphylactic reaction** was
reported across the study.

## Case example

`[03:04]`–`[04:04]` — A 64-year-old man, 20-year history of Dupuytren's disease, no ectopic disease, no prior
treatment, presenting with difficulty shaking hands. Baseline contracture: **ring finger MP 40°, ring finger
PIP 15°, little finger MP 50°.** Two injections were placed **between the distal palmar crease and proximal
finger crease**, deliberately avoiding flexion creases and seeking skin not tethered over the cord. Typical
side effects (pain, swelling, ecchymosis) followed. At **day 61**, post-manipulation: **ring finger MP and PIP
0°, little finger MP 5°** — described as excellent range of motion achieved without any additional therapy.

## Symptoms and body signals addressed

- **Difficulty shaking hands**, the specific functional complaint that brought the case-example patient to
  treatment `[03:04]`.
- **Pain, swelling, and ecchymosis**, the typical side effects following collagenase injection, distinguished
  from the more serious but rarer laceration and anaphylaxis events `[04:04]`.

## Source-stated confidence

Pess discloses the study's sponsor (Auxilium) directly, echoing a disclosure he notes was also made by a
co-presenter (Dr. Hurst). He reports the elevated overall laceration rate candidly rather than omitting it,
and is specific that the reduced laceration rate at 72 hours is his own interpretation ("may help it calm down
a little") rather than a mechanistically proven explanation. The one anaphylactic reaction is reported plainly
alongside the otherwise favorable safety data rather than downplayed.

## Conflicts with other sources

**Directly complements `bKXguv2CscM`** (Badalamente's "Concurrent Collagenase Injections" talk) — these two
talks report the same underlying trial from different angles: Badalamente's talk presents the overall joint-
pairing efficacy and safety data, while this talk isolates the specific question of manipulation timing
flexibility, a practical scheduling question that talk does not address. **Relevant to `OwSS6grbMAc`**
(Badalamente's original CORD I trial talk) — this study's laceration-rate comparison is made directly against
CORD I/II's historical safety data, useful context for interpreting the elevated laceration rate reported here.
**Relevant to `eyucbReVKDI`** (Nordenskjold's local anesthesia before collagenase talk) — both talks focus on
practical, patient-experience-oriented refinements to collagenase administration technique (anesthesia timing
there, manipulation timing here) rather than on drug efficacy itself.
