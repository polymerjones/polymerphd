# Drs. Ilse Degreef and Marieke Torrekens "Full-Thickness Skin Grafting" 2015 Dupuytren Symposium

## Central claim

**Reviewing 47 patients at a mean 8-year-4-month follow-up, this study found a 0% true recurrence rate
underneath full-thickness skin grafts — confirming Hueston's original claim — even though the patients
studied had high diathesis scores that would predict high recurrence risk under ordinary surgery.**
Torrekens states the headline result directly: **"the true recurrence rate underneath the skin graft was
zero percent, which confirmed the early findings of Hueston. The mean [diathesis] score was 4.5 — so quite
high, meaning a higher risk for recurrence, but we didn't see any of it in our study"** `[04:06]`.

## Three outcomes, carefully separated

`[03:05]`–`[04:06]` — The study's methodological core is distinguishing three outcomes that are often
conflated in the literature:

- **True recurrence**: new Dupuytren's lesions developing *within* the previously operated area.
- **False recurrence**: joint contracture or extrinsic tendon imbalance arising from surgical complications
  (a non-developing scar contracture), not from new disease activity.
- **Extension**: new lesions appearing *outside* the previously operated area, where no disease had
  previously been detected.

## Results

`[04:06]`–`[05:08]` — Cohort: 47 patients, 87% male / 13% female, mean follow-up 8 years 4 months. A
diathesis score based on named risk factors was calculated for each patient alongside a QuickDASH score and
clinical examination.

- **True recurrence under the graft: 0%.**
- **False recurrence: 50% of cases** — attributed to graft infection/incomplete take in one case, and in
  others to pre-existing scar tissue from earlier, unrelated surgery in the same area.
- **Extension: 83% of cases**, predominantly affecting the **PIP joint of the fifth digit**. The extension
  group's mean diathesis score was **4.7**, versus **3.5** in patients without extension — the diathesis
  score predicted extension, even though it did not predict any true recurrence under the graft itself.
- **Patient satisfaction**: 91% rated the outcome good or excellent; the remaining 9% reported complaints of
  finger stiffness or cold sensitivity.

## Interpreting the finding

`[05:08]`–`[06:09]` — The authors' reading: **replacing the skin itself appears to be doing something
important**, since true recurrence was absent even in high-diathesis patients who would otherwise be expected
to recur. They note the extension pattern is plausibly linked to the same underlying fibrosis diathesis, and
that the choice to graft is itself a surgeon's judgment call — typically reserved for patients already
recognized as recurrence-prone — carrying its own trade-offs (a more elaborate procedure, with risks
including infection and flap-related complications that can compromise outcome).

## A historical tribute to Hueston

`[00:02]`–`[02:04]`, `[06:09]`–`[08:11]` — The talk opens and closes with an explicit homage to Hueston,
noting that many concepts used today were anticipated in his historical writing — sometimes independently
reinvented by later researchers rather than directly copied — and that **Hueston attempted a collagenase-like
product as early as 1971**, published in "Poor Trans" (a garbled journal name in the transcript). The authors
frame full-thickness grafting as still having a defined place today: for **disease control, avoiding severe
recurrent disease, use in complex revision surgery, and technical skin coverage** — closing with the
observation that **myofibroblasts have been seen to physically attach to skin**, offered as one plausible
reason the graft interrupts recurrence.

## Symptoms and body signals addressed

- **Finger stiffness and cold sensitivity**, the specific complaints among the minority of patients not rating
  their outcome good or excellent `[05:08]`.
- **Joint contracture and extrinsic tendon imbalance from surgical complication**, explicitly distinguished
  from true disease recurrence `[03:05]`.

## Glossary terms introduced

- **True recurrence** — new Dupuytren's lesions within the previously operated area `[03:05]`.
- **False recurrence** — contracture or tendon imbalance from surgical complication, not new disease activity
  `[03:05]`.
- **Extension** — new lesions outside the previously operated area `[04:06]`.

## Source-stated confidence

Both presenters disclose no conflicts of interest. They are explicit that this is a retrospective review with
a specific, narrow question (does true recurrence occur under a graft) rather than a randomized comparison,
and they candidly note the risk of research scope creep — posing additional questions (follow-up length,
diathesis importance) beyond the original one. They also note the historical literature on full-thickness
grafting shows a cycle of high enthusiasm followed by lower enthusiasm and reported recurrences, adding that
it is **"not clear... this was actually extensions or occurrences"** in that older literature — i.e., the same
true-recurrence/extension conflation this study set out to resolve may have affected earlier reports too.

## Conflicts with other sources

**Directly extends the historical grafting rationale referenced across this library's surgical-technique
talks** — this study is a modern, carefully defined re-test of Hueston's original "no recurrence under a
graft" claim, distinguishing it methodologically from studies that don't separate true recurrence from
extension. **Relevant to `R4WPhcOpAMo`** (Selles's consensus recurrence definition talk) — this study's
three-way true-recurrence/false-recurrence/extension distinction is a more granular breakdown than that
consensus definition's single angular-contracture threshold, and illustrates concretely why measurement
definition matters so much for reported recurrence rates. **Relevant to any note discussing diathesis or
fibrosis-prone patients as a distinct risk category** — this talk's finding that diathesis score predicted
extension but not true recurrence under a graft is a specific, quantified data point for that broader pattern.
