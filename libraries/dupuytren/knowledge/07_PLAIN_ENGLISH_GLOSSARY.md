# Plain-English Glossary

Terms introduced across this library's sources, defined in plain English. Alphabetical. No
citations here — see the individual source for the origin of each term.

**Arthrolysis** — surgical release of a joint's own soft-tissue restrictions (separate from cutting
the diseased cord itself), used when a joint doesn't fully correct after the cord is divided.

**Beta-catenin** — a signaling protein central to the Wnt pathway; implicated both in disease
spread through the matrix and, separately, in TNF's effect on Dupuytren's fibroblasts.

**Collagenase clostridium histolyticum (Xiaflex)** — an enzyme injected directly into a diseased
cord to dissolve its collagen, avoiding a mechanical cut or open incision.

**Conditioned matrix / conditioned collagen** — collagen that has been exposed to factors secreted
by disease cells, shown to convert genetically normal cells into disease-marker-expressing cells
when those normal cells are grown on it.

**Cord** — the mature, rope-like fibrous band that pulls a finger into contracture, as distinguished
from an earlier, non-contracting nodule.

**Dermofasciectomy** — surgical removal of both the diseased fascia and the overlying skin, with
the skin replaced by a graft; considered a biology-modifying rather than purely mechanical
procedure because of the graft's apparent recurrence-protective effect.

**Diathesis** — a bundle of soft, unformalized risk markers (family history, early onset, ectopic
disease, bilateral involvement) associated with higher recurrence risk, without being a formal
predictive score.

**Ectopic disease** — Dupuytren's-related fibrotic disease appearing away from the palm, such as
Ledderhose disease (sole of the foot) or Peyronie's disease (penis).

**Extracellular matrix (ECM)** — the structural material surrounding cells; argued in this
collection to actively participate in disease spread, not just passively support diseased cells.

**Fasciectomy (limited, open, palmar, digital)** — surgical excision of diseased fascia, in several
named variants differing in scope and location.

**Fasciotomy** — division (rather than removal) of a diseased cord, either through an open incision
or, more commonly in this collection, percutaneously with a needle.

**Fire break implant** — a thin, resorbable barrier (in this collection, cellulose) placed within a
surgical incision to physically separate cut cord ends and reduce early rebound.

**Funnel plot** — a statistical chart with control limits, used here both to detect inter-hospital
variation in surgical rates and to monitor individual surgeon performance.

**GRADE approach** — a formal system for rating the certainty of evidence in a systematic review or
meta-analysis.

**Ledderhose disease** — a fibrotic condition of the plantar (sole) fascia, treated in this
collection as a related manifestation of the same underlying diathesis as Dupuytren's.

**MCID (minimum clinically important difference)** — the smallest change in a patient-reported
outcome score that actually matters to the patient; named in this collection as a benchmark the
field has not yet formally established.

**Michigan Hand Outcomes Questionnaire (MHQ)** — a patient-rated outcome instrument used to measure
hand function and appearance, among other domains.

**MMP / TIMP (matrix metalloproteinases / tissue inhibitors of metalloproteinases)** — the paired
system controlling collagen breakdown; in Dupuytren's tissue, this balance is shifted toward
inhibition, meaning less collagen gets degraded.

**Myofibroblast** — a specialized, contractile cell type (not an ordinary fibroblast) treated
across this collection as the central driver of both contraction and, through matrix signaling,
disease spread.

**Nodule** — an early, palpable lump of diseased tissue in the palm, distinguished from a cord by
not yet pulling the finger into contracture.

**Percutaneous needle fasciotomy (PNF, needle aponeurotomy)** — division of a diseased cord using a
needle inserted through intact skin, without an open surgical incision.

**Periostin** — a matrix-associated signaling protein raised as a candidate marker in the
matrix-mediated disease-spread hypothesis.

**Peyronie's disease** — a fibrotic condition of the penis's tunica albuginea, reported in this
collection to co-occur with Dupuytren's in roughly a fifth of a large patient series.

**Post-mitotic shortcut** — the proposed mechanism by which radiotherapy disables actively
proliferating myofibroblasts before they can complete division, rather than killing already-mature
cells directly.

**PROM (patient-rated outcome measure)** — a standardized way of capturing a patient's own
assessment of an outcome, now treated as a required component of clinical research in this
collection's sources rather than an optional supplement to objective measurement.

**Propensity score matching** — a statistical technique for comparing treatment groups from
real-world (non-randomized) data by matching patients with similar characteristics across groups.

**Recontracture** — mechanical loss of a correction achieved at treatment, whether or not new
disease tissue has actually formed; distinguished in this collection from true recurrence.

**Recurrence (true)** — the reappearance of active disease tissue after treatment, as distinguished
from recontracture; one of this collection's most contested and inconsistently defined terms (see
`05_RECURRENCE_AND_OUTCOME_MEASUREMENT.md`).

**Skeletal traction** — a technique using external pins and tension to gradually stretch severely
contracted tissue prior to fasciotomy, used for severe PIP joint contracture.

**Tubiana staging** — a severity classification system for Dupuytren's contracture, with specific
degree ranges attached to each numbered stage.

**Wnt/beta-catenin signaling** — a cell-signaling pathway implicated in disease spread and, via a
TNF-GSK-3-beta interaction, in myofibroblast contractility.
