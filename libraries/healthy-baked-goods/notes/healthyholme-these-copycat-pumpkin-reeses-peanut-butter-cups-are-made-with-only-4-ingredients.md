# Copycat Pumpkin Reese's Peanut Butter Cups

## Central claim

**A no-bake, four-ingredient copycat of Reese's peanut butter cups molded into pumpkin shapes,
sweetened with maple syrup instead of refined sugar.** `[¶1]` "These copycat Pumpkin Reeses
Peanut Butter Cups are made with only 4 ingredients and are refined sugar free!" The Instagram
caption gates the printable recipe and mold link behind a comment (`[¶1]` "Comment 'recipe' and
I'll send you the link to the silicone mold and the printable recipe"), so the full recipe is
drawn from the creator's own blog post, fetched here as a second source.

## Ingredients

`` `pumpkin-reeses-cups-healthyholme`[¶1] `` — the blog's own recipe card (yields 8 peanut butter
pumpkins):

| Ingredient | Amount | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Natural peanut butter | 1 cup | Room temperature "so it's drippy" — only peanuts and sea salt |
| Pure maple syrup | ⅓ cup | The sole sweetener |
| Coconut flour | 2 tbsp | Thickens the filling |
| Dark chocolate | 1 cup, melted | Chosen for "lower sugar content" |
| Coconut oil or MCT oil | 1 tsp | Optional, thins the chocolate for dipping |

## Method

`` `pumpkin-reeses-cups-healthyholme`[¶1] `` Mix the peanut butter, maple syrup, and coconut flour;
let sit a minute to thicken, then press into a pumpkin-shaped silicone mold and freeze until solid
(at least 1 hour). Unmold and dip each piece in melted dark chocolate (thinned with the optional
oil), then set on parchment or a silicone mat. Drizzle any leftover chocolate over the top once all
pieces are dipped. Store in the refrigerator.

Two troubleshooting notes from the blog: freeze the peanut-butter centers completely solid before
dipping so they hold their shape in the hot chocolate, and dip/flip/remove quickly rather than
leaving them submerged (`[¶1]`). To get the mold's decorative pumpkin detail to show through the
chocolate rather than being obscured, the blog describes an alternate build order — line the mold
with chocolate first, freeze 2 minutes, fill with the peanut butter mixture, freeze again, then cap
with more chocolate — flagged as more failure-prone: "sometimes the chocolate cracks if the mold is
not completely covered with a thick layer of chocolate" (`[¶1]`).

per both the Instagram caption and the blog (`[¶1]`).
