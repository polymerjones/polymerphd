# Healthy Pumpkin Spice Cheesecake Bites

## Central claim

**A no-bake, freezer-set cheesecake bite built on plain Greek yogurt and pumpkin purée
instead of cream cheese, pressed into a graham-cracker crumb base in muffin molds.**
`[¶7]` "1 cup plain Greek yogurt / ½ cup pumpkin purée / 2 tbsp maple syrup or honey / 1 tsp
vanilla extract / 1 tsp pumpkin pie spice / ⅓ cup brown sugar / 1 cup graham cracker
crumbs / 2 tbsp melted coconut oil or butter." Optional chopped pecans or walnuts.

## Ingredients

Plain Greek yogurt (1 cup), pumpkin purée (½ cup), maple syrup or honey (2 tbsp), vanilla
extract (1 tsp), pumpkin pie spice (1 tsp), brown sugar (⅓ cup), graham cracker crumbs
(1 cup), melted coconut oil or butter (2 tbsp). Optional: chopped pecans or walnuts.

## Method

`[¶9]` Mix the Greek yogurt, pumpkin purée, maple syrup, vanilla, brown sugar, and pumpkin
pie spice until smooth. Separately, mix the graham cracker crumbs with the melted coconut
oil or butter. Press the crumb mixture into silicone muffin molds, then spoon the pumpkin
cheesecake mixture on top. Freeze 2–3 hours, or until firm. `[¶10]` Optional: drizzle with
maple syrup and crushed pecans/walnuts before serving.

## Cravings, occasions, and dietary needs addressed

`[¶1]` Framed explicitly as a "brrr months"/fall craving, positioned as an indulgence with
a nutritional upside: `[¶3]` "protein and calcium from the Greek yogurt, along with fiber,
potassium and vitamin A from the pumpkin." `[¶4]` Also framed as an evening/post-workout
treat, since "Greek yogurt also contains casein, a slower-digesting protein." `[¶5]` The
creator explicitly invites substitution: "you could try chocolate, peanut butter, berries
or lemon depending on what you're craving" in place of pumpkin.

## Glossary terms introduced

**Casein** — `[¶4]` described by the creator as "a slower-digesting protein" found in
Greek yogurt, offered as the reason this treat suits an evening/post-workout timing; kept
here as the creator's own stated reasoning, not verified against outside nutrition science.

cracker crunch, and taste like cheesecake without feeling quite as heavy") as tested fact,
but frames the casein/digestion reasoning in § Glossary as an explanation offered alongside
the recipe rather than a citation to any study — preserved as her own stated rationale.

## Conflicts with other sources

`fantabulosity-blog-coconut-cheesecake` bakes its cheesecake in an oven with a full-fat
cream-cheese base; this recipe is unbaked and frozen, and substitutes Greek yogurt
throughout — a substantially different technique for the same "cheesecake" category, not a
disagreement so much as two different approaches to the same dessert name.
