# Double Chocolate Protein Muffins (No Protein Powder)

## Central claim

**A one-bowl chocolate muffin that reaches 8g of protein per muffin — "more than double" a
typical muffin's 3–4g — using only whole-food ingredients (Greek yogurt, eggs, milk) instead
of protein powder, deliberately, so the creator's kids would eat them and so the texture and
taste stay closer to a bakery muffin.** `[¶1]` "My kids say these taste like chocolate
cupcakes, and they happen to have 8g (no protein powder) and 3g fiber so they're filling and
satisfying too!" `` `lindsaypleskot-rd-double-chocolate-protein-muffins-blog`[¶1] `` "Most
chocolate protein muffins rely on protein powder, but not these beauties. That was
intentional. For one, I wanted my kids to be able to eat them too, and honestly, I just don't
love the taste of protein powder."

## Ingredients

`[¶4]` Plain Greek yogurt (1¼ cups, 2–5% m.f.), eggs (2, large), maple syrup (½ cup), olive
oil (⅓ cup), milk (⅓ cup), pure vanilla extract (1 Tbsp), all-purpose flour (1½ cups + 2
Tbsp), unsweetened cocoa powder (½ cup), baking powder (2½ tsp), baking soda (½ tsp), salt (1
tsp), instant espresso powder (1 tsp, optional), chocolate chips (⅔ cup, plus more for
topping). The creator's own blog version of this recipe states slightly different amounts for
three of these — `` `lindsaypleskot-rd-double-chocolate-protein-muffins-blog`[¶1] `` milk
(½ cup rather than ⅓), flour (1¾ cups rather than 1½ cups + 2 Tbsp), and baking powder (3 tsp
rather than 2½) — a real discrepancy between the two publications of the same recipe, not
reconciled here.

## Method

`[¶5]` Preheat to 350°F; line a muffin tin. Whisk the Greek yogurt, eggs, maple syrup, olive
oil, milk, and vanilla in a large bowl. Into the same bowl, add the flour first, then cocoa
powder and chocolate chips, then sprinkle the baking powder, baking soda, and salt evenly over
the top — "since we are doing this all in one bowl, this will help ensure even distribution of
the ingredients." Mix "until just combined, being careful not to overmix." Divide between
muffin cups, filling about ¾ full; top with extra chocolate chips if desired. Bake 22–24
minutes "until the muffins are set and a toothpick inserted into the middle comes out mostly
clean with a few moist crumbs." Cool in the pan 10 minutes, then finish cooling on a rack. The
blog version adds one step not in the caption: `` `lindsaypleskot-rd-double-chocolate-protein-muffins-blog`[¶1] ``
"let the batter rest for 10 minutes before baking (this hydrates the flour, helping them rise
better!)."

## Cravings, occasions, and dietary needs addressed

A chocolate-cupcake craving reframed as a protein- and fiber-forward, nut-free, kid-approved
lunchbox muffin. `[¶1]` "They're also nut-free and I promise they won't come back home in
lunchboxes."

## Source-stated confidence

The creator, a registered dietitian, frames this as an iteratively tested recipe rather than a
first attempt: `` `lindsaypleskot-rd-double-chocolate-protein-muffins-blog`[¶1] `` "I Tested
These Chocolate Muffins A LOT, So You Don't Have To." She reports specific rejected
alternatives and why — whole wheat flour "consistently muted the chocolate flavour and made
the muffins dry out over time," despite adding "less than 1 gram of extra fiber" over
all-purpose; banana was tested for moisture but dropped because "the flavour came through more
than I wanted"; and the batter-rest step was added only after early batches didn't rise the
way she wanted. She also explicitly caveats the protein framing itself: "you don't need 20
grams of protein in a muffin for it to be nourishing. Balanced nutrition matters far more than
chasing numbers."

## Conflicts with other sources

No other note in this library builds protein into a baked good purely from Greek yogurt, eggs,
and milk while explicitly avoiding protein powder — `theflexibledietinglifestyle-172-cal-oreo-protein-cheesecake`
and `rationfit-apple-cheesecake` both lean on Greek yogurt too, but for a crustless cheesecake
base rather than a flour-based bake, and neither states a reason for skipping protein powder.
No genuine conflict; this is a distinct technique for the same underlying goal.
